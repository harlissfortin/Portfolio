# The 2-Minute Rule: One Shift on a Patch Line

A narrated motion-graphics safety training video (about 6:26) about the **2-minute rule**: a short pause, on purpose,
at the point of work, before you start and again after any break, handover, interruption or change. It is set at a
transdermal patch manufacturing site. It teaches the rule and where its limits are, models a routine pause that finds
nothing, then follows one shift on a patch line through four **two-minute saves**. A shift strip shows where each one
happens: the routine Go at the start of the shift, Save 1 early, Save 2 just after the break, Save 3 mid-shift and
Save 4 late. In every save the video stops twice and asks the viewer first: *What would make you stop here?* and
*Your call?* Each save is impactful without being morbid: what the pause caught, the call and why, the steps, and
what it prevented.

**Audience:** frontline staff at a transdermal patch manufacturing site (mixing, coating and drying, laminating,
slitting, die cutting, pouching and packaging, warehouse and roll staging, cleaning, maintenance, contractors) and
their supervisors.

**By the end, the learner can:**
- say when to run the pause: before you start, and after any break, handover, interruption or change
- run Stop · Look · Ask · Decide with the four questions: *What's different? What could hurt us? Checked or assumed?
  What if it goes wrong?*
- choose **Go**, **Fix it** (only if trained, authorized and it's a step in your procedure) or **Stop & get help** (no
  permit yet, someone else's unfinished work, not authorized, or not sure), and say why. The video asks for the
  call in all four saves ("Your call?"); the huddles practise it with local sorting cards and a decision check.
- give an example from their own area where a lamp, a note, a lock, a card or a PPE board would be the catch (a huddle
  activity in the facilitator guide)

## The four saves

| | Save | When in the shift | Trigger | Question that catches it | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Go | A routine Go: moving a laminate roll with the roll lifter | Start of shift | Before you start | All four come back clean; "What if it goes wrong?" is answered only here ("Nobody's in the aisle") | Go | Nothing different, grip tested |
| 1 | Solvent transfer | Early | Before you start | Checked or assumed? | Fix it | A step in your procedure |
| 2 | Roll staging | Just after the break | After a break | What's different? | Stop & get help | Not authorized: trained on the pallet truck, not the forklift |
| 3 | Die cutter | Mid-shift | Handover | Checked or assumed? | Stop & get help | Someone else's unfinished work |
| 4 | Potent product | Late | Change | What could hurt us? | Fix it | Your own gowning step |

Two Fix it, two Stop & get help, and all four end in Go once it's safe.

Each save follows the same template so a facilitator can show it on its own:
1. A one-second Stop · Look · Ask · Decide sting.
2. The setup, with the clue already in the picture. The top band names the save and its trigger, and the shift strip
   under it marks where in the shift the save happens.
3. **"What would make you stop here?"** A 3-second hold with a filling ring. Viewers look first.
4. The catch: the question lights on the rail and a red ring lands on the clue.
5. **"Your call?"** A 2.4-second hold with the three calls in outline. The right call fills in on the reveal.
6. The call, with its reason spoken and shown in the case card.
7. The steps, in the correct order, and who does them.
8. "What it prevented", stated plainly.

## Design choices

- **One shift.** The four saves happen across one shift on one patch line, from the mix room to the slitter. The shift
  strip under each save's title shows where it falls; only its dot moves, so each chapter still plays alone in a
  huddle.
- **The viewer does something.** Eight prediction holds turn four demonstrations into four practice items, and a
  "Your call?" in the routine Go makes Go a live answer too. Solo viewers are told to pause; in a huddle, the
  facilitator pauses at each hold (the facilitator guide lists the timestamps).
- **The decision boundary is taught, not just shown.** *Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in
  your procedure. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop & get help. Not sure, or the
  fix doesn't work? Stop & get help.* Every call in every save carries its reason, the button simply says "Fix it",
  and the recap shows both halves of the rule. The contrast card's examples are patch-line jobs that are not the saves,
  so the holds test the rule, not recall.
- **Each save teaches a different part of the rule.** Save 1: a step in your procedure (and "Won't go green? Stop & get
  help" for a fix that doesn't work). Save 2: not authorized. Save 3: someone else's unfinished work. Save 4: your own
  gowning step.
- **The routine pause is modelled.** Most pauses find nothing and take seconds, so the first worked example is one of
  those. It also sets up Save 2's contrast: roll handling is a Go with the lifter you're trained on, and a Stop with a
  truck you're not.
- **Pressure and speaking up are shown.** In Save 2 a co-worker means well ("Line's waiting. Just grab the
  forklift!"), and you answer kindly: "Thanks, but I'm not trained on that one. I'll call our lead."
- **Impactful, not morbid.** No death, injury, flame, falling load or overdose is shown or described. The impact comes
  from the moment of the catch and a plain line on what it prevented, such as "No static spark where the vapor comes
  out" or "Nobody drives a truck they're not trained on". Save 1's spark is drawn dashed, as a "what if". Save 4
  speaks of small, repeated doses through the skin, with no exposure-band numbers.
- **Drawn safely.** Save 2 never shows anyone on a truck they're not trained on, climbing the rack or under the forks.
  Everyone stands clear of the forks and the truck's path, the driver wears the seatbelt, and the trip down is shown
  as a slow dissolve to the end state, so no forklift is seen moving near people. "Go" is heard only once the pallet
  is down and the forks are flat.
- **Proportionate.** The four saves split two fixes and two calls for help, and every one ends in Go once it's safe.
- **Natural delivery.** Each line is voiced in one pass, so the voice keeps its intonation and punctuation carries the
  pauses. The spoken prompts at the holds vary from save to save while the cards stay the same, and every save names
  its trigger out loud.
- **Plain language for a multilingual floor.** About 150 words a minute while speaking, a calm conversational pace.
  Jargon is glossed on first use: bonding, die station, potent ("strong in tiny amounts"), roll lifter, forklift and
  pallet truck, roll staging, PPE ("your protective gear", on screen) and checked or assumed. The transcript describes
  what is on screen as well as what is said.
- **Who's on screen.** An operator in solvent PPE, a co-worker who wants to help, your lead in a teal vest, a trained
  forklift driver, the technician who owns the lock, and an operator in a coat who adds double gloves and sleeve
  covers. Faces are never drawn.
- **Sound.** A quiet pad sits about 25 dB under the voice so pauses aren't dead air, and soft cues mark each save, each
  catch, each call, each Go and the seconds of each hold. Everything is synthesized in [`tooling/mix.py`](tooling/mix.py)
  (no samples, no licences).
- **Illustrative and unnamed.** The use cases are composites built from researched transdermal-manufacturing hazards.
  No employer or person is named, and no failure is pinned on a worker.
- **Earlier versions.** The first version used a real 2011 fatality as its example; it was replaced at the user's
  request with focused, non-morbid use cases. The versions after it, up to version 4, were set in pharmaceutical
  manufacturing, with six saves (8:57 in version 4). Version 5 is this one: a transdermal patch site, four saves on
  one shift, and a material-handling Save 2 in place of a drying-oven save, at the user's request. See
  [`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md).

## Sources

On the end card:
- [21 CFR 211.130(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-G/section-211.130), packaging and labeling facilities inspected immediately before use (line clearance)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147), the control of hazardous energy (lockout/tagout), including (e)(3) on removing another person's lock (Save 3)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.106(e)(6)(ii)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.106) and NFPA 77, *Recommended Practice on Static Electricity*: bond the nozzle and the container before dispensing Category 1 or 2 flammable liquids, or Category 3 below 100 °F (Save 1)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178(l)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.178), powered industrial truck operator training and evaluation for each type of truck, and (m)(2), no one under raised forks (Save 2). OSHA issues no forklift licence or card: the employer trains, evaluates and certifies each operator.
- NIOSH skin-exposure guidance: wash skin with soap and water (Save 4). NIOSH's fentanyl guidance was written for responders, so it is background only; on a patch line, PPE comes from the site's own exposure assessment.

Before release, check the cited paragraphs on eCFR: this project confirmed 1910.178(c), (l) including (l)(6), (m)(2), (m)(5), (n)(4) and (q)(7); 1910.147(c)(5)(ii)(D), (e)(3) and (f)(4); 1910.106(e)(6)(ii); and 1910.176 only through
reproductions (the same list is in the rollout guide).

## How it was made

1. **Research.** For the pharma versions, parallel researchers mapped the rule's variants and evidence, and pharma
   task hazards with their controls and citations. For version 5, researchers walked through a transdermal patch plant
   (mixing, coating and drying, laminating, slitting, die cutting, pouching), its hazards and candidate saves, and a
   verifier corrected the facts (for example, how bonding prevents static sparks, the full lock-removal procedure,
   soap and water rather than an alcohol rub, and no exposure-band numbers).
2. **Script.** The first short draft had a drying-oven Save 2. At the user's request it became a material-handling
   save: three candidates were researched, and two AI judges, one in an EHS role and one in a trainer role, chose
   "Pallet truck only". Three shorter drafts were then written from different angles and scored by two independent AI
   judges. The best was merged with trims from the others, reviewed for EHS accuracy, natural speech and teaching
   length, and a verifier read all 76 lines aloud in order.
3. **Stakeholder review.** AI reviewers read the version 3 script as a safety manager, an operations director and a
   site lead ([`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md)).
4. **Master-trainer review, version 4 and version 5.** Four AI reviewers rated version 3 as master trainers
   (engagement, instructional design, learner fit, transfer) and a calibrator checked their evidence: 6.5 / 10.
   Version 4 implemented every fix and was re-rated at 7.0, then a naturalness and flow pass rewrote the delivery.
   Version 5 has not been re-rated. See [`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md).
5. **Narration.** [Kokoro](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) TTS (voice `af_heart`, speed 0.80). Each
   caption line is synthesized in one pass, so caption times are measured, not estimated. `synth.py` also
   finds the pauses in each line and aligns the caption's phrases to them, so on-screen beats land on measured word
   times rather than a guess from the text.
6. **Picture.** Each scene is HTML/SVG in [`source/`](source/): `scenes-a.js` (shared helpers, the save template, the
   prediction holds, and scenes 1–6), `scenes-b.js` (the four saves, scenes 7–10), `scenes-c.js` (scenes 11–13 and
   the per-frame hooks), `td-art.js` (the patch-line drawings: mix room, roll staging, die cutter, slitter and the
   close-up insets) and `art.js` (the person figure and the stopwatch). Every frame is a pure function of time, screenshotted
   with Playwright and encoded with ffmpeg.
7. **Sound.** [`tooling/events.js`](tooling/events.js) reads the sound-cue times from the scene code;
   [`tooling/mix.py`](tooling/mix.py) mixes the voice, the music bed (ducked under the voice) and the cues.
8. **QA.** Contact sheets and frames pulled from the encoded MP4 are reviewed for layout, accuracy and accessibility,
   and every confirmed finding is fixed and re-checked. Audio-to-caption sync is checked with silence detection.

### Rebuild

```bash
# once: Kokoro model files in ./kokoro (GitHub release thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx model-files-v1.0)
pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile
python tooling/from_script.py source/script.json   # script + source/pacing.json -> source/narration.json, storyboard.md
python tooling/synth.py                            # narration -> build/narration.wav, source/timing.json, captions.vtt
python tooling/transcript.py                       # transcript.md from measured timings and on-screen text
python tooling/checkrefs.py                        # every cue/word time reference in the scene code resolves (0 bad refs)
python tooling/pause_points.py                     # chapter and prediction-hold times into facilitator-guide.md
node tooling/events.js                             # sound-cue times from the scene code -> build/events.json
python tooling/mix.py                              # voice + music bed + cues -> build/mix.wav (--no-music for voice + cues only)
node tooling/stills.js                             # optional: one still per caption line, as contact sheets in build/sheets/
node tooling/render.js --workers 3                 # frames + audio (two-pass loudnorm, -16 LUFS) -> two-minute-rule.mp4 + poster.png
python tooling/qa_frames.py [--at mid]             # optional: frames pulled from the encoded MP4, in build/qa/
```

- **Localize the "Who do you call?" slate.** It is a fill-in prompt for learners. To put real contacts on screen, edit
  the slate in `source/scenes-c.js` (scene 13, the recap card) and re-render.
- **Match your own line.** The patch-line drawings are in `source/td-art.js`. If your site differs (for example, manual
  pallet jacks instead of a powered pallet truck, or a badge reader instead of a card), change the drawing there and
  re-render; the site checks are listed in [`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md).
- **Change the narration.** Edit `source/script.json` and re-run from `from_script.py`; the animation and sound cues
  follow the new timings. In a cue's `tts`, " | " adds a short pause.
- **Use a studio voice.** Record one file per line, fill in `source/timing.json` with the real cue times, and render.

**Captions.** The master has open captions burned in, so they show in any player. Don't also attach `captions.vtt`
as a captions track to this file, or every line will show twice. For player-drawn captions in other languages, render a
caption-free master with `node tooling/render.js --clean --out two-minute-rule-clean.mp4` and attach a VTT per language.

## Before deployment

Work through the rollout guide in [`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md) and the
[`facilitator-guide.md`](facilitator-guide.md). Three items are prerequisites:

1. **A signed stop-work and non-retaliation policy.** It must cover contractors and agency staff.
2. **A response standard.** Set who comes when someone stops, and how fast, on every shift. Include who sends a
   trained forklift driver to roll staging.
3. **A QA position.** A safety hold is not a deviation in itself.

Then check the four saves against your site (the list is in the stakeholder review: who drives which truck and how
authorization is shown, the lock-removal procedure, potent-product PPE from your own exposure assessment), and run the
portfolio's SME, instructional-design and accessibility review gates, with real people in those roles. The perspective
reviews in this folder are a starting point for them, not a substitute. Record the video as awareness training, not
lockout, solvent-bonding, forklift-operator or potent-compound handling training.
