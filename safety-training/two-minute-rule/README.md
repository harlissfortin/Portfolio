# The 2-Minute Rule: six saves on a pharma floor

An 8½-minute (8:37) narrated motion-graphics safety training video about the **2-minute rule**: a short pause, on
purpose, at the point of work, right before you start and again whenever something changes. It teaches the rule and
where its limits are, models a routine pause that finds nothing, then walks through six practical **two-minute saves**
from pharmaceutical manufacturing. In every save the video stops twice and asks the viewer first: *What would make you
stop here?* and *Your call?* Each save is impactful without being morbid: what the pause caught, the call and why, the
steps, and what it prevented.

**▶ [`two-minute-rule.mp4`](two-minute-rule.mp4)** · 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC, open captions burned in, voice with
a quiet music bed and sound cues at −16 LUFS, chapter markers · [`transcript.md`](transcript.md) (narration plus a text
description of the screen) · [`captions.vtt`](captions.vtt) · [`storyboard.md`](storyboard.md) · [`poster.png`](poster.png)

**To run it:** [`facilitator-guide.md`](facilitator-guide.md) (launch session and six practice huddles, sorting cards,
decision check, observation checklist, measures) · [`job-aid/`](job-aid/) (pocket card and machine sign, print-ready) ·
[`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md) (three-perspective review and rollout guide) ·
[`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md) (master-trainer rating of version 3 and what version 4 changed).

> The stakeholder and master-trainer reviews in this folder were simulated, role-based reviews by AI reviewers. They
> are not sign-off by real site staff. The video still needs your own EHS, operations, QA and training review.

**Audience:** frontline staff in pharmaceutical manufacturing (operators, technicians, maintenance, cleaning, packaging,
contractors) and their supervisors.

**By the end, the learner can:**
- say when to run the pause: before starting, and after a break, a handover, an interruption or any change
- run Stop · Look · Ask · Decide with the four questions: *What's different? What could hurt us? Checked or assumed?
  What if it goes wrong?*
- choose **Go**, **Fix it** (only if trained, authorized and it's a step in your procedure) or **Stop & get help** (no
  permit yet, someone else's unfinished work, not authorized, or not sure), and say why. The video asks for the
  call in all six saves ("Your call?"); the huddles practise it with local sorting cards and a decision check.
- give an example from their own area where a gauge, indicator, note or glove would be the catch (a huddle activity in
  the facilitator guide)

Each save follows the same template so a facilitator can show it on its own:
1. A one-second Stop · Look · Ask · Decide sting.
2. The setup, with the clue already in the picture.
3. **"What would make you stop here?"** A 3-second hold with a filling ring. Viewers look first.
4. The catch: the question lights on the rail and a red ring lands on it.
5. **"Your call?"** A 2½-second hold with the three calls in outline. The right call fills in on the reveal.
6. The call, with its reason spoken and shown in the case card.
7. The steps, in the correct order, and who does them.
8. "What it prevented", stated plainly.

## Design choices

- **The viewer does something.** Twelve prediction holds turn six demonstrations into six practice items, and a silent
  "Your call?" in the routine Go makes Go a live answer too. Solo viewers are told to pause; in a huddle, the facilitator
  pauses at each hold (the facilitator guide lists the timestamps).
- **The decision boundary is taught, not just shown.** *Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in
  your procedure. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop and get help. Not sure, or the
  fix doesn't work? Stop and get help.* Every call in every save carries its reason, the button simply says "Fix it", and the recap shows both halves of the
  rule.
- **The routine pause is modelled.** Most pauses find nothing and take seconds, so the first worked example is one of
  those.
- **Impactful, not morbid.** No death, injury, collapse or rescue is shown or described. The impact comes from the
  moment of the catch and a plain line on what it prevented, such as "Your hand stays out of a machine that still had
  stored energy" or "Nobody leans into air with too little oxygen".
- **Proportionate.** The six saves split three fixes and three calls for help, and every one ends in Go once it's safe.
- **Plain language for a multilingual floor.** About 145 words a minute while speaking, with pauses between list items.
  Jargon is glossed on first use (inerted, CIP, API, isolated, line break, manway, checked or assumed). The transcript
  describes what is on screen as well as what is said.
- **Who's on screen.** Operators, a press setter, a lead, a contractor (Save 3), a cleaner (the routine Go), a gowned
  operator (Save 5) and an operator in a face shield (Save 6). Faces are never drawn.
- **Sound.** A quiet pad sits about 25 dB under the voice so pauses aren't dead air, and soft cues mark each save, each
  catch, each call, each Go and the seconds of each hold. Everything is synthesized in [`tooling/mix.py`](tooling/mix.py)
  (no samples, no licences).
- **Illustrative and unnamed.** The use cases are composites built from verified pharma hazards. No employer or
  person is named, and no failure is pinned on a worker.
- **An earlier version** used a real 2011 fatality as its example. It was replaced at the user's request with this
  focused, non-morbid set of use cases.

- Isolator glove programs: visual check at each use and physical integrity (leak) testing; EU GMP Annex 1 (2022) for aseptic isolators

## How it was made

1. **Research.** Parallel researchers mapped the rule's variants and evidence, and the pharma task hazards with their
   controls and citations. Candidate incidents were independently verified for facts and fit.
2. **Script.** Three full drafts were written from different angles and scored by two independent judges. The best was
   merged with ideas from the others, then reviewed for technical accuracy, tone and pedagogy.
3. **Stakeholder review.** AI reviewers read the script as a safety manager, an operations director and a site lead
   ([`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md)).
4. **Master-trainer review and version 4.** Four AI reviewers rated version 3 as master trainers (engagement,
   instructional design, learner fit, transfer) and a calibrator checked their evidence: 6.5 / 10. Version 4 implements
   every fix. Its script went through a second four-lens review (EHS accuracy, plain language, instructional design,
   flow and length) before narration. See [`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md).
5. **Narration.** [Kokoro](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) TTS (voice `af_heart`, speed 0.80). Each
   caption line is synthesized separately, so caption times are measured, not estimated.
6. **Picture.** Each scene is HTML/SVG in [`source/`](source/) (`scenes-a.js` shared helpers, the save template and the
   prediction holds, plus scenes 1–6; `scenes-b.js` the six saves; `scenes-c.js` the closing scenes and hooks). Every
   frame is a pure function of time, screenshotted with Playwright and encoded with ffmpeg.
7. **Sound.** [`tooling/events.js`](tooling/events.js) reads the sound-cue times from the scene code;
   [`tooling/mix.py`](tooling/mix.py) mixes the voice, the music bed (ducked under the voice) and the cues.
8. **QA.** Contact sheets and frames pulled from the encoded MP4 were reviewed for layout, accuracy and accessibility,
   and every confirmed finding was fixed and re-checked. Audio-to-caption sync was checked with silence detection.

### Rebuild

```bash
# once: Kokoro model files in ./kokoro (GitHub release thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx model-files-v1.0)
pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile
python tooling/from_script.py source/script.json   # script + source/pacing.json -> source/narration.json, storyboard.md
python tooling/synth.py                            # narration -> build/narration.wav, source/timing.json, captions.vtt
python tooling/transcript.py                       # transcript.md from measured timings and on-screen text
python tooling/checkrefs.py                        # every cue/word time reference in the scene code resolves (0 bad refs)
python tooling/pause_points.py                     # chapter and prediction-hold times into facilitator-guide.md
node tooling/events.js                             # sound-cue times from the scene code -> build/events.json
python tooling/mix.py                              # voice + music bed + cues -> build/mix.wav (--no-music for voice + cues only)
node tooling/stills.js                             # optional: one still per caption line, as contact sheets in build/sheets/
node tooling/render.js --workers 3                 # frames + audio (two-pass loudnorm, -16 LUFS) -> two-minute-rule.mp4 + poster.png
python tooling/qa_frames.py [--at mid]             # optional: frames pulled from the encoded MP4, in build/qa/
```

- **Localize the "Who do you call?" slate.** It is a fill-in prompt for learners. To put real contacts on screen, edit
  the slate in `source/scenes-c.js` (scene 15) and re-render.
- **Change the narration.** Edit `source/script.json` and re-run from `from_script.py`; the animation and sound cues
  follow the new timings. In a cue's `tts`, " | " adds a short pause.
- **Use a studio voice.** Record one file per line, fill in `source/timing.json` with the real cue times, and render.

**Captions.** The master has open captions burned in, so they show in any player. Don't also attach `captions.vtt`
as a captions track to this file, or every line will show twice. For player-drawn captions in other languages, render a
caption-free master with `node tooling/render.js --clean --out two-minute-rule-clean.mp4` and attach a VTT per language.

## Before deployment

Work through the rollout guide in [`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md) and the
[`facilitator-guide.md`](facilitator-guide.md). Three items are prerequisites:

1. **A signed stop-work and non-retaliation policy.** It must cover contractors.
2. **A response standard.** Set who comes when someone stops, and how fast.
3. **A QA position.** A safety hold is not a deviation in itself.

Then run the portfolio's SME, instructional-design and accessibility review gates, with real people in those roles.
The perspective reviews in this folder are a starting point for them, not a substitute. Record the video as awareness
training, not lockout or confined-space training.
