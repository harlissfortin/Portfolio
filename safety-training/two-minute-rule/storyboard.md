# The 2-Minute Rule: One Shift on a Patch Line — storyboard

## 1. title-open — Intro

**Purpose.** Hook on three everyday patch-line jobs (solvent, a pallet of rolls, slitting), respect what the viewer already knows, set calm stakes (today might be different) and land on the pause. The subtitle and the shift strip set the visual frame used by every save; nothing about the shift is spoken.

**Visual.** (~14 s) Light theme on a warm off-white background. Line 1: three white cards pop in across the top, left to right, each on its word, each a single icon over a label: a teal drum-and-pump 'Pump solvent' on 'pump', a plain wood-brown pallet (not amber, which is kept for Fix it) carrying two wrapped rolls standing on end 'Move a pallet' on 'pallet', and a blue roll with a small round blade 'Slit a roll' on 'slit'. Line 2: the cards dim to about 30%, and a plain silhouette fades in at centre stage below them. On 'different', a small amber '?' pops beside the silhouette's head. Line 3: on 'hands', a raised-hand icon pops beside the silhouette's shoulder; on 'two', an amber ring draws once, clockwise, around the hand, and 'Two minutes' fades in in amber to its right. The cards, silhouette and ring fade; a stopwatch reading '2:00' pops in at top centre and its amber ring sweeps once while the digits stay at '2:00'. The title 'The 2-Minute Rule' rises beneath it, then the muted subtitle 'One shift on a patch line'. Last, the shift strip draws in under the subtitle from left to right, with 'Start of shift' and 'End of shift' at its ends and a small cup icon about a third of the way along for the break. The title card holds about 1 s after the line.

## 2. the-rule — The rule

**Purpose.** Name and define the rule in the imperative: the 2-minute rule is a pause at the point of work, before you start and after any break, handover, interruption or change. Two minutes is a guide.

**Visual.** (~11 s) Light theme. Line 1: on 'rule', the heading 'The 2-minute rule' rises top-left. A simple patch-line station (a roll on a stand feeding a flat web) sits centre stage with the silhouette beside it. On 'pause', the raised-hand icon pops at the silhouette's shoulder; on 'point', a map pin drops onto the station and 'At the point of work' rises under it; on 'start', the first amber-edged trigger pill pops in under the station: 'Before you start' (play icon). Line 2: four more pills pop into the row, each on its word, each with its icon: 'Break' (cup), 'Handover' (two people), 'Interruption' (bell) and 'Change' (two swapping arrows). These icons are reused on every trigger chip later. Line 3: the station fades; a stopwatch with a soft dotted ring sits at the right, and 'A guide, not an exact time' rises beside it. The pills hold to the end of the scene.

## 4. how-to-run-it — Stop · Look · Ask · Decide

**Purpose.** Teach Stop · Look · Ask · Decide, the four questions and the three calls, and build the cards and buttons every save reuses. Define 'Checked or assumed?' once and 'call' once ('That's your call', which every 'Your call?' hold reuses). Then teach the boundary as a side-by-side contrast, with the three locked lines word for word. The contrast-card examples are deliberately not the four saves, and none is a forklift or a truck, so the holds test the rule, not recall.

**Visual.** (~43 s) Light theme, no silhouette, same build as the current video. The STOP, LOOK, ASK and DECIDE tiles drop into a row across the top; each lights amber while it is taught. ## 5. routine-go — A routine Go

**Purpose.** Model the pause people do most often, before any catch is shown: the first job, moving a laminate roll with the roll lifter. All four questions come back clean in seconds (the only place Q4 is answered aloud: if the roll slips, nobody's in the aisle), the spoken 'Your call?' hold is answered Go, and the job goes ahead. Name it 'Moving a laminate roll' so it never reads as 'Roll staging'.

## 8. save-2-material-handling — Save 2 · Roll staging

**Purpose.** Q1, What's different?, after a break, in material handling. Roll staging is on the warehouse side of the line's material airlock, away from the coating, oven and solvent areas and out of classified rooms. Before your break, the pallet with the line's next master roll of backing film sat on the floor in the NEXT ROLL spot: a pallet-truck job. Now it is up on the rack's top beam: a forklift job. Your card says pallet truck only, and a friendly co-worker says 'Line's waiting. Just grab the forklift!' Stop & get help: not authorized (trained on the pallet truck, Class III, not the sit-down forklift, Class I; 29 CFR 1910.178(l) ties training to each type of truck, and a co-worker's 'just grab it' is neither training nor supervision). You keep off the forklift, don't reach the roll any other way (no climbing the rack, no pulling it down), speak up kindly and call your lead. The lead thanks you and sends a trained, authorized driver, belted in, who brings the pallet down with everyone clear of the forks and the truck's path (1910.178(m)(2)): back out until the pallet clears the beam, stop, then lower before moving. You take it to the line with your pallet truck and load the roll with your lifter (still your steps), then Go, spoken only once the pallet is down and the forks are flat. The lead fixes the system without blame: next rolls stay at floor level. Prevented: nobody drives a truck they're not trained on. It also models well-meant production pressure and how to speak up, which the earlier version only stated.
