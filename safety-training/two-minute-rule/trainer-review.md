# The 2-Minute Rule: master-trainer review, version 4 and version 5

Version 3 of the video was rated from a master trainer's point of view: is it engaging, and does it teach the lesson it
intended? Version 4 implements every fix that review asked for, in the video and in the facilitation around it.
Version 5 then rebuilt the video for a transdermal patch site, shorter, with four saves on one shift. It has not been
re-rated; see [Version 5: transdermal, shorter](#version-5-transdermal-shorter) at the end. On 2026-11-06, at the
user's request, its Save 4 was re-themed to cut-resistant gloves at the slitter knives. On 2026-11-09, also at the
user's request, it became a blade change: replacing the slitter's worn blades at its knife station, still a
cut-resistant-glove save. Neither change was re-rated; see [Save 4 re-themed](#save-4-re-themed-cut-resistant-gloves-after-version-5)
and [Save 4 re-themed again](#save-4-re-themed-again-blade-change-after-version-5).

> **What this review is, and isn't.** The rating was a simulated, role-based review. Four AI reviewers each took one
> master-trainer lens and worked from the video, transcript, storyboard and frames pulled from the encoded MP4. A fifth
> checked their claims against the evidence and calibrated the scores. The version 4 script then went through a second
> four-lens review (EHS accuracy, plain language, instructional design, flow and length). The version 5 script checks,
> and the judges and verifiers of the Save 4 re-themes, were AI role-based reviews too. None of this is sign-off by a
> real trainer or site. Your own EHS, operations, QA and training leads still need to review the video before use.

## The rating of version 3

**Overall: 6.5 / 10.** On this scale 5 is a typical corporate safety video and 8 is clearly above industry practice.

| Lens | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement and attention | 6 | Respectful and clear, but no stakes in the hook, a voice-only synthetic track, one layout for 3 minutes, and every catch shown before the viewer can look for it. |
| Instructional design | 6 | A well-built worked-example video, but the viewer never has to do anything, and the Fix / Get help boundary is shown six times without being taught. |
| Learner fit and credibility | 6.5 | Credible and blame-free for English-fluent staff; too fast and jargon-heavy for a mixed-literacy, multilingual floor. |
| Transfer to the floor | 6.5 | Ties the pause to real triggers, but never models the routine pause that finds nothing, and leaves no cue at the point of work. |

**Engaging?** Moderately: watchable, but it didn't grip. **Teaches the lesson?** Partly: when to pause and the four
questions, at awareness level. Choosing Go, Fix or Get help was illustrated, not taught, and Save 1 blurred it.

## Naturalness and flow pass

After the version 4 rating, the delivery was reworked so it sounds like one calm person talking. A narration editor, a
voice-delivery specialist, a whole-film flow editor and a terminology checker reviewed it (simulated, role-based
reviews); their edits were merged under the same constraints (safety meaning, the master-trainer fixes, and wording
quoted in print), and a verifier read the whole script aloud in order.

- **Voice.** Lines had been split into separately voiced phrases (103 splits), which reset the intonation every few
  words. Each line is now voiced in one pass and punctuation carries the pauses; two measured splits remain, so "Go,
  fix it" doesn't run together. Speaking pace is about 155 words a minute.
- **Wording.** Choppy fragments are joined into sentences ("The guard is open, the tooling is half in, and there's no
  lock on the power switch"). The six spoken hold prompts vary their lead-ins ("Before you start…", "Look closely…",
  "Your turn…") while the cards stay the same, and the save openings, Go endings and get-help reasons no longer repeat
  word for word. Every save says its trigger out loud.
- **Flow.** The opening sets calm stakes ("You know these jobs. But today, something might be different."). Bridges join
  the scenes ("Here's how", "Let's try it on a routine job", "But sometimes…", "When you stop…"). The two silent
  stretches are voiced: the routine Go's "Your call?" and the line after "All six end in Go". The recap speaks the full
  Fix-it rule and "When in doubt, get help".
- **Related words.** Captions name the calls as the buttons do ("Stop & get help", "Fix it"); "call" is defined once
  ("make one of three calls"); the phone icon now always means calling for help, as on the pocket card; the slate's
  labels match the pocket card; "SOPs" became "procedures" on the end card; Save 6 says its permit out loud, so it no
  longer seems to clash with "No permit, no Go".
- **Visuals on the word.** Beats are tied to words in the narration. They had been placed by estimating each word's time
  from its position in the line, which drifted by up to 0.9 s once pauses became natural. The build now measures the
  pauses in every line's audio and aligns the phrases to them, so buttons, checks and padlocks land on the word that
  names them (checked against the audio to within about 0.3 s).
- **A safety catch in the merge.** A shortened Save 3 line briefly read "you open a filter dryer full of nitrogen", the
  very act the save stops; the verifier caught it and it reads "you'll open".

### Save 2: from the drying oven to material handling

The first transdermal draft's Save 2 was a drying oven with its safety key left in BYPASS after a break. The user
asked: "Replace the dryer example with a material handling example." Three material-handling saves were researched,
and two AI judges scored them, one in an EHS role and one in a trainer role:

| Candidate | EHS judge | Trainer judge | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pallet truck only**: the next pallet of rolls was moved up onto the rack during your break; you're trained on the pallet truck, not the forklift | 6.5 | 8.5 | **15.0** |
| The roll over the limit: a roll heavier than the roll lifter's rated capacity | 8.5 | 6 | 14.5 |
| The bend in the upright: a bent rack upright | 7.5 | 5.5 | 13.0 |

**Why "Pallet truck only".** It teaches the cleanest "Not authorized" of the three: you're trained on one truck and
asked to use another. No other save covers that part of the rule (Save 1 is a step in your procedure, with "Won't go
green?" for a fix that doesn't work; Save 3 is someone else's unfinished work; Save 4 is your own PPE step). Its
standards basis is the strongest: 29 CFR 1910.178(l) ties operator training and evaluation to each type of truck. And
it is the only save that shows production pressure and speaking up, which version 4 only stated ("even on a late
batch").

The judges split, and the margin was half a point. The EHS judge's objections were met in the design:
- **A forklift beside a solvent coating line is unrealistic.** The scene is roll staging, on the warehouse side of the
  line's material airlock, away from the coating, oven and solvent areas and out of classified rooms.
- **A key left in the truck models poor key control and reads as a second catch.** No key is drawn.
- **The link to "What's different?" is indirect.** A "Before your break" inset shows the same pallet on the floor in
  the NEXT ROLL spot, so the change is visible.
- **The risk is never stated.** "A forklift's a different truck, with its own training."
- **Most viewers already know not to drive a truck they aren't trained on.** The save teaches noticing that the job
  changed into one you aren't authorized for during your break, and handling well-meant pressure kindly.

It is also drawn safely: everyone stands clear before the forks rise (1910.178(m)(2)), the driver wears the seatbelt,
nobody climbs the rack, you walk in empty-handed, and "Go" is heard only once the pallet is down and the forks are
flat. Your lead says "Thanks. What did you see?" and fixes the system ("Fixed: next rolls stay at floor level", since removed);
nobody is blamed. The video never says OSHA issues forklift licences or cards: the employer trains, evaluates and
certifies each operator, and the card on your lanyard stands for whatever your site uses.

The other two candidates are good facilitator discussion prompts for the Save 2 huddle. Save 2 runs about 53 s,
against about 42 to 48 s for the others; the reviewers accepted that because it is the only save that shows pressure
and speaking up, and the extra time pays for a slow, calm lowering.

### What was cut for length

The video went from 8:57 to about 6:26, and from about 1,100 to about 760 spoken words.
- **Two saves.** Six became four, and with them went the confined-space, CIP line-break, isolator-glove and
  blister-packer content.
- **Setup lines.** For example: "It's a short pause you take on purpose… where you do the job", "What matters is the
  pause", "right before each run", "But you're the one at the job, so you can see what the plan missed", "Here's how",
  "make one of three calls" (now "That's your call"), "When you stop," before the leaders line, and "So" at the start
  of the title line and the recap. "That's good for people, product and the line" moved to the screen (since removed).
- **Not cut.** Both holds in every save, the four questions (spoken three times), the gloss for "checked or assumed",
  the Fix-it rule (spoken twice), the three boundary lines, "That's the 2-minute rule: line clearance for people.", and
  the slate and end lines. About 330 of the spoken words are fixed in this way, Save 2's designed lines included.

The target was about 650 to 700 words and 6:00. The script stayed above it because the order of precedence puts
length last: safety accuracy, then structure and locked wording, then natural speech, then length. The last round of
safety and plain-English fixes added a few words rather than cutting them. A few optional trims (about 8 words, about 4 s) are listed in the script
notes, each with a cost, and were not taken.

### Save 4 re-themed: cut-resistant gloves (after version 5)

On 2026-11-06 the user asked: "Change the ppe scenario to be themed around cut gloves instead of high potency
product." Save 4 had been a potent product at the slitter, with your own gowning step. It is now **Save 4 · Slitter
knives**: at changeover you've locked out the slitter to move its knives to new widths, the knives are razor-sharp,
and your thin gloves aren't made for blades. Its place in the shift (late), its trigger (Change), the question that
catches it (**What could hurt us?**) and its call (**Fix it**) are unchanged, so two saves are still Fix it, two are
Stop & get help, and all four end in Go. The reason is now "Your own PPE step". The potent-product theme is gone from
the video. (Superseded 2026-11-09: Save 4 is now **Save 4 · Blade change**, replacing the slitter's worn blades; see
[Save 4 re-themed again](#save-4-re-themed-again-blade-change-after-version-5).)

Three drafts were written from new research, and two AI judges scored them, one in an EHS role and one in a trainer
role:

| Draft | EHS judge | Trainer judge | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slitter knives**: move the slitter's knives to new widths at a changeover, locked out under your own locks | 58 | 58 | **116** |
| Knife change: change a slitter blade, locked out, and drop the used blade into a box | 59 | 54 | 113 |
| Knife job: a hand knife at a roll change, with the line stopped but not locked out | 50 | 48 | 98 |

**Why "Slitter knives".** It had the highest combined score and one lesson: the right gloves, with the limit that
cut-resistant isn't cut-proof ("Even in those gloves, hold each knife by its holder, never the edge"). Moving the
knives to new widths is the operator's own setup task at a changeover, so Fix it is legitimate, and the temptation it
catches is real: "it's only a quick nudge in the gloves I've got on". No blade leaves its holder, so nothing loose,
dropped or thrown away is shown, and the prevented line ("No cut from a knife that's stopped, but still sharp")
teaches that a lockout stops the knives moving but doesn't make them blunt. The knife-change draft, the EHS judge's
pick by one point, gave its strengths to the merge: "cut-resistant" rather than "cut gloves" in the narration, air
as well as power locked out for the pneumatic knife holders, DO NOT OPERATE tags, and a posted card (SLITTER KNIVES:
Lock out · Cut-resistant gloves) so "your procedure" has something to point at. Its blade swap into a box was left
out: it read as a falling blade and added a third lesson. The hand-knife draft's prevented line implied that the
gloves stop a slipping knife, it was the only draft to show an open blade cutting, and its roll change echoed Save 2.

An EHS verifier and a read-aloud verifier then checked the merged save, and every issue they found was fixed. The
main ones:
- **Go comes after the gloves come off.** "Go" is the last word of "Then slide the knives to their new widths.
  Cut-resistant gloves off before it runs. Go.", so it never lights while the cut-resistant gloves are on.
- **Your own locks, on power and air.** Your teal locks are on POWER and AIR, each with a DO NOT OPERATE tag, and one
  YOURS pill sits under them, so they can't be read as Save 3's someone-else's lock.
- **Nothing moves by itself.** Each knife moves only as your gloved fist slides its holder in the close-up, and the
  control screen is dark rather than reading "STOPPED".
- **No minor-servicing shortcut.** Setting knives at a changeover is servicing (1910.147(b)), so it is done locked
  out, and the huddle doesn't offer the minor-servicing exception for it.

**Drawn safely** (superseded 2026-11-09). The slitter is locked out (power and air) under your own locks, each with
its tag, from first frame to last, and its control screen is dark. Nobody puts on or removes a lock on screen. The cut-resistant gloves go on
over your thin gloves only while it's locked out, and come off, back on their hook, before Go ("Cut-resistant gloves
off before it runs"), because a glove can be caught by moving parts. Each knife is held by its holder, never the edge, and moves
only as your gloved fist slides it in the close-up. No blade leaves its holder, and no cut level, brand or injury is
shown: the site sets the gloves from its own hazard assessment.

**Around the save** (superseded 2026-11-09: the end card now says "blade-handling training", and the tile and the

**Basis.** A blade change is a tool change, and 29 CFR 1910.147(b) lists tool changes as servicing, so it is done
locked out. The minor-servicing exception (1910.147(a)(2)(ii)) covers only minor tool changes during normal production
that are routine, repetitive and integral, with alternative effective protection. A blade change at a changeover isn't
normal production, so the huddle doesn't offer that exception. The research reached these texts only through search
summaries: check the wording against the primary text before release (see "Unverified text" below).

**Kept out of the video.** No blade falls, is set down, carried, passed, caught or chipped, and no hand goes into the
box. No blade tool, sleeve, blade log, cut level, brand or injury is shown. The facilitator guide's Save 4 huddle covers
these points instead: where blades are changed (in place or at a bench), a blade tool, blade logs or counts, a chipped
blade (Stop & get help), a dropped blade (let it fall, step back, warn others), and changing or sanitizing your
product gloves afterwards.
