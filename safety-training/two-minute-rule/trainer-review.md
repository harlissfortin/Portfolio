# The 2-Minute Rule: master-trainer review and version 4

Version 3 of the video was rated from a master trainer's point of view: is it engaging, and does it teach the lesson it
intended? Version 4 implements every fix that review asked for, in the video and in the facilitation around it.

> **What this review is, and isn't.** The rating was a simulated, role-based review. Four AI reviewers each took one
> master-trainer lens and worked from the video, transcript, storyboard and frames pulled from the encoded MP4. A fifth
> checked their claims against the evidence and calibrated the scores. The version 4 script then went through a second
> four-lens review (EHS accuracy, plain language, instructional design, flow and length). None of this is sign-off by a
> real trainer or site. Your own EHS, operations, QA and training leads still need to review the video before use.

## The rating of version 3

**Overall: 6.5 / 10.** On this scale 5 is a typical corporate safety video and 8 is clearly above industry practice.

| Lens | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement and attention | 6 | Respectful and clear, but no stakes in the hook, a voice-only synthetic track, one layout for 3 minutes, and every catch shown before the viewer can look for it. |
| Instructional design | 6 | A well-built worked-example video, but the viewer never has to do anything, and the Fix / Get help boundary is shown six times without being taught. |
| Learner fit and credibility | 6.5 | Credible and blame-free for English-fluent staff; too fast and jargon-heavy for a mixed-literacy, multilingual floor. |
| Transfer to the floor | 6.5 | Ties the pause to real triggers, but never models the routine pause that finds nothing, and leaves no cue at the point of work. |

**Engaging?** Moderately: watchable, but it didn't grip. **Teaches the lesson?** Partly: when to pause and the four
questions, at awareness level. Choosing Go, Fix or Get help was illustrated, not taught, and Save 1 blurred it.

## What changed in version 4

| Review finding | What version 4 does |
|---|---|
| The viewer is passive for all 5:41 | Every save now stops twice and asks: **"What would make you stop here?"** (about 3 s, with the clue visible and a ring filling) and **"Your call?"** (about 2.4 s, with Go, Fix it and Stop & get help in outline until the right one fills on the reveal). The six-saves intro tells viewers the video will wait. |
| The Fix / Get help boundary is illustrated, not taught | A taught rule with a contrast card: *Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure.* *No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop and get help.* *Not sure, or the fix doesn't work? Stop and get help.* Every call now carries a spoken reason, shown in the case card. The button simply reads "Fix it", and the recap shows both halves of the rule on screen. |
| Save 1 blurred the boundary (you called for help, then did the work) | Your lead comes, thanks you ("Thanks. What did you see?") and checks with the last shift. An **authorized press setter** locks out, tries START, finishes and counts the tooling, then removes their own lock and tests the interlock. |
| The routine pause that finds nothing is never shown | A new **"A routine Go"** scene: cleaning a weighing booth at the start of a shift, all four questions answered clean in a few seconds, then Go. It also covers manual cleaning work. |
| Synthetic voice, no music, silence between lines | Slower voice (about 145 words a minute while speaking, was 167) with short pauses between list items. A quiet music bed sits about 25 dB under the voice. Soft sound cues mark each save's opening, each catch, each call, each Go and the seconds of each prediction hold. |
| Jargon on a multilingual floor | Glossed on first use: *inerted* (filled with nitrogen, oxygen kept out), *CIP* (the cleaning cycle), *API* (a potent active ingredient), *checked or assumed* (did you look or test it yourself, or are you guessing?), *isolated* (energy supply cut off and locked), *line break* (opening a pipe), *manway* (opening). "Ground" replaces "earth". Several idioms were replaced. |
| No contractor, cleaning or gowned protagonist | Save 3 is now a **contractor** (a stop-and-get-help save, so the call stays correct). The routine Go is a cleaning task. Save 5's operator is gowned and masked. |
| Save 6 face shield read as a hood; blister packer read as a monitor | A clear visor face shield; a blister packer redrawn with film reel, forming station, blister pockets and sealing station. |
| The recap shrank the triggers to two phrases | The recap names all five: before you start, after a break, handover, interruption, change. |
| A silent 30-second tail and a circular "Your site" slate | The slate is voiced ("Find out who to call for help: days, nights and weekends") and gives blanks to fill in on the pocket card. The 30 seconds of near-silence at the end are gone: the last screens are voiced and short, with the music bed under them. |
| Save 6 billed as a Question 4 save but lit Question 3 | Question 4 stays lit through the catch. |
| Headings overlapped during two crossfades | Fixed. |
| Nothing survives at the point of work; huddles are talk, not practice; measures miss behaviour | See [`facilitator-guide.md`](facilitator-guide.md) and [`job-aid/`](job-aid/): practice-based huddles (recall instead of replay, the crew answers at the holds, sorting cards for Go / Fix / Get help, a talk-through at a real machine, supervisor response rehearsal), a three-scenario decision check, a non-punitive handover observation checklist, safety-hold codes against a baseline and a comparison area, and a pocket card and machine sign with the triggers, questions, calls and local help numbers. The rollout guide in [`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md) matches. |

**One reviewer recommendation not taken as proposed.** The script review proposed making Save 6's protagonist a
contractor. The EHS lens objected: a contractor who finds a permitted line still under pressure should stop and go back
to the permit issuer, not fix it. Technical safety accuracy wins, so Save 6 stays an operator working under their own
line-break procedure. The contractor went to Save 3, where the call is Stop & get help.

**The cost.** The fixes add practice and plain language, so the video is longer: 8:56 instead of 5:41 (after the naturalness pass below). The chapters
keep each save to about 45 seconds for huddle use. The facilitator guide plays the full video once at launch, then one
save per huddle.

## The rating of version 4

The same four lenses re-rated version 4 from the encoded video, transcript, frames and facilitation package, and a
calibrator checked their evidence (again a simulated, role-based review).

**Overall: 7.0 / 10** (version 3: 6.5).

| Lens | v3 | v4 | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engagement and attention | 6 | 6.5 | The prediction holds give a clear "your turn" rhythm, the routine Go and "All six end in Go" give an arc, and the sound bed removes dead air. The hook and the single save template still hold it back. |
| Instructional design | 6 | 7.5 | The boundary is now a stated rule with a reason on every call; every objective is practised and checked across the video and huddles. |
| Learner fit and credibility | 6.5 | 7.5 | Slower speech, glosses, a screen-describing transcript, and a contractor, a cleaner and a gowned operator on screen. |
| Transfer to the floor | 6.5 | 7.5 | Practice-based huddles, a point-of-work card and sign, a decision check and behaviour measures. |

**Engaging?** More than version 3. As a single screening it's watchable rather than gripping; as the huddle series it
was designed for (one save, paused holds, card votes, a talk-through at a real machine) it's engaging.
**Teaches the lesson?** Mostly, yes. Together with the huddles it teaches all four objectives; the video alone teaches
when to pause, the four questions and a stated Fix / Get help rule.

**Fixed after this rating** (on-screen and timing changes only, no new narration): the button reads "Fix it" everywhere;
the "Your call?" options are readable outlines (they were about 1.8:1 contrast) and the right one fills on the reveal;
the contrast card's examples are no longer the saves themselves, so the hold tests the rule rather than recall; the recap
shows the Stop & get help criteria; Save 5's reason names the "not authorized" category; Save 3 shows "Permit: none yet ·
Gas test: not done" during the hold; the all-six tiles carry each call's reason; the prediction card tells solo viewers
to pause; the routine Go has a "Your call?" hold where the answer is Go; the slate adds "No answer? Call" and "Report
what I caught to"; Interruption uses a bell everywhere, so the phone only ever means calling for help; the limit on
stopping running processes is larger; and Save 1 labels the disconnect as the power switch.

**Still open** (each needs new narration, a recording or a design change, not a quick fix):
- **The hook** still sets no stakes, and the first prediction hold comes 2½ minutes in.
- **One save template** runs for about five minutes, with faceless figures; the calls alternate Stop / Fix through the
  six saves, so later holds are guessable (the routine Go hold and the huddle twists offset this).
- **One synthetic voice.** A human narrator, and a second voice for the lead's "Thanks. What did you see?", would add
  warmth and credibility. The pipeline supports a studio voice (see README).
- **Pressure and speaking up** are stated ("even on a late batch") but never shown: no save models production pressure
  or one worker stopping another.
- **Measurement fidelity:** huddle delivery is measured by attendance, and observations are few and uncalibrated.

## Naturalness and flow pass

After the version 4 rating, the delivery was reworked so it sounds like one calm person talking. A narration editor, a
voice-delivery specialist, a whole-film flow editor and a terminology checker reviewed it (simulated, role-based
reviews); their edits were merged under the same constraints (safety meaning, the master-trainer fixes, and wording
quoted in print), and a verifier read the whole script aloud in order.

- **Voice.** Lines had been split into separately voiced phrases (103 splits), which reset the intonation every few
  words. Each line is now voiced in one pass and punctuation carries the pauses; two measured splits remain, so "Go,
  fix it" doesn't run together. Speaking pace is about 155 words a minute.
- **Wording.** Choppy fragments are joined into sentences ("The guard is open, the tooling is half in, and there's no
  lock on the power switch"). The six spoken hold prompts vary their lead-ins ("Before you start…", "Look closely…",
  "Your turn…") while the cards stay the same, and the save openings, Go endings and get-help reasons no longer repeat
  word for word. Every save says its trigger out loud.
- **Flow.** The opening sets calm stakes ("You know these jobs. But today, something might be different."). Bridges join
  the scenes ("Here's how", "Let's try it on a routine job", "But sometimes…", "When you stop…"). The two silent
  stretches are voiced: the routine Go's "Your call?" and the line after "All six end in Go". The recap speaks the full
  Fix-it rule and "When in doubt, get help".
- **Related words.** Captions name the calls as the buttons do ("Stop & get help", "Fix it"); "call" is defined once
  ("make one of three calls"); the phone icon now always means calling for help, as on the pocket card; the slate's
  labels match the pocket card; "SOPs" became "procedures" on the end card; Save 6 says its permit out loud, so it no
  longer seems to clash with "No permit, no Go".
- **Visuals on the word.** Beats are tied to words in the narration. They had been placed by estimating each word's time
  from its position in the line, which drifted by up to 0.9 s once pauses became natural. The build now measures the
  pauses in every line's audio and aligns the phrases to them, so buttons, checks and padlocks land on the word that
  names them (checked against the audio to within about 0.3 s).
- **A safety catch in the merge.** A shortened Save 3 line briefly read "you open a filter dryer full of nitrogen", the
  very act the save stops; the verifier caught it and it reads "you'll open".
