# The 2-Minute Rule: stakeholder review and rollout guide

The video is now **"The 2-Minute Rule: One Shift on a Patch Line"** (about 6:26), set at a transdermal patch
manufacturing site. It follows one shift through four saves: solvent transfer, roll staging, the die cutter and a
potent product. This document has two parts: the leadership review and where its changes stand in this version, and
the rollout guide.

> **What this review is, and isn't.** Every review in this document was simulated: independent AI reviewers, each
> briefed to read the script in one role (a site safety (EHS) manager, an operations director and a manufacturing site
> lead; for version 5, an EHS reviewer, a plain-language reviewer, a teaching-length reviewer and a read-aloud
> verifier). They are not the views or sign-off of real people at a real site. Use them to prepare your own review.
> Your site's EHS, operations, QA and training leads must review and approve the video before it is shown.

> **Version note.** The three leadership perspectives reviewed version 3, an earlier pharma script with six saves.
> They have not re-reviewed this version. Version 4 implemented a master-trainer review, and version 5 rebuilt the
> video for a transdermal patch site, shorter and with four saves on one shift; see [`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md).
> The tables below show where each change the leaders asked for stands now. The **rollout guide** at the end of this
> document is current for version 5 and matches [`facilitator-guide.md`](facilitator-guide.md).

## The four saves

| Save | When in the shift | Trigger | Question that catches it | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Solvent transfer | Early | Before you start | Checked or assumed? | Fix it | A step in your procedure: bonding clamps on bare metal, lamp green, before you pump flammable solvent from a drum into the adhesive mix tank. "Won't go green? Stop & get help." |
| 2 · Roll staging | Just after the break | After a break | What's different? | Stop & get help | Not authorized: trained on the pallet truck, not the forklift. |
| 3 · Die cutter | Mid-shift | Handover | Checked or assumed? | Stop & get help | Someone else's unfinished work: the guard is off the die station and someone else's lock is on the power switch. |
| 4 · Potent product | Late | Change | What could hurt us? | Fix it | Your own gowning step: double gloves and sleeve covers, no skin showing. |

A routine Go comes first: moving a laminate roll with the roll lifter, before you start. It is the only place
"What if it goes wrong?" is answered aloud ("Nobody's in the aisle"). Two saves are Fix it, two are Stop & get help,
and all four end in Go.

## What still works

These points from the leadership review carry over to the patch line:

- **"Line clearance for people."** Patch-line operators already clear the line for product ("No laminate, labels or
  pouches left", 21 CFR 211.130(e)), so the rule doesn't land as a new safety program.
- **Proportion.** "Most pauses end in Go", two fixes, two calls for help, and all four end in Go. Stopping reads as a
  correct decision, not a brake on output.
- **Technically specific controls.** Bonding clamps on bare metal on the drum and the pump before the drum is opened,
  a conductive hose, then a green lamp. Only the lock's owner removes it, or a supervisor under the site's
  lock-removal procedure (29 CFR 1910.147(e)(3)). Everyone stands clear before the forks rise (29 CFR 1910.178(m)(2)).
  Hands and wrists washed with soap and water, not hand sanitizer, before the product's gloves go on.
- **No villains.** Every catch comes from a system gap: a pallet moved up high during your break, a handover note that
  says "ready to run", a changeover to a stronger product. The co-worker in Save 2 means well and agrees ("Fair
  enough."), and the lead fixes the system, not a person.
- **Huddle-friendly structure.** The save template, the one-second sting and the recap card make each save usable on
  its own. The shift strip shows where each save happens, but only its dot changes, so each chapter still plays alone.

The line the three reviewers found most memorable ("The screen reports the cycle. The gauge reports your line.") was
in the CIP save, which is not in this version.

Where two reviewers proposed different wordings for the same issue, technical safety accuracy won, then the brief
(focused, practical, not morbid), then operational realism, then adoption. The version 5 script checks used the same
idea: safety accuracy first, then structure and locked wording, then natural speech, then length.

## Save 2: roll staging

Save 2 used to be a drying-oven save (a safety key left in BYPASS). The user asked for a material-handling example
instead. Three researched candidates were scored by two AI judges, one in an EHS role and one in a trainer role, and
**"Pallet truck only"** won (15.0, against 14.5 for a roll over the roll lifter's rated limit and 13.0 for a bent rack
upright). It teaches the cleanest "Not authorized", which no other save covers, and it is the only save that shows
production pressure and speaking up. The full story is in [`trainer-review.md`](trainer-review.md).

What it teaches, and what it doesn't claim:
- OSHA ties operator training and evaluation to each type of truck an operator will drive (29 CFR 1910.178(l)). A
  powered pallet truck and a sit-down forklift are different types. A co-worker's "just grab it" is neither training
  nor supervision.
- Nobody stands or walks under raised forks (29 CFR 1910.178(m)(2)). In the video, everyone stands clear of the forks and
  the truck's path, the driver wears the seatbelt, and the trip down is shown as a slow dissolve, so no forklift is
  seen moving near people.
- OSHA does not issue forklift licences or cards. The employer trains, evaluates and certifies each operator
  (1910.178(l)(6)). The card on your lanyard in the video stands for whatever your site uses.

The other two candidates, a roll over the lifter's rated limit and a bent rack upright, are good discussion prompts
for the Save 2 huddle.

## Rollout guide

The three reviewers' deployment advice overlapped heavily. It is merged here into one checklist that matches the
[facilitator guide](facilitator-guide.md), which has the session scripts, huddle notes, sorting cards, decision check,
observation checklist and glossary. The video makes promises on leaders' behalf, so the first section has to be true
before anyone sees it.

### Before the first showing
- **Stop-work and non-retaliation policy, signed.** Legal, HR and the site leader sign it. It covers employees,
  temporary and agency staff, warehouse and dock staff, and contractors. It says that stopping means stopping the job;
  running processes, such as a coater or a drying oven, are shut down only under operating and emergency procedures.
- **A response standard on every shift, nights and weekends included.** It says who comes when someone stops, how
  fast, who covers when they are tied up or don't answer, and how quickly the person hears what changed. It also says
  who sends a trained, authorized forklift driver to roll staging on each shift, and who covers the driver's breaks.
  The cover goes on the job aid as "No answer? Call". Every supervisor, lead and on-call leader does the response
  rehearsal in the facilitator guide before launch: go to the point of work, thank first, ask "What did you see?",
  decide together, close the loop. Retire "Why is the line down?".
- **QA position, co-signed.** The video makes no SOP or batch-record change. A pause or a hold to get help is not
  itself a deviation; a stop is logged against the job, never against a person. QA lists the holds that do have GMP
  consequences on your lines, for example a validated hold time for a mixed adhesive blend or a coated roll, a coating
  run stopped mid-web, and rejects and die-cut waste that must be reconciled. QA also approves the job-aid sign as a
  controlled posting, including its material (laminated, wipe-clean or a version approved for classified rooms). The
  site or area head says the position live at launch.
- **Change control first.** Make every change below through your site's change control: the safety-hold code and its
  leader-arrival field in MES/OEE (a validated system), equipment labels and posted signs, staging rules, and any SOP,
  permit or handover-record change. Allow for the lead time before the four-week baseline.
- **Jam and re-thread boundary for each machine, through change control.** Each coating, laminating, slitting,
  die-cutting and pouching area lists its frequent interventions (web breaks and re-threading, splices, die-station
  jams, pouch-sealer jams) and confirms which fall under minor servicing with alternative protection and which need
  lockout. Set-up before production is not minor servicing. Mark the boundary on the machine.
- **Role-based fix list in each area, written to the boundary rule.** **Fix it** only if you're trained and authorized,
  and it's a step in your procedure: for example, put used solvent wipes in the metal waste can and shut the lid, put
  on the face shield your sampling procedure lists, or bleed air to zero through a lockable dump valve under your own
  lock. **Stop & get help** when there's no permit yet, it's someone else's unfinished work, or you're not authorized
  for it: for example, a guard that won't close, a web wrapped on a roll you're not trained to clear, an alarm you
  don't recognize, someone else's half-finished roll change, an emergency stop that fails its test, or a truck you're
  not trained on. Not sure, or the fix doesn't work? Stop & get help. In the saves: in Save 1, "you" is the operator
  whose transfer procedure has the bonding step; in Save 2, "you" are trained on the pallet truck and the roll lifter,
  and only a driver trained, evaluated and authorized on that forklift brings the pallet down; in Save 3, only the
  lock's owner, or a supervisor under the documented lock-removal procedure, removes the lock; in Save 4, putting on
  the product's protective gear is the operator's own gowning step. Each area confirms its list. The sorting cards are
  built from it, and none of them is one of the four saves, so they test the rule, not recall of the video.
- **Check the four saves against your site.** The video shows one composite patch line. Where yours differs, say so at
  that save's huddle, or change the drawing and re-render (see the [README](README.md)). Confirm each of these with EHS and the
  area lead:
  - **Save 2, roll staging** (the new save; most of its details depend on the site):
    - *Who drives what.* Do line operators drive powered pallet trucks? If the pallet truck stays on the line side,
      it is the staging pallet truck. If operators use manual pallet jacks, the catch works the same way: a manual
      jack is not a powered industrial truck, so 1910.178 operator certification doesn't apply to it (the general
      material-handling rules in 1910.176 still do), but the forklift still needs its own training.
    - *How authorization is shown.* A card, a badge endorsement, a list posted at the truck, or a badge or PIN reader
      on the truck. Tell the crew which one you use. With a badge, the pressure line becomes "use my badge", and the
      call is the same.
    - *Where trucks go.* Whether line equipment crosses the material airlock, whether rolls are racked in staging,
      and whether forklifts are kept out of classified rooms and solvent areas (1910.178(c) limits which trucks may
      be used in hazardous locations).
    - *The forklift.* Which operator restraint your forklifts have (the video shows a lap belt). A truck left
      unattended has its forks fully lowered, controls in neutral, power off and brakes set (1910.178(m)(5)); key
      control is site practice.
    - *Food and drink* rules in staging and the airlock (the video shows you walking in empty-handed).
    - *Cover.* A trained driver on every shift, nights and weekends included, and where the line's next rolls wait.
      If the line has to wait for a driver, the lead decides that and owns it.
    - *For the huddle, not the screen.* The forklift pre-use check (1910.178(q)(7)), slowing down and sounding the
      horn at cross aisles (1910.178(n)(4)), the roll lifter's rated capacity, and reporting damaged racking.
  - **Save 1, solvent transfer:** the mix room's PPE for solvent transfer; clamp points and hose type from your
    procedure. With a fixed ground reel and a pump interlock, the clue is the red lamp alone.
  - **Save 3, die cutter:** whether lockout tags carry a name or a badge number; your documented lock-removal
    procedure (the owner is not on site, every reasonable effort is made to reach them, and they are told before they
    are back at work); and who signs off the restart.
  - **Save 4, potent product:** PPE comes from your site's own exposure assessment (OEL or OEB), with no band numbers
    on screen; whether a coat is standard in the room; whether potent products run only in dedicated suites with
    gowning outside the room; and your skin-contact reporting steps.
  - **The rule card's Fix-it example:** whether operators may clean drug-in-adhesive drips on potent lines. If not,
    use the trim-waste bag example alone.
  - **Not shown:** lines with DEA-controlled products. Add your own accountability steps for those lines.
  - **Before release, check the cited text on eCFR:** 1910.178(c), (l) including (l)(6), (m)(2), (m)(5), (n)(4) and
    (q)(7); 1910.147(c)(5)(ii)(D), (e)(3) and (f)(4); 1910.106(e)(6)(ii); 1910.176. This project confirmed them only
    through reproductions.
- **A job aid for every person and every point of work.** Print the [job aid](job-aid/): an A6 pocket card for each
  person, and a sign (A4 or US Letter) at each point of work. An optional badge card carries the same words in small
  print. Fill in the local help: your lead (days, nights, weekends), radio or phone, who to call if there's no answer,
  and where to report a catch. Contractors write the host area lead or their permit issuer. It is not a form: no
  signatures, no tick boxes. In classified rooms and potent-product suites, post the sign outside the gowning room or
  airlock door, or outside the viewing window, or use a wipe-clean version approved by QA. Pocket cards stay outside
  the gowning point unless your site has approved a version for inside. The video's closing slate asks "Who do you
  call for help?", with blanks for days, nights, weekends and the radio channel or extension, and says "Write it on
  your pocket card." The job aid is where the answer goes.
- **Sorting cards and call cards printed, with local names.** Swap in your own equipment and permit names. The area
  lead and EHS confirm the model answers on the cards marked "check locally" against the fix list. Each card set keeps
  at least one Fix it and one Stop & get help.
- **A safety-hold downtime code** ("Safety hold – get help") in MES/OEE, with no reduction target, and a field where the
  leader enters the time they reached the point of work. Misses on schedule adherence caused by a coded hold don't
  count against the shift. Switch it on in the pilot and comparison areas at least four weeks before launch, and
  announce it in both with the same short shift briefing, separate from the video, so the baseline counts real holds.
- **Baseline measures started.** In the pilot and comparison areas, before launch: handover observations, the decision
  check (set A) on a sample of each shift, and the pulse question (see Measure). Trial the decision-check items first
  with about five people from an area that is in neither group.
- **Facilitators ready.** Each has watched the full video, read the facilitator guide, done the response rehearsal, and
  has their sorting cards, call cards and the huddle sheet.
- **Record it as awareness training only.** Don't credit it toward 1910.147 lockout, 1910.178(l) powered-truck
  operator training or evaluation, solvent-bonding or potent-compound handling training, or toward a procedure
  qualification. Watching Save 2 authorizes nobody to drive anything.
- **Localize and translate.** Use local permit, equipment and truck names on the job aid and sorting cards. Non-US sites
  swap in their own line-clearance, lockout, flammable-liquid and lift-truck references (e.g. EU GMP Part I Ch. 5 for
  line clearance). Translate captions, voice, job aid, cards and decision check with the one locked glossary in the
  facilitator guide, and test each language with people from the floor. Have a copy of the video with each language's
  captions burned in ready before launch, on the huddle devices. Keep the plain wording: the floor is mixed-literacy
  and multilingual.

### Launch
- **Pilot first, for about six weeks:** the launch session, four weekly huddles, then the decision check. Run it in one
  mixing and coating area and one converting and packaging area (laminating, slitting, die cutting, pouching), with a
  similar non-pilot area as the comparison. Cover all shifts, including warehouse and roll staging, cleaning,
  maintenance and at least one contractor crew. Test three things: do people make the right call (the 3-scenario
  decision check); does the equipment look like theirs; and do leaders show up when someone stops.
- **A launch session of about 25 minutes, on every shift.** The site or area head opens it live, not on a recording,
  and says the leader commitments and the QA position, as scripted in the facilitator guide: *"I'd rather lose two
  minutes than see anyone hurt or a batch lost. Anyone can stop a job, staff or contractor, even on a late batch. No
  punishment, no blame. When you stop, a leader comes quickly, thanks you, fixes the cause and tells you what changed.
  A stop to get help never counts against your shift. It's not a form. Report what you caught the usual way."* Then the
  head names who comes on each shift, and who to call if they don't answer, and says: *"A pause, or a hold to get help,
  is not a deviation in itself. If you stop, it's logged against the job, never against you."* Then the full video, a
  short discussion that ends with "What would make it hard to stop here?", and the pocket card handed out with the
  contacts checked. With a large crew and no big screen, run it twice, in groups of up to six.
- **Four weekly huddles, one save each: practice, not re-watching.** Each takes 10–15 minutes, held beside a real
  machine so nobody walks: at shift start, at a planned stop, or with the crew split in two so the line stays covered.
  Don't replay the rule chapters. The crew recalls the five triggers, the four questions and the three calls from
  memory. Then it plays one save chapter and answers at the built-in holds ("What would make you stop here?", "Your
  call?"), voting with call cards, with the facilitator pausing as long as needed. It talks through the reason, votes
  again on a twist (one changed fact), and sorts three cards or captures a local example. Last, at the machine, one
  operator talks through their two minutes aloud and the supervisor rehearses the response: *"Thanks. What did you
  see?"* Everyone then does their two minutes at their own station before they start. With only 10 minutes, shorten
  the other steps, but never drop the talk-through.
- **Every area runs all four saves.** The default order is 1 to 4, the order of the shift: Fix it, Stop & get help,
  Stop & get help, Fix it. Areas may start with the save closest to their work: mixing and coating, Save 1;
  warehouse, staging and roll handling, Save 2; die cutting and other converting, Save 3; slitting and potent-product
  suites, Save 4. The huddle notes are written by save, so any order works.
- **The Save 2 huddle.** Use the two candidates that were not chosen as extra twists or discussion prompts: a roll
  heavier than the roll lifter's rated capacity, and a bent rack upright. Cover the forklift pre-use check and the horn
  at cross aisles here, not as a reason to drive: they are the trained driver's steps. Invite a trained driver or the
  warehouse lead if you can.
- **Contractors and new starters.** Add the video to contractor induction and permit-issue briefings; stop-work runs
  both ways. Add it to new-starter and agency-staff induction with a buddy who demonstrates the pause at their first
  handover. Give both the pocket card with the local help filled in.

### Sustain
- **Keep practising.** After the four huddles, hold one practice huddle a month, with the same steps: a save, three
  sorting cards, or a local save. Don't fall back to re-showing the whole video.
- **Build the pause into existing procedures, through change control and without a new form.** Lockout,
  permit-to-work, solvent transfer, handover, changeover and roll staging. At permit issue, permit issuers remind
  people: *"Do your two minutes at the point of work, and call me if anything's different."* When they visit the job,
  they ask an open question: *"What did your two minutes turn up?"* Don't add a 2-minute box to the permit. Where a
  pre-task card already exists, keep it and coach the conversation, not the tick.
- **Fix the handover, through change control.** Record lock, guard and interlock state and work in progress. Add the
  orderly transfer of locks at shift change (1910.147(f)(4)) and schedule overlap time. Save 3 is exactly this gap,
  and the handover observations show where it still breaks.
- **Fix staging.** Decide where each line's next rolls wait, so the line's own pallet truck can reach them, and who
  drives when a forklift is needed, breaks included. Save 2's fix is exactly this: "next rolls stay at floor level".
- **Engineer catches out** under management of change. Examples: a bonding monitor that keeps the drum pump off until
  the bond is proven; a die-station guard interlock; floor-level spots for each line's next roll; a PPE board that
  changes with the product at changeover.
- **Recognize catches without a quota.** Thank people in the moment, share stories with consent, and post fixes on a
  "You caught it, we fixed it" board. No targets, prizes or league tables.
- **Guard investigations against blame.** "Failed to perform the 2-minute rule" is not an acceptable root cause or a
  reason for discipline. There are no signed 2-minute cards. Decision-check and observation results are never used for
  discipline, appraisal or qualification.
- **Refresh.** Show the full video again at onboarding, and at lockout retraining and forklift refresher training as a
  supplement: it doesn't count toward either refresher, and is recorded separately as awareness training. After the
  first quarter, build a local save in the same template from a real good catch, used with consent and without names.
  The facilitator guide has the template.

### Measure: the calls and the response, not signed cards
Measure the pilot and the comparison area the same way, at the same times, from a baseline at least four weeks before
launch to the end of the pilot. Report by area, never by name. Split by shift only where a group has at least five
people, and don't report any group smaller than five.
- **The right call.** The 3-scenario decision check, replacing "can people say the four questions". An EHS partner, a
  trainer or a lead from another area runs it one to one, not the person's own supervisor, with a plain card of the
  three calls and no job aid. Set A before launch, with no answer feedback; a parallel set B within two weeks after the
  fourth huddle; set A again about three months later, in the pilot area only (the comparison area has had the video
  by then). No scenario is one of the four saves. An answer is right when the call is right and the reason names the
  boundary: trained and authorized, and a step in your procedure; or no permit yet, someone else's unfinished work, or
  not authorized for it; or nothing different, and checked. An unsafe miss is Go when the answer is Fix it or Stop &
  get help, or Fix it when the answer is Stop & get help. Report the share with all three right and the share with any
  unsafe miss. Pilot guide: at least 8 in 10 all right, fewer than 1 in 10 with an unsafe miss, better than baseline,
  and better than any change in the comparison area. No individual scoring is used for discipline.
- **Own-area examples.** Local examples captured in the huddles, and the share of people who give a real example in
  the decision check: one that names a specific piece of their own equipment, the clue and a call.
- **The pause at handover and changeover.** Sampled, non-punitive observations by someone from outside the crew, two
  per area per week across all shifts, with no names: the share with a pause before hands-on work, and the share with
  lock, guard and interlock state handed over. Report by area in 4-week blocks, with shifts pooled (at least eight
  observations per block). EHS holds the raw sheets, tallies them, then destroys them; they never go into the area file
  or to the line supervisor.
- **Safety-hold codes** by area, against the pre-pilot baseline and the comparison area. Both areas heard about the
  code at the same time. Expect a rise.
- Help-call response time, from the call to a leader at the point of work. The leader enters the arrival time in the
  safety-hold entry in MES/OEE; the observers' "minutes to arrive" is a spot check.
- Share of catches closed, with feedback to the finder, within target; median days to close.
- Repeat findings with the same cause, a sign the catch was fixed but not engineered out.
- Lockout and permit audit findings at handover and changeover.
- Good-catch reports per 100 workers by area. Expect an early rise and treat it as success.
- One pulse-survey item: *"I can stop a job without blame."* Split it by shift and language group, at baseline and at
  the end of the pilot.
- **Cadence.** Weekly during the pilot for holds, response times, observations and huddles held; a monthly summary
  shared with the crews; an end-of-pilot review against baseline and the comparison area before any wider rollout.
