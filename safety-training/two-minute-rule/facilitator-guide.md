# The 2-Minute Rule: facilitator guide

This guide is for the launch session and the six weekly huddles that go with the video *The 2-Minute Rule: six saves on
a pharma floor*.

This is awareness training. It builds a habit. It is not lockout, confined-space or permit training, and it does not
qualify anyone to do any fix. Your site's procedures, permits and lockout always come first.

## 1. Who it's for and what you need

### Who runs it

- **Launch session.** The site head or area head opens it live. A supervisor, area lead or trainer runs the rest.
- **Huddles.** The crew's own supervisor or team lead. An EHS partner or trainer can co-run the first one.
- **Decision check and observations.** An EHS partner, a trainer, or a lead from another area. Not the crew's own
  supervisor.
- **Who attends.** Everyone who works in the area, on every shift: operators, technicians, maintenance, cleaning,
  packaging, and the contractors working there. Nights and weekends get their own sessions.

### What learners can do

By the end of the six huddles, each person can:

| | The learner can | Where it's practised | How it's checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Say when to pause: before you start, after a break, at handover, after an interruption, after any change | Huddle step 1 (recall) | Decision check: "What made this a time to pause?" |
| 2 | Run Stop · Look · Ask · Decide, with the four questions | Hold 1 in each save; the step 5 talk-through | Decision check: "What's the clue?" and "Which question catches it?" |
| 3 | Choose Go, Fix it or Stop & get help using the boundary rule, and say why. Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. No permit yet, someone else's unfinished work, or not authorized: Stop & get help. Not sure, or the fix doesn't work: Stop & get help. | Hold 2, the twist, sorting cards | Decision check: the call and the reason |
| 4 | Give an example from their own area where a gauge, indicator, note or glove would be the catch | "Our version" (saves 2, 3 and 5) | Decision check, Part B |

### Before you run anything

These must be in place first. They are in the rollout guide in
[`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md#rollout-guide).

- A signed stop-work and non-retaliation policy that covers contractors.
- A response standard on every shift: who comes when someone stops, how fast, and who covers when they don't answer.
- The QA position, co-signed.
- Your area's fix list and jam boundaries, posted in the area (through change control).
- The safety-hold code ("Safety hold – get help") live in MES/OEE, and announced in the pilot and comparison areas.
- The job aid printed, with the local contacts filled in, and the sign approved by QA for posting.

If any of these is missing, don't launch. The video makes promises on leaders' behalf.

### Get yourself ready

1. Watch the full video once, start to end.
2. Read this guide.
3. Do the supervisor response rehearsal (section 5) with your peers.
4. Know your area's fix list: what your crew may fix, and what always needs help.
5. Get your sorting cards and call cards printed, with your own equipment names (section 4).

### What you need

| | Launch session | Each huddle |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | About 25 min, once per shift | 10–15 min, once a week for six weeks, plus 10 min to prepare |
| **When** | At shift start or a planned stop | At shift start before the line runs, at a planned stop, or with the crew split in two so the line stays covered |
| **Place** | A quiet room or area near the work | Beside the machine you'll use for the talk-through (step 5), so nobody has to walk |
| **Screen and sound** | A screen the whole group can see, and speakers | A tablet or laptop. Sound loud enough over the line. |
| **Player** | Plays the full video | Can pause and jump to a time. Download the video to the device before the shift; don't stream it. |
| **If you don't have it** | Large crew, or no big screen: run it twice, in groups of up to six around a laptop | No device: read the save aloud from its huddle note (see "No device?" in section 3) |
| **Job aid** | A pocket card for each person, and the sign posted at each point of work | The posted sign, and spare pocket cards |
| **Local contacts** | Filled in on the job aid before the session | The same |
| **Other** | Pens, and a way to write down concerns | Call cards for everyone (collect them after). Sorting cards (saves 1, 4, 6) or a flipchart or whiteboard (saves 2, 3, 5). This week's huddle note and the huddle sheet. |

**Captions and language.** The main video has English captions burned in. For each other language on your floor, the
training team makes a copy with that language's captions burned in, before the launch, and puts it on the huddle
devices. For a mixed crew, play the version in the language most of the crew reads best. Give each person the job aid
and cards in their own language, and ask a bilingual colleague to repeat each hold question. Don't add a captions file
to a copy that already has captions, or every line shows twice.

### The job aid

The job aid is the point-of-work cue. It comes in two formats: a pocket card for each person, and a sign posted where
the job starts (on the machine, at the room door, or by the permit board). It has:

- the five times to pause: before you start, after a break, at handover, after an interruption, after any change
- Stop · Look · Ask · Decide, and the four questions, each with a plain meaning
- the three calls, with the boundary rule: when you may Fix it, and when you Stop & get help
- blanks for local help: your lead (days, nights, weekends), radio or phone, who to call if there's no answer, and
  where to report a catch

It is not a form. No signatures, no tick boxes. Nobody hands it in.

The video ends on a slate that asks "Who do you call for help?", with blanks for days, nights, weekends and the radio
channel or extension, and says "Write it on your pocket card." The job aid is that card.

**Clean and contained areas.** QA approves the sign as a controlled posting, including its material. In classified,
aseptic or potent-compound areas, post the sign outside the gowning room or airlock door, or outside the viewing
window, or use a cleanroom-compatible version approved by QA that can be wiped with your disinfectant. Pocket cards
stay outside the gowning line unless your site has approved a cleanroom version.

### Fill in the local contacts first

Before the launch, fill in the blanks for each area and shift.

| Blank on the job aid | What goes there |
|---|---|
| Your lead: days | The person who comes when you stop on day shift |
| Your lead: nights | The person who comes on nights |
| Your lead: weekends | The person who comes on weekends |
| Radio or phone | How to reach your lead: the radio channel or phone extension |
| No answer? Call | Who covers when your lead is tied up or doesn't answer, from the response standard: for example, the shift manager, the control room or the on-call leader |
| Report catches to | Your usual reporting route |

Contractors: your lead is the host area lead or your permit issuer.

If you can't fill a blank, that's a gap in the response standard. Close it before the launch.

## 2. The launch session (about 25 minutes)

The goal: everyone sees the full video once, hears a leader make the commitments live, and leaves with a filled-in job
aid.

| Part | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Live opening: leader commitments and the QA position | About 3 min | Site or area head |
| 2. The full video | Start to end | Facilitator |
| 3. Discussion | About 8 min | Facilitator, with the head staying |
| 4. Close | About 2 min | Facilitator |

With a few minutes to settle and hand out the job aid, that's about 25 minutes. Book the full time. Don't cut the
discussion to fit.

### Part 1. Live opening (site or area head)

Say it live, on every shift, nights and weekends included. A recording doesn't count. Use your own words, but keep every
commitment below. Don't read it from a slide.

> "I'd rather lose two minutes than see anyone hurt or a batch lost.
> Anyone can stop a job, staff or contractor, even on a late batch. No punishment, no blame.
> When you stop, a leader comes quickly, thanks you, fixes the cause and tells you what changed.
> A stop to get help never counts against your shift.
> It's not a form. Report what you caught the usual way.
> On this shift, the person who comes is [role]. On nights it's [role]. On weekends it's [role]. If they don't answer,
> call [role]."

Then say the QA position:

> "QA's position: this video changes no procedure and no batch record. A pause, or a hold to get help, is not a deviation
> in itself. If you stop, it's logged against the job, never against you. Some holds do need QA, like a hold time or a
> glove breach. Your lead brings QA in. That's still not on you."

Then stay for the discussion. Your presence is the message.

### Part 2. Play the full video

1. Say: "In each save, the video stops twice and asks you. Call out your answer."
2. Play it start to end. Pause at Save 1's two holds so the crew sees how they work, and let the rest run. The huddles are where you practise.
3. If someone can't see or hear well, move them closer. For a large crew with no big screen, run the session twice, in
   groups of up to six around a laptop.

### Part 3. Discussion (about 8 minutes)

Ask, then listen. Write down what people say. Don't defend or argue. Let people answer in their own language, and ask
a colleague to translate.

1. "Which save felt closest to our work?"
2. "Where would the catch be on our equipment? A gauge, an indicator, a note, a glove?"
3. "Who do you call on this shift? On nights? On weekends? And if they don't answer?"
   Everyone checks their pocket card. Fix any blank now.
4. "What would make it hard to stop here?"
   This is the most important question. Thank people for honest answers. The head says what they will do about it, and
   when they will come back.

### Part 4. Close (about 2 minutes)

1. Hand out the pocket card. Show where the sign is posted.
2. Explain the huddles: "Every week for six weeks, 10 to 15 minutes, at a machine, one save. You make the calls."
3. Say when you'll come back on the concerns raised.
4. The head closes: "Anyone can stop a job."

**After the session**

- Record attendance in your usual training system, as awareness training only.
- Send the list of concerns to the area head. Report back on them at the first huddle.

## 3. Six weekly huddles (10–15 minutes each)

Huddles are practice, not re-watching. After the launch, don't replay the rule chapters. Each week the crew recalls the
rule from memory, makes the calls in one save, and practises at a real machine.

Hold each huddle beside the machine you'll use for the talk-through, and play the save on the tablet there. Nobody
walks. Run it at shift start before the line runs, at a planned stop, or with the crew split in two so the line stays
covered.

Print this week's huddle note (each one fits on a page) and the [huddle sheet](job-aid/huddle-sheet.pdf), and take them
to the floor.

### At a glance

| Default week | Save | The call | Step 4 | Real machine for the talk-through |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Save 1 · Tablet press handover | Stop & get help | Sorting cards 4, 7, 8 | Any machine with a guard and interlock, where handovers happen |
| 2 | Save 2 · Powder charge | Fix it | Our version | A charge point, drum transfer or any grounding clamp |
| 3 | Save 3 · Inerted filter dryer | Stop & get help | Our version | A vessel, tank, manway or other place that needs a permit (outside only) |
| 4 | Save 4 · Blister packer jam | Fix it | Sorting cards 1, 5, 6 | A machine with compressed air and a gauge or dump valve |
| 5 | Save 5 · Potent ingredient | Stop & get help | Our version | An isolator, glovebox, or a station with a PPE check |
| 6 | Save 6 · Line break after CIP | Fix it | Sorting cards 2, 3, 9 (spare: 10) | A line-break point, transfer panel, or any line with a gauge |

**Order.** Run them 1 to 6. The calls switch between Stop & get help and Fix it each week, and the boundary rule
between them is what you're teaching. An area may start with the saves closest to its work (OSD and packaging: 1 and
4; API and chemical: 2, 3 and 6; sterile and HPAPI: 5 and 6). Run all six. The huddle notes are written by save, so
they work in any order.

### Find the save and its holds

Use the chapter markers in your player to jump to the save. If your player doesn't show chapters, drag the slider to
the save's start time in the table below. Each save stops twice:

- **"What would make you stop here?"** A short hold, about 3 seconds. The clue is in the picture.
- **"Your call?"** A short hold, about 2.5 seconds, with three dimmed buttons: Go, Fix it, Stop & get help.

Pause the player at each one for as long as your crew needs.

On screen and on the job aid, the button reads **"Fix it"**, and it always means the boundary rule: you're trained and
authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. In the routine Go, the video also holds on "Your call?" with no narration,
and the right answer there is **Go**. Use it to show that most pauses end in Go.

Pause as soon as you hear the question. If you miss it, go back 5 seconds.

**No device?** Read the save aloud from its huddle note: the setup line, then "What would make you stop here?" (wait
for answers), then the clue; then "Your call?" (the crew votes), then the call and the reason.

### How the crew votes

At every "Your call?" hold, every twist and every sorting card, the crew votes the same way.

1. Everyone has a set of call cards: green Go, amber Fix it, red Stop & get help. Each has its word and icon, so it
   works in any language and without colour.
2. Say: "On three, hold up your call. One, two, three."
3. Everyone holds one up at the same time. Nobody waits to see what others pick.
4. Ask for a volunteer from each answer to say why. Then go on.

No call cards? Everyone points at the call on the back of their pocket card, at the same time.

### Every huddle runs the same six steps

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Recall | 1.5 min | The crew says the five times to pause, the four questions and the three calls |
| 2. Play the save | 3–4 min | The crew answers at both holds, before the video does |
| 3. The reason and the twist | 2 min | Why that call; then one changed fact, and the crew votes again |
| 4. Cards or our version | 3 min | Sort three cards, or find our own version |
| 5. Talk-through at the machine | 3 min | One operator talks through their two minutes; the supervisor rehearses the response |
| 6. Close | 1.5 min | What changed, one catch story, who you call, the next save |

**Step 1. Recall (1.5 min)**

1. Ask the whole crew, not one person: "When do we pause?"
   You want: before you start, after a break, at handover, after an interruption, after any change.
2. "What are the four questions?"
   You want: What's different? What could hurt us? Checked or assumed? What if it goes wrong?
3. "What are the three calls? When is it Fix it, and when is it Stop & get help?"
   You want: Go, Fix it, Stop & get help. Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your
   procedure. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop & get help. Not sure, or the fix
   doesn't work? Stop & get help.
4. Let the crew fill gaps for each other. Point to the job aid only after they've tried.

In your first huddle, build the list together. From your third huddle on, make it about real work: "Name a time you
paused this week. Which trigger was it? Which question did the work?" Then ask for anything still missing.

**Step 2. Play the save (3–4 min)**

1. Say: "It will stop twice. Answer out loud before it moves on."
2. Play from the start of the save chapter.
3. At **"What would make you stop here?"**, pause the player. Ask the question again. Wait. Get two or three answers.
   Don't say who's right yet. Play on.
4. At **"Your call?"**, pause the player. The crew votes with call cards. Ask a volunteer from each answer why. Play on.
5. Let the reveal play to the end of the chapter. Stop there. Don't play the next chapter.

**Step 3. The reason and the twist (2 min)**

1. Ask: "Why that call, and not the other one?"
2. Bring it back to the boundary rule: when it's Fix it, and when it's Stop & get help. Use the model reason in the
   save's note.
3. If someone picked a different call, ask what they saw.
   - They chose Stop & get help where the answer was Fix it: "That's safe. When in doubt, get help. Here's why this one
     is in your procedure."
   - They chose Go or Fix it where the answer was Stop & get help: walk through the boundary rule again, kindly.
   - They chose Go where something was still wrong: ask "Checked or assumed?" Go back to the picture at hold 1 and find
     the clue together.
4. If it comes up: "No permit yet" is Stop & get help. If you hold the permit and the step is in it, it can be Fix it
   (Save 6).
5. **The twist.** Read the one changed fact in the save's note. The crew votes again. Ask one volunteer why. Keep it
   short.

**Step 4. Sorting cards or our version (3 min)**

- Sorting cards (saves 1, 4, 6): see section 4.
- Our version (saves 2, 3, 5): use the prompt in the save's note, and run it as in section 8. Then ask: "What would
  make our example a Fix it, and what would make it Stop & get help?"

**Step 5. Talk-through at the machine (3 min)**

1. Use a machine like the one in the save (see the table above). Pick one that's stopped or between batches, or stand
   well back from a running one and don't distract whoever is running it. Leave it as it is.
2. Ask for a volunteer. Rotate each week. Never pick someone to test them. They can talk in their own language, hold
   the job aid and read from it, or do it as a pair.
3. The volunteer talks through their two minutes out loud, as if about to start:
   - Stop: "Hands off."
   - Look: around the job, and compare it with the plan. They point at what they would look at.
   - Ask: the four questions, out loud, with what they would check here.
   - Decide: they say the call.
4. Then say: "Now imagine you find this:" and read the situation in the save's note. The volunteer stops and says the
   call, in any words. The supervisor responds as in section 5, starting with: **"Thanks. What did you see?"**
5. Then say to everyone: "When you get back to your station, do your two minutes before you start. I'll come by this
   shift and ask one of you to talk me through it." Then do it.
6. Keep it safe. This is talk only. Nobody touches controls, guards, valves, locks or gloves. Stay outside guarding and
   barriers. In cleanrooms and potent-compound areas, don't break gowning or containment rules for a huddle: use a
   station outside, or a photo. If the volunteer spots something real, treat it as a real catch.

**Step 6. Close (1.5 min)**

1. Close the loop: "Last time we raised [catch or concern]. Here's what changed." If nothing has changed yet, say when
   it will.
2. "Did anyone use it this week? Catch anything?" Take one story. Thank them. Note any others for after the huddle.
3. "Who do you call on this shift if you stop? And if they don't answer?" Point at the job aid.
4. Thank the volunteer.
5. Name the next save.
6. In your last huddle, add: "In the next two weeks, some of you will be asked three quick questions, one to one. No
   names. After that, we keep one practice huddle a month."

**If you only have 10 minutes**

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| 1. Recall | 1.5 min |
| 2 and 3. Play the save, then the reason in one line (skip the twist) | 4 min |
| 4. One sorting card, or one local example | 1.5 min |
| 5. Talk-through | 2.5 min |
| 6. Close | 0.5 min |

Never drop step 5. Pick up the twist and the other cards at next month's practice huddle.

**After the huddle (2 min).** Record attendance the usual way. Copy the flipchart into the local examples log (section
8). Pass on any catch today.

### Huddle notes

Each note is written by save, so it works in whatever order your area runs them. Each fits on one printed page.

#### Save 1 huddle · Tablet press handover

You take over a tablet press at handover. The last shift left a tooling change half done.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: the handover note says the last shift started a tooling change. The guard is open, the tooling
     is half in, and there's no lock on the power switch.
   - Hold 2, the call: **Stop & get help.**
3. **The reason.** It's someone else's unfinished work, and it's guard and interlock work. You can't know what the last
   shift did or didn't do. Your lead comes, thanks you ("Thanks. What did you see?"), and checks with the last shift.
   An authorized press setter locks out, tries START, then finishes and counts the tooling. Guard closed, area clear,
   setter's lock removed, interlock tested. Then the start-up checks your procedure requires: the right tooling for the
   batch, line clearance, and QA where needed. Then Go.
   - If someone says "I'm a press setter, I'd just finish it": it still starts with Stop & get help. Your lead checks
     with the last shift first. Then an authorized press setter takes it on under their own lock.
   - Prevented: nobody starts the press with tooling half in.
   - **Twist:** "What if the note says the change is finished, there's no lock on, the guard is closed and the
     interlock light is green?" Model call: **Go**, after the start-up checks your procedure requires.
4. **Sorting cards 4, 7, 8.** The theme: whose work is it? Your own PPE step is yours. Another person's lock, and the
   interlock, are not.
5. **Talk-through.** Any machine with a guard and interlock, where handovers happen.
   "Now imagine you find this: the handover note says a guard was opened, and you can't tell if the job was finished."
6. **Close.** As step 6, plus: "At handover this week, ask the person going off shift about locks, guards and anything
   half done."

#### Save 2 huddle · Powder charge

You charge powder into an inerted reactor holding flammable solvent.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: the grounding indicator is red, and the clamp is off. The oxygen is below the limit: that
     part was checked.
   - Hold 2, the call: **Fix it.**
3. **The reason.** Your procedure lets you clip the grounding clamp onto the drum, and you're trained and authorized to
   do it. The indicator turns green, and the drum liner is static-safe. Then Go. If the indicator is still red, get
   help.
   - "Checked or assumed?" worked twice here: the oxygen was checked, and the ground was caught.
   - Prevented: a static spark at the manway.
   - **Twist:** "What if the clamp cable is frayed?" Model call: **Stop & get help.** A damaged clamp or cable, or an
     indicator that stays red after you clip on, is no longer a step in your procedure.
4. **Our version.** Ask: "Where do we have an indicator, gauge or light that tells us something before we start? What
   would it show if something was wrong? What's our call?" Capture one or two (section 8).
5. **Talk-through.** A charge point, drum transfer, or any grounding clamp.
   "Now imagine you find this: you clipped the clamp on, and the indicator is still red."
6. **Close.** As step 6.

#### Save 3 huddle · Inerted filter dryer

You're a contractor. At a product change, you'll open a filter dryer full of nitrogen to inspect it.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: no test, no permit yet. Is the nitrogen line closed with a solid plate (blanked off)? Has the
     air inside been tested?
   - Hold 2, the call: **Stop & get help.**
3. **The reason.** Opening it needs a gas test and a permit. The steps belong to the permit: isolate (blank off the
   nitrogen and every other line, and lock out the agitator), test, ventilate, retest oxygen and vapor, then the
   permit. An authorized team isolates, tests, ventilates and retests, not you. The permit is issued. Then Go, under
   the permit. No permit, no Go.
   - Nitrogen has no color or smell. It gives no warning. "Just a quick look" is not safe. Don't lean in.
   - Prevented: nobody leans into low-oxygen air.
   - **Twist:** "What if the gas test is done, and the permit is issued to you?" Model call: **Go**, within what the
     permit allows. You still do your two minutes at the manway.
4. **Our version.** Ask: "Which of our vessels, tanks, pits or enclosures need a gas test or a permit before anyone
   opens one or leans in? What's the permit called here?" Capture the answers. After the huddle, check them against your
   site's permit list. Correct anything wrong at the next huddle.
5. **Talk-through.** Outside a vessel, tank or manway with a permit sign. Nobody opens anything. No vessel in your
   area? Use any place that needs a permit before work, such as a pit, drain, roof hatch or electrical panel, and talk
   from outside it.
   "Now imagine you find this: the manway's open, and nobody has tested the air."
   Coaching point: keep everyone back from the opening. Nitrogen can spill out.
6. **Close.** As step 6.

#### Save 4 huddle · Blister packer jam

A blister packer jams where your jam tool can't reach. You lock out power and air.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: the gauge still shows trapped air.
   - Hold 2, the call: **Fix it.**
3. **The reason.** Bleeding to zero through the lockable dump valve, under your own lock, is a step in your procedure,
   and you're trained and authorized to do it. Bleed to zero, try START: nothing moves. Then Go.
   - This is a jam your tool can't reach. Routine jams use your area's minor-servicing method. Show the crew where your
     jam boundary is marked on the machine.
   - Where Fix it ends: stand clear while you bleed, because parts held up by air can drop. If the gauge won't go to
     zero through the dump valve, if it creeps back up, or if anything moves when you try START, it's no longer a step
     in your procedure. Stop & get help.
   - Stopped isn't the same as isolated.
   - Prevented: hands in a machine that still had stored energy.
   - **Twist:** "What if there's no lockable dump valve?" Model call: **Stop & get help.** It isn't a step in your
     procedure. Cards 5 and 6 come back to this.
4. **Sorting cards 1, 5, 6.** Do 5 and 6 one after the other: same jam, different answer.
5. **Talk-through.** A machine with compressed air and a gauge or dump valve.
   "Now imagine you find this: you've locked out and opened the dump valve, but the gauge won't go to zero."
6. **Close.** As step 6.

#### Save 5 huddle · Potent ingredient

Back from break, you weigh a potent active ingredient (API) at an isolator.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: one glove has a cut. The pressure display is in range, but it won't show a small cut.
   - Hold 2, the call: **Stop & get help.**
3. **The reason.** A cut glove is a break in the barrier. Your lead and QA must know. It's Stop & get help even if
   you've changed gloves before. No tape, no quick repair. An authorized person changes the glove and leak-tests it.
   Then Go.
   - Stop means hands off. Don't put your hands in the gloves, and don't switch the isolator off: it keeps air flowing
     in through the cut. Keep others back, and call your lead.
   - Your check by eye catches cuts. Pinholes are found by the leak test.
   - Prevented: exposure to a compound that acts at very low doses.
   - **Twist:** "What if the glove's fine, but the pressure display is out of range?" Model call: **Stop & get help.**
4. **Our version.** Ask: "What do we check by eye before we start? Where could we be assuming instead of checking?"
   Capture one or two (section 8).
5. **Talk-through.** An isolator, glovebox, or a station with a PPE check.
   "Now imagine you find this: there's a mark on the glove, and you're not sure if it's a cut."
6. **Close.** As step 6.

#### Save 6 huddle · Line break after CIP

You're the operator about to open a pipe at the transfer panel: a line break, under the line-break permit and your own
line-break procedure. The CIP (clean-in-place) screen says the cleaning cycle is complete.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1, the clue: the screen only reports the cycle. Good answers: Are the supply and pump locked out? What does
     the gauge say? Is the line hot? Where would it spray?
   - Then you lock out supply and pump, step aside, and check the gauge. It shows pressure, and the line is hot.
   - Hold 2, the call: **Fix it.**
3. **The reason.** Your line-break procedure says: wait, vent, check for zero pressure and a cool line. Those steps
   are in your procedure, under your lockout and the permit, and you're trained and authorized for them. Then Go. "The
   screen reports the cycle. The gauge reports your line."
   - "Doesn't a permit job mean Stop & get help?" Only when there's no permit yet. You hold the line-break permit, and
     these steps are in it and in your line-break procedure, so it can be Fix it.
   - If the gauge won't stay at zero after venting, something is still feeding the line. Stop & get help.
   - Contractors use the same rule and the same calls. A contractor who finds a permitted line still under pressure
     goes back to the permit issuer: Stop & get help. Stopping a job works both ways.
   - Prevented: a spray of hot cleaning chemical or steam.
   - **Twist:** "What if there's no line-break permit yet?" Model call: **Stop & get help.**
4. **Sorting cards 2, 3, 9.** Card 10 is the spare.
5. **Talk-through.** A line-break point, transfer panel, or any line with a gauge.
   "Now imagine you find this: you've vented, but the gauge is creeping back up."
6. **Close.** As step 6.

#### After the last huddle

- The decision check (section 6) runs within two weeks. An EHS partner, a trainer or a lead from another area runs it,
  not you.
- Keep one practice huddle a month: a save, three sorting cards, or a local Save 7. Use the same six steps.

## 5. Supervisor response rehearsal

**When.** Before the launch: every supervisor, lead and on-call leader, on every shift, in pairs, for about 20 minutes.
Then in every huddle, at step 5.

### What to do when someone stops

| Step | Do | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Come | Go to the point of work, as fast as your response standard says. If you can't, send your backup and tell the person who's coming. | "I'm on my way. Keep hands off till I get there." |
| 2. Thank | Thank them first, before anything else. | "Thanks for stopping." |
| 3. Ask and look | Let them show you. Listen. Don't finish their sentences. | "What did you see?" · "Show me." |
| 4. Decide together | Agree the call. Keep the job stopped until it's safe. Bring in who's needed: setter, maintenance, permit issuer, EHS, QA. If it turns out to be a Fix it in their own procedure, say so without blame. | "Good call. Let's get the setter here." · "That one's in your procedure. Go ahead, and thanks for checking." |
| 5. Close the loop | Use the safety-hold code, and enter the time you reached the point of work. Report the catch. Fix the cause, or pass it to someone who can. Tell the person, and the crew, what changed. | "Here's what we changed." · "I'll tell you by [day] what we're doing about it." |

### What never to say

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Why is the line down?" | It makes the stop the problem. | "Thanks. What did you see?" |
| "Just get it running." · "We're behind." | Pressure undoes the rule. | "We'll sort this, then we run." |
| "You could have fixed that yourself." | It punishes a call for help. | "Thanks for checking." |
| "Who left it like this?" | It blames a person, not the handover. | "Let's find out what happened at handover." |
| "It's always like that." · "That light's always red." | It teaches people to assume. | "Let's check it." |
| "Don't log it." | It hides the catch. | "Log it as a safety hold." |
| "Where's your signed 2-minute card?" | It's not a form. | "What did you catch?" |
| "Why didn't you do your two minutes?" (after an incident) | It's not a root cause, and not a reason for discipline. | "What made the pause hard here?" |

### Practice prompts

Work in pairs. One plays the person who stops, one responds. Two minutes each, then swap. A third person can watch.

1. End of a late batch. An operator stops the [cartoner] because a guard won't close.
2. A contractor fitter stops a line break: "I locked out, and the gauge still shows pressure."
   Coaching point: thank them, and bring in the permit issuer. A contractor goes back to the permit issuer when a
   permitted line is still under pressure.
3. An operator at the isolator finds a cut glove, ten minutes before a hold time runs out.
   Coaching point: don't push to beat the hold time. Bring QA in. A lost hold time is a batch problem, not a person
   problem.
4. Nights. An operator calls: "The handover note says the tooling change is 'nearly done'. I'm not starting."
5. An operator stops to ask about a grounding clamp that's in their own procedure.
   Coaching point: thank them anyway. When in doubt, get help.

**What the watcher looks for**

- Came to the point of work, or sent a named backup.
- Thanked first.
- Asked "What did you see?", and listened.
- Kept the job stopped until it was safe, and brought in the right help.
- Said when the person will hear what changed.
- Used none of the "never say" lines.

## 6. Decision check (3 scenarios)

This is the pilot's learning check. It replaces "can people say the four questions". It checks the call, because the
call is what keeps people safe. It also checks objectives 1, 2 and 4 (section 1).

### Who and when

- **Who runs it.** An EHS partner, a trainer, or a lead from another area. Not the person's own supervisor, so it
  doesn't feel like a test from the boss.
- **Who takes it.** A sample of people in each pilot area, on every shift, including contractors where you can. Aim for
  at least 10 people per shift. Do the same in the comparison area.
- **When, and which set.**

| Round | Pilot area | Comparison area |
|---|---|---|
| Before the launch (baseline) | Set A | Set A |
| Within two weeks after the last huddle | Set B | Set B |
| About three months later | Set A | Not run: the comparison area gets the video after the second round |

### Before the baseline

- Keep the scenarios out of huddles, sorting cards and posters.
- Trial both sets with about five people from an area that is in neither the pilot nor the comparison. Replace any
  item that nearly everyone gets right with a new one that has the same call.
- EHS checks both sets, and any local names you swap in.

### How to run it (about 5 minutes per person)

1. One person at a time, privately. In a group, people copy.
2. Say: "This checks the training, not you. No names. It doesn't count for anything."
3. Show a plain card with the three calls on it, and no rule text. Don't use the job aid. Say the same three lines
   every round: "Go: you start the job. Fix it: you put it right yourself. Stop & get help: hands off, and you call
   someone."
4. Read each scenario aloud, in the person's language if you can. Show a picture if you have one. Nobody has to write.
5. For each scenario, ask:
   - "What's the clue?"
   - "Which question catches it?"
   - "Your call: Go, Fix it, or Stop & get help?"
   - "Why?"
   For scenario 3, also ask: "What made this a time to pause?"
6. **Part B (own area: not scored for the person, counted for the area).** Ask: "Tell me one place in your own area
   where a gauge, indicator, note or glove could be the catch. What would your call be?"
7. **Feedback.**
   - At the baseline, and every time in the comparison area: no answer feedback. Just thank them.
   - In the pilot area after the launch: give feedback straight away (see the pass guidance).
   - Any round: if an answer shows a real hazard in their work now, treat it as a real catch.
8. Fill in the tally sheet after the person has left. No names.

### Set A

Read them as written. Swap the names in [brackets] for local ones.

1. You hold a line-break permit to change a filter housing on a [hot water line]. You've locked out the supply valve and
   the pump under your own lock. Standing to one side, you check the gauge. It shows a little pressure. Your permit and
   procedure name the drain valve as the vent point, and you're trained and authorized to vent it.
2. A hose on the [vessel heating line] is dripping at a fitting. You have the right wrench. Tightening it would take a
   minute. Maintenance does this work, and there's no permit.
3. After your break, you go back to the [blender] you were charging. Same batch, same materials. You look: the grounding
   indicator is green, the guards are closed, and nothing has moved. The log shows nobody worked on it.

### Set B (EHS checks before use)

1. You're the technician changing filter bags on the [dust collector]. Your procedure says: lock out the fan and the
   air supply, then bleed the pulse-air tank to zero through its drain valve. You're trained and authorized for this
   job. Your locks are on the fan and the air supply. The tank's gauge still shows pressure.
2. The filter door on the [fluid-bed dryer] won't latch fully. You can see the latch bracket is bent. You have pliers,
   and bending it back would take a minute. Repairs to the dryer are maintenance work.
3. Your lead calls you away from the [capsule filler] for five minutes. You come back. Same batch, same materials. You
   look: the guards are closed, the interlock light is green, and the counter shows the same number as when you left.
   The log shows nobody used it while you were away.

### Answer key

| # | The clue | Which question catches it | Model call | Reasons that fit | Other calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

"I'd stop and ask my lead" counts as Stop & get help.

### Pass guidance

**What counts as right.** The right call, plus a reason that names the boundary:

- Fix it: you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure.
- Stop & get help: there's no permit yet, it's someone else's unfinished work, or you're not authorized for it (such
  as a repair).
- Go: nothing is different, and it was checked.

**Misses.**

- **An unsafe miss** is Go when the answer is Fix it or Stop & get help, or Fix it when the answer is Stop & get help.
- **A safe miss** is Stop & get help when the answer is Fix it or Go.
- Fix it when the answer is Go (scenario 3) is a miss, not an unsafe miss.

**For the person** (pilot area, after the launch only; said on the spot, never written down with a name)

- All three right: "You've got it."
- A safe miss: "That's safe. When in doubt, get help. Here's why this one is yours to fix." Or, for scenario 3: "Most
  pauses end in Go."
- An unsafe miss: talk it through now, kindly, using the job aid. Nobody is marked down.

**For the area (what you report)**

- The share of people with all three right (the call, and a reason that fits).
- The share with any unsafe miss.
- The share who gave a real own-area example in Part B. A real example names a specific piece of their own equipment,
  the clue and a call.
- The share who named the time to pause in scenario 3, and a question that fits (objectives 1 and 2).

**Pilot guide.** Aim for at least 8 in 10 people with all three right, and fewer than 1 in 10 with an unsafe miss.
Both should be better than the baseline, and better than any change in the comparison area. If an area falls short,
re-run sorting cards on the missed calls in the next practice huddles, check the fix list is clear in the area, and
check again. Fix the training, not the person.

**Never** use the results for discipline, appraisal, qualification or a training record. Don't report any group smaller
than five people.

## 7. Handover observation checklist

**Purpose.** To see whether the pause happens at handover and changeover, and what makes it hard. It looks at the
practice, not at people. No names.

### How to observe

- **Who observes.** An EHS partner, a trained peer, or a lead from another area. Not the crew's own supervisor.
- **Sample.** Two handovers or changeovers per area per week, spread across shifts, nights and weekends included. Do the
  same in the comparison area. Start four weeks before the launch, for a baseline.
- **Tell the crew.** "I'm looking at how handovers work, not at you. No names go on this sheet."
- **Watch** the first few minutes after the handover or changeover, from a safe spot. Don't coach while you watch. If you

## 8. Local examples and "Save 7"

### Capturing local examples in huddles

This is how learners meet objective 4 (section 1): *give an example from your own area where a gauge, indicator, note
or glove would be the catch.*

**The activity: "Our version" (saves 2, 3 and 5, about 3 minutes)**

1. Ask the prompt in that save's note.
2. Give pairs 30 seconds to talk first, so everyone talks, not just the most confident.
3. Take one or two examples. For each, ask: "What's the clue? Which question catches it? What's our call? Who would we
   call?" Then: "What would make it a Fix it, and what would make it Stop & get help?"
4. Write four things on a flipchart or whiteboard, in the crew's words: **Clue · Our call · Who we'd call · Action
   needed.** No names. Take a photo if your site allows it, or copy it down.
5. If an example is a live hazard right now, treat it as a real catch. Report it today.

After the huddle, take 2 minutes to copy the flipchart into the log and fill in the other columns.

**Local examples log**

| Date | Area and shift | Task or equipment | The clue (gauge, indicator, note, glove, other) | The question that catches it | Our call | Who we'd call | Action needed? | OK to use as Save 7? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | |

Keep the log with the area's huddle records. Review it monthly with EHS. It shows what the crew sees as the risks, and it
feeds Save 7.

### Building a local "Save 7"

After the first quarter, turn a real good catch into a local save in the same template as the video.

1. **Pick** a real catch from the log, the "You caught it, we fixed it" board, or the good-catch reports. Pick one
   where the cause was fixed.
2. **Ask for consent.** Ask the person who made the catch if their story can be used. They can say no, with no effect on
   them. On the log, note "consent given" and the date. Don't write their name.
3. **Take out anything that points to a person:** names, the shift if it would identify someone, batch numbers, dates.
   Don't blame the last shift or anyone else.
4. **Write it in the save template:**
   - **Save 7:** [the task, in one line]. When: [before you start / after a break / at handover / after an
     interruption / after any change]
   - **Hold 1, "What would make you stop here?"** The clue: [what was seen]
   - **Hold 2, "Your call?"** [Go / Fix it / Stop & get help], because [the boundary rule reason]
   - **Twist:** [one changed fact that would flip the call, and the new call]
   - **Talk-through:** "Now imagine you find this:" [a situation at a real machine]
   - **The steps, in order:** [what happened next]
   - **Prevented:** [one plain line, no injury shown]
   - **What we changed:** [how the cause was fixed]
5. **Check it** with the area lead, EHS and QA for accuracy. The person who made the catch reads it first.
6. **Use it** as a practice huddle, with the same six steps. Use a photo of the equipment with no people in it (follow
   your site's photo rules), or a simple drawing. Pause at the two holds yourself.

## 9. Measurement

What you're testing: do people make the right call, do they pause at handover, and do leaders show up when someone stops.

### Rules for every measure

- Report by area, never by name. Split by shift only where the group has at least five people (for observations, at
  least eight in the block). Don't report any group smaller than five.
- Nothing here is used for discipline, appraisal or a quota.
- Measure the same way, at the same times, in the pilot and the comparison area, up to the end of the pilot.

### Baseline and comparison area

- **Baseline.** Start four weeks before the launch. At the start, announce the safety-hold code in both the pilot and
  the comparison area with the same short shift briefing, separate from the video: what the code is for, and that it
  never counts against the shift. Then start the handover observations, run the decision check (set A, no answer
  feedback), and ask the pulse question.
- **Comparison area.** A similar area that isn't in the pilot, such as another packaging hall. It gets the same
  safety-hold briefing and keeps all its usual procedures. It gets the video after the second decision-check round. It
  shows whether a change came from the pilot or from something site-wide, like a campaign, an audit or the season.

### What to measure

| Measure | What it shows | How | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|

### Cadence

- **Weekly, during the pilot.** Safety-hold codes, response times, observations and huddles held. The area lead and EHS
  look at them for ten minutes.
- **Monthly.** Show the crews what you found and what changed, at a huddle.
- **End of the pilot (about week 8: the launch, six huddles, then the check).** Compare with the baseline and the
  comparison area. Fix what got in people's way, then decide on a wider rollout. The three-month check follows in the
  pilot area.

### How to read it

- More safety holds and more good-catch reports early on are a good sign. People are using it.
- Both areas heard about the safety-hold code at the same time, so a rise in the pilot beyond any change in the
  comparison area is the signal.
- Response time and catches closed are the leaders' measures, not the crew's.
- If holds stay flat while observations show catches, ask the crew what stops them calling.

## 10. Glossary (locked terms)

Translate each term once, then lock it. Use the same words in the captions, voice, job aid, sorting cards, decision check
and posters. The job aid prints these words exactly. Words in [brackets] in this guide are local names and are not
locked.

| Term (locked) | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| 2-minute rule | A short, deliberate pause at the point of work. Two minutes is a guide. A repeat task takes seconds. |
| Point of work | The place where you do the job, before your hands go on it. |
| Before you start · After a break · At handover · After an interruption · After any change | The five times to pause. On screen, the video uses short forms (Before you start · Break · Handover · Interruption · Change). These phrases are their locked long forms. |
| Stop | Hands off. |
| Look | Around the job, and compare it with the plan. |
| Ask | Ask yourself the four questions. |
| What's different? | Anything new, changed, or not as planned? |
| What could hurt us? | Moving parts, power, pressure, heat, sparks, chemicals, low oxygen? |
| Checked or assumed? | Did you look or test it yourself, or are you guessing? |
| What if it goes wrong? | Where does it go? Who is in the way? |
| Decide | Make one of three calls. |
| Go | You found nothing wrong. Start the job. |
| Fix it | Only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. |
| Stop & get help | Hands off. Leave it as it is. Call your lead. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? The fix doesn't work? |
| When in doubt, get help | Not sure which call? Stop & get help. |
| Boundary rule | When you may Fix it yourself, and when you must Stop & get help. |
| Your own procedure | The written steps for your job that you were trained on. |
| Trained and authorized | You were trained on it, and your site has said you may do it. You need both. |
| Someone else's unfinished work | A job another person or shift started and hasn't finished or handed back. |
| Repair | Putting broken or worn equipment right, like a leak, a bent part or a faulty guard. |
| Your lead | The person who comes when you stop. Named on your job aid for days, nights and weekends. |
| Catch | Something your pause found before it could hurt anyone. |
| Safety hold | A stop to get help. It has its own code, and it never counts against your shift. |
| Handover | One person or shift passes the job to the next. |
| Changeover | The line switches to a new product or batch. |
| Lock out | Cut off every energy source, and put your own lock on each one. |
| Isolate · Isolated | Cut off and lock out every energy source or line. Stopped isn't the same as isolated. |
| Stored energy | Energy left after you lock out: trapped air, pressure, heat. |
| Bleed to zero | Let trapped air out through the valve your procedure names, until the gauge reads zero. |
| Dump valve | A valve that lets trapped air out of a machine. A lockable one takes your own lock. |
| Vent | Let pressure out of a line, through the vent point your procedure names. |
| Try START | After lockout, make sure everyone is clear, then press start to prove nothing moves. Then set it back to off. |
| Minor servicing | A routine jam or small task you may do with the approved tool and method, without lockout. |
| Jam boundary | The mark on a machine that shows where minor servicing ends. Past it, lock out. |
| Permit | Written permission for a higher-risk job, like a line break or opening a vessel. "No permit yet" means Stop & get help. If you hold the permit and the step is in it, it can be Fix it. |
| Line break | Opening a pipe or hose that has held product, chemicals, steam or pressure. |
| Manway | The opening in the top of a vessel. |
| Gas test | Measuring oxygen and vapor before anyone opens or enters. |
| Blank off | Close a pipe with a solid plate, so nothing can get through. |
| Inerted | Filled with nitrogen to keep oxygen out. |
| Low oxygen | Air with too little oxygen. You can't see it or smell it. |
| Grounding clamp · Grounding indicator | A clamp that connects equipment to ground (earth), so static can't build up. The indicator shows whether it's connected. |
| Guard · Interlock | A guard keeps hands out. An interlock stops the machine when the guard opens. |
| Isolator | A sealed box you work into through gloves. |
| Leak test | A test that checks a glove for holes too small to see. |
| Hold time | The longest a batch may wait at a step. |
| CIP (clean-in-place) | Cleaning equipment without taking it apart. |
| Line clearance | Checking the line is clear before each run. The 2-minute rule is line clearance for people. |
| Awareness training | Training that builds a habit. It doesn't qualify you for lockout, confined-space work or any procedure. |

**Translation notes**

- Keep the four questions as questions.
- Keep the three calls short, and clearly different from each other.
- "Isolate" and "isolator" must use different words. In many languages they share a root: pick words that can't be
  confused.
- "Line" means the production line, or a pipe (as in line break). This guide calls the limit between Fix it and Stop &
  get help the "boundary rule", never "the line". Keep it that way in translation.
- "Ground" and "earth" mean the same thing. Pick one word per language and use it everywhere.
- Test each translation with two or three people from the floor before you print.

  interlock work, opening a vessel"). Make sure each example is a Stop & get help job on your fix list.
- **Check locally:** sorting cards 3, 4, 5 and 6, against your fix list.
