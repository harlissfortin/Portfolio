# The 2-Minute Rule: facilitator guide

This guide is for the launch session and the four weekly huddles that go with the video *The 2-Minute Rule: One Shift
on a Patch Line* (about 6:26). The video follows one shift on a transdermal patch line: a routine Go, then four saves.

This is awareness training. It builds a habit. It is not lockout, solvent-bonding, forklift-operator or potent-compound
handling training, and it does not qualify anyone to do any fix. Your site's procedures, permits and lockout always
come first. The saves are illustrative composites, not real incidents. Your EHS, operations, QA and training leads
review this guide and the video against your site before use.

## 1. Who it's for and what you need

### Who runs it

- **Launch session.** The site head or area head opens it live. A supervisor, area lead or trainer runs the rest.
- **Huddles.** The crew's own supervisor or team lead. An EHS partner or trainer can co-run the first one.
- **Decision check and observations.** An EHS partner, a trainer, or a lead from another area. Not the crew's own
  supervisor.
- **Who attends.** Everyone who works on or around the patch line, on every shift: mix room, coating, laminating,
  die cutting, slitting, pouching and packaging; roll handlers and drivers; technicians, maintenance and cleaning; QA on
  the floor; and contractors. Nights and weekends get their own sessions.

### What learners can do

By the end of the four huddles, each person can:

| | The learner can | Where it's practised | How it's checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Say when to pause: before you start, and after any break, handover, interruption or change | Huddle step 1 (recall) | Decision check: "What made this a time to pause?" |
| 2 | Run Stop · Look · Ask · Decide, with the four questions | Hold 1 in each save; the step 5 talk-through | Decision check: "What's the clue?" and "Which question catches it?" |
| 3 | Choose Go, Fix it or Stop & get help using the boundary rule, and say why. Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop & get help. Not sure, or the fix doesn't work? Stop & get help. | Hold 2, the twist, sorting cards | Decision check: the call and the reason |
| 4 | Give an example from their own area where a lamp, a note, a lock or tag, a training card or a PPE board would be the catch | "Our version" (saves 2 and 4) | Decision check, Part B |

### Before you run anything

These must be in place first. They are in the rollout guide in
[`stakeholder-review.md`](stakeholder-review.md#rollout-guide).

- A signed stop-work and non-retaliation policy that covers contractors.
- A response standard on every shift: who comes when someone stops, how fast, and who covers when they don't answer.
- The QA position, co-signed.
- Your area's fix list and jam boundaries, posted in the area (through change control).
- The safety-hold code ("Safety hold – get help") live in MES/OEE, and announced in the pilot and comparison areas.
- The job aid printed, with the local contacts filled in, and the sign approved by QA for posting.

If any of these is missing, don't launch. The video makes promises on leaders' behalf.

### Get yourself ready

1. Watch the full video once, start to end.
2. Read this guide.
3. Do the supervisor response rehearsal (section 5) with your peers.
4. Know your area's fix list: what your crew may fix, and what always needs help. Know who is trained on which trucks.
5. Do the "Check first" items in each huddle note (section 3).
6. Get your sorting cards and call cards printed, with your own equipment names (section 4).

### What you need

| | Launch session | Each huddle |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | About 22 min, once per shift | 10–15 min, once a week for four weeks, plus 10 min to prepare |
| **When** | At shift start or a planned stop | At shift start before the line runs, at a planned stop, or with the crew split in two so the line stays covered |
| **Place** | A quiet room or area near the work | Beside the machine or area you'll use for the talk-through (step 5), so nobody has to walk |
| **Screen and sound** | A screen the whole group can see, and speakers | A tablet or laptop. Sound loud enough over the line. |
| **Player** | Plays the full video | Can pause and jump to a time. Download the video to the device before the shift; don't stream it. |
| **If you don't have it** | Large crew, or no big screen: run it twice, in groups of up to six around a laptop | No device: read the save aloud from its huddle note (see "No device?" in section 3) |
| **Job aid** | A pocket card for each person, and the sign posted at each point of work | The posted sign, and spare pocket cards |
| **Local contacts** | Filled in on the job aid before the session | The same |
| **Other** | Pens, and a way to write down concerns | Call cards for everyone (collect them after). Sorting cards (saves 1 and 3) or a flipchart or whiteboard (saves 2 and 4). This week's huddle note and the huddle sheet. |

**Captions and language.** The main video has English captions burned in. For each other language on your floor, the
training team makes a copy with that language's captions burned in, before the launch, and puts it on the huddle
devices. For a mixed crew, play the version in the language most of the crew reads best. Give each person the job aid
and cards in their own language, and ask a bilingual colleague to repeat each hold question. Don't add a captions file
to a copy that already has captions, or every line shows twice.

### The job aid

The job aid is the point-of-work cue. It comes in two formats: a pocket card for each person, and a sign posted where
the job starts (on the machine, at the room door, or by the permit board). It has:

- the five times to pause: Before you start, After a break, Handover, Interruption, Change
- Stop · Look · Ask · Decide, and the four questions, each with a plain meaning
- the three calls, with the boundary rule: when you may Fix it, and when you Stop & get help
- blanks for local help: your lead (days, nights, weekends), radio or phone, who to call if there's no answer, and
  where to report a catch

It is not a form. No signatures, no tick boxes. Nobody hands it in.

The video ends on a slate that asks "Who do you call for help?", with blanks for days, nights, weekends and the radio
or phone, and says "Write it on your pocket card." The job aid is that card.

**Classified and potent-product areas.** QA approves the sign as a controlled posting, including its material. In
classified or potent-product rooms, post the sign outside the gowning room or airlock door, or outside the viewing
window, or use a cleanroom-compatible version approved by QA that can be wiped with your cleaning agent. Pocket cards
stay outside the gowning line unless your site has approved a cleanroom version.

### Fill in the local contacts first

Before the launch, fill in the blanks for each area and shift.

| Blank on the job aid | What goes there |
|---|---|
| Your lead: days | The person who comes when you stop on day shift |
| Your lead: nights | The person who comes on nights |
| Your lead: weekends | The person who comes on weekends |
| Radio or phone | How to reach your lead: the radio channel or phone extension |
| No answer? Call | Who covers when your lead is tied up or doesn't answer, from the response standard: for example, the shift manager, the control room or the on-call leader |
| Report catches to | Your usual reporting route |

Contractors: your lead is the host area lead or your permit issuer.

If you can't fill a blank, that's a gap in the response standard. Close it before the launch.

## 2. The launch session (about 22 minutes)

The goal: everyone sees the full video once, hears a leader make the commitments live, and leaves with a filled-in job
aid.

| Part | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Live opening: leader commitments and the QA position | About 3 min | Site or area head |
| 2. The full video | About 7 min (6:26, plus pauses at Save 1's holds) | Facilitator |
| 3. Discussion | About 8 min | Facilitator, with the head staying |
| 4. Close | About 2 min | Facilitator |

With a few minutes to settle and hand out the job aid, that's about 22 minutes. Book 25. Don't cut the discussion to
fit.

### Part 1. Live opening (site or area head)

Say it live, on every shift, nights and weekends included. A recording doesn't count. Use your own words, but keep every
commitment below. Don't read it from a slide.

> "I'd rather lose two minutes than see anyone hurt or a batch lost.
> Anyone can stop a job, staff or contractor, even on a late batch. No punishment, no blame.
> When you stop, a leader comes quickly, thanks you, fixes the cause and tells you what changed.
> A stop to get help never counts against your shift.
> It's not a form. Report what you caught the usual way.
> On this shift, the person who comes is [role]. On nights it's [role]. On weekends it's [role]. If they don't answer,
> call [role]."

Then say the QA position:

> "QA's position: this video changes no procedure and no batch record. A pause, or a hold to get help, is not a deviation
> in itself. If you stop, it's logged against the job, never against you. Some holds do need QA, like a material that
> doesn't match the batch record. Your lead brings QA in. That's still not on you."

Then stay for the discussion. Your presence is the message.

### Part 2. Play the full video

1. Say: "In each save, the video stops twice and asks you. Call out your answer."
2. Play it start to end. Pause at Save 1's two holds so the crew sees how they work, and let the rest run. The huddles
   are where you practise.
3. If someone can't see or hear well, move them closer. For a large crew with no big screen, run the session twice, in
   groups of up to six around a laptop.

### Part 3. Discussion (about 8 minutes)

Ask, then listen. Write down what people say. Don't defend or argue. Let people answer in their own language, and ask
a colleague to translate.

1. "Which save felt closest to our work?"
2. "Where would the catch be on our line? A lamp, a note, a lock or tag, a training card, a PPE board?"
3. "Who do you call on this shift? On nights? On weekends? And if they don't answer?"
   Everyone checks their pocket card. Fix any blank now.
4. "What would make it hard to stop here?" If people need a start: "In Save 2, a co-worker said 'Line's waiting. Just
   grab the forklift.' Where do we hear that kind of thing?"
   This is the most important question. Thank people for honest answers. The head says what they will do about it, and
   when they will come back.

### Part 4. Close (about 2 minutes)

1. Hand out the pocket card. Show where the sign is posted.
2. Explain the huddles: "Every week for four weeks, 10 to 15 minutes, at the line, one save. You make the calls."
3. Say when you'll come back on the concerns raised.
4. The head closes: "Anyone can stop a job."

**After the session**

- Record attendance in your usual training system, as awareness training only.
- Send the list of concerns to the area head. Report back on them at the first huddle.

## 3. Four weekly huddles (10–15 minutes each)

Huddles are practice, not re-watching. After the launch, don't replay the rule chapters. Each week the crew recalls the
rule from memory, makes the calls in one save, and practises at the real line.

Hold each huddle beside the machine or area you'll use for the talk-through, and play the save on the tablet there.
Nobody walks. Run it at shift start before the line runs, at a planned stop, or with the crew split in two so the line
stays covered.

Print this week's huddle note (each one fits on a page) and the [huddle sheet](job-aid/huddle-sheet.pdf), and take them
to the floor.

### At a glance

| Default week | Save | When | The call | Step 4 | Real place for the talk-through |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Save 1 · Solvent transfer | Before you start | Fix it | Sorting cards 1, 5, 6 | A solvent drum station, a bonding reel, or any bonding clamp and lamp |
| 2 | Save 2 · Roll staging | After a break | Stop & get help | Our version | Roll staging: a rack bay, the next-roll spot or where your pallet truck parks |
| 3 | Save 3 · Die cutter | Handover | Stop & get help | Sorting cards 4, 7, 8 | Any machine with a guard and a lockout point, where handovers happen |
| 4 | Save 4 · Potent product | Change | Fix it | Our version | A gowning point or PPE board outside a potent-product room |

**Order.** Run them 1 to 4. That's the video's order and the order of the shift, and the calls go Fix it, Stop & get
help, Stop & get help, Fix it. The boundary rule between those calls is what you're teaching. An area may start with
the save closest to its work (mix room: 1; roll handling: 2; die cutting and other converting: 3; slitting and potent
products: 4). Run all four. The huddle notes are written by save, so they work in any order.

### Find the save and its holds

Use the chapter markers in your player to jump to the save. If your player doesn't show chapters, drag the slider to
the save's start time in the table below. Each save stops twice:

- **"What would make you stop here?"** A short hold, about 3 seconds. The clue is in the picture.
- **"Your call?"** A short hold, about 2.5 seconds, with three outlined buttons: Go, Fix it, Stop & get help. The right
  one fills in on the reveal.

Pause the player at each one for as long as your crew needs. Save 2 also has a short beat with no words while the
driver brings the pallet down. It isn't a question: let it play.

On screen and on the job aid, the button reads **"Fix it"**, and it always means the boundary rule: you're trained and
authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. In the routine Go, the video also asks "Your call?", and the right
answer there is **Go**. Use it to show that most pauses end in Go.

Pause as soon as the question card appears. If you miss it, go back 5 seconds.

**No device?** Read the save aloud from its huddle note: the setup line, then "What would make you stop here?" (wait
for answers), then the clue; then "Your call?" (the crew votes), then the call and the reason.

### How the crew votes

At every "Your call?" hold, every twist and every sorting card, the crew votes the same way.

1. Everyone has a set of call cards: green Go, amber Fix it, red Stop & get help. Each has its word and icon, so it
   works in any language and without colour.
2. Say: "On three, hold up your call. One, two, three."
3. Everyone holds one up at the same time. Nobody waits to see what others pick.
4. Ask for a volunteer from each answer to say why. Then go on.

No call cards? Everyone points at the call on the back of their pocket card, at the same time.

### Every huddle runs the same six steps

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Recall | 1.5 min | The crew says the five times to pause, the four questions and the three calls |
| 2. Play the save | 3–4 min | The crew answers at both holds, before the video does |
| 3. The reason and the twist | 2 min | Why that call; then one changed fact, and the crew votes again |
| 4. Cards or our version | 3 min | Sort three cards, or find our own version |
| 5. Talk-through at the line | 3 min | One person talks through their two minutes; the supervisor rehearses the response |
| 6. Close | 1.5 min | What changed, one catch story, who you call, the next save |

**Step 1. Recall (1.5 min)**

1. Ask the whole crew, not one person: "When do we pause?"
   You want: before you start, and after any break, handover, interruption or change.
2. "What are the four questions?"
   You want: What's different? What could hurt us? Checked or assumed? What if it goes wrong?
3. "What are the three calls? When is it Fix it, and when is it Stop & get help?"
   You want: Go, Fix it, Stop & get help. Fix it only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your
   procedure. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? Stop & get help. Not sure, or the fix
   doesn't work? Stop & get help.
4. Let the crew fill gaps for each other. Point to the job aid only after they've tried.

In your first huddle, build the list together. From your third huddle on, make it about real work: "Name a time you
paused this week. Which trigger was it? Which question did the work?" Then ask for anything still missing.

**Step 2. Play the save (3–4 min)**

1. Say: "It will stop twice. Answer out loud before it moves on."
2. Play from the start of the save chapter.
3. At **"What would make you stop here?"**, pause the player. Ask the question again. Wait. Get two or three answers.
   Don't say who's right yet. Play on.
4. At **"Your call?"**, pause the player. The crew votes with call cards. Ask a volunteer from each answer why. Play on.
5. Let the reveal play to the end of the chapter. Stop there. Don't play the next chapter.

**Step 3. The reason and the twist (2 min)**

1. Ask: "Why that call, and not the other one?"
2. Bring it back to the boundary rule: when it's Fix it, and when it's Stop & get help. Use the reason in the save's
   note, and the "Listen for" lines.
3. If someone picked a different call, ask what they saw.
   - They chose Stop & get help where the answer was Fix it: "That's safe. When in doubt, get help. Here's why this one
     is in your procedure."
   - They chose Go or Fix it where the answer was Stop & get help: walk through the boundary rule again, kindly.
   - They chose Go where something was still wrong: ask "Checked or assumed?" Go back to the picture at hold 1 and find
     the clue together.
4. If it comes up: "No permit yet" is Stop & get help. If you hold the permit and the step is in it, it can be Fix it.
   None of the four saves needs a permit; sorting cards 2 and 9 practise it.
5. **The twist.** Read the one changed fact in the save's note. The crew votes again. Ask one volunteer why. Keep it
   short.

**Step 4. Sorting cards or our version (3 min)**

- Sorting cards (saves 1 and 3): see section 4.
- Our version (saves 2 and 4): use the prompt in the save's note, and run it as in section 8. Then ask: "What would
  make our example a Fix it, and what would make it Stop & get help?"

**Step 5. Talk-through at the line (3 min)**

1. Use a machine or area like the one in the save (see the table above). Pick one that's stopped or between batches,
   or stand well back from a running one and don't distract whoever is running it. Leave it as it is.
2. Ask for a volunteer. Rotate each week. Never pick someone to test them. They can talk in their own language, hold
   the job aid and read from it, or do it as a pair.
3. The volunteer talks through their two minutes out loud, as if about to start:
   - Stop: "Hands off."
   - Look: around the job, and compare it with the plan. They point at what they would look at.
   - Ask: the four questions, out loud, with what they would check here.
   - Decide: they say the call.
4. Then say: "Now imagine you find this:" and read the situation in the save's note. The volunteer stops and says the
   call, in any words. The supervisor responds as in section 5, starting with: **"Thanks. What did you see?"**
5. Then say to everyone: "When you get back to your station, do your two minutes before you start. I'll come by this
   shift and ask one of you to talk me through it." Then do it.
6. Keep it safe. This is talk only. Nobody touches controls, guards, valves, locks, clamps or trucks. Stay outside
   guarding and barriers, and out of truck aisles. In the mix room and in potent-product rooms, don't break PPE,
   gowning or entry rules for a huddle: use a station outside, or a photo. If the volunteer spots something real,
   treat it as a real catch.

**Step 6. Close (1.5 min)**

1. Close the loop: "Last time we raised [catch or concern]. Here's what changed." If nothing has changed yet, say when
   it will.
2. "Did anyone use it this week? Catch anything?" Take one story. Thank them. Note any others for after the huddle.
3. "Who do you call on this shift if you stop? And if they don't answer?" Point at the job aid.
4. Thank the volunteer.
5. Name the next save.
6. In your last huddle, add: "In the next two weeks, some of you will be asked three quick questions, one to one. No
   names. After that, we keep one practice huddle a month."

**If you only have 10 minutes**

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| 1. Recall | 1.5 min |
| 2 and 3. Play the save, then the reason in one line (skip the twist) | 4 min |
| 4. One sorting card, or one local example | 1.5 min |
| 5. Talk-through | 2.5 min |
| 6. Close | 0.5 min |

Never drop step 5. Pick up the twist and the other cards at next month's practice huddle.

**After the huddle (2 min).** Record attendance the usual way. Copy the flipchart into the local examples log (section
8). Pass on any catch today.

### Huddle notes

Each note is written by save and fits on one printed page. "Check first" lists what depends on your site: check it
before the huddle, and tell the crew where your site differs from the video.

#### Save 1 huddle · Solvent transfer

Mix room, before you start: you'll pump flammable solvent from a drum into the adhesive mix tank with an air drum
pump. You're already in your solvent PPE.

**Check first.** Your mix-room PPE. Your bonding set-up: a reel and a BOND lamp, as in the video, or a fixed ground
reel with a pump interlock (then the clue is the red lamp alone). Where the clamps go, and which hose, from your
procedure.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1 ("Hands off. What would make you stop here?"), the clue: both bonding clamps hang on their hook, nothing is
     clipped to the drum or the pump, and the BOND lamp is red. The question that catches it: **Checked or assumed?**
   - Hold 2 ("Your call?"), the call: **Fix it.**
3. **The reason.** Bonding is a step in your procedure, and you're trained and authorized to do it. Clamps first, on
   bare metal on the drum and the pump, before the drum is opened. The hose is the conductive type. The lamp turns
   green: then Go. Won't go green? Stop & get help.
   - Why it matters: an unbonded drum or pump can hold a static charge and spark where solvent vapor comes out, mostly
     at the tank opening as it fills.
   - Listen for: "I'd clip on and start" (look at the lamp first); "I'd open the drum while I find the clamps" (clamps
     first); "That lamp's always red" (that's a catch in itself: report it).
   - Prevented: no static spark where the vapor comes out.
   - **Twist:** "What if one clamp's jaw is broken and won't grip?" Model call: **Stop & get help.** A damaged clamp is
     a repair, not a step in your procedure. Don't pump.
4. **Sorting cards 1, 5, 6.** Do 5 and 6 one after the other: same jam, different answer.
5. **Talk-through.** A drum station, a bonding reel, or any bonding clamp and lamp. If the mix room needs PPE or a
   permit to enter, stand at its door or use a photo. Nobody opens a drum or moves a clamp.
   "Now imagine you find this: you've clipped both clamps onto bare metal, and the lamp is still red."
6. **Close.** As step 6, plus: "Before every transfer this week: clamps on, lamp green, then open the drum."

#### Save 2 huddle · Roll staging

After your break, you go to roll staging, on the warehouse side of the line's material airlock, for the line's next
roll of backing film. You're trained and authorized on the powered pallet truck and the roll lifter, not on the
sit-down forklift.

**Check first.**

- **Who drives what.** Do line operators drive powered pallet trucks here? If they use manual jacks, the catch works
  the same way: the jack is yours, the forklift isn't.
- **How "trained on" shows.** A card, a badge endorsement, a posted list, or a reader on the truck? OSHA doesn't issue
  forklift licences or cards: your employer trains, evaluates and certifies each operator. With a badge, the pressure
  line becomes "Just use my badge." The call is the same.
- **The airlock and staging.** Do pallets cross the airlock, or are they exchanged at it? Are rolls racked in staging?
  Who drives the forklift on each shift? If your forklifts have a restraint other than the video's lap belt, say so.

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1 ("Look closely. What would make you stop here?"), the clue: the pallet of rolls is now up on the rack's top
     beam (before your break it was on the floor), and your TRAINED ON card shows Pallet truck ✓ and an empty Forklift
     box. The question that catches it: **What's different?**
   - Then a co-worker who wants to help says: "Line's waiting. Just grab the forklift!"
   - Hold 2 ("So, your call?"), the call: **Stop & get help.**
3. **The reason.** Not authorized: you're trained on the pallet truck, not the forklift. A forklift is different to
   drive and needs its own training: under 29 CFR 1910.178(l), your employer trains and evaluates you on each type of
   truck before you drive it on your own. A co-worker's "just grab it" is neither training nor supervision. And don't
   reach the roll any other way: no climbing the rack, no pulling it down.
   - What happens next: you say no kindly and call your lead. Your lead thanks you and sends a trained driver.
     Everyone stands clear of the forks and the truck's path: nobody stands or walks under raised forks
     (1910.178(m)(2)). Once the pallet is down, your own steps: pallet truck, roll lifter, Go.
   - The lead fixes the cause without blame: the next rolls stay at floor level. Nobody is blamed: not the co-worker,
     not the driver, not whoever moved the pallet.
   - Listen for: "I've driven one before" or "It's only a few feet" (your site authorizes you for each type of truck;
     driving one somewhere else isn't that); "I'd pull it down with the pallet truck" (not any other way either);
     "I'd feel bad saying no" (that's why the video gives you the words, and the co-worker says "Fair enough").
   - Prevented: nobody drives a truck they're not trained on.
   - **Twist:** "What if your card says Forklift ✓ too, and you're authorized to drive it on this shift?" Model call:
     **Go**, as the driver: pre-use check done, belt on, everyone clear of the forks and the truck's path.
4. **Our version.** Ask: "Which trucks, lifters and hoists do we use? Which ones are you trained on? Where could a job
   change, during a break or a handover, into one you're not trained for?" Capture one or two (section 8).
5. **Talk-through, with the kind speak-up.** Roll staging, out of truck aisles and away from any working truck.
   Nobody gets on a truck.
   "Now imagine you find this: the pallet you need is up high, and a co-worker says, 'Line's waiting. Just grab the
   forklift.'" The volunteer answers kindly, in their own words, and says who they'll call. The video's line is a good
   model: **"Thanks, but I'm not trained on that one. I'll call our lead."** The supervisor responds as in section 5.
   If there's time, swap roles.
6. **Close.** As step 6, plus: "Know which trucks your card covers. If a job changes into one it doesn't, call your
   lead."

**Discussion prompts** (if you have time, or at a monthly practice huddle; check each against your site's rules)

- **Pre-use check.** Trucks are checked before use, at least daily (1910.178(q)(7)). "What if the horn or the brakes
  fail it?" Model call: **Stop & get help.** The truck comes out of service.
- **Horn at aisles.** Drivers slow down and sound the horn at cross aisles and blind spots (1910.178(n)(4)). "Where are
  ours? Where do you walk?"
- **The lifter's rated capacity.** "What if the roll's label says it's heavier than the plate on your roll lifter?"
  Model call: **Stop & get help.** It needs other equipment and the people trained on it.
- **Damaged racking.** "What if you see a bent upright or a knocked beam in staging?" Model call: **Stop & get help.**
  Keep loads off it and people clear. Someone competent checks it before it's used again.

#### Save 3 huddle · Die cutter

At handover, you take over the rotary die cutter as it comes back to production after a die change. The note on the
panel says "Die change done. Ready to run ✓".

**Check first.** Do lockout tags carry a name or a badge number here? What does your site's lock-removal procedure
say, and who signs off the restart?

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1 ("Now, what would make you stop here?"), the clue: the die station's clear guard is off, leaning on the
     frame, and someone else's lock and tag are still on the power switch. The question that catches it: **Checked or
     assumed?** The note is assumed; the guard and the lock are what you checked.
   - Hold 2 ("What's your call?"), the call: **Stop & get help.**
3. **The reason.** It's someone else's unfinished work. You never touch the lock, the tag or the guard, and you call
   your lead. Only the lock's owner removes it, or a supervisor under the site's lock-removal procedure (29 CFR
   1910.147(e)(3)): the owner isn't on site, every reasonable effort is made to reach them, and they're told before
   they're back at work. The owner finishes, puts the guard back and removes their own lock. Then your restart checks:
   guard on, area clear, and the checks your procedure lists. Then Go.
   - Listen for: "The note says it's ready" (a note is someone's word); "I'd put the guard back myself" (it's part of
     their work); "I'm trained on die changes, I'd finish it" (it still starts with Stop & get help: the work is theirs
     until they hand it back).
   - Prevented: the die cutter never runs with its guard off and the work unfinished.
   - **Twist:** "What if the guard is on, there's no lock or tag, and the technician tells you face to face that the
     die change is finished?" Model call: **Go**, after your restart checks.
4. **Sorting cards 4, 7, 8.** Whose work is it? Your own PPE step is yours. Someone else's unfinished work, and the
   interlock, are not.
5. **Talk-through.** Any machine with a guard and a lockout point, where handovers happen. The die cutter is ideal if
   it's stopped.
   "Now imagine you find this: the handover note says a guard was taken off, and you can't tell if the job was
   finished."
6. **Close.** As step 6, plus: "At handover this week, ask the person going off shift, face to face, about locks,
   guards and anything half done."

#### Save 4 huddle · Potent product

The slitter changes over to a potent product: strong in tiny amounts. You're in the coat, with single gloves and no
sleeve covers, handling sticky trim.

**Check first.** Each product's PPE comes from your site's own exposure assessment, not from this video; don't quote
exposure-band numbers. Is a coat standard in the room? Do potent products run only in dedicated rooms, with gowning
outside? Where is the potent waste? What are your steps if product touches skin?

1. **Recall.** As step 1.
2. **Play.**
   - Hold 1 ("Your turn. What would make you stop here?"), the clue: a close-up shows bare skin at your wrist, between
     the glove cuff and the coat sleeve. The PPE board by the door now says POTENT PRODUCT, with double gloves and
     sleeve covers marked NEW. The question that catches it: **What could hurt us?** ("What's different?" also fits:
     the board changed.)
   - Hold 2 ("And your call?"), the call: **Fix it.**
3. **The reason.** Putting on this product's protective gear is your own gowning step, and you're trained on it. Bring
   the slitter to a normal stop and put the trim in the potent waste. At the gowning point, take off your gloves as
   your procedure says, and wash hands and wrists with soap and water, not hand sanitizer. Then inner gloves, sleeve
   covers, and outer gloves over the cuffs. No skin showing. Then Go.
   - Why it matters, said calmly: the product is made to pass through skin. The concern is small, repeated doses, not
     one touch. Soap and water washes it off; hand sanitizer doesn't, and may help it into the skin. If product touches
     skin: wash it off and report it, as your site says.
   - Listen for: "I'd finish the roll first" (stop the slitter first); "I'd tape the gap" (the board says what to
     wear); "Will one touch hurt me?" (answer calmly: one touch isn't the worry).
   - Prevented: no small, repeated doses from a product made to pass through skin.
   - **Twist:** "What if there are no sleeve covers at the gowning point?" Model call: **Stop & get help.** You can't
     do your gowning step, so you don't handle the potent product. Call your lead.
4. **Our version.** Ask: "What changes here at a product changeover? The PPE board, the procedure, the waste bins, the
   labels? Where could we be assuming the last product's set-up still fits?" Capture one or two (section 8).
5. **Talk-through.** A gowning point or PPE board outside a potent-product room. Don't go in or break gowning for a
   huddle: use a station outside, or a photo.
   "Now imagine you find this: halfway through a roll, you see a split in your outer glove." (Model: normal stop, step
   away, change gloves and wash as your procedure says: your own gowning step.)
6. **Close.** As step 6, plus: "At every changeover this week, read the board before you go back to the line."

#### After the last huddle

- The decision check (section 6) runs within two weeks. An EHS partner, a trainer or a lead from another area runs it,
  not you.
- Keep one practice huddle a month: a save, sorting cards 2, 3 and 9, the Save 2 discussion prompts, or a local
  Save 5. Use the same six steps.

## 5. Supervisor response rehearsal

**When.** Before the launch: every supervisor, lead and on-call leader, on every shift, in pairs, for about 20 minutes.
Then in every huddle, at step 5.

### What to do when someone stops

| Step | Do | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Come | Go to the point of work, as fast as your response standard says. If you can't, send your backup and tell the person who's coming. | "I'm on my way. Keep hands off till I get there." |
| 2. Thank | Thank them first, before anything else. | "Thanks for stopping." |
| 3. Ask and look | Let them show you. Listen. Don't finish their sentences. | "What did you see?" · "Show me." |
| 4. Decide together | Agree the call. Keep the job stopped until it's safe. Bring in who's needed: a setter, maintenance, a trained driver, the permit issuer, EHS, QA. If it turns out to be a Fix it in their own procedure, say so without blame. | "Good call. I'll get a trained driver." · "That one's in your procedure. Go ahead, and thanks for checking." |
| 5. Close the loop | Use the safety-hold code, and enter the time you reached the point of work. Report the catch. Fix the cause, or pass it to someone who can. Tell the person, and the crew, what changed. | "Here's what we changed." · "I'll tell you by [day] what we're doing about it." |

### What never to say

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Why is the line down?" | It makes the stop the problem. | "Thanks. What did you see?" |
| "Just get it running." · "We're behind." | Pressure undoes the rule. | "We'll sort this, then we run." |
| "Just grab the forklift." · "Use my badge." | Not authorized means not authorized. It undoes the training and the stop. | "I'll get a trained driver. Thanks for waiting." |
| "You could have fixed that yourself." | It punishes a call for help. | "Thanks for checking." |
| "Who left it like this?" · "Who put that pallet up there?" | It blames a person, not the handover or the system. | "Let's find out what happened, and fix it." |
| "It's always like that." · "That lamp's always red." | It teaches people to assume. | "Let's check it." |
| "Don't log it." | It hides the catch. | "Log it as a safety hold." |
| "Where's your signed 2-minute card?" | It's not a form. | "What did you catch?" |
| "Why didn't you do your two minutes?" (after an incident) | It's not a root cause, and not a reason for discipline. | "What made the pause hard here?" |

### Practice prompts

Work in pairs. One plays the person who stops, one responds. Two minutes each, then swap. A third person can watch.

1. End of a late batch. An operator stops the [pouching machine] because a guard won't close.
2. An operator calls from roll staging: "The next roll's up on the top beam, and I'm not trained on the forklift."
   Someone in the background says, "We're losing the line."
   Coaching point: thank them first and send a trained driver. If the line waits, you own that. Then fix where the
   next rolls are staged.
3. A contractor servicing the exhaust fan on the [coater], under a permit, stops: "I've locked out, but the fan's
   still turning."
   Coaching point: thank them, and bring in the permit issuer. A contractor goes back to the permit issuer when the
   isolation doesn't hold.
4. Nights. An operator calls: "The handover note says the die change is 'nearly done', and someone's lock is still on.
   I'm not starting."
   Coaching point: never ask them to remove the lock or finish the job. Reach the owner, or follow the site's
   lock-removal procedure.
5. At a potent-product changeover, an operator calls to check they should stop and put on sleeve covers, which is in
   their own gowning procedure. The line is behind plan.
   Coaching point: thank them anyway. When in doubt, get help. Don't let the plan show in your voice.

**What the watcher looks for**

- Came to the point of work, or sent a named backup.
- Thanked first.
- Asked "What did you see?", and listened.
- Kept the job stopped until it was safe, and brought in the right help.
- Said when the person will hear what changed.
- Used none of the "never say" lines.

## 6. Decision check (3 scenarios)

This is the pilot's learning check. It replaces "can people say the four questions". It checks the call, because the
call is what keeps people safe. It also checks objectives 1, 2 and 4 (section 1).

### Who and when

- **Who runs it.** An EHS partner, a trainer, or a lead from another area. Not the person's own supervisor, so it
  doesn't feel like a test from the boss.
- **Who takes it.** A sample of people in each pilot area, on every shift, including contractors where you can. Aim for
  at least 10 people per shift. Do the same in the comparison area.
- **When, and which set.**

| Round | Pilot area | Comparison area |
|---|---|---|
| Before the launch (baseline) | Set A | Set A |
| Within two weeks after the last huddle | Set B | Set B |
| About three months later | Set A | Not run: the comparison area gets the video after the second round |

### Before the baseline

- Keep the scenarios out of huddles, sorting cards and posters. None of them is one of the four saves.
- Trial both sets with about five people from an area that is in neither the pilot nor the comparison. Replace any
  item that nearly everyone gets right with a new one that has the same call.
- EHS checks both sets, and any local names you swap in.

### How to run it (about 5 minutes per person)

1. One person at a time, privately. In a group, people copy.
2. Say: "This checks the training, not you. No names. It doesn't count for anything."
3. Show a plain card with the three calls on it, and no rule text. Don't use the job aid. Say the same three lines
   every round: "Go: you start the job. Fix it: you put it right yourself. Stop & get help: hands off, and you call
   someone."
4. Read each scenario aloud, in the person's language if you can. Show a picture if you have one. Nobody has to write.
5. For each scenario, ask:
   - "What's the clue?"
   - "Which question catches it?"
   - "Your call: Go, Fix it, or Stop & get help?"
   - "Why?"
   For scenario 3, also ask: "What made this a time to pause?"
6. **Part B (own area: not scored for the person, counted for the area).** Ask: "Tell me one place in your own area
   where a lamp, a note, a lock or tag, a training card or a PPE board could be the catch. What would your call be?"
7. **Feedback.**
   - At the baseline, and every time in the comparison area: no answer feedback. Just thank them.
   - In the pilot area after the launch: give feedback straight away (see the pass guidance).
   - Any round: if an answer shows a real hazard in their work now, treat it as a real catch.
8. Fill in the tally sheet after the person has left. No names.

### Set A

Read them as written. Swap the names in [brackets] for local ones.

1. You're about to splice a new roll of [release liner] at the [coater unwind]. Your splice procedure says: unwind
   stopped, splice on the splice table, and cut-resistant gloves for the splice knife. You're trained and authorized
   to splice. The unwind is stopped. You have your normal work gloves on. Your cut-resistant gloves are on the shelf
   beside you.
2. A fitting on the [coater's solvent wash line] is weeping. You have the right wrench, and tightening it would take a
   minute. Maintenance does this work, and there's no permit.
3. After your break, you go back to the [laminator] you were running. Same batch, same materials. You look: the nip
   guards are down, there are no locks or tags, and the roll on the unwind is the one you loaded. The relief operator
   says nothing changed while you were away, and the log agrees.

### Set B (EHS checks before use)

1. You're about to wipe adhesive off the nip rolls on the [laminator] with solvent. It's locked out under your own
   lock, and you've tried START: nothing moves. Your cleaning procedure says solvent-resistant gloves, and you're

2. The cable to the sealing-jaw heater on the [pouching machine] has a split in its outer cover, where it bends. You

### Pass guidance

**What counts as right.** The right call, plus a reason that names the boundary:

- Fix it: you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure.
- Stop & get help: there's no permit yet, it's someone else's unfinished work, or you're not authorized for it (such
  as a repair).
- Go: nothing is different, and it was checked.

**Misses.**

- **An unsafe miss** is Go when the answer is Fix it or Stop & get help, or Fix it when the answer is Stop & get help.
- **A safe miss** is Stop & get help when the answer is Fix it or Go.
- Fix it when the answer is Go (scenario 3) is a miss, not an unsafe miss.

**For the person** (pilot area, after the launch only; said on the spot, never written down with a name)

- All three right: "You've got it."
- A safe miss: "That's safe. When in doubt, get help. Here's why this one is yours to fix." Or, for scenario 3: "Most
  pauses end in Go."
- An unsafe miss: talk it through now, kindly, using the job aid. Nobody is marked down.

**For the area (what you report)**

- The share of people with all three right (the call, and a reason that fits).
- The share with any unsafe miss.
- The share who gave a real own-area example in Part B. A real example names a specific piece of their own equipment,
  the clue and a call.
- The share who named the time to pause in scenario 3, and a question that fits (objectives 1 and 2).

**Pilot guide.** Aim for at least 8 in 10 people with all three right, and fewer than 1 in 10 with an unsafe miss.
Both should be better than the baseline, and better than any change in the comparison area. If an area falls short,
re-run sorting cards on the missed calls in the next practice huddles, check the fix list is clear in the area, and
check again. Fix the training, not the person.

**Never** use the results for discipline, appraisal, qualification or a training record. Don't report any group smaller
than five people.

## 7. Handover observation checklist

**Purpose.** To see whether the pause happens at handover and changeover on the patch line, and what makes it hard. It
looks at the practice, not at people. No names.

### How to observe

- **Who observes.** An EHS partner, a trained peer, or a lead from another area. Not the crew's own supervisor.
- **Sample.** Two handovers or changeovers per area per week, spread across shifts, nights and weekends included. Do the
  same in the comparison area. Start four weeks before the launch, for a baseline.
- **Tell the crew.** "I'm looking at how handovers work, not at you. No names go on this sheet."
- **Watch** the first few minutes after the handover or changeover, from a safe spot, outside any gowned or potent
  room. Don't coach while you watch. If you see danger, stop the job: anyone can.
- **Ask one question after:** "What made the pause easier or harder today?"
- **Feed back** to the crew in general terms at the next huddle: "At handovers this week, we saw..." Never "X didn't...".
- **Keep it anonymous.** On a small crew, a sheet can point to one person even without a name. EHS holds the raw sheets,
  tallies them into the area totals each week, then destroys them. Sheets never go into the area file or to the line
  supervisor. Results are shared only by area, in 4-week blocks, with shifts pooled (at least eight observations per
  block).

PPE or trained driver · Other: ________

## 8. Local examples and "Save 5"

### Capturing local examples in huddles

This is how learners meet objective 4 (section 1): *give an example from your own area where a lamp, a note, a lock or
tag, a training card or a PPE board would be the catch.*

**The activity: "Our version" (saves 2 and 4, about 3 minutes)**

1. Ask the prompt in that save's note.
2. Give pairs 30 seconds to talk first, so everyone talks, not just the most confident.
3. Take one or two examples. For each, ask: "What's the clue? Which question catches it? What's our call? Who would we
   call?" Then: "What would make it a Fix it, and what would make it Stop & get help?"
4. Write four things on a flipchart or whiteboard, in the crew's words: **Clue · Our call · Who we'd call · Action
   needed.** No names. Take a photo if your site allows it, or copy it down.
5. If an example is a live hazard right now, treat it as a real catch. Report it today.

After the huddle, take 2 minutes to copy the flipchart into the log and fill in the other columns.

**Local examples log**

| Date | Area and shift | Task or equipment | The clue (lamp, note, lock or tag, card, board, other) | The question that catches it | Our call | Who we'd call | Action needed? | OK to use as Save 5? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | |

Keep the log with the area's huddle records. Review it monthly with EHS. It shows what the crew sees as the risks, and it
feeds Save 5.

### Building a local "Save 5"

After the first quarter, turn a real good catch into a local save in the same template as the video's four.

1. **Pick** a real catch from the log, the "You caught it, we fixed it" board, or the good-catch reports. Pick one
   where the cause was fixed.
2. **Ask for consent.** Ask the person who made the catch if their story can be used. They can say no, with no effect on
   them. On the log, note "consent given" and the date. Don't write their name.
3. **Take out anything that points to a person:** names, the shift if it would identify someone, batch or lot numbers,
   dates. Don't blame the last shift or anyone else.
4. **Write it in the save template:**
   - **Save 5:** [the task, in one line]. When: [Before you start / After a break / Handover / Interruption / Change],
     and where it falls in the shift
   - **Hold 1, "What would make you stop here?"** The clue: [what was seen]
   - **The question that catches it:** [one of the four]
   - **Hold 2, "Your call?"** [Go / Fix it / Stop & get help], because [the boundary rule reason]
   - **Twist:** [one changed fact that would flip the call, and the new call]
   - **Talk-through:** "Now imagine you find this:" [a situation at a real machine]
   - **The steps, in order:** [what happened next, ending in Go once it was safe]
   - **Prevented:** [one plain line, no injury shown]
   - **What we changed:** [how the cause was fixed]
5. **Check it** with the area lead, EHS and QA for accuracy. The person who made the catch reads it first.
6. **Use it** as a practice huddle, with the same six steps. Use a photo of the equipment with no people in it (follow
   your site's photo rules), or a simple drawing. Pause at the two holds yourself.

## 9. Measurement

What you're testing: do people make the right call, do they pause at handover, and do leaders show up when someone stops.

### Rules for every measure

- Report by area, never by name. Split by shift only where the group has at least five people (for observations, at
  least eight in the block). Don't report any group smaller than five.
- Nothing here is used for discipline, appraisal or a quota.
- Measure the same way, at the same times, in the pilot and the comparison area, up to the end of the pilot.

### Baseline and comparison area

- **Baseline.** Start four weeks before the launch. At the start, announce the safety-hold code in both the pilot and
  the comparison area with the same short shift briefing, separate from the video: what the code is for, and that it
  never counts against the shift. Then start the handover observations, run the decision check (set A, no answer
  feedback), and ask the pulse question.
- **Comparison area.** A similar area that isn't in the pilot, such as another converting or packaging area. It gets
  the same safety-hold briefing and keeps all its usual procedures. It gets the video after the second decision-check
  round. It shows whether a change came from the pilot or from something site-wide, like a campaign, an audit or the
  season.

### What to measure

| Measure | What it shows | How | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision check | People make the right call | Section 6: share with all three right; share with any unsafe miss | Baseline (set A), after the last huddle (set B), about 3 months (set A, pilot only) |
| Own-area examples | Objective 4 | Decision check Part B; examples in the local log | Each huddle, and with the check |
| Handover observations | The pause happens in practice | Section 7: share with a pause before hands-on work; share with lock, guard and interlock state handed over | 2 per area per week; reported by area in 4-week blocks, shifts pooled |
| Safety-hold codes | People use Stop & get help | Count of "Safety hold – get help" per [1,000 run hours or per shift], from MES/OEE, by area | Weekly. Expect a rise. No reduction target. |
| Help-call response time | Leaders show up | From the call to a leader at the point of work. The leader enters the arrival time in the safety-hold entry in MES/OEE. Spot-check with the observation sheet's "Minutes to arrive". | Weekly |
| Catches closed | Leaders close the loop | Share closed with feedback to the finder within target; median days to close | Monthly |
| Repeat findings | Causes are engineered out | Findings with the same cause | Monthly |
| Lockout, permit and truck audit findings | Handover, changeover and truck use hold up | Your usual audits | As scheduled |
| Good-catch reports | People report | Per 100 workers, by area. Expect an early rise, and treat it as success. | Monthly |
| Pulse question | Trust | *"I can stop a job without blame."* Split by shift and language group. | Baseline, end of pilot |
| Huddles held | The plan is delivered | Huddles held against planned, by shift | Weekly |

### Cadence

- **Weekly, during the pilot.** Safety-hold codes, response times, observations and huddles held. The area lead and EHS
  look at them for ten minutes.
- **Monthly.** Show the crews what you found and what changed, at a huddle.
- **End of the pilot (about week 6: the launch, four huddles, then the check).** Compare with the baseline and the
  comparison area. Fix what got in people's way, then decide on a wider rollout. The three-month check follows in the
  pilot area.

### How to read it

- More safety holds and more good-catch reports early on are a good sign. People are using it.
- Both areas heard about the safety-hold code at the same time, so a rise in the pilot beyond any change in the
  comparison area is the signal.
- Response time and catches closed are the leaders' measures, not the crew's.
- If holds stay flat while observations show catches, ask the crew what stops them calling.

## 10. Glossary (locked terms)

Translate each term once, then lock it. Use the same words in the captions, voice, job aid, sorting cards, decision check
and posters. The job aid prints these words exactly. Words in [brackets] in this guide are local names and are not
locked.

| Term (locked) | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| 2-minute rule | A short, deliberate pause at the point of work. Two minutes is a guide. A repeat task takes seconds. |
| Point of work | The place where you do the job, before your hands go on it. |
| Before you start · After a break · Handover · Interruption · Change | The five times to pause, word for word as on the video's recap card and the job aid. In running speech: before you start, and after any break, handover, interruption or change. The rule scene's pill says "Break". |
| Stop | Hands off. |
| Look | Around the job, and compare it with the plan. |
| Ask | Ask yourself the four questions. |
| What's different? | Anything new, changed, or not as planned? |
| What could hurt us? | Moving parts, power, heat, sparks, solvents, heavy loads, potent product? |
| Checked or assumed? | Did you look or test it yourself, or are you guessing? |
| What if it goes wrong? | Where does it go? Who is in the way? |
| Decide | Make one of three calls. |
| Go | You found nothing wrong. Start the job. |
| Fix it | Only if you're trained and authorized, and it's a step in your procedure. |
| Stop & get help | Hands off. Leave it as it is. Call your lead. No permit yet? Someone else's unfinished work? Not authorized? The fix doesn't work? |
| When in doubt, get help | Not sure which call? Stop & get help. |
| Boundary rule | When you may Fix it yourself, and when you must Stop & get help. |
| Your own procedure | The written steps for your job that you were trained on. |
| Trained and authorized | You were trained on it, and your site has said you may do it. You need both. |
| Someone else's unfinished work | A job another person or shift started and hasn't finished or handed back. |
| Repair | Putting broken or worn equipment right, like a leak, a damaged cable or a faulty guard. |
| Your lead | The person who comes when you stop. Named on your job aid for days, nights and weekends. |
| Catch · Save | Something your pause found before it could hurt anyone. A save is a pause where a question caught a problem. |
| Safety hold | A stop to get help. It has its own code, and it never counts against your shift. |
| Handover · Changeover | One person or shift passes the job to the next. At a changeover, the line switches to a new product or batch. |
| Line clearance | Checking the line is clear before each run. The 2-minute rule is line clearance for people. |
| Bonding · BOND lamp | Clamps and wires that join the drum, the pump and the tank, so static charge can't build up and spark. The lamp shows it's connected: red, not bonded; green, bonded. |
| Potent | Strong in tiny amounts. |
| PPE · PPE board | Your protective gear. The board at the room door shows the PPE for the product running now. |
| Gowning step · Gowning point | Putting on the PPE the board and your procedure name, and the place by the door where you do it. |
| Roll staging | Where rolls wait for the line, on the warehouse side of the line's material airlock. |
| Pallet truck · Forklift | A pallet truck is walk-behind and moves pallets at floor level. A forklift is ride-on and lifts loads high. They are different types of truck, and each needs its own training. |
| Trained on | Your site's record of the trucks you're trained, evaluated and authorized to drive: a card, a badge or a posted list. OSHA doesn't issue licences or cards. |
| Roll lifter | Lifts a roll by its core. |
| Lock out | Cut off every energy source, and put your own lock on each one. |
| Lock's owner · Lockout owner | The person whose lock and tag are on. Only they remove it, or a supervisor under the site's lock-removal procedure: the owner isn't on site, every reasonable effort is made to reach them, and they're told before they're back at work. |
| Isolate · Isolated | Cut off and lock out every energy source or line. Stopped isn't the same as isolated. |
| Stored energy | Energy left after you lock out: trapped air, springs, heat. |
| Bleed to zero | Let trapped air out through the valve your procedure names, until the gauge reads zero. |
| Dump valve | A valve that lets trapped air out of a machine. A lockable one takes your own lock. |
| Try START | After lockout, make sure everyone is clear, then press start to prove nothing moves. Then set it back to off. |
| Minor servicing · Jam boundary | A routine jam or small task you may do with the approved tool and method, without lockout. The jam boundary marks where it ends: past it, lock out. |
| Permit | Written permission for a higher-risk job, like reaching into a vessel or hot work. "No permit yet" means Stop & get help. If you hold the permit and the step is in it, it can be Fix it. |
| Guard · Interlock | A guard keeps hands out. An interlock stops the machine when the guard opens. |
| Die station | Where the die cutter cuts the patches. |
| Awareness training | Training that builds a habit. It doesn't qualify you for lockout, solvent bonding, driving a truck, potent-product handling or any procedure. |

**Translation notes**

- Keep the four questions as questions.
- Keep the three calls short, and clearly different from each other.
- "Pallet truck" and "forklift" must be clearly different words. Some languages use one word for both: pick two that
  can't be confused, because the difference is the whole point of Save 2.
- "Bonding" and "grounding" are different ideas. Bonding joins two things; grounding connects them to earth. If your
  language has one word for both, add the plain meaning the first time.
- "Potent" means strong in tiny amounts. Don't translate it as "poison" or "toxic".
- "Line" means the production line. This guide calls the limit between Fix it and Stop & get help the "boundary rule",
  never "the line". Keep it that way in translation.
- Test each translation with two or three people from the floor before you print.

  `job-aid/job-aid.html`.
- **Check locally:** the example line under Stop & get help on the sign. Make sure each example is a Stop & get help
  job on your fix list.
- **Check locally:** sorting cards 3, 4, 5 and 6, against your fix list.
- **Check locally:** the "Check first" items in each huddle note.
