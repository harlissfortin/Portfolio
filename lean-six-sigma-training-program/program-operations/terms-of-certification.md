# Terms of Certification

The rules a candidate agrees to, and the obligations the program accepts in return. Published
to candidates before enrollment and linked from every credential verification page. Written to
be read by a learner, not by a lawyer — a certification whose terms are unreadable cannot be
enforced fairly.

*This is a program-policy document, not legal advice; jurisdiction-specific contract, privacy,
and consumer-protection terms are layered on by counsel before commercial launch.*

---

## 1. What certification means, and what it does not

Certification attests that the holder met the published requirements of that level at the time
of award — completing the learning, passing the assessment, and (from Yellow Belt up)
demonstrating application through reviewed work.

It is **not** a license, not a guarantee of employment or competence in any specific job, and
not transferable to another person. Each level's exit-competence statement defines the claim;
holders are asked not to represent their credential as more than it states, and the program
publishes each level's scope and exclusions precisely so that no one has to guess.

## 2. Candidate obligations

1. **Do your own work.** The assessment and the applied work must be the candidate's own.
2. **Tell the truth in applied work.** Observations, baselines, data, and results must be
   real. Honest failure passes; fabricated success does not.
3. **Respect other people in your submissions.** Describe processes, not individuals. Do not
   submit personal data about colleagues, patients, or customers, and do not submit your
   employer's confidential information — the program does not want it and will not store it.
4. **Do not share assessment content.** Item banks are shared assets; distributing items
   degrades the credential for every holder.
5. **Keep your contact details current** while any assessment or review is open.

## 3. Program obligations

1. **Publish the requirements, scope, and exclusions** for every level, before enrollment.
2. **Set cut scores by a documented method** (modified Angoff) and publish the method and the
   date it was run — never a number chosen by convention.
3. **Assess against the requirements published at enrollment**, even if standards change
   mid-cohort.
4. **Use calibrated reviewers**, and separate coaching from assessment from Green Belt up.
5. **Return reviews within the published turnaround** (10 business days for project and
   applied-work review) and include one included revise-and-resubmit cycle where the level
   provides it.
6. **Provide accommodations** (§6).
7. **Keep verification resolvable permanently**, including if the holder's account closes.
8. **Audit ourselves** — annual re-review of a published percentage of awarded credentials —
   and publish pass rates and sampling rates.
9. **Protect learner data** per §7, including not passing individual assessment responses to
   an employer.

## 4. Academic integrity

Violations: proxy completion; sharing or receiving assessment content; fabricating data,
observations, or results; submitting another person's project; misrepresenting a credential.

Process: a suspected violation is put to the candidate in writing with the evidence, and the
candidate has **14 days to respond** before any decision. Outcomes range from resubmission, to
a failed attempt, to revocation of the credential and, for repeated or severe cases, a bar on
future enrollment. Decisions are made by the assessment lead, not by the candidate's instructor.

Revocation resolves the public verification page to "revoked" with a date and no further
detail — the program does not publish the reason.

## 5. Appeals

Any candidate may appeal an item, a score, a rubric decision, an integrity finding, or a
revocation.

| Stage | Who | Window |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Review request | Assessment lead re-examines; for a rubric decision, a **second calibrated reviewer** re-scores blind to the first score | Request within 14 days of the decision; answered within 10 business days |
| 2 — Appeal panel | Two program reviewers plus one external practitioner not involved in the case | Request within 14 days of stage 1; answered within 20 business days |

No fee at stage 1. Stage 2 carries a fee that is **refunded if the appeal succeeds in any
part**. A sustained item challenge credits every candidate who saw that item and retires it
(assessment policy §4). Stage 2 decisions are final within the program.

## 6. Accommodations

Candidates may request accommodations for disability, neurodivergence, or language, without
disclosing a diagnosis beyond what the accommodation requires. Standard provisions, granted as
a matter of course: extended or removed time limits; screen-reader and keyboard-only paths;
captions and transcripts; a reader or scribe; alternative-format materials; and breaks.

Structural accommodations are built in rather than requested where possible — White Belt has
no time limits at all, no drag-and-drop interactions, and full transcripts by default. A
request is answered within 5 business days and never noted on the credential.

## 7. Data, privacy, and retention

- **Collected:** enrollment and identity data, learning records, assessment responses and
  scores, and applied work submitted for review.
- **Shared with a corporate sponsor:** completion and credential status, plus aggregated
  cohort results. **Not shared:** individual item-level responses, reflection text, or
  pre-assessment scores. Applied work (e.g. the White Belt observation) is shared with the
  sponsor's named CI lead **because that is the exercise's purpose**, and the candidate is
  told so before they submit.
- **Never used** for the sponsor's performance management; a sponsor found doing so is in
  breach of the rollout agreement.
- **Retention:** credential records permanently (verification depends on it); assessment
  responses 3 years for item analysis; applied work 3 years, or removed sooner on request
  where the credential record can stand without it.
- Candidates may request export or deletion of their personal data, subject to keeping the
  minimum record needed to keep a credential verifiable.

## 8. Fees, refunds, transfers

Published per level at enrollment. In summary: cohort programs are transferable to a later
cohort once, without fee, up to 14 days before start; refunds available before course access
is granted, and pro-rated thereafter per the published schedule. Retake allowances are part of
tuition as published per level; further attempts carry the retake fee and the waiting period.
**Certification is never contingent on additional purchase** — no level's pass depends on
buying coaching, a retake bundle, or the next belt.

## 9. Recertification

- **White Belt:** none. Evergreen awareness credential.
- **Yellow Belt:** none required; optional refresher offered.
- **Green and Black Belt:** three-year cycle on CEUs plus evidence of practice, earnable at no
  cost through the alumni community, with a modest administrative fee for the review.
- **Master Black Belt:** no recertification; active-status directory listing requires biennial
  evidence of practice.

Lapsing a Green or Black Belt recertification moves the credential to `inactive` on the
verification page. It is never deleted, and it is reinstated on completing the current cycle's
requirements — the program does not erase work that was genuinely done.

## 10. Changes to these terms

Material changes are published with an effective date and do not apply retroactively to
candidates already enrolled. Every version is retained and linked from the current one.
