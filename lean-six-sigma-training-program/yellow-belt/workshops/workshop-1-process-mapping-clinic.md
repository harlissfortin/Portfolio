# Workshop 1 — Process Mapping Clinic (Facilitator Guide)

**Duration:** 120 minutes, live virtual (extended from 90 on review — the original
schedule had no buffer and reliably overran into the closing debrief) · **Class size:**
8–24 (breakouts of 3–4)
**Prerequisite for attendees:** Unit 1 complete; each brings a SIPOC + draft swimlane map of
a real process they touch.
**Facilitator:** certified program instructor (GB+). **Producer recommended** above 16
attendees (manages breakouts/tech so the facilitator can float).

**Workshop outcomes.** Every attendee leaves with (1) their own map upgraded through peer +
instructor critique, (2) the experience of *walking* a map against reality, (3) a marked-up
map: handoffs counted, waits and rework loops flagged, one candidate focus area circled.

## Materials & setup
- Virtual whiteboard (Miro/Mural/…) — one board per breakout, pre-loaded with the program's
  swimlane frame, symbol legend, and annotation stickers (⚡ error, ⏱ wait, 🔁 rework).
- The "Vendor Setup" demo map (deliberately flawed — see 0:15 segment) on the main board.
- Timer visible; parking-lot frame; this guide's checklists.
- Tech check 10 min before start; attendees' draft maps uploaded to their breakout board
  **before** session (chase stragglers the day prior — the workshop fails for anyone who
  arrives empty-handed; have one spare practice process ready for them anyway: "employee
  onboarding," pre-drafted at 9 steps).

## Run of show

### 0:00–0:10 — Welcome & the point of maps
- One breath of theory, no re-lecture: *"A map is a camera, not a brochure. Today we find
  out what your camera missed."*
- Poll: "How many steps in your draft map?" — display spread. Note aloud: fewer than 8
  usually means brochure; more than 20 means drowning. Both get fixed today.
- Ground rules: critique maps, never map-owners; every question about someone's process is
  a gift to them.

### 0:10–0:27 — Live demo: interrogating a flawed map
Facilitator walks the pre-built "Vendor Setup" map and models the interrogation out loud,
recruiting answers from the room. The map contains **five planted flaws** — find at least
four with the group (keep one in your pocket for time):
1. A step written as a department name, not verb+noun ("Finance" instead of "Verify tax form").
2. A decision diamond with unlabeled exits.
3. A missing rework loop everyone knows exists (rejected duplicates go back — but no
   backward arrow).
4. A "happy-path lie": the map shows one approval; reality has a pre-check email that isn't
   drawn (work-as-imagined vs. work-as-done).
5. Zero time annotations — the map can't say where the 12 days go.

Model the **map interrogation questions** (attendees get these as a sticker on their board):
- "What happens when this goes wrong?" (finds rework loops)
- "Who exactly does this — and do they do anything first?" (finds hidden steps)
- "How long does the work sit before this step?" (finds waits)
- "Is this what the SOP says, or what actually happens?" (finds the gap)

### 0:27–1:07 — Breakout round 1: peer map walks (40 min)
Groups of 3–4. Each attendee gets 9 minutes in the hot seat (4 × 9 = 36, plus a 4-minute
buffer for rotation — the buffer is not optional):
- **2 min:** owner walks their map silently-ish (peers hold questions).
- **5 min:** peers interrogate using the sticker questions; owner edits the map **live** —
  adding loops, splitting steps, labeling diamonds, marking ⚡⏱🔁.
- **1 min:** owner states the one biggest change to their map.
Roles rotate. Facilitator + producer float; the most common interventions:
- A group being polite instead of curious → model one sharp question and leave.
- An owner defending instead of editing → *"You're not wrong — the map is. Fix the map."*
- Maps ballooning past 20 steps → *"Which step hides a world? Draw a box around it and
  label it 'own map later.'"*

### 1:07–1:22 — Regroup: the handoff count (15 min)
- Each attendee posts two numbers in chat: **handoffs** (lane crossings) and **rework loops**
  now on their map vs. their draft. Read a few deltas aloud — the point lands by itself
  ("your process didn't get worse in the last hour; your camera got better").
- Mini-teach (5 min): annotating waits with rough times; why the *longest queue* on the map
  outweighs the busiest step; %C&A in one sentence as the bridge to VSM reading (Unit 1.3).

### 1:22–1:50 — Breakout round 2: where's the treasure? (28 min)
Same groups. Each owner, with peers:
1. Adds rough time annotations (best honest guesses; ranges welcome) to waits and the 2–3
   biggest steps.
2. Counts: total elapsed vs. touch time (the White Belt running-clock, now on their own
   process).
3. **Circles ONE candidate focus** — biggest wait, worst rework loop, or nastiest handoff —
   and writes one sentence: *"If I only improved one thing here, it would be ___ because ___."*
Facilitator prompt for the stuck: "Where would a customer standing next to the work item
sigh the loudest?"

### 1:50–2:00 — Close & commitments (10 min)
- Three volunteers (recruit in breakouts, don't cold-call) each get 60 seconds: one thing
  your map was hiding.
- Homework, said plainly: **walk your process for real within 7 days** — follow one work
  item, fix the map again, post the before/after to the cohort channel. The circled focus
  is a legitimate mini-project seed (route it through the Unit 5 triage first).
- Preview Workshop 2: "You've found where it hurts. Next we practice finding *why* —
  bring the problem statement you'll draft in Unit 4."

## Facilitator notes & failure modes
- **Time discipline is the hard part.** Round 1 hot seats WILL overrun; appoint a
  timekeeper per group and enforce rotation over completeness — an unfinished good critique
  beats one perfect map and two untouched ones. The schedule now carries buffers; if you
  are still behind at 1:22, shorten round 2 to the time annotations and the circled focus
  — never the close. The close is where the homework commitment happens.
- **The "process I don't actually touch" attendee** (mapped someone else's work from
  hearsay): pair them as *interrogator-in-chief* in round 1 and have them re-scope to a
  process they own before round 2.
- **Corporate cohorts mapping the same process:** convert round 1 to build one shared map
  per group, then round 2 compares the groups' maps — the *differences between maps of the
  same process* become the work-as-imagined lesson, and it's a better one.
- **Sensitive discoveries** (a map reveals a compliance workaround): acknowledge, park,
  and follow the program's escalation guidance — never let it become theater in session.
- Scoring/observation: this workshop is required for certification attendance but not
  graded; facilitator flags attendees whose maps suggest a Green-Belt-sized problem for the
  progression-pathway follow-up (see program blueprint).
