# Unit 2 — Workplace Organization: 5S, Visual Management & Standard Work (1.5 h)

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this unit the learner can:
1. Run a 5S implementation in a real physical **or digital** workspace, event-style, using
   the red-tag method and the audit sheet.
2. Design one visual control that makes an abnormality obvious at a glance.
3. Draft a simple standard-work document (best current way, owned by the people who do it).

---

## 2.1 5S implementation, not 5S appreciation (35 min)

White Belt taught what 5S is. Yellow Belt *does* it. The unit walks a full mini-event
(typical footprint: one workstation, one supply area, or one shared drive; half a day of
effort spread over a week or two).

**Step 0 — Baseline.** Photograph everything as-is (or screenshot the drive/inbox). Score the
area with the audit sheet ([`templates/five-s-audit-sheet.md`](../templates/five-s-audit-sheet.md))
*before* touching anything. The before/after pair is your evidence — and your storyboard's
best exhibit.

**Sort — the red-tag technique.** Everything in the area gets one of three fates: keep (used
here, regularly), relocate (used, but not here/rarely), or **red-tag** (unclear). Red-tagged
items go to a dated quarantine zone with a tag naming the item and date. Untouched in 30
days → dispose/archive per the area's rule. The red tag turns "we might need it someday"
from an argument into an experiment.
*Digital sort:* archive-don't-delete plays the same quarantine role; anything unopened in the
archive after the agreed window gets purged per retention policy.

**Set in Order — by frequency, then by ergonomics.** Daily-use items within arm's reach;
weekly within a step; monthly in labeled storage elsewhere. Then make positions
self-explaining: shadow boards, taped outlines, labeled bins, one-item-one-home.
*Digital:* pinned/current at top level; naming convention `YYYY-MM-DD_project_doc_v#` or the
team's agreed pattern; one folder = one purpose; "current" is marked structurally (a folder),
never by filename adjectives ("final_FINAL_v3").

**Shine — clean to inspect.** While cleaning, log every anomaly found (leak, frayed cable,
broken link, stale dashboard). The anomaly list is the deliverable; the cleanliness is the
by-product.

**Standardize — make the new state the documented norm.** Photo standards posted at the
point of use ("this is what ready-for-shift looks like"), a 5-minute end-of-shift/end-of-week
reset routine, and owner names on the standard.

**Sustain — put it on a calendar with a name attached.** A 5-minute weekly self-audit
(audit sheet), a monthly peer or leader audit, and a visible score trend. The score isn't
for punishment — a falling score is a signal that the *standard* no longer fits the work and
needs revisiting. Sustain fails silently when audits stop; the countermeasure is boring and
absolute: scheduled, owned, visible.

**Try:** Score two photographed workspaces (one physical, one digital) with the audit sheet;
compare against the instructor's scoring and rationale.

## 2.2 Visual management: the workplace that speaks (25 min)

The goal-state of workplace organization: **status obvious at a glance, abnormality
impossible to miss, no login or meeting required.**

Three levels of visual control, in rising strength:
1. **Visual indicator** — tells you something (label, sign, color code). Easy to ignore.
2. **Visual signal** — grabs attention when a threshold is crossed (andon light, min/max
   line on a bin, dashboard tile turning red, WIP-limit column filling up).
3. **Visual control / physical constraint** — makes the wrong thing hard or impossible
   (fixture that only accepts the part one way, form field that rejects invalid input,
   kanban square that physically caps the queue).

Design rules:
- **Answer one question per visual.** "Are we ahead or behind, right now?" "Is anything
  missing?" A visual that needs explaining has failed.
- **Put it at the point of use,** not in a report.
- **Make normal vs. abnormal binary.** Green zone/red zone beats a number people must
  interpret. A min/max line on a supply bin outperforms an inventory report every time.
- **Prefer moving the answer up a level:** wherever a signal keeps firing, ask what
  constraint (level 3) would make the signal unnecessary.

**Examples across verticals:** shadow board with one missing wrench outline (instantly shows
*what's* missing); color-banded patient charts by discharge-readiness; a team board with an
aging column — any ticket older than 3 days physically moves to a red zone.

**Try:** For a scenario in your vertical, sketch (describe) one visual for each of the three
levels; peer-vote in the community gallery for the clearest.

## 2.3 Standard work basics (20 min)

**Standard work** is the current best-known way to do a task, documented simply enough that
the team actually uses it: the steps in sequence, the few quality/safety-critical points and
*why* they matter, and roughly how long it takes.

What Yellow Belts must internalize:
- **Standards are the baseline for improvement, not a cage.** Without a standard there is no
  "better" — only different. Kaizen means improving *the standard*, not freelancing around it.
- **Written by/with the people who do the work.** A standard imposed from outside describes
  work-as-imagined and gets quietly ignored.
- **One page, verbs first, critical points flagged.** If it reads like a legal document, it's
  shelf-ware. Photos beat paragraphs.
- **Living document:** date + version + owner. When someone finds a better way, the path is
  "improve the standard," and the standard changes for everyone. When someone *can't follow*
  the standard, that's a process signal, not a discipline issue — investigate before
  enforcing (Deming's 94% still applies).

**Try:** Draft a one-page standard for a task you do weekly, with at least two flagged
critical points and their reasons. This can seed your mini-project.

## 2.4 Wrap: 5S as a mini-project candidate (5 min)

A 5S implementation with baseline/after audit scores and photos is one of the three
sanctioned **mini-project patterns** for Yellow Belt certification (the others: a
standard-work fix and a waste elimination via PDCA — Unit 5). If you plan to use 5S,
start your baseline photos and audit **now**; sustainment evidence (two audit cycles) takes
calendar time you can't compress at the deadline.

**Key takeaways**
- Baseline first; red-tag beats arguments; frequency dictates placement; cleaning is
  inspection; sustain = scheduled + owned + visible.
- Visuals should answer one question at a glance; the strongest visual is a constraint.
- Standard work is the platform improvement stands on — owned by the people who do it.
