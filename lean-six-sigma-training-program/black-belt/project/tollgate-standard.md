# Black Belt Tollgate Standard — Five Gates and Closure

*Five gates on the 16-week calendar (weeks 2, 5, 7, 12, 16) and a closure review after
handover. Each gate is fifteen minutes, one page plus an appendix, the sponsor deciding and
an independent reviewer present. Every checklist item is keyed to the rubric item it
evidences — [`review-rubric.md`](review-rubric.md). Mandatory rubric items are marked ★. The
charter the gates refer to is
[`charter-and-strategic-linkage.md`](charter-and-strategic-linkage.md); the Green Belt
standard these gates grow from is
[`../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md`](../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md).*

---

## The standard: fifteen minutes, one page plus appendix, decisions requested

**One page plus an appendix.** The page is the A3 or readout page for the phase: exhibits
over prose, every chart labeled with its metric, units, period, n and data source (S1). The
appendix holds what the page points to — the MSA results, the logbook, the model output with
its diagnostics, the FMEA, the control plan, the decision log, the signed pages. Every
appendix item is numbered and referenced from the page; an appendix item nothing on the page
points to is not read. The page and the appendix reach the reviewer and the sponsor two
business days before the gate. A deck is neither.

**Decisions requested, first.** The page opens with the decisions you are asking the sponsor
— or the steering group, where the decision-rights table says so — to make at this gate,
each written as a sentence the sponsor can answer with *yes*, *yes with conditions* or *no,
because*. This is the executive-readout form the rubric scores at L2 (≤ 15 minutes, one
page, decisions requested and obtained). You use it five times before the readout that
counts.

**Fifteen minutes.** You present for fifteen minutes, timed. Then fifteen minutes of
questions, then ten minutes in which the sponsor decides and the decisions are written on the
page. Forty minutes per project; the calendar holds regardless of how the project feels.
Nobody from the program presents, prompts or rescues: you lead the room, which is the point
of the level.

**Who is in the room and what each does**

| Role | At the gate |
|---|---|
| **Black Belt candidate** | Presents the page; answers questions; writes each decision on the page before leaving; enters the decisions in the decision log |
| **Sponsor** | Asks; decides; signs the decision line. Convenes the steering group at the gate when a decision in the table belongs to it. The gate exists for this person |
| **Functional heads (steering group)** | Present when a decision in their class is requested — always at Tollgates 1, 4 and 5; at Tollgate 3 when a trial on their process is requested. A head who signed the charter and is absent when their decision is requested has not decided; the clock in the decision-rights table starts |
| **Process owner(s)** | From Tollgate 2 on: confirms the baseline is the process as worked; at Tollgate 4 signs the control plan; at Tollgate 5 explains the chart without you |
| **Finance partner** *(or mission-metric executive)* | In the room for the Finance minutes at Tollgates 2, 4 and 5 (≈ 1 hour each on the time contract); by signature at Tollgate 1; validates at closure |
| **Tollgate reviewer** | The Master Black Belt reviewer of record, assigned blind by the assessment lead before week 1 and named to you and your sponsor at Tollgate 1: not your coach, not your instructor, not from your organization. Attends all five gates; asks questions for the record; records; does not coach. Scores the closed project and signs the certificate |
| **Master Black Belt coach** | Not in the room. Receives the record after you do; reads it before your next coaching session |
| **Instructor** | Not in the room. The weekly project checkpoints run in the live lab; the gate is yours |
| **Team members** | Welcome; not required. The Green Belt or team member you coach (L3) attends as a team member, not as a presenter |

The reviewer's questions serve the record and the sponsor's decision. If the sponsor turns to
the reviewer and asks "what should they do?", the reviewer answers with what the record shows
and what the rubric asks for, and says that *how* is the coach's conversation. The reviewer
who suggests a countermeasure has become a second coach, and the project has no assessor
left.

**The record is formative; the score is given once.** The reviewer's tollgate record tells
you which rubric items are evidenced so far and which are at risk. It carries no points. The
150-point score is given once, on the closed project, against the full rubric, by the same
reviewer — who does not carry an item forward on trust because it looked evidenced at a gate.

**The five decisions a sponsor can make**

| Decision | Meaning | What happens to the calendar |
|---|---|---|
| **Proceed** | The phase is done to the standard | Next phase starts this week |
| **Proceed with conditions** | Done, with named items due by a named date | Next phase starts; conditions are checked at the next gate and in the reviewer's record |
| **Hold** | One specific item is missing; return within 10 business days with that item | The next gate stays on the calendar; the hold is cleared in a 15-minute follow-up with the sponsor, the reviewer present (by call is fine) |
| **Re-scope** | Scope, target or primary metric changes — a steering-group decision under the decision-rights table; the charter is re-signed and the linkage arithmetic redone | Return to the affected gate; the 9-month closure window from week 16 still applies |
| **Stop** | The sponsor closes the project honorably: problem gone, priority changed, cause or countermeasure outside the steering group's authority | You are matched to a new project through the sponsor or the partner pool. A stop after Tollgate 2 usually means joining the next cohort's calendar from the affected gate; the assessment lead decides placement and the closure window restarts from that cohort's week 16 |

A hold is not a failure. Most projects take one. What the reviewer records on a hold is the
item, not a judgment. A decision that belongs to the steering group and is not made at the
gate is recorded as *pending*, with the decision-rights clock (decided at the next tollgate
or within 10 working days), and the escalation path applies.

**What the reviewer records, at every gate**

- Rubric items due at this gate, each marked *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced*, with
  one sentence per ★ item (A2, I1, C1, F1, L1) whenever the item is due or in progress.
- The decisions requested on the page and the decisions obtained, with conditions and dates;
  who decided each, checked against the decision-rights table; any decision pending, with its
  clock.
- Who was in the room against who the charter says must be.
- A Finance line at every gate: partner named; basis seen, stated or confirmed; rows classed;
  double-counting checked — or *not yet due*.
- A leadership line: the L1 evidence seen (canvas version and date, decision-log entries,
  a resistance episode recorded as it happened), not a judgment of how you seemed.
- A data-ethics line: *none observed* or the specific concern. Evidence of removed data
  points, a changed operational definition between before and after, a baseline period
  chosen after seeing the data, or a baseline reconstructed from memory is recorded and sent
  to the assessment lead the same day. At a tollgate this is a warning and a conversation; at
  final review it is a stop.
- A one-line coaching flag for the Master Black Belt coach — a topic, not advice. It is the
  only channel from the reviewer to the coach.
- Where the project points beyond this level — a portfolio, deployment or system-design
  problem — the note that the Master Black Belt fork or the specialist fork applies, to be
  named in the final feedback.

The record goes to you, your sponsor and, after you have it, your coach within 2 business
days, and to the reviewer's own file. It is written in the reviewer's format from
[`../practicum/green-belt-tollgate-reviews.md`](../practicum/green-belt-tollgate-reviews.md),
which is also the format you write in when you sit in the reviewer's chair (requirement 5,
below).

## Tollgate 1 — Define (week 2, after Module 1)

**Rubric items due:** D1, D2, D3, D4; F1 and F2 at charter level (the impact threshold,
[`charter-and-strategic-linkage.md`](charter-and-strategic-linkage.md) §3). **Started, not
due:** ★ L1 (canvas dated, kickoff held, decision log opened); M1 (the MSA plan is week 3's
project work); M3 (every baseline number on the page carries its source).

### Before the gate — the page and the appendix

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Decisions requested, written first: scope, linkage, decision rights, time, Finance partner, target status | L2 form |
| ☐ | Problem and opportunity statement: cause-free, blame-free, quantified in the business's own metric; the gap to the objective, not the project target | D1 |
| ☐ | Strategic linkage statement, four parts with the arithmetic on the objective's scale and period; the sponsor's initials on the statement itself; what the project does *not* close, in one sentence | D2 |
| ☐ | Charter rebuilt in the week 2 clinic and signed by the sponsor and every functional head in scope; start and stop points; handoffs the project may and may not change; constraints | D3 |
| ☐ | Decision-rights table, five classes minimum, by class not name; escalation path with a clock on every trigger, the four standard triggers plus one written for this project | D3 |
| ☐ | Team roster with time contracts signed by each member's manager; the candidate manages nobody on it | D3, L1 |
| ☐ | VOC method stated and run or dated (interviews, Kano survey, complaint analysis); CTQ table with targets and the specification basis for each; the customer named (internal counts) | D4 |
| ☐ | Primary metric with its operational definition, basis and data type; secondary metrics and guardrails, one guardrail per function whose work may change; baseline stated as measured (value, period, n) or "to be measured by week 4" | D2, M2 |
| ☐ | Target labeled provisional (confirmed at Tollgate 2) or confirmed | D2 |
| ☐ | Impact threshold: the arithmetic with every number's source and date; benefit classes listed separately, never summed; Finance partner (or mission-metric executive) named, has seen the arithmetic and has written the basis they will accept; equivalence tests answered where clinical or service impact is claimed; double-counting check done | F1, F2 |
| ☐ | Stakeholder strategy canvas, dated, with at least one named resistance risk and the planned response; kickoff run-of-show; decision log opened with the first entries | ★ L1 |
| ☐ | Data access confirmed: who gives you which extract, by what date; the first go-see done, dated | M3 |
| ☐ | The send-back checklist (charter §5) run in the clinic, every line answered on the page | D2, D3, F1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Is this the problem you want solved, at this scope, across these functions, with these
   start and stop points? Is every function whose head signed actually in the room or
   represented, and does each head accept the decisions the table assigns them, on the clock
   stated?
2. Is the linkage arithmetic yours? If the primary metric moves as written, will you report
   that movement against the objective you are accountable for? (If the sponsor wants a
   different metric or a different objective, that is a re-scope now, not at Tollgate 4.)
3. Are the named people released for the signed hours, is the data access real, and is the
   Finance partner or mission-metric executive the person who will validate at closure?
4. Do you accept the target as provisional until the baseline is measured — and the impact
   class the charter claims (financial, clinical/service, or both)?
5. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

**Show (MFG).** The molding-cell scrap project opens its page with three decisions: "Approve
the four functions in scope and the trial class *≤ 8 h on one press, production manager
decides, 5 working days' notice*; confirm the $86K projected hard row is the number you will
report against the CoPQ objective, one sixth of the gap; accept the plant cost accountant as
Finance partner on the standard-cost basis." The maintenance manager, who signed the charter,
is on the call because the trial class is his decision to accept. All figures illustrative.

### The reviewer records

D1–D4 status; whether the charter carries every functional head's signature (D3 is not
evidenced with "IT will support" and no signature); whether the linkage is arithmetic and
initialed (D2 is not evidenced by an adjective); whether every decision class names a class
and a clock; whether the CTQs carry a specification basis; the Finance line — partner named,
arithmetic seen, basis written, classes separate — or the hold; whether any charter smell
from §4 survived the clinic; decisions obtained and by whom; the L1 line (canvas version 1,
dated; decision log opened); data-ethics line (at this gate, about how the baseline will be
collected and whether any quoted number came from memory); coaching flag.

### Common holds at Tollgate 1

- Linkage in adjectives, or an objective the sponsor is not accountable for.
- A decision-rights table with names, or a trigger with no clock.
- A function in scope whose head did not sign, or a team every member of which reports to
  the candidate.
- The threshold cleared by summing a soft row into a hard one; or a Finance partner who has
  not seen the arithmetic. The charter's rule is explicit: Tollgate 1 holds on it.
- A countermeasure already chosen — a purchase, a go-live, a decided policy. This is a
  send-back, not a hold.

## Tollgate 2 — Measure (week 5, after Module 3)

**Rubric items due:** M1, M2, M3. **Revisited:** D2 (the target is confirmed or revised
against the measured baseline; the linkage arithmetic is re-run on the measured value); F1
(the Finance partner sees the metric and states the calculation basis in writing). **In
progress:** ★ L1 (canvas revised if the MSA or the baseline changed who cares).

### Before the gate — the page and the appendix

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Decisions requested, written first: accept the baseline as fixed; accept the measurement systems and what was done about them; confirm or revise the target; ratify any provisional operational-definition decisions; confirm the Finance basis | L2 form |
| ☐ | MSA for every primary and secondary metric, matched to data type and situation — Gage R&R (crossed or nested), attribute agreement with kappa, destructive-test design, or a system-generated field with its provenance walk and paired audit; acceptance criteria stated before the study; the sponsor sentence for each; inadequate or marginal systems fixed and re-run, both results shown side by side. "We trust the system" is not a result | M1 |
| ☐ | Baseline chart in time order with the subgrouping rationale; stability stated in a sentence; special causes investigated and explained, never deleted | M2 |
| ☐ | Distribution identified with the physical reason and the fit comparison; capability stated only after stability, with the distribution named and the transformation or fit shown where used; specification source is the customer's, not the control limits; expected PPM beside the observed rate | M2 |
| ☐ | Sample size for the coming comparison from the agreed δ in the sponsor's words, with the power you have if the window shrinks; where the process is unstable, the special-cause investigation replaces the capability line and the page says so | M2 |
| ☐ | Provenance record: source system and extract date for every dataset; the operational definition and confirmation it has not changed since Tollgate 1, or the change dated with both versions; every exclusion with the rule that excluded it; who cleaned what, reproducible by a stranger | M3 |
| ☐ | Guardrail baselines, each on its own operational definition | D2 |
| ☐ | Target confirmed or revised against the measured baseline, with the sponsor's initials; linkage arithmetic re-run on the measured value | D2 |
| ☐ | Finance partner (or mission-metric executive) has seen the metric as measured and has written the calculation basis they will accept at closure; any benefit row that changed class now that the baseline is measured | ★ F1 |
| ☐ | Decision log current; any escalation trigger fired since Tollgate 1 recorded with what happened | ★ L1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you accept this as *the* baseline — collected this way, over this period, with these
   points in it, per stratum where the data are a mixture? After this gate the baseline is
   fixed: no point is removed, no definition changes between before and after, no start date
   moves.
2. Do you accept each measurement-system result and what was done about it? A system that
   failed and was fixed before the baseline is the project working as designed; the sponsor
   hears it here, not at closure.
3. Given the measured baseline, is the target still the target, and is the linkage arithmetic
   still the number you will report? Confirm or revise now, in writing.
4. Do you ratify the operational-definition decisions the candidate took provisionally under
   the decision-rights table, and do you accept the Finance partner's stated basis?
5. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

**Show (HC).** The sepsis project's provenance walk found that the "first antibiotic
administered" timestamp is the barcode scan at the bedside on the day shift and a
retrospective chart entry, median 22 minutes later, on nights. The page asks the chief
medical officer to accept a baseline stated per shift with the night-shift definition
flagged, and to ratify the candidate's provisional decision that "administered" means the
scan time, with nights re-measured for two weeks under a scan-first instruction before the
comparison. The chief quality officer, as validator, confirms the three equivalence tests
against the measured median. Illustrative.

### The reviewer records

M1–M3 status with a sentence on M1 for each metric (study fits the situation; interpreted;
fixed and re-run; or not evidenced) and a sentence on M2 (stability stated before capability;
distribution identified with a reason; sample size from a stated δ); whether the
specification source is the customer's; the target decision with the sponsor's initials; the
Finance line — basis written, rows classed; decisions obtained and by whom; the L1 line;
data-ethics line (any points removed? any definition drift since Tollgate 1? any baseline
period chosen after seeing the data? any appraiser or batch dropped from an MSA?); coaching
flag.

### Common holds at Tollgate 2

- An MSA skipped because the data are system-generated, with no provenance walk and no
  paired audit.
- Capability on an unstable process, or a normal Cpk on data the fit comparison rejected.
- A subgroup that mixes streams, presses, shifts or sites, so the limits are wide and the
  chart is blind.
- A sample-size sentence with no δ, or a δ the sponsor never stated.
- A baseline period that starts conveniently after a bad month. The reviewer asks why that
  start date.
- No written Finance basis. The basis argued at closure is a closure delay, and F1 is at
  risk from here.

## Tollgate 3 — Analyze (week 7, after Module 4)

**Rubric items due:** A1, ★ A2 (where the cause is verified by a model, a non-parametric
comparison or a designed comparison), A3, A4. **Started, not due:** ★ I1 (the experiment or
pilot path chosen from the Module 5 checklist, the prediction written and dated); I2 (the
FMEA is on the new settings, which do not exist yet). **In progress:** ★ L1 (a resistance
episode usually appears in Analyze, when a function's favored cause does not verify).

Where the experiment *is* the verification — the cause structure names factors and the
process can take an experiment — A2 is recorded as *in progress, method named*, and the
reviewer notes that A2 and I1 will be read together at Tollgate 4.

### Before the gate — the page and the appendix

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Decisions requested, written first: accept the verified cause(s) as the target for countermeasures; accept the rejected causes as off the list; approve the experiment or pilot path with its bounds, under the decision class that owns it; escalate, park or re-scope causes outside scope | L2 form |
| ☐ | Cause structure from a wide set to a testable few, with the narrowing logic on the page — C&E matrix, FMEA, graphical stratification; the model's terms named before it was fitted | A1 |
| ☐ | ★ Verification: method matched to the question and the data — multiple regression with four diagnostic plots and VIF, logistic with the events-per-predictor count, a rank test with its shift estimate, two-way ANOVA, or a designed comparison; assumptions checked and stated; effect in process units at named settings with its interval; alternative explanations addressed; the observational caveat where it applies; the sentence you would tell your sponsor | ★ A2 |
| ☐ | Restraint: at least one question answered graphically or by a two-group comparison instead of a model, or a model declined, with the reason — insufficient data, question visible on a stratified chart, measurement system too weak | A3 |
| ☐ | Every X and interaction tested that did not verify, with result and interval; the sponsor's favored cause among them if it was tested; sample-size limits acknowledged ("with n = 18 per group we could detect a shift of about X") | A4 |
| ☐ | Causes outside scope or authority named, with a proposed home: escalation under the path, parking lot, the next cohort's charter | A4, D3 |
| ☐ | The path to Improve: Module 5 checklist answered in writing — experiment on the process, or controlled pilot with a comparison group or period; the prediction of the primary metric's movement, written and dated before any run; the bounds (hours, cases, presses, shifts) and the decision class that approves a trial on a live process | ★ I1 |
| ☐ | Provenance record updated with every exclusion rule and the duplicate check | M3 |
| ☐ | Canvas revised where a function's position changed; the resistance episode, if one has happened, in the five lines (what was observed, diagnosis, what you did, what happened, what you would do differently); decision log current | ★ L1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you accept the verified cause or causes as the target for countermeasures? The sponsor
   is not asked to agree with a coefficient; the sponsor is asked whether the sentence in
   plain language, with its effect size in process units, is something they would act on.
2. For causes that did not verify — including any the sponsor or a functional head believed
   in — do you accept that they are off the list, on this evidence, for now?
3. Do you approve the experiment or pilot as bounded on the page, and does the functional
   head whose process it runs on decide it now, under the trial class in the table, with the
   notice the table states? Where the process cannot take an experiment, do you accept the
   restraint decision and the pilot design, and the DOE practicum as where the experiment
   happens?
4. For causes outside scope: escalate, park, or re-scope?
5. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

**Show (TXN).** The freight-billing project's logistic model puts the odds of a dispute at
3.1 times higher (95% interval 2.4 to 4.0) for invoices with an accessorial charge added
after proof of delivery, with 1,900 events for six predictors; contract type, the sales
team's favored cause, is inert and is on the page with its interval. The decisions
requested: accept the post-delivery accessorial as the verified cause; accept contract type
as off the list; approve a two-factor experiment on the billing validation rules on a
customer group of ≤ 500 invoices per cell, the billing and collections managers deciding
jointly per the table, sales informed. Illustrative.

### The reviewer records

A1–A4 status with a sentence on ★ A2: the method, whether it fits the question and the data
type, whether diagnostics were shown and read, whether the effect is stated in process units
with an interval and a plain-language sentence, whether alternative explanations were
addressed — and, explicitly, whether the direction the team is heading targets the verified
cause and not an asserted one; whether restraint is shown and reasoned (a model that did not
need to exist is noted here); whether rejected causes are shown with the same care; the
prediction and its date; the trial decision and who made it against the table; the L1 line
(episode recorded as it happened, not reconstructed); data-ethics line (subgroups chosen
after the test? terms dropped to make a model fit? an exclusion rule written after seeing the
data?); coaching flag; the fork note where the cause structure exceeds the charter.

### Common holds at Tollgate 3

- A model with an R-sq and no diagnostics; a p-value with no effect size and no sentence.
  The hold is one sentence long.
- A model where a stratified chart answered the question. Restraint is a rubric item because
  this is common.
- Only the surviving cause shown. Where did the other terms go?
- No prediction, or a prediction written after the first run.
- An experiment promised on a process that cannot take one, or a trial with no bounds and
  no owner in the decision-rights table.
- A cause verified, and a countermeasure already chosen for a different one.

## Tollgate 4 — Improve (week 12, after Modules 5 and 6)

**Rubric items due:** ★ I1, I2; ★ A2 and A4 where the experiment is the verification; M3
(the logbook); ★ C1 drafted and signed by the process owner; C3; C2 drafted. **Started,
not due:** I3 and I4 (future-state design and the kaizen event are Module 7, weeks 13–14 —
the event is chartered and dated here). **Revisited:** F1 and F2 (the Finance partner in the
room to confirm the basis before the evidence window opens; project costs to date). **In
progress:** ★ L1 (canvas revised — a control plan hands work to functions you do not manage).

### Before the gate — the page and the appendix

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Decisions requested, written first: approve implementation beyond the experiment or pilot; confirm the process owner who signed; approve the kaizen event's scope and date; confirm the Finance partner's basis and the evidence window; agree the closure date | L2 form |
| ☐ | ★ Path A — experiment on the process: DOE planning canvas; design with its justification (factors, levels, fraction and resolution, blocks, center points, run count and power); the logbook with run order and every deviation; effects table and ANOVA; residual plots read; operating window with its interval; confirmation runs done (Module 5 timing) or, where the process's window did not allow them, dated on the page; the sponsor sentence | ★ I1 |
| ☐ | ★ Path B — controlled pilot: the dated prediction; comparison group or period; the same operational definition as baseline, unchanged; the control chart continued from the baseline or the paired comparison; result against the prediction, honestly read — met, partial, unmet or not yet run, stated as such | ★ I1 |
| ☐ | Where the experiment verified the cause: the effect with its size and interval in process units, and the alternative explanations the design ruled out — what randomization and blocking bought; inert factors shown as findings | ★ A2, A4 |
| ☐ | Options generated and selected against stated criteria (impact, effort, risk); FMEA on the new operating settings or the new process steps; mistake-proofing designed in; residual risks named | I2 |
| ☐ | ★ Control plan: metric, chart, subgroup, frequency, owner, response plan per rule; limits from a declared post-improvement phase; the process owner's signed acceptance, dated — the 60-day clock runs from that signature and from live data | ★ C1 |
| ☐ | Control strategy justification: why this chart and not the Green Belt default; the subgroup with what it contains and excludes; frequency reasoned from shift size and tolerable time-to-detect; EWMA, CUSUM, short-run or rare-event chart chosen, or considered and rejected, with the reason | C3 |
| ☐ | The owner's one-page chart guide, drafted with them: what the chart shows, what each signal means, what they do | C2 |
| ☐ | Kaizen event chartered: scope, dates, team, observer or video review booked (requirement 4, [`../practicum/kaizen-facilitation.md`](../practicum/kaizen-facilitation.md)); implementation plan with owners and dates | I4 |
| ☐ | Finance partner's basis confirmed in writing against the countermeasure as it will be implemented; benefit rows re-classed if the countermeasure changed them; project costs to date — hours from the time contracts, spend — totalled; the evidence window named (start, end, what is measured); double-counting re-checked against any project that started since the charter | ★ F1, F2 |
| ☐ | Canvas revised at the functions the control plan touches; decision log current; the resistance episode recorded if not already; the sponsor's statement on how you led requested, with a date | ★ L1 |
| ☐ | Closure date proposed: handover date, day 0 of monitoring, readout date at handover + 60 days minimum, submission inside 9 months of week 16 | C1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you approve the countermeasure for implementation beyond the experiment or pilot — at
   the operating window derived, on the process as bounded — or, if confirmation runs are
   still dated, do you approve implementation conditional on their result? Day 0 of live
   monitoring cannot fall before this decision.
2. Who owns the control plan? The sponsor confirms the process owner who signed and their
   authority to run the response plan, in the presence of that owner's functional head.
   Without a signature and authority here, C1 cannot start its clock.
3. Do you approve the kaizen event as chartered, and is the functional head whose area it
   runs in deciding that now?
4. Do you commit the Finance partner (or mission-metric executive) to validate at closure,
   on the basis they have written, over the evidence window named?
5. Closure date agreed: handover, day 0, readout, submission?
6. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

**Show (MFG).** The molding-cell experiment on press 2 (2⁴ full factorial, two blocks by
shift, three center points) finds the barrel-temperature × hold-pressure interaction accounts
for most of the scrap effect; the operating window predicts scrap of 2.3% (interval 1.8 to
2.9) against a baseline of 5.4%, and three confirmation runs came in at 2.1%, 2.6% and 2.4%
per shift. The page asks the plant manager to approve the window on all three presses, the
cell supervisor — who signed the control plan — to confirm the setter's response plan, and
the cost accountant to confirm the standard-cost basis over a 10-week evidence window.
Illustrative.

### The reviewer records

★ I1 status in a sentence: design justified or pilot controlled; analysis correct; operating
window with interval; confirmation runs in the record or dated; result read honestly against
the prediction; ★ A2 where the experiment verified it; I2 status; ★ C1 draft status — chart,
subgroup, frequency, owner, response plan, signature and date, and whether the named owner
was in the room with authority; C3 in a sentence (justified, or default by habit); C2
readiness; the kaizen event decision and who made it; the Finance line — basis confirmed,
rows classed, window named, costs totalled; the closure date; the L1 line (canvas version and
date; statement requested); data-ethics line (was the pilot window chosen after seeing early
results? were runs excluded without a logged cause? is the after definition the before
definition?); coaching flag.

### Common holds at Tollgate 4

- An experiment analyzed without residual plots, or an operating window with no interval.
- Confirmation runs "to follow" with no date, or a pilot with no comparison group or period.
- After-data collected by a different method — a new system, a different clerk, a shorter
  definition — than the baseline.
- A control plan with a chart and a frequency but no signature, or a signature without
  authority.
- A default chart on a process that needed rational subgrouping or a small-shift chart, with
  no reasoning either way.
- A benefit row whose class changed with the countermeasure and was not re-classed; a
  Finance partner who was not in the room.

## The six coaching sessions and the five gates

Two chairs, kept apart by design: **the coach says how to earn it; the reviewer says whether
it was earned.** The six Master Black Belt coaching sessions are placed on the calendar so
that each one either prepares a gate or reads the record of the last one — never both in the
same room, never with both people present.

| Session | Week | Relation to the gates | The coach has read | You bring | Coaching flag picked up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | Before Tollgate 1, in the same week | Your pre-course charter agreement; the send-back checklist | Charter, linkage statement, decision-rights table, first canvas, VOC plan | — |
| 2 | 4 | Before Tollgate 2 (week 5) | The Tollgate 1 record | MSA plan with every metric's row filled or dated; provenance walk; baseline chart family and the sample-size sentence | From Tollgate 1 |
| 3 | 8 | After Tollgate 3 (week 7); before the experiment or pilot is designed | The Tollgate 2 and 3 records | Verified causes with diagnostics; rejected hypotheses; the restraint decision; the DOE planning canvas draft or the pilot design; the dated prediction | From Tollgates 2 and 3 |
| 4 | 12 | Before Tollgate 4, in the same week | The Tollgate 3 record | Experiment analysis and confirmation-run plan; FMEA on the new settings; control strategy draft | From Tollgate 3 |
| 5 | 16 | Before Tollgate 5, in the same week | The Tollgate 4 record | Control plan live (days so far); Finance rows and basis; readout page; L1 package; kaizen report; requirement 5 schedule | From Tollgate 4 |
| 6 | Closure period | After Tollgate 5; before submission | The Tollgate 5 record; the monthly checkpoints | The full package; ≥ 60 days of monitoring; the readout as delivered; Part A and Part B or C | From Tollgate 5 |

The rules that keep the chairs apart:

- **The record flows one way, in order.** The reviewer's record goes to you and your sponsor
  within 2 business days, and to the coach only after you have it. The coach reads it with
  you at the session and turns each *at risk* into a piece of work; the coach does not
  interpret what the reviewer "really meant" — the record and the rubric say what is and is
  not there.
- **Coaching notes never enter the project file.** They are stored outside it and are not
  discoverable by the reviewer. The reviewer scores the package; the coach's opinion of the
  package is not in it.
- **The coach is not in the room at a gate; the reviewer is not in a session.** The reviewer
  attends five gates and the closure review; the coach attends six sessions and the monthly
  checkpoints. Neither attends the other's. The instructor's weekly checkpoints in the live
  lab are coaching and follow the coach's rules.
- **One channel, one direction.** The coaching flag on the record is the only communication
  from reviewer to coach, and it names a topic ("subgrouping"; "the episode was
  reconstructed"), not advice. There is no channel from coach to reviewer about a live
  project. A coach who wants to tell the reviewer something writes a statement of fact for
  the file at closure, with no opinion on the score.
- **Same standard, different jobs.** Coach and reviewer both score the calibration anchor
  set (a clean pass; a high-scoring fail on an undiagnosed model; an honest-failure pass; a
  Finance-unsigned fail) before the cohort and quarterly; the coach must agree with the
  anchor on every ★ item, because the coach coaches toward the standard the reviewer scores.
  Coach–reviewer ★ agreement for the cohort is published by the assessment lead after the
  final reviews are returned, not before.
- **Assignment prevents the overlap.** The assessment lead assigns the reviewer of record
  before week 1 from a bench of at least three Master Black Belts none of whom coach or
  instruct the cohort they review, none from the candidate's organization or a client of it. If a conflict
  surfaces later — a change of employer, a recognized process — the reviewer stops, tells the
  assessment lead, and a replacement is assigned from the bench; the prior records stand.
  Full duties and the bench rules are in
  [`../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md`](../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md).

What a coaching session is *for*, in tollgate terms: session 1 turns the send-back checklist
into a charter that passes it; session 2 turns "to be measured by week 4" into a baseline the
sponsor will fix; session 3 turns a verified cause into a design with a dated prediction;
session 4 turns an analysis into an operating window the owner will sign for; session 5 turns
a project into a page that opens with a decision; session 6 turns a package into one the
reviewer can score without asking you anything. The coach's question at every one of them is
the same: *what would the reviewer need to see?*

## Requirement 5 — your two Green Belt tollgate reviews

You formally review two Green Belt tollgates from the reviewer's chair, to the reviewer
standard, under the supervision of that Green Belt project's calibrated reviewer of record.
The practicum rubric that scores you is
[`../practicum/green-belt-tollgate-reviews.md`](../practicum/green-belt-tollgate-reviews.md);
the standard you review to is
[`../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md`](../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md)
and [`../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md`](../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md)
(the Green Belt ★ items are M3, A3, I3 and C1 — different numbers from your own rubric; do not
carry yours into their chair). The role is the Green Belt system's *second reader*
([`../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md`](../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md)):
you score independently, compare afterward, sign nothing, and contact the learner only in the
room.

**When you are eligible to sit.** After Lab 16, when your written score on the case package
has been calibrated against the supervising reviewer's on the anchor set and every ★
disagreement has been resolved to the sentence on the page that decided it. Your two live
reviews therefore fall in the closure period, on a running Green Belt cohort's gates (its
weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8), and are scheduled by the end of week 16 on your Tollgate 5 page.

**Which gates.** The assessment lead places you on a Green Belt cohort and names your
supervising reviewer — the reviewer of record for those projects, so that the record of file
and your record are of the same page. The two reviews are:

- at two different gates (two phases), at least one at Tollgate 2, 3 or 4 where a ★ item is
  due — a reviewer who has only ever recorded a Define gate has not written the hard sentence;
- on two different Green Belt projects where the cohort's calendar allows;
- never on a Green Belt you coach (your L3 evidence) or have coached, never on a project from
  your own organization or a client of it, and never on a project your own sponsor sponsors.
  The Green Belt reviewer conflict rules apply to you exactly as to the bench.

**At the gate.** You sit in the reviewer's chair; the supervising reviewer sits beside you.
You have read the one-pager two business days before, ★ items first. You ask the questions;
you write the record live in the reviewer's format — items due marked *evidenced* / *at
risk* / *not evidenced*, one sentence per ★ item, the sponsor's decision and conditions with
dates, the data-ethics line, the coaching flag as a topic, the Black Belt invitation where the
cause structure exceeds the scope. The supervising reviewer writes their own record; theirs is
the record of file and the one the Green Belt receives. You do not suggest a countermeasure,
do not say how to write the sentence, do not soften a hold because the Green Belt worked
hard, and do not decide: the decision is the sponsor's. The observer's count in Lab 16 — how
many times the reviewer coached — is the count your supervisor keeps here.

**After the gate.** Within 2 business days you and the supervising reviewer compare the two
records item by item. Every ★ item must agree; a ★ disagreement is discussed until you can
both name the sentence on the page that decided it, and is recorded. The supervising reviewer
scores you on the practicum rubric — the record, the questions, the silence, the feedback —
pass or redo. A redo is a further gate, not a rewrite of this one.

a team lead as owner and a weekly p chart; in the questions it emerges the team lead cannot
