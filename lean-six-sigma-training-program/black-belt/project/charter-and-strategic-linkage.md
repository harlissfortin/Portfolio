# Black Belt Project Charter and Strategic Linkage — Template, Examples and the Scale Test

*Two pages, plus the signed attachments. You bring a sponsor-signed charter agreement to
week 1 (it is a prerequisite); you rebuild it in the week 2 charter-at-scale clinic
([`../modules/m1-strategic-define.md`](../modules/m1-strategic-define.md), §1.5); your sponsor
and the functional heads sign the rebuilt version before Tollgate 1 at the end of week 2. The
charter is scored under rubric items D1, D2 and D3, and it is the reference point for every
tollgate, coaching session and Finance conversation after that — see
[`review-rubric.md`](review-rubric.md) and [`tollgate-standard.md`](tollgate-standard.md).*

**What is different from your Green Belt charter.** Everything the Green Belt charter carried
([`../../green-belt/project/charter-template.md`](../../green-belt/project/charter-template.md))
still applies: cause-free problem statement, one primary metric with an operational
definition, a guardrail, start and stop points, a timeline against the calendar. A Black Belt
charter adds five things, and the rubric scores each:

| Addition | Why it exists | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic linkage statement, sponsor-confirmed | The project moves a number the executives review, by an amount you can state | D2 |
| Cross-functional scope with a decision-rights table and an escalation path | Nobody who owns a decision you need reports to you | D3 |
| Impact threshold met at charter — ≥ $75K annualized, or the clinical/service equivalent | The prerequisite for a Black Belt seat; Finance or the mission-metric executive checks it | F1, F2 |
| Team roster with time contracts signed by managers | You lead people you do not manage; their time is a promise their manager makes | D3, L1 |
| Coaching cadence and a timeline to closure within 9 months of week 16 | Six Master Black Belt sessions and five tollgates are on the calendar before you start | C1, L2 |

**Who fills it:** you, with the sponsor, the process owner(s) and every functional head whose
decisions appear in the table, in the room or on the call. A decision-rights table written
alone and sent for signature is a list of promises nobody made.

### Problem and opportunity statement (D1)

What is wrong, where, since when or how often, how big, and what it costs — in the
business's own metric, the one the sponsor is measured on. No cause, no blame, no solution.
The opportunity is the gap between the current value and the objective's value, not the
project's target.

> _______________________________________________________________________________

### Strategic linkage statement (D2)

Four parts, in this order; the sponsor initials the whole statement, not the charter's
signature line alone. The arithmetic is on the objective's scale and time basis; every number
carries its source, and any number that came from memory is listed as a Measure-phase task.

| Part | Write it as |
|---|---|
| **1. Objective, quoted** | The line from the plan, scorecard or quality dashboard the executives review, with its target and date. *"Copied from [document], [date]."* |
| **2. Primary metric** | Name, operational definition, basis, data type, and baseline — *measured (value, period, n)* or *to be measured by week 4 with a validated measurement system*. |
| **3. Mechanism** | A chain of "therefore" steps from the metric to the objective, each of which someone could check against data or by observation. |
| **4. Arithmetic** | If the project reaches its target, how much of the objective's gap closes, on the objective's own scale and period; and, in one sentence, what the project does *not* close and what would. |

> **Sponsor confirmation.** *I have read the linkage statement. The objective is one I am
> accountable for; if the primary metric moves as stated, I will report the movement against
> that objective.* — initials ____ date ____

The linkage test, from Module 1: *if this project fully succeeds, which number on the
sponsor's scorecard moves, by how much, by when?* Three answers, or it is a Green Belt
project — see §5.

Mark the target ☐ provisional (confirmed at Tollgate 2 against the measured baseline) or
☐ confirmed.

### Coaching cadence (included in the program)

Six 1:1 sessions with your Master Black Belt coach, 45 minutes each, are on the calendar
before week 1. You bring the deliverables the module names; the coach reads the previous
tollgate record first. Sessions 1–5 fall in the cohort; session 6 is booked by you in the
closure period, before submission.

| Session | Week | Before | You bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | Tollgate 1 | Charter, linkage statement, decision-rights table, first stakeholder strategy canvas, VOC plan |
| 2 | 4 | Tollgate 2 | MSA plan for every metric; provenance walk for system fields; baseline chart family and sample-size sentence |
| 3 | 8 | Design of the experiment or pilot | Verified causes with diagnostics; rejected hypotheses; the restraint decision (A3); DOE planning canvas draft |
| 4 | 12 | Tollgate 4 | Experiment analysis and confirmation-run plan; FMEA on the new settings; control strategy draft |
| 5 | 16 | Tollgate 5 | Control plan live (days so far); Finance basis; readout page; L1 package; kaizen event report; Green Belt tollgate-review schedule |
| 6 | Closure period | Submission | The full package; the reviewer's Tollgate 5 record; ≥ 60 days of monitoring |

Between week 16 and closure the coach also takes a monthly written checkpoint: you send the
one-page status and the live control chart; the coach replies within 5 business days.
Weekly project checkpoints during the cohort run in the live lab with the instructor; the

### 2.1 MFG — Reduce scrap at the injection-molding cell

| | |
|---|---|
| **Problem and opportunity** | The three-press molding cell scrapped 5.4% of parts molded over the last 26 weeks (n ≈ 2.1 million parts; per production day; from the press counters and the scrap-bin weigh station), against a plant standard of 2.0%. Scrap at the cell is the largest single line in the plant's cost of poor quality, which stands at 3.9% of cost of sales against the plant objective of ≤ 2.5% by fiscal year-end. |
| **Objective, quoted** | "Cost of poor quality ≤ 2.5% of cost of sales by FY-end (current 3.9%)." — plant scorecard, Q2 review. |
| **Primary metric** | Scrap rate: parts scrapped ÷ parts molded, per production day, all three presses, all shifts; scrapped parts counted at the weigh station by standard part weight; start-up purge excluded and logged separately. Baseline 5.4% (26 weeks) — to be re-measured in weeks 3–4 after the weigh-station audit. |
| **Mechanism** | Scrap consumes resin, press time and energy at standard cost → those costs post to the scrap and variance lines → those lines are two of the five that make up plant CoPQ → CoPQ ÷ cost of sales is the scorecard metric. |
| **Arithmetic** | 4.2 million parts per year × (5.4% − 2.5%) ≈ 121,800 parts not scrapped × $0.71 standard cost (resin + conversion, costing system, dated Q2) ≈ **$86K per year, projected, hard**. Plant cost of sales ≈ $38M; CoPQ gap 3.9% − 2.5% ≈ $530K; this project closes about one sixth of the gap. The remainder is in warranty and rework lines this project does not touch; the changeover project in the portfolio addresses the next largest. |
| **Sponsor confirmation** | Plant manager initials on the statement; "one sixth" written in his hand. |
| **Functions in scope** | Molding production (cell supervisor), tooling and maintenance (maintenance manager), materials and purchasing (resin lot handling), quality (inspection plan). Out: resin supplier selection; press replacement; the assembly cell downstream. |
| **Decision-rights table (abbreviated)** | Change to the press setup sheet → cell supervisor decides, Black Belt and tooling consulted. Trial on a press ≤ 8 h → production manager, 5 working days' notice, planner consulted. Resin lot hold → quality manager, purchasing consulted. Spend ≤ $5,000 → sponsor. Scope or target change → steering group. Definition of "scrap" → Black Belt provisionally; sponsor ratifies. |
| **Project-specific escalation trigger** | "If a trial raises cycle time on any press by more than 5% for a shift, the trial pauses and the production manager decides within one working day whether to resume." |
| **Impact class and partner** | Hard: scrap and variance lines. Finance partner: plant cost accountant (basis: standard cost per part, scrap-line movement over the evidence window). No double counting: the changeover project claims press time, not scrap. |
| **Team and time contracts** | Two setters (one per shift, 2 h/week × 14 weeks, signed by the cell supervisor); tooling technician (2 h/week, maintenance manager); materials handler (1 h/week, purchasing lead); quality technician (2 h/week, quality manager); plant cost accountant (as above). The candidate is a process engineer with no reports. Coaches the day-shift setter, who is a Yellow Belt starting a Green Belt cohort in month 3 (L3). |
| **Guardrails; start/stop** | Cycle time per press and first-pass dimensional yield at inspection not worse; raw resin at the dryer → part passes or fails inspection. |
| **Timeline** | Cohort calendar; experiment on press 2 in weeks 9–11 (four factors, two blocks by shift); confirmation runs week 12; handover week 15; closure targeted at handover + 75 days. |

### 2.2 HC — Reduce time from emergency-department arrival to first antibiotic for sepsis

| | |
|---|---|
| **Problem and opportunity** | For patients meeting the hospital's sepsis screening criteria in the emergency department, the median time from arrival to the first antibiotic dose is 118 minutes and the 75th percentile is 196 minutes (90 days, n = 268 patients, all hours; EHR timestamps). The hospital's sepsis bundle compliance, reported to the board's quality committee, is 58% against an objective of ≥ 75% by fiscal year-end; the antibiotic-timing element is the element most often missed. |
| **Objective, quoted** | "Sepsis bundle compliance ≥ 75% by FY-end (current 58%)." — quality committee dashboard, monthly. |
| **Primary metric** | Minutes from ED arrival timestamp (registration or triage, whichever is first) to the administration timestamp of the first antibiotic dose in the medication record, per patient flagged by the sepsis screen; patients who arrive with an antibiotic already administered by transport are excluded and logged. Baseline: median 118 min (90 days) — to be re-stated at Tollgate 2 after the timestamp provenance audit (who scans the dose, and when). |
| **Mechanism** | The antibiotic element is met when the first dose is administered within the bundle's time window → the bundle is scored all-or-none → the antibiotic element is currently failed in 39% of screened patients while the other elements together fail in 14% → moving the antibiotic element raises composite compliance by the share of patients who fail *only* that element. |
| **Arithmetic** | Of screened patients, 27% fail the antibiotic element alone (chart review, n = 268). A median of ≤ 60 minutes with the 75th percentile inside the window would, on the baseline distribution, bring antibiotic-element compliance from 61% to about 88% and composite compliance from 58% to about **80%** — the whole gap to 75%, if the other elements hold. About 1,100 screened patients per year are affected. This project does not address the 14% who fail other elements; the lactate re-measurement mini-project covers the largest of those. |
| **Sponsor confirmation** | Chief medical officer initials; "if the other elements hold" underlined by her. |
| **Functions in scope** | ED nursing (nurse manager), ED physicians (medical director), pharmacy (ED pharmacy lead), laboratory (core lab supervisor — lactate turnaround gates the physician's order in the current sequence). Out: inpatient sepsis care after admission; the screening criteria themselves (set by the sepsis committee); antibiotic formulary. |
| **Decision-rights table (abbreviated)** | Change to the ED nursing sepsis protocol → nurse manager, with the sepsis committee informed. Change to the order set → medical director, pharmacy consulted first. Stocking a first-dose antibiotic in the ED automated cabinet → pharmacy lead, nursing consulted. Trial on a shift → nurse manager and medical director jointly, 5 working days' notice. Spend ≤ $5,000 → sponsor. Definition of "arrival" → Black Belt provisionally; sponsor ratifies. |
| **Project-specific escalation trigger** | "If any trial patient receives an antibiotic without the ordering physician's review being documented within the protocol's time, the trial stops at once and the medical director decides within one working day whether to resume." |
| **Impact class and partner** | Clinical, no money claimed. Mission-metric executive: chief medical officer (sponsor) with the chief quality officer as the named validator, so the sponsor does not validate her own project. Equivalence (§3.2): the metric is board-reported (test 1); the target closes the whole stated gap (test 2); ≈ 1,100 patients per year reached (test 3). |

## 3. The impact threshold — how it is judged

The prerequisite reads: *target impact ≥ $75K annualized, or an equivalent clinical or
service-level impact.* Three rules govern how that is checked, at charter and again at
closure.

### 3.1 The financial test

1. **Projected, at charter, by run rate.** (Baseline − target) × annual volume × unit cost or
   rate, with the source and date of every number written beside it; capped at 12 months;
   no growth, roll-out or volume increase assumed. This is the same annualization rule
   Finance applies at closure ([`../../green-belt/project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md`](../../green-belt/project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md), Part B3), applied to the target instead of the result.
2. **Any class counts toward the threshold, by name.** Hard, soft, cost avoidance and
   revenue rows are listed separately and never added into one headline. A project that
   reaches $75K only by adding a soft row is over the threshold *with a soft row*, and the
   charter says so; the reviewer sees the classes, not a total.
3. **The Finance partner has seen the arithmetic** before the charter is signed and has
   written the basis they will accept. Without that line the threshold is asserted, not
   checked, and Tollgate 1 holds on it.

The threshold gates entry. At closure, ★ F1 and F2 score what was measured over the evidence
window, netted and annualized only by Finance; a project that entered at a projected $86K and
closes at a measured $41K over 10 weeks with an honest reading passes F1 and F2 on the
evidence. A project that entered at $86K and claims $86K at closure by extrapolation fails F2.

### 3.2 The clinical or service equivalence test

Money is not the unit of a clinical or service project, and converting a clinical outcome to
money to clear a threshold is the wrong move: it invites a number nobody will sign. Equivalence
is judged instead by the mission-metric executive — the executive accountable for the
objective in the linkage statement, or their named equivalent when the sponsor is that
executive — against three tests, each answered on the charter:

| Test | Passes when | Written on the charter as |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Reviewed at the top** | The metric in the linkage statement is one the board, the quality committee, a regulator, an accreditation body, or a signed service-level agreement reviews on a fixed cycle | The document and the review cycle, quoted |
| **2. Closes a stated share of the gap** | The project's target, through the linkage arithmetic, closes at least a quarter of the annual gap to the objective — or the executive states in writing that the movement is one they would report as material even if it is smaller | The fraction, from the arithmetic; or the executive's sentence, initialed |
| **3. Reaches a stated population** | The change reaches a stated number of patients, customers or cases per year, from a source | The count and its source |

All three, initialed by the executive, make the project equivalent. A monetary conversion may
sit beside the tests as information ("Finance would value the bed-hours at about $X at its
stated rate") but is not claimed at F1 unless Finance signs it as its own row. At closure the
mission-metric executive validates the measured movement against the same three tests, in
the Part C format; the badge's impact class reads *clinical/service* with the metric named.

### 3.3 Both

A project that claims money and a mission metric carries both partners and both tests. The
threshold is met by either route; the charter says which, so the reviewer does not read a
mission metric as a hedge for a weak financial case, or the reverse.

## 4. Charter smells at Black Belt scale

The Green Belt smells still apply — solution in the title, cause in the problem statement,
"lack of", percentage without a denominator, a rare process, a team of one. These are the
ones that appear at this scale.

| Smell | What it looks like | Why it costs you later |
|---|---|---|
| **Linkage in adjectives** | "Supports the patient-experience pillar" | D2 asks for arithmetic; the sponsor initials a sentiment and reports nothing |
| **Objective the sponsor is not accountable for** | The linkage points to another executive's scorecard line | The sponsor cannot confirm it; the mission-metric executive is someone nobody named |
| **Decision-rights table with names** | "Maria decides trials" | Maria changes role in month four and the table is renegotiated at Tollgate 4 |
| **Escalation without a clock** | "Escalate if needed" | Nobody knows when "needed" is; the analyst is gone six weeks before anyone escalates |
| **A function in scope whose head did not sign** | "IT will support" with no signature and no hours | The data access hold at Tollgate 1 or the withdrawn analyst at week 9 |
| **The candidate manages the team** | Every member reports to the candidate | D3 asks for people the candidate does not manage; L1 has nothing to navigate |
| **Threshold by one soft row** | $22K hard + $58K of "hours freed" summed to $80K | Finance separates the rows at closure; the credential's impact class reads what Finance signs |
| **Mission metric as hedge** | "$45K hard, plus improved patient safety" | Neither route is fully evidenced; §3.3 asks which |
| **Impact from a roll-out not in scope** | "$75K when applied to all four plants" | Scope is one cell; the arithmetic is one cell; F2 forbids the extrapolation |
| **Double counting** | The changeover project and the scrap project both claim press time | Finance signs one; the other's F1 is unearned |
| **A guardrail for the candidate's function only** | The change moves work into pharmacy; no pharmacy guardrail | The gain is bought from a function that did not agree to pay |
| **Experiment promised on a process that cannot take one** | "DOE on live sepsis patients" | The restraint decision (A3) should already be on the charter; the DOE practicum is where the experiment happens |
| **Closure "when the results are in"** | No closure date; no monitoring start date | ★ C1 needs 60 live days; the 9-month window is on the calendar, not the project's mood |

## 5. Not Black-Belt-scale — the send-back checklist

Run this in the week 2 clinic and again at Tollgate 1. A project that fails any line in the
first table is a Green Belt project — a good one, probably — and the candidate is placed on a
different project through the sponsor or the partner pool before Tollgate 1. Sending a
project back is a routing decision, not a judgment on the candidate or the sponsor: it means
the organization has a Green Belt project it should run and a Black Belt seat that should
carry something bigger.

**Any one of these sends the project to Green Belt scope**

| ✓ | Send back when | Rubric item it cannot earn |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | One function is in scope, or the sponsor has line authority over every step between the start and stop points and every decision in the table | D3 |
| ☐ | The candidate manages everyone on the team | D3, L1 |
| ☐ | No number on an executive-reviewed scorecard moves if the project fully succeeds — the linkage test has fewer than three answers | D2 |
| ☐ | Projected impact below $75K annualized by the §3.1 arithmetic, and the §3.2 equivalence tests are not all met | Prerequisite; F1 |
| ☐ | The Finance partner or mission-metric executive has not seen the arithmetic | F1 |
| ☐ | The countermeasure is already chosen: a purchase, a system go-live, a policy the sponsor has decided | ★ A2, ★ I1 |
| ☐ | The candidate's own Green Belt project, or its extension to the same process at the same scope | D3 |

**These do not send a project back on their own** — they are things the coach and reviewer
watch:

- *The cause will probably be verified with a two-sample comparison, not a model or an
  experiment.* Method fit outranks sophistication; A3 rewards restraint. The DOE requirement
  is met on the practicum apparatus when the process cannot take an experiment. Scale is
  scope, linkage and impact, not the statistics.
- *The process is clinical and no money is claimed.* The equivalence test exists for this.
- *The functions in scope are inside one department.* Two units of one department with
  different heads and different decision rights are two functions; one head who owns both
  is one.
- *The impact clears the threshold only with a soft row.* Allowed, labeled, and the charter
  says so.

**The reverse case.** A Green Belt tollgate reviewer who sees a cross-functional cause, an
interaction that needs a designed experiment, or a control that needs a system-level change
records the Black Belt invitation on the Green Belt's tollgate record. That project, or the
problem it revealed, is a candidate for this charter in the next cohort — with the Green Belt
project's verified cause and baseline as the starting evidence, provenance recorded (M3).
