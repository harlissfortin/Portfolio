# Black Belt — Instructor, Reviewer and Coaching System

Who may teach a Black Belt cohort, who coaches each capstone, who signs the certificate, and
how the program keeps the last two apart. At Green Belt the product is a verified project. At
Black Belt it is a verified project **with a named Master Black Belt's reputation attached to
it**: the reviewer of record's name is printed on the certificate and on the public
verification page, linked to their own credential. Everything in this document exists so that
the person whose name is on the certificate decided alone, from the evidence, and so that the
coaching on the other side of that wall was good enough that the decision was rarely close.

Governs: cohort instructors, Master Black Belt coaches, producers, practicum observers,
reviewers of record, supervising reviewers for the Green Belt tollgate requirement, the
assessment lead and the engagement lead. Agrees with the
[review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md) (which controls where they differ), the
[charter and coaching cadence](../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md), the
[tollgate standard](../project/tollgate-standard.md), the
[terms of certification](../../program-operations/terms-of-certification.md) §4–5 and §9,
and the [credential evidence policy](../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md)
§4–5. The Green Belt
[facilitator and coaching system](../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md)
is the pattern; this document states what changes at Black Belt and why.

---

## 1. Roles and the one rule

| Role | Who | Does | Does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instructor** | A Master Black Belt, or a senior Black Belt with observed facilitation (§2) | Runs the sixteen weekly labs, the weekly project checkpoints inside them, office hours and the Tollgate 5 readout rehearsal; leads or co-leads both immersions | Score any capstone from a cohort they taught; know who will review their candidates |
| **Master Black Belt coach** | A Master Black Belt on the coaching bench; the instructor may be one of them if they are a Master Black Belt | The six 1:1 sessions, the monthly written checkpoint to closure, the coaching notes; carries at most 8 candidates | Score, sign, or advise the reviewer; sit at a tollgate; attend the reviewer's reading of the file; sit as reviewer of record on any file in the cohort |
| **Producer** | Program staff or a facilitator in the pathway | Breakouts, datasets, apparatus logistics for Immersion 1, chat triage, timing, accessibility support, in every lab above 8 candidates and in both immersions | Teach content |
| **Practicum observer** | The instructor, plus a second calibrated observer (a bench Master Black Belt or a certified instructor) in each immersion | Scores the DOE practicum and the change-leadership practicum pass/redo on their rubrics; observes or video-reviews the kaizen event | Score the capstone; read the coaching notes |
| **Reviewer of record** | A Master Black Belt on the reviewer bench, calibrated on the anchor set in the last quarter, who did not instruct, coach or observe this cohort | Sits all five gates and writes their records; the 150-point review of the closed project; the Finance and sponsor validation checks; the ≤ 4-sentence public feedback; the day-90 sustainment check; **signs the certificate** (§3) | Coach, suggest methods or countermeasures, attend a coaching session or a monthly checkpoint, or read the coaching notes |
| **Supervising reviewer** | The reviewer of record of a running Green Belt cohort | Seats the candidate for the two Green Belt tollgate reviews (requirement 5), compares records afterward, scores the practicum rubric | Contact the Green Belt learner on the candidate's behalf; count as the candidate's coach or reviewer |
| **Assessment lead** | Program | Assigns reviewers blind (§4.2); runs reviewer calibration; owns appeals, integrity cases and data-ethics stops; publishes the cohort's coach–reviewer agreement | Coach or review live capstones |
| **Engagement lead** | Program (corporate deployments) | Readiness gate, steering-group briefings, portfolio report, per the [corporate deployment playbook](corporate-deployment-playbook.md) | Any assessment role |

The one rule is the same sentence as at Green Belt, and it is repeated here because at this
level both sides of it are Master Black Belts and the temptation to merge them is stronger:
**the coach says how to earn it; the reviewer says whether it was earned.** A Master Black
Belt coach who has spent six sessions helping a candidate design an experiment cannot then
decide whether ★ I1 was earned, whatever their integrity; they have already decided, in
session 3, and the review would be a memory. A reviewer who tells a candidate which model to
run has coached, and the program has no assessor left on that file. When you feel the pull,
say the rule out loud and route the question.

## 2. Who may teach

### 2.1 Two routes to the instructor's chair

Black Belt labs are taught by one of two people, and the program publishes which on the
cohort record.

**A Master Black Belt** who holds the program's facilitator certification at Green Belt (or
its equivalent for an external Master Black Belt, bridged through the Green Belt calibration
assessment) and has completed the Black Belt shadow stage below.

**A senior Black Belt with observed facilitation.** A certified Black Belt who meets all of:

- at least three years since Black Belt certification and at least five projects closed to
  Finance-validated or mission-metric-validated results, of which at least two included a
  designed experiment run on a real process — not the practicum apparatus;
- Green Belt facilitator status held for at least two cohorts taught, with both annual
  observations met;
- the Black Belt pathway below completed through observed solo.

A senior Black Belt instructs. They do not coach the six Master Black Belt sessions, do not sit
as reviewer of record, and do not sign certificates; those three things are what the
credential says a Master Black Belt did, and the verification page has to be true. A senior
Black Belt instructor who is also in the Master Black Belt fellowship may count the cohort as
their assessed teach-back with the fellowship's consent.

A Black Belt below that bar, however strong their projects, teaches Green Belt. External
Master Black Belts join through the Green Belt facilitator calibration and are bridged, not
waived. No licensed client facilitator delivers any part of Black Belt; certification
authority at this level stays in-house at every scale, and so does teaching.

### 2.2 The pathway

The Green Belt pathway (calibrate → shadow → co-teach → observed solo) applies with these
differences. Nobody skips a stage on seniority; what is certified is delivery of this course.

| Stage | What changes at Black Belt | Evidence to advance |
|---|---|---|
| **0 · Calibrate** | Score the Black Belt anchor set (rubric, "Calibration"): clean pass; high-score fail on an undiagnosed model; honest-failure pass; Finance-unsigned fail | Within 8 points of the anchor total on all four; exact agreement on every ★ item |
| **1 · Shadow** | Produce one full cohort including both immersions; attend six coaching sessions as observer with the candidates' consent; write a debrief of each lab and each immersion day | Producer duties performed; debriefs on file |
| **2 · Co-teach** | Lead at least five of the sixteen labs including Lab 10 (the DOE practicum week), Lab 6 or 7 (modeling) and Lab 16 (reviewer calibration and tollgate simulation); co-observe one immersion with the scoring rubric | Observation record on each lab led; practicum scores compared with the certified observer's, every pass/redo disagreement discussed and recorded |
| **3 · Observed solo** | Instructor of record for a full cohort; a Master Black Belt observes three labs live — one of them Lab 10 or the DOE practicum itself, one of them Lab 16 — and one immersion day | All observations meet the standard; cohort L1 items on lab and immersion quality ≥ 4.0; no data-ethics stop traced to teaching |
| **Certified** | Instructor of record without observation; eligible as a practicum observer | Recorded on the instructor's own verification page |

Fastest path is three consecutive cohorts, roughly fourteen months, because Black Belt
cohorts are longer and the program runs fewer of them. Stages 1 and 2 are paid at the
producer and co-facilitator rates.

### 2.3 What the observer records

Everything in the Green Belt observation record — talk ratio, fidelity to the run-of-show,
the failure moment, the protected debrief, coaching language, accessibility — plus three
things specific to this level.

- **Restraint taught, not just sophistication.** Did the instructor let a team run the
  regression the case did not need, and then let the debrief find that a stratified box plot
  answered the question? A3 is a rubric item; an instructor who rewards the fanciest model in
  the room is teaching against the rubric.
- **The experiment was allowed to go wrong.** The DOE practicum kit has a planted problem
  (a factor whose effect is confounded with the run order if the design is not randomized; an
  apparatus that drifts). An observer records whether the instructor let a team discover it
  from their residuals or announced it at the briefing.
- **The reviewer's chair was kept silent.** In Lab 16's tollgate simulation, an instructor who
  models the reviewer role by coaching from the chair has undone §8.6 of Module 8 in front of
  the room. The observer counts, and the count goes on the record.

Two consecutive missed observations return the instructor to co-teaching for one cohort, as
at Green Belt. The usual miss at this level is a Master Black Belt lecturing on the method
they love; the correction is co-teaching beside someone who runs the 60/40 standard.

### 2.4 Keeping the status

Instructor status stays live while: at least one Black Belt cohort is taught in any 24
months; the anchor set is scored each quarter a cohort is taught; both annual observations
are met; cohort L1 items on lab quality, immersion quality and coaching average ≥ 4.0 across
the year; and, for a senior Black Belt, the Green Belt facilitator status is also live.
Instructors are named on their own verification page and on every cohort's course record.

## 3. The reviewer of record

### 3.1 What the name on the certificate means

The Black Belt certificate carries two names: the candidate's and the reviewer of record's.
The verification page shows the reviewer's name linked to their own credential page, the
review date, the rubric dimensions and scores, the reviewer's ≤ 4-sentence feedback, and the
sustainment check result (credential evidence policy §4). A prospective employer can walk the
chain: this Black Belt was certified by this Master Black Belt, who was certified by this
panel. That chain is the program's answer to the certificate mill, and it only works if the
reviewer's name means "I read the whole file, alone, and I would sign it again."

A reviewer consents to being named as a condition of the bench (credential evidence policy
§5). A reviewer who leaves the program stays named on the credentials already issued, and
their page notes their period of service. There is no anonymous review at this level, and no
review by committee: one Master Black Belt, one signature.

### 3.2 Duties

For each file assigned, the reviewer of record:

1. **Reads every tollgate package before the gate, and sits the gate.** The page and the
   appendix reach the reviewer two business days before each calendar tollgate (weeks 2, 5,
   7, 12, 16); the reviewer reads them, ★ items first, and is in the room at all five. At the
   gate the reviewer asks questions for the record, writes what happened, and does not coach;
   the sponsor decides. Within 2 business days the record goes back in the format of the
   [tollgate standard](../project/tollgate-standard.md): the status of each ★ item due
   (A2, I1, C1, F1, L1) and of the requirements due, one sentence each, *evidenced / at risk /
   not evidenced*; the decisions requested and the decisions obtained, with conditions, dates
   and who decided each against the decision-rights table; who was in the room against who the
   charter says must be; the Finance and leadership lines; a data-ethics line; a coaching flag
   that names a topic; no advice. The record is what the reviewer saw and heard.
2. **Scores the full rubric** on the closed project — the same Master Black Belt who sat the
   five gates — within 20 business days of submission, ★ items first, in the rubric's feedback
   format. The tollgate records are formative and carry no points: no item is carried forward
   on trust because it looked evidenced at a gate. Re-scores the whole file on the one included
   resubmission, again carrying nothing over.
3. **Checks the validations.** Reads the Finance partner's signature against the calculation
   basis on the benefit-classification form, and the sponsor's statement on leadership (L1)
   against the canvas and the decision log. Where money is claimed and Finance has not signed,
   F1 is unearned and the review says so; the reviewer does not telephone Finance to obtain
   the signature, because obtaining evidence is the candidate's job.
4. **Confirms the other four gates are recorded** — exam, DOE practicum, kaizen facilitation,
   two Green Belt tollgate reviews — before signing. The reviewer does not re-score them; they
   confirm the records exist and are signed by the people the program says sign them.
5. **Writes the public feedback**: at most four sentences, per the credential evidence
   policy, that a stranger can read on the verification page. Two strengths, the highest-
   leverage finding, and the fork named (Master Black Belt track or a specialist
   micro-credential) where the project points to one.
6. **Signs.** The signature releases the credential. A reviewer who is not ready to sign
   writes what is missing and does not sign.
7. **Runs the day-90 sustainment check**: 90 days after signing, one message to the process
   owner and the sponsor asking whether the control plan held, with the current chart; the
   flag (`sustained`, `not sustained`, `unverified`, `not applicable`) goes on the
   verification page and into the outcomes figures. Only the reviewer of record sets it.
8. **Stops on data ethics.** Removed points, an operational definition changed between
   baseline and after, a baseline period chosen after the data were seen, an experiment
   re-run until it "worked" with the earlier runs missing from the logbook: any of these goes
   to the assessment lead the same day, in writing, per the terms of certification §4. The
   reviewer records what they saw and does not adjudicate.

Turnaround is published and measured: tollgate records within 2 business days of the gate and
final reviews within 20, each ≥ 95%. A reviewer carries **at most 8 live capstone files** at
any time, because each file brings five forty-minute gates and their two-day pre-reads, and
because a Black Belt file with a designed experiment, a logbook, diagnostics, a future-state
model and a leadership package takes a working day to read properly — and the 20-day promise
has to be kept while the reviewer also has a job.

### 3.3 Liabilities — what the name is answerable for

The program states this plainly at bench onboarding, because a reviewer should know what
they are signing.

| The reviewer of record is answerable for | The reviewer of record is not answerable for |
|---|---|
| Having read the whole file, including the experiment logbook and the raw diagnostics, not the A3 alone | A candidate's fabrication that a competent reading of the file could not have detected (the terms of certification §4 process handles the candidate; the reviewer's review is read, not blamed) |
| Every ★ decision, individually: if the annual 10% re-review finds a ★ item signed as earned that the anchor standard says was not, that is the reviewer's finding to answer, and it re-calibrates them before their next review | The sponsor's or Finance partner's judgment about the business: the reviewer checks that the signatures exist and match the basis written on the form, not whether the client's loaded rate is right |
| Keeping the separation: no coaching session or checkpoint attended, no coaching note read, no advice in a record or in the room at a gate. A breach voids the review; two breaches in a year end the bench place for the year | The candidate's later conduct in role: the credential attests to the work reviewed, on the date reviewed |
| The public feedback being true, specific and free of anything from the file the candidate did not consent to publish | Sustainment beyond the day-90 check: the flag is a fact on one date, not a warranty |
| The `sustained` flag being set only from the monitoring record, never from a sponsor's assurance without the chart | Revocation decisions: those belong to the assessment lead; a revoked credential resolves to "revoked" with a date and the reviewer's page carries no mark unless the re-review found their review at fault |

If a credential the reviewer signed is revoked for reasons a competent review should have
caught, the assessment lead reads the review, the re-review finding goes on the improvement
board with its cause, and the reviewer's bench place is decided by the assessment lead with
the appeal route open. This has to be a real consequence for the name on the certificate to
mean anything, and it has to be a fair one for anyone to accept the role.

### 3.4 The bench

The reviewer bench at this level is **at least three Master Black Belts** who do not coach the
cohort they review (the level README names this as an open item before launch, and this
section is why the number is three). The arithmetic: a full cohort of 16 files at a cap of 8
per reviewer needs two reviewers; the coach of the cohort is excluded; if the instructor is
also a Master Black Belt, they are excluded too. Three is the floor for one cohort with one
Master Black Belt coaching. Two concurrent cohorts need five. When the bench cannot cover a
cohort under these rules the cohort start waits — the program delays the start, not the
independence.

## 4. Coach and assessor: the separation, mechanically

### 4.1 What each side sees

| | Master Black Belt coach | Reviewer of record |
|---|---|---|
| The candidate's name, project, sponsor, Finance partner | Yes | Yes (needed for the credential evidence record and the validation checks) |
| The instructor's and coach's names | — | **No.** The file carries a cohort ID and the candidate's name; no roster, no faculty |
| Coaching notes and the monthly written checkpoints | Writes them | **Never.** Stored outside the project file, not discoverable |
| Tollgate packages and the sponsor's decisions | Yes | Yes — the package two business days before each gate, the decisions in the room |
| The reviewer's tollgate records | Yes, after the candidate receives them | Writes them |
| The DOE practicum score, the kaizen observation, the Green Belt review records | Yes | Yes — as records to confirm, not to re-score |
| The final score and feedback | Yes, after the candidate receives it | Writes it |
| The exam result | No | No |

The reviewer learns the coach's identity when the assessment lead publishes the cohort's
coach–reviewer ★ agreement after the last review is returned (§7). The coach learns the
reviewer's name at Tollgate 1, with the candidate and sponsor, and may not contact them.

### 4.2 Blind assignment

The assessment lead assigns the reviewer of record for every candidate **before week 1**, so
that the same Master Black Belt sits all five gates, reads all five packages and scores the
final file. The procedure follows the Green Belt one with tighter conflict rules, because the
bench is smaller and the reviewer's name is public:

1. Start from the reviewer bench roster.
2. Remove any Master Black Belt who: instructs, coaches or observes this cohort; has coached
   or instructed this candidate at any level in the last 36 months; has been employed by,
   consulted to, or held a contract with the candidate's organization in the last three
   years; sits on the corporate account's engagement team; supervised the candidate's Green
   Belt project; or is the candidate's Master Black Belt fellowship adviser. Corporate
   deployments add: no reviewer from the client, ever, and no reviewer who will be named in
   the client's Master Black Belt succession plan.
3. Assign round-robin from the remaining bench at a cap of 8 live files.
4. Log the assignment — reviewer ID, project ID, conflicts checked, date — in the assessment
   record, which the annual audit re-review reads first.
5. Tell the candidate and the sponsor at Tollgate 1. Tell nobody else.

If a conflict surfaces later — the candidate's employer acquires the reviewer's client; the
reviewer recognizes a process from a past engagement — the reviewer stops, tells the
assessment lead, and a replacement is assigned by the same procedure. The replacement
re-reads all prior tollgate records; the prior records stand.

*TXN example (illustrative).* A cohort of 12 at a logistics company's billing and collections
function. The bench holds five Master Black Belts. One coaches the cohort; one consulted to
the company's parent three years ago on a different site — excluded, because the rule is the
organization, not the site; one supervised two of these candidates' Green Belt projects two
years ago and is excluded from those two files only. That leaves three reviewers for twelve
files at a cap of eight: four–four–four, with the two constrained files placed first.

### 4.3 Tollgate records are assessment, not coaching

Five tollgates, all on the calendar: fifteen minutes presented, fifteen of questions, ten in
which the sponsor decides and the decisions are written on the page, with the steering group
there at Tollgates 1, 4 and 5. The reviewer is in the room at all five, having read the page
and the appendix two business days before, and asks questions — for the record and for the
sponsor's decision, never about how to fix anything. Within 2 business days the reviewer
returns the record to the candidate and the sponsor, and to the coach once the candidate has
it: what is evidenced, what is at risk, what is not there, one sentence per ★ item due, the
decisions obtained and who made them, and the data-ethics line. Nothing about how to fix it.

Being in the room is what makes the record evidence rather than a reading of paper: the
reviewer hears the question the sponsor asks and the answer given, sees who was there when a
decision in their class was requested, and writes what happened. It is also where the
separation is most easily lost. If the sponsor turns to the reviewer and asks what the
candidate should do, the reviewer answers with what the record shows and what the rubric asks
for, and says that *how* is the coach's conversation. A reviewer who names a countermeasure at
a gate has become a second coach, and the file has no assessor left on it.

A candidate who reads "★ A2 at risk — model reported without residual diagnostics; effect
size stated as a coefficient with no practical sentence" at Tollgate 3 knows exactly what the
final review will look for four months before it happens. The coach reads the record with the
candidate at the next session. The record is the reviewer's most useful output, and the
**hold** — one missing item, one date — is the most useful record. Module 8 teaches candidates
to give holds from the reviewer's chair; the reviewer bench has to model it.

### 4.4 Final review, resubmission, appeal

As at Green Belt: one revise-and-resubmit inside the 9-month closure window, re-scored in full
by the same reviewer. Appeals follow the terms of certification §5: a second calibrated Master
Black Belt re-scores blind to the first score, and the coach is not consulted. The coach may
file a statement of fact — what was submitted when, what the sponsor decided at each gate —
with no opinion on the score. Stage 2 adds an external practitioner, which at this level will
usually be a Master Black Belt from outside the program.

### 4.5 The other four gates

The separation rule protects the capstone. The other gates are assessed by people who may
also teach, because they are observed performances on a rubric, not the candidate's own
project file:

- **DOE practicum** — scored pass/redo by the two practicum observers, one of whom may be the
  instructor; a pass/redo disagreement is discussed until it resolves and both signatures go
  on the record. The candidate's coach does not observe.
- **Kaizen facilitation** — observed live by the instructor or a bench Master Black Belt, or
  video-reviewed by two observers when live observation is not possible; scored on the
  facilitation rubric. The candidate's coach may attend the event's report-out as a guest and
  does not score.
- **Green Belt tollgate reviews** — supervised by the reviewer of record of the Green Belt
  cohort concerned, who scores the practicum rubric. That reviewer is never the candidate's
  own reviewer of record or coach, and the candidate never reviews a Green Belt they coach.
- **Exam** — proctored; no faculty involvement.

## 5. Coaching

### 5.1 The six sessions

Every candidate in every format — live-virtual hybrid, corporate 4 × 4-day waves — gets six
45-minute 1:1 sessions with a named Master Black Belt coach, on the calendar before week 1,
with the deliverables the [charter](../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md) names.
Sessions 1–5 fall in the cohort; session 6 is booked by the candidate in the closure period,
before submission. Weekly project checkpoints happen in the live lab with the instructor;
the sessions are where the coach reads the previous tollgate record and asks the harder
questions.

| Session | Week | Sits before | The coach leaves with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Chartered at scale** | 2 | Tollgate 1 | Whether the strategic linkage is a written line the sponsor initialed or a sentence the candidate believes; whether the decision-rights table names classes, not people; whether any function in scope has not signed; the VOC method and its date |
| **2 · Measured with the right system** | 4 | Tollgate 2 | The MSA plan per metric and what happens if a system field fails provenance; the chart family and the distribution question; the sample-size sentence; who owns the data pull on a Tuesday |
| **3 · Verified, restrained, ready to experiment** | 8 | The experiment or pilot design | Which causes verified, with which diagnostics; what did not verify and whether it is written down (A4); the restraint decision (A3) stated as a reason; the DOE planning canvas: factors, levels, response, randomization, blocking, the run the plant will actually allow |
| **4 · Analyzed and confirmed** | 12 | Tollgate 4 | The effects and interactions in the sponsor's words; the operating window and the confirmation-run plan; the FMEA on the new settings; the control strategy draft, chart choice reasoned |
| **5 · Live and led** | 16 | Tollgate 5 | Days of live monitoring so far and who signed the control plan; the Finance basis agreed in writing; the readout page; the L1 package — canvas revisions, the resistance episode, the decision log, the sponsor's statement requested; the kaizen report; the Green Belt review schedule |
| **6 · Submission** | Closure period | Submission | Whether ★ C1 has 60 live days on the chart; whether Finance signed the figure on the form; whether a stranger can follow the A3; what the candidate is choosing not to claim |

Session timing is on the calendar, not "when the candidate is ready." A candidate who arrives
at session 3 with no verified cause still has the session; the note records why, because the
causes are almost always team time contracts not honored, a data pull that needs a request
nobody raised, or a function that did not sign — all designable, none the candidate's
character.

### 5.2 The monthly written checkpoint to closure

From week 17 until submission, the candidate sends a one-page status and the live control
chart each month; the coach replies within 5 business days in the note format below. Two
consecutive months with no movement on the closure calendar trigger a three-way conversation
— candidate, sponsor, coach — about scope: finish a bounded slice with honest evidence, or
close as documented learning without the credential this cycle. The rubric passes honest
failure; it does not pass a project that never ends, and at nine months the window closes.

Coaching load: **no Master Black Belt coach carries more than 8 candidates.** A cohort of 9–16
has two coaches from week 1, both in the room at Lab 1, files taken by round-robin.

### 5.3 The coaching note

One note per session and per monthly checkpoint, written during or immediately after,
shared with the candidate (who may add a line), stored in the coaching record, retained
three years, never placed in the project file, never scored.

```
COACHING NOTE — BLACK BELT
Candidate · Project ID · Cohort ID · Vertical · Session (1–6 / monthly-N) · Date · Coach

1. Where the project is vs. the calendar
   Phase reached; the tollgate this maps to; days ahead/behind; months left in the 9-month window.

2. Mandatory items as the coach sees them today (status, not a score; the reviewer decides)
   ★ A2  causes short-listed / method chosen / run with diagnostics / effect sentence written
   ★ I1  design or pilot planned / prediction written / run / result vs prediction stated
   ★ C1  owner named / plan drafted / owner signed / live since <date> (<n> days)
   ★ F1  Finance partner named / basis agreed in writing / rows classified / signed
   ★ L1  canvas dated / revision dated / episode written / sponsor statement requested

3. Requirements
   DOE practicum · kaizen event · Green Belt review 1 · Green Belt review 2 · exam
   Each: scheduled <date> / done / redo scheduled

4. The one thing before the next session
   A single action, with the template or tool, and the date.

5. Risks (steering group · team time contracts · data · plant access for the experiment ·
   Finance · scope · time), each with the mitigation agreed

6. What the candidate decided
   Their decision, in their words. The coach records; the coach does not decide.

7. Flags (tick any; each opens a same-day route)
   [ ] data-ethics concern (removed points, changed definition, baseline chosen after seeing data,
       experiment runs missing from the logbook)
   [ ] safety / regulatory / patient- or customer-harm risk in the experiment or pilot
   [ ] sponsor or steering-group change; a function in scope withdrawn
   [ ] impact threshold at risk (projected impact below the charter's basis)
   [ ] scope exceeds Black Belt (a system redesign, a new-process design → DFSS, a deployment
       question) → route to the fork conversation
   [ ] no venue for the kaizen event or the experiment → partner pool
   [ ] accessibility or accommodation need
```

Field 2 is phrased as status, never as earned, and the coach never predicts the score.
"I think A2 will pass" teaches the candidate to argue with the reviewer instead of building
the diagnostics. Field 5's "plant access for the experiment" line exists because the single
most common Black Belt slippage is a production manager who, reasonably, will not let a
factorial experiment run on a line that is meeting its schedule; the mitigation is designed
in session 3, not discovered in week 10.

*HC example (illustrative).* Session 3, a project on ED arrival-to-first-antibiotic time for
sepsis-screened patients, calendar basis, primary metric the median minutes per patient.
Field 2: ★ A2 — logistic regression of "dose within 60 minutes" on arrival shift, screening
location and pharmacist presence; odds ratios and diagnostics run; the plain sentence not yet
written; the effect of pharmacist presence is the largest and the candidate has stated it as
"OR 2.9" and nothing else. ★ I1 — no designed experiment possible on patients; a controlled
pilot of pharmacist presence in the ED on alternating weeks is drafted, prediction not yet
written. Field 3: DOE practicum scheduled week 10 on the simulator; kaizen event venue not
yet agreed with the ED manager. Field 4: write the A2 sentence in minutes, not odds ("patients
seen while a pharmacist is in the ED reach first dose a median 41 minutes sooner; that is
about a third of the gap to the 60-minute target"), and write the pilot prediction in the
same units before the first alternating week starts. Field 5: plant access — the alternating-
week pilot needs the pharmacy director's sign-off for the staffing; steering group convened
at Tollgate 4 at the latest. Field 7: nothing ticked.

### 5.4 Live-lab and immersion standards

The Green Belt lab standards — 60/40, case first then own project, protect the debrief,
software parity, accessible by default — hold in every Black Belt lab. Three additions:

- **Restraint is on the run-of-show.** Every modeling and DOE lab has a moment where the
  sophisticated method is the wrong one, and the debrief asks who chose not to use it.
- **The experiment is the lab in weeks 8–11.** Candidates design on the canvas, run on the
  apparatus or simulator, and read residuals before they read effects. An instructor who
  hands out the design has removed the lab.
- **Immersion observation ratio.** In both immersions, one calibrated observer per 8
  candidates, and every candidate is observed in at least two roles in the change-leadership
  practicum. Below that ratio the practicum cannot be scored honestly and the immersion is
  rescheduled, not diluted.

*MFG example (illustrative).* Lab 9, fractional factorials, on the machining case: a
2^(4−1) design with a planted confounding of a two-factor interaction with the main effect
the team cares most about. Three teams report the main effect as real; the fourth reads the
alias structure and says the design cannot tell. The debrief is about resolution and the
fold-over run that resolves it, and about which teams would have shipped the wrong operating
window. An instructor who names the alias at the briefing has removed the lab.

## 6. Cohort cap of 16, and why

The level README caps a Black Belt cohort at 16 per instructor. At Green Belt the cap is 20
and the reason is lab quality. At Black Belt, four more things run out at the same time:

| Resource | At 16 | At 20 |
|---|---|---|
| **Master Black Belt coaching** — six 45-minute sessions plus up to nine monthly written checkpoints per candidate | ≈ 72 h of sessions and up to ≈ 70 h of written checkpoints per cohort: two coaches at the cap of 8 | Three coaches; the third is usually the one the bench does not have |
| **Reviewer bench** — cap 8 live files, coach and instructor excluded | Two reviewers plus the coach: the three-person floor | Three reviewers plus the coach: four Master Black Belts, or a second cohort cannot run |
| **DOE practicum** — four candidates per apparatus station, one observer per two stations, every candidate presents an experiment end to end in two days | Four stations, two observers, sixteen 20-minute presentations ≈ 5.5 h of the second day | Five stations, three observers, and the presentations no longer fit beside the runs |
| **Change-leadership practicum** — each candidate observed in two roles at 20 minutes each, one observer per 8 | ≈ 11 observer-hours over two days with two observers | ≈ 13 with three, and the third observer is a certified instructor the program has not got spare |
| **Green Belt tollgate reviews** — two supervised reviews per candidate | 32 supervised gate seats: one running Green Belt cohort of 12–16 (48–64 gate packages) covers it | 40 seats: a second Green Belt cohort must be running in the same weeks |

Sixteen is where one instructor, two coaches, two observers and three bench reviewers cover a
cohort without any of them doing two jobs. The cap is not a room size; it is the number at
which the separation in §4 still holds. Above 16 the cohort splits, whatever the buyer asks,
and the corporate playbook says so before the sale.

## 7. What this system measures about itself

Reported per cohort to the assessment lead and into the
[evaluation plan](../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md)'s coaching-quality section:

- Session on-time rate: sessions and monthly checkpoints held within the calendar window
  (target ≥ 90%).
- Coaching-note completeness on a 20% sample, all seven fields (target ≥ 95%).
- **Coach–reviewer ★ agreement** on A2, I1, C1, F1, L1: the coach's last-session status set
  against the reviewer's decision (target ≥ 85%). A miss is a calibration session, then an
  observation; never a ranking.
- Reviewer turnaround: tollgate records within 2 business days of the gate and final reviews
  within 20, each ≥ 95%.
- **Separation audit**, quarterly, on a 20% sample of live assignments: conflicts logged; no
  faculty name in the reviewer's file; no coaching note in the project file; no coaching
  session or monthly checkpoint attended by the reviewer; no advice in a tollgate record
  ("try", "consider", "you should" are findings). Target zero breaches; a breach voids the
  review and re-assigns.
- Reviewer calibration: every active reviewer within 8 points on all four anchor files and
  exact on every ★ item, each quarter they review; drift events by reviewer; annual 10%
  re-review agreement.
- Practicum observer agreement: pass/redo disagreements per immersion, resolved and recorded.
- Resubmission rate by coach and by reviewer, read against caseload and vertical first.
- Cohort L1 items on lab quality, immersion quality and coaching, per instructor and coach.

None of this ranks faculty publicly. The outcomes page carries program-wide figures; each
instructor, coach and reviewer sees their own.
