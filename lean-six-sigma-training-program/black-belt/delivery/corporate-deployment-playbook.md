# Black Belt — Corporate Deployment Playbook

For an organization building an internal continuous-improvement function, certifying **two to
eight Black Belts a year**. What is sold is not seats on a sixteen-week course. It is a
portfolio of cross-functional, strategically linked projects, each above the impact threshold,
each with an executive sponsor and a steering group, closed to Finance-validated or
mission-metric-validated results within nine months of week 16 — and, at the end of it, people
who can lead the next portfolio, coach the organization's Green Belts, and in three years put
forward one of their own for Master Black Belt. The course is how that happens. This playbook
is how the program makes sure the portfolio, the governance and the Finance partnership exist
before week 1, because a Black Belt cohort without them produces exam passes, a stack of open
projects, and an organization that has learned that Black Belt means "trained."

[instructor, reviewer and coaching system](instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md)
(who coaches, who reviews, why the client never reviews, why the cap is 16), the
[Black Belt Guild](black-belt-guild.md) (where the certified Black Belts go next, and how
they coach in-house), the [review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md), the
[charter and strategic linkage template](../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md), the
[tollgate standard](../project/tollgate-standard.md), the

whose cohort reports are where this portfolio comes from.

## 1. What an internal CI function is, and what it is not

An internal function is not a headcount of belts. It is four things the organization owns:

1. **A portfolio** of chartered projects with an entry rule, a governance rhythm and a
   register (§8).
2. **A Finance partnership** that classifies benefits before a project starts and validates
   them when it closes, so the function's reported impact is Finance's number (§5).
3. **Coaching capacity**: Black Belts who coach the organization's Green Belts on the
   program's bench, so that the Green Belt pipeline is not dependent on the program's
   faculty forever (§9).
4. **A path to a Master Black Belt** who will own the first three things, calibrate the
   organization's own reviewers and steward certification integrity inside it — the Master
   Black Belt fellowship's whole purpose (§9).

Two to eight Black Belts a year is the range where this works. Below two, a single candidate
has no peer and joins a public cohort instead; the program will say so. Above eight a year,
the organization is running a deployment and should already have a Master Black Belt
candidate on the fellowship, or the portfolio will outgrow its governance within eighteen
months. The program's rule is the portfolio, not the ratio; illustrative ratios organizations
use (one Black Belt per 100–150 employees in a site; one Master Black Belt per eight to ten
Black Belts) are a check on ambition, not a target.

## 2. Where the projects come from

### 2.1 Three sources

The Green Belt cohort report already contains the Black Belt portfolio's first draft. Three
sources, in order of quality:

- **Black Belt invitations.** Every Green Belt reviewer of record flags projects that revealed
  a Black-Belt-sized problem: a cross-functional cause outside the sponsor's process, an
  interaction that needs a designed experiment, a control plan that needs a system-level
  change. The engagement lead brings those flags into the QBR as next year's seats *and their
  projects*. These are the best candidates because the cause structure has already been
  partly measured by someone with a baseline.
- **Held candidates.** The Green Belt selection filter's "no single owner" rejects: problems
  the White Belt themes and Yellow Belt storyboards surfaced that crossed functions and were
  parked. They arrive with the observations that found them.
- **The strategy cascade.** Objectives on the executive dashboard that are not moving, run
  backward to the processes that drive them. Organizations that run strategy deployment
  formally publish the chain; most do not, and the engagement lead builds it with the
  executive sponsor in the readiness phase. This is the source of the strategic linkage
  statement (rubric D2), and a project that cannot be run backward from an objective the
  executives review is not a Black Belt project however large its savings.

### 2.2 The selection filter at Black Belt scale

Run every candidate through seven questions with the CI lead and the executive sponsor. A
candidate that fails one is not a Black Belt project; it may be a Green Belt project, a
Just-Do-It, a rollout, or a Master Black Belt deployment question, and saying which is a
service.

| Question | Black Belt project if… | Otherwise it is… |
|---|---|---|
| Is the cause unknown? | Nobody can say with evidence why the problem happens; the functions disagree | A rollout; the program declines it because ★ A2 cannot pass a decided cause |
| Does it cross functions with no single owner? | Two or more functions in scope; the decision rights have to be written because nobody holds them | A Green Belt project — one process, one owner — and a better one for it |
| Is it strategically linked? | A written line from the primary metric to a stated organizational objective, which the sponsor will initial | A worthwhile local improvement, funded locally |
| Does it meet the impact threshold? | ≥ $75K annualized projected on a basis Finance initials, or a clinical or service-level impact the mission-metric executive signs as material (§3) | A Green Belt project, or two of them |
| Can the cause be tested with an experiment or a controlled pilot? | The process can be disturbed on purpose, or a comparison group or period exists | A monitoring project; ★ I1 cannot be earned by observation alone |
| Is it bounded? | Closable within nine months of week 16 by one Black Belt at 8–10 hours a week with a team of four to six under signed time contracts | Split it, or hold it for the Master Black Belt deployment capstone |
| Does the sponsor sit at the right level? | An executive who owns the objective in the linkage statement, will convene a steering group of the functional heads, and will make five tollgate decisions | The sponsor is a process owner: re-sponsor upward, or charter it as a Green Belt project |

The seventh question loses the most candidates and is the one to hold. A process owner who
sponsors a cross-functional project has no authority over the other function, and by
Tollgate 3 the candidate is negotiating between two rational managers each protecting their
staff's time — the Green Belt failure mode, at higher stakes.

### 2.3 Pipeline sizing

Charter **1.5 candidates per seat** into the pipeline at the readiness gate; expect a third
to fall to the threshold check, a function that will not sign, or a sponsor who moves. Six
seats need nine chartered candidates at week −8; the surplus is next year's pipeline, already
filtered, and the first evidence that the portfolio outlives any one cohort.

*HC example (illustrative).* A regional hospital's second Green Belt cohort report carried
three Black Belt invitations: medication reconciliation across pharmacy and nursing (the
re-chartered half from the Green Belt cohort), ED boarding time across ED, bed management
and inpatient units, and OR first-case on-time starts across anesthesia, surgery and
sterile processing. The cascade added sepsis bundle compliance (an objective on the quality
committee dashboard at 58% against 75%) and readmissions within 30 days. Of five, the
readmissions candidate failed the sixth question — closable in nine months only as a slice,
which became "discharge-medication counseling for heart-failure patients," a Green Belt
project. Four Black Belt candidates for three seats; the ED boarding project's steering group
took a month to assemble because the bed-management function's head sat in a different
division, which the readiness gate exists to discover at week −6 rather than week 6.

## 3. The impact threshold

The prerequisite for a seat is a project with a **projected annualized impact of at least
$75,000, or an equivalent clinical or service-level impact**. The threshold exists because a
Black Belt project costs the organization roughly 250 hours of the candidate's time, a team's
time contracts, an executive's five tollgates and Finance's attention; below the threshold, a
Green Belt runs it cheaper and the credential is honest.

### 3.1 How it is checked

On the charter, before week 1, and re-stated at Tollgate 2 against the measured baseline:

- The arithmetic in full: (baseline − target) × volume × unit cost or rate, with the source
  and date of every number written beside it. Run rate over twelve months at most; labeled
  *projected*.
- The benefit class named (hard, soft, cost avoidance, revenue, mission or clinical) per the
  [benefit-classification guide](../templates/benefit-classification-guide.md), because a
  $75K soft benefit and a $75K hard benefit are different promises and Finance treats them
  differently.
- **Finance initials the basis**, not the total. Finance is agreeing that if the metric moves
  as targeted, this is how the money would be counted; Finance is not agreeing that it will.
- For mission-metric projects — sepsis bundle compliance, patient harm, service-level
  attainment, days sales outstanding — the **mission-metric executive named on the charter**
  signs that the targeted movement is material to the objective they own. The program does
  not convert clinical outcomes to dollars to clear a threshold, and a candidate who
  arrives with a dollar figure attached to avoided harm is asked to remove it.

### 3.2 When it is not met

A candidate whose honest arithmetic comes to $48K is a Green Belt project, and a good one.
Two things are refused: inflating volume or rate to clear the line (Finance will not initial
it, and if they do, the closure validation will not match it), and bolting two unrelated
processes together to add their savings (the second question fails — there is no cause
structure across them). A candidate whose projected impact falls below the threshold at
Tollgate 2, after the baseline is measured honestly, continues if the steering group so
decides; the charter is re-scoped, the impact class on the eventual badge is what Finance
validated, and the coaching note flags it. The rubric passes honest measurement.

## 4. Readiness gate (before any seat is provisioned)

Nine conditions, in writing, signed by the executive sponsor. The program will not seat a
candidate without all of them, and says so before the sale.

| Condition | Evidence | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Executive sponsor and steering group per project** — the executive who owns the objective, and the functional heads whose processes are in scope | Named on the charter; each functional head has signed for hours and decision rights | Do not seat. A function that "will support" without a signature withdraws its analyst in week 9 |
| **Charter agreement signed with the impact threshold checked** | The program's charter with the arithmetic, Finance's initials on the basis, or the mission-metric executive's signature | Delay the seat, not the sale; the week 2 clinic rebuilds charters, it does not create them |
| **Finance partner named and the basis agreed** (§5) | Named at week −6; has read the benefit-classification guide; internal rates and cost bases for the portfolio written down at week −4 | Do not launch. Finance first heard of at closure rejects half the claims, and the portfolio cannot be reported |
| **Candidate prerequisites verified by the program** | Certified Green Belt (this program, or external plus the calibration assessment with gaps bridged) and one completed DMAIC project with sponsor verification | Bridge first; the calibration assessment is scheduled at week −6 and the bridging modules are done before week 1 |
| **Candidate time** — ≈ 6.5 h/week structured plus 8–10 h/week project work for sixteen weeks, then 4–6 h/week to closure, during working hours | Written; confirmed by the candidate's own manager where the manager is not the sponsor; in the launch comms | Resolve first. This is the top predictor of tollgate slippage, and slippage is reported |
| **Team time contracts signed** — four to six team members with hours per week signed by *their* managers | On the charter's team table | The candidate leads people they do not manage; a promise their manager did not make is not a promise |
| **A venue for the experiment and the kaizen event** — the process owner has agreed in writing that a designed experiment or controlled pilot may run on the process in weeks 8–12, and that a 3–5 day kaizen event may be held in weeks 13–14 | Named process, named owner, the weeks blocked | Design the alternative now: the practicum apparatus or simulator covers gate 3 but not ★ I1; a partner-pool organization covers the kaizen event |
| **Green Belt gate seats for requirement 5** — a Green Belt cohort (the organization's own or the program's) running in weeks 15–20 with a calibrated reviewer of record who will supervise | Cohort ID and reviewer confirmed by the assessment lead | Schedule against a public Green Belt cohort; a candidate cannot complete gate 5 without one |
| **Program capacity confirmed** — instructor, two Master Black Belt coaches above eight candidates, two practicum observers, and a reviewer bench of at least three Master Black Belts with no conflict | Confirmed by the assessment lead at week −6 | The start waits. The program delays the start, not the independence |

Plus the practical checks: data access for every primary and secondary metric confirmed by
name at week −2; the DOE practicum kit shipped or the process simulator provisioned for
transactional cohorts; proctoring for the exam arranged on site.

## 5. Finance partnership, set up before week 1

The Finance partner is one named person from the organization's Finance function, at a level
that can commit a cost basis, and their calendar is written into the engagement. At Black
Belt they do more than at Green Belt because the sums are larger, the projects cross cost
centers, and the portfolio is reported as a total.

| When | Finance does |
|---|---|
| Week −6 | Named. Reads the benefit-classification guide and the validation form; agrees which internal rates, loaded costs, and cost bases the portfolio will use, once, in writing |
| Week −4 | Initials the impact-threshold basis on every charter; declines any basis they cannot trace |
| Tollgate 2 (week 5) | Re-reads the basis against the measured baseline; agrees the benefit class per project on the charter — ≈ 1 hour |
| Tollgate 4 (week 12) | Reads the experiment or pilot result against the projected impact; states what evidence window closure validation will need — ≈ 1 hour |
| Tollgate 5 (week 16) | Reviews the benefit rows and the cost-of-project rows (rubric F2); flags any double count with another project in the portfolio or with a Green Belt project — ≈ 1 hour |
| Closure | Validates the money claimed, on the form, from the before/after evidence in the file; may reduce or decline; **signs ★ F1**. The program publishes Finance's figure, never the candidate's |
| Annual portfolio readout | Signs the portfolio's validated total by benefit class, in the measured periods, with the annualized figure shown separately and labeled |

Three rules, stated at the briefing and printed on the register. A benefit is counted in the
period it was measured; annualization is Finance's, labeled, and never appears on a program
surface as the result. A benefit is counted once across the whole organization — a Black Belt
project that grew from a Green Belt slice credits the Black Belt project, notes the origin,
and the Green Belt figure is netted out; the organization's own CI dashboard usually double
counts, and the portfolio register is the place that does not. And the cost of the project
is netted: the candidate's hours, the time contracts and any spend, on the F2 line, so that
the function's reported impact survives an audit.

*TXN example (illustrative).* A freight company's Black Belt project on invoice disputes
projected $410K annualized at charter, hard benefit, on days-sales-outstanding improvement
at the company's cost of working capital. Finance initialed the basis at week −4 with one
change: the rate used was the marginal borrowing rate, not the weighted cost of capital. At
closure the dispute rate had fallen from 9.2% to 4.1% over two monthly closes; Finance
validated $61K for the two months measured, hard, at the agreed rate, and declined the
candidate's inclusion of collector hours saved because the collections team had not been
reduced or redeployed — that row became soft benefit, capacity, separately stated. The badge
shows the impact class Finance validated. The company's dashboard shows the annualized figure
with Finance's label. Nobody argued at closure, because the argument had been had at week −4.

## 6. Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor (one per project) | Client executive who owns the objective | Signs the linkage statement; convenes the steering group; makes five tollgate decisions; attends the executive readout; answers the day-90 check |
| Steering group | The functional heads in scope | Sign time contracts and decision rights; decide re-scopes and spend at Tollgates 1, 4 and 5; are the audience of the readout |
| Finance partner | Client Finance | §5 — before week 1, five calendar touches, closure validation, portfolio total |
| Mission-metric executive | Client clinical or service executive, where the impact is not money | Signs the threshold equivalence; validates the mission-metric result at closure |
| CI lead / deployment lead | Client | Runs the portfolio (§8); the pipeline; sponsor and data-access coordination; receives the portfolio report; **never reviews** |
| Candidate | Client | Leads one chartered project; the five gates |
| Team members | Client Green Belts, Yellow Belts, frontline staff | Under signed time contracts; the Green Belt on the team is often the candidate's L3 coaching evidence |
| Instructor | Program, certified per the instructor system | Labs, office hours, the immersions or the waves |
| Master Black Belt coach(es) | Program coaching bench | Six sessions; monthly written checkpoints to closure |
| Reviewer of record | Program reviewer bench; **a Master Black Belt, never from the client** | Tollgate records; the 150-point review; validation checks; signs the certificate; day-90 check |
| Engagement lead | Program | Readiness gate; briefings; the portfolio council seat until the client's Master Black Belt takes it; portfolio report; showcase |

Managers of team members get the one-page brief: honor the hours you signed for; ask "what
did the experiment show?" once a month; do not ask the team to implement before Tollgate 4.

### 7.2 The 4 × 4-day wave variant with virtual coaching between

The classic deployment model: four on-site waves a month apart, the project worked between
them, coaching virtual throughout. The content hours are the same order — each wave day is
≈ 6 hours of contact (≈ 96 hours), plus ≈ 16 hours of short self-paced pre-reads before each
wave — so the variant carries slightly more structured time than the hybrid, because both
immersions are embedded in the waves and the pre-reads are shorter than the hybrid's
self-paced modules. The phase lock holds. The tollgates and the coaching sessions stay on the
calendar; only the teaching moves.

| When | What | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks −10 to −1 | Identical to §7.1 | — |
| Weeks −3 to 0 | M1 pre-read (≈ 4 h) with a deadline enforced by the CI lead | Virtual |
| **Wave 1 (week 1, 4 days)** | Day 1: portfolio placement, strategic linkage, charter-at-scale clinic with steering groups present for the last two hours. Day 2: advanced VOC on the case, then own customers; team launch without authority. Day 3: advanced MSA lab — destructive, attribute-complex, system-field provenance; MSA plan per metric drafted. Day 4: distribution identification and the baseline chart family; **coaching session 1 held 1:1 during breakouts**. Ends with every charter rebuilt and signed by the functional heads | On site; producer present; two coaches above 8 |
| Week 2 | **Tollgate 1 — Define**: sponsor and steering group, ten minutes, virtual; reviewer's record within five business days | Virtual |
| Weeks 2–5 | MSA studies and baseline collection on own metrics; M3 pre-read (power, sample size, intervals; ≈ 4 h); weekly 60-minute virtual clinic with the instructor; **session 2 (week 4)** virtual | Virtual |
| Week 5 | **Tollgate 2 — Measure**, virtual, on the calendar. A project without a baseline goes into Wave 2 anyway and the gap is on the coaching note and the record | Virtual |
| **Wave 2 (week 6, 4 days)** | Day 1: non-normal capability, transformations, confidence and prediction intervals on the case, then own baseline. Days 2–3: modeling — multiple regression with diagnostics, logistic, non-parametrics, two-way ANOVA; each on the case, then on own data; the restraint moment on the run-of-show. Day 4: full factorials — design on the canvas, run on the apparatus or simulator, read residuals before effects | On site |
| Week 7 | **Tollgate 3 — Analyze**, virtual; the model run on own data during Wave 2 day 3 | Virtual |
| Weeks 7–9 | M5 pre-read (fractional factorials, blocking, center points; ≈ 4 h); DOE planning canvas drafted; **plant access for the experiment negotiated with the process owner**; **session 3 (week 8)** virtual | Virtual |
| **Wave 3 (week 10, 4 days)** | Days 1–2: fractional factorials and confounding, blocking, curvature, sequential strategy, response surface introduction. Days 3–4: **the DOE practicum (Immersion 1, embedded)** — every candidate designs, runs, analyzes and presents an experiment end to end; two observers; pass/redo. Own-process experiments scheduled to run in weeks 10–11 | On site; kit on site or simulator provisioned |
| Weeks 10–12 | Experiment or controlled pilot on the process; confirmation runs; M6 pre-read (advanced SPC; ≈ 2 h) and one virtual lab; **session 4 (week 12)** virtual | Virtual |
| Week 12 | **Tollgate 4 — Improve**, virtual; steering group present | Virtual |
| Weeks 12–14 | Control strategy designed; M7 pre-read (≈ 2 h); **kaizen event held (requirement 4)**, observed live by the instructor or a bench Master Black Belt, or videoed for two observers | On site, the event |
| **Wave 4 (week 15, 4 days)** | Days 1–2: lean at system level — future-state design, Little's Law, balancing and leveling, setup reduction, TPM/OEE or demand-capacity; kaizen event debriefs. Day 3: **the change-leadership practicum (Immersion 2, embedded)** — observed role-play, two observers, each candidate in two roles. Day 4 morning: reviewer calibration and tollgate simulation (Lab 16); afternoon: program economics and the Finance clinic with the Finance partner in the room; **sponsors and steering groups attend the last two hours for the Tollgate 5 readout rehearsal** | On site |
| Week 16 | **Tollgate 5 — Control and readout rehearsal**; **session 5**; Green Belt tollgate reviews scheduled with the supervising reviewer | Virtual or the last afternoon of Wave 4 |
| Week 17 | Exam, proctored on site; L1 survey | On site |
| To week 52 | As §7.1 | Virtual; readout on site |

Four things the wave variant needs that the hybrid does not. The **pre-read deadlines are
enforced by the CI lead**, because a wave day where half the room has not read the modeling
pre-read becomes the lecture the lab standard forbids. **Data access is confirmed before
Wave 1**, because there are four weeks between Waves 1 and 2 to run an MSA and collect a
baseline with the right distribution. **Plant access for the experiment is agreed before
Wave 3**, in writing, because the experiment on the process runs in the fortnight after the
practicum and a production manager who first hears of it that week will, reasonably, say no.
And **the two immersions are embedded, not dropped**: a wave variant that trades the DOE
practicum for more slides has removed gate 3, and the program does not run it.

*MFG example (illustrative).* A four-plant manufacturer runs a six-seat wave cohort at its
largest plant. Between Waves 2 and 3 the candidate with the coatings project learns that the
line will be released for eight runs, not the sixteen a full 2^4 needs. Session 3 is spent on
the half-fraction, the alias structure and which two-factor interactions the team is
prepared to give up; the process engineer on the team names the two the plant's experience
says are real, and the design confounds the other pair with the effects nobody believes in.
Eight runs in week 11, a fold-over of four runs offered by the plant manager after seeing the
first results, Tollgate 4 on the calendar. The alternative — a full factorial refused in week
10 and ★ I1 falling to a pilot — was designed against in week 8, not discovered in week 10.

### 7.3 Sponsor and steering-group briefing (two hours, week −4)

Every executive sponsor and every functional head on a steering group, together. What they
are told, in substance:

> **What you are sponsoring.** One person, a problem that crosses your functions, one metric
> tied to an objective you own, nine months after the course. The candidate will find out why
> the problem happens — with an experiment where the process allows one — before proposing
> anything. That is slower than deciding, and it is the point.
>
> **What you do.** Five times, ten minutes each, on the calendar, you hear one page and
> decide: proceed, proceed with a condition, or hold. At Tollgates 1, 4 and 5 the steering
> group is in the room, because the decisions there — decision rights, implementation across
> your functions, handover — are yours together. Between tollgates you honor the hours your
> people signed for, you let the experiment run when the calendar says, and when the
> candidate tells you the cause you believed did not verify, you say "good — what did?" At
> the readout you make the decisions the page asks for. At day 90 you answer one question.
>
> **What you must not do.** Do not name the countermeasure before Tollgate 4. Do not move the
> metric, its operational definition or its measurement window after Tollgate 2. Do not ask
> for the experiment to be skipped because production is meeting schedule this month —
> decide that at Tollgate 3, on the calendar, with the alternative on the page. Do not ask
> Finance for a bigger number; Finance's figure is the one the program publishes, and
> everyone in this room knows that today.
>
> **What you get.** A verified cause for a problem you have been managing across a
> boundary, a countermeasure with experimental or piloted evidence, a control plan an owner
> signed, a validated figure, and a person who can lead the next one — and coach your Green
> Belts while they do.

## 8. Project portfolio governance

### 8.1 The portfolio council

Monthly, sixty minutes: the executive sponsors (or one delegated among them), the CI lead,
the Finance partner, the engagement lead — replaced by the organization's own Master Black
Belt when there is one. It reads the register, hears no presentations, and makes three kinds
of decision: enter a project, re-scope or stop a project (only at a tollgate, on the
reviewer's record and the sponsor's decision), and accept the validated figure into the
portfolio total. It does not score projects and never sees a coaching note.

### 8.2 The register

One row per project, kept by the CI lead, printed for every council meeting:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Project ID · candidate · sponsor · steering group · Finance partner | Names, roles |
| Strategic objective and linkage statement | The sponsor-initialed line |
| Primary metric, operational definition, basis | Fixed at Tollgate 2 |
| Projected impact and class; threshold basis initialed (date) | The charter arithmetic |
| Tollgate status | 1–5, each: on calendar / held / re-scoped, with the sponsor's decision and the date |
| ★ items at risk on the latest reviewer record | Copied verbatim from the record; no interpretation |
| Requirements | DOE practicum · kaizen · Green Belt reviews · exam: scheduled / done |
| Validated impact and class; measured period | Finance's or the mission-metric executive's figure, entered only at closure validation |
| Origin and netting | The Green Belt slice or Just-Do-It it grew from; the figure netted out |
| Status | Chartered → In cohort → In closure → Submitted → **Certified** → **Sustained (day 90)** / Not sustained / Closed as documented learning |

Three rules on the register's footer. **Nothing enters without the threshold basis initialed
and the charter signed** by every function in scope. **"Closed" means the reviewer signed and
Finance validated**; a project with a countermeasure implemented and no signature is "in
closure," and the portfolio total does not include it. **Certified and sustained are
different columns**, and the annual readout shows both, because a portfolio of certified
projects with a 50% day-90 sustainment rate has a control-plan problem the next cohort will
inherit.

### 8.3 The impact threshold as a portfolio rule

The threshold governs entry, not reporting. A project that entered at $120K projected and
validated at $61K in its measured period is a success the portfolio reports at $61K. A
portfolio whose validated total is consistently a third of its projected total has a
threshold-arithmetic problem — usually volume or rate assumptions Finance initialed too
readily at week −4 — and the council reads the ratio each year and tightens the basis, not
the candidates.

| 3 | Steady state: one or two cohorts a year on the organization's own pipeline | Steady state: 2–8 a year, sized by the portfolio | One Black Belt on the Green Belt reviewer bench — reviewing *other* organizations' files, never this one's | **Master Black Belt candidate** from among the year-1 Black Belts: three years, five projects with two as coach or reviewer, and now the function to lead. The fellowship's deployment capstone is this organization's governance re-architected; the assessed coaching hours are earned in this organization's cohorts |
| 4+ | — | — | The Master Black Belt coaches Black Belt sessions on the program's bench for the organization's own candidates (coaching is not assessment) | The Master Black Belt takes the council seat, calibrates the organization's own Green Belt second readers, and stewards integrity; **certification and the reviewer of record stay with the program** at every level and every scale |

The line that does not move, stated to the buyer at the gate and again in year 3: the
organization's Black Belts coach, the organization's Master Black Belt governs and coaches,
and the reviewer who signs any credential in this organization is the program's and has never
worked for it. That is the independence the organization is paying for, and the reason its
Black Belts' credentials are worth something to the people who hold them when they leave.

## 10. The annual portfolio readout

A 60-minute readout to the executive sponsors with Finance in the room, delivered by the
engagement lead in year 1 and by the CI lead or Master Black Belt after: exam pass rates;
tollgates held on calendar, by gate; the five gates completed per candidate; projects
submitted, certified, resubmitted, closed as documented learning; **Finance-validated impact
by class in the measured periods, netted of project cost, with the annualized figure shown
separately and labeled**; mission-metric results signed by their executives; day-90
sustainment where available; Green Belt files coached by the organization's own Black Belts;
the coaching-hours log totals; and the L1 items on steering-group engagement and time
contracts, by function, verbatim — a function scoring low on "my steering group made the
tollgate decisions" has a governance problem the next portfolio will inherit.

The next conversation writes itself: *"Six seats, five certified, four sustained at day 90,
validated total $340K hard and $190K soft in the measured periods. Two experiments, three
controlled pilots. Your Black Belts coached fourteen Green Belt files this year. The pipeline
has four candidates above the threshold and one of your Black Belts meets the Master Black
Belt criteria in eleven months."*

## 11. Common deployment failure modes

| Failure | Signature | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold gaming | Projected impact clears $75K on a volume figure nobody can source; validated total is a fifth of projected across the portfolio | Finance initials the *basis* at week −4 and declines what it cannot trace; the council reads the validated-to-projected ratio annually and tightens the basis |
| Sponsor at the wrong level | A process owner sponsors a cross-functional project; by Tollgate 3 the other function has withdrawn its analyst | Selection filter question 7; steering group signed at the gate; re-sponsor upward before week 1 |
| The rollout wearing a charter | The executive already knows the answer; the fishbone has one bone; the "experiment" is a before/after of the decided change | Question 1; the briefing's "do not name the countermeasure before Tollgate 4"; ★ A2 explained to the sponsor as the reason the program declines it |
| The experiment refused | Week 10: production will not release the line; the candidate falls back to observation and ★ I1 is at risk | Venue agreed in writing at the gate; session 3 designs the fraction the plant *will* allow; Tollgate 3 decides the alternative on the calendar, not week 10 |
| No kaizen venue | Weeks 13–14 arrive and no process owner has blocked the days | Gate condition; the partner pool as the fallback named at week −2, not week 13 |
| No Green Belt gates to sit | Requirement 5 waits four months for a cohort | Gate condition: a running Green Belt cohort and supervising reviewer confirmed at week −6, or seats in a public cohort booked |
| Team time contracts broken | The candidate's team members are reassigned by their managers in week 6; the candidate is doing the team's work alone | Contracts signed by the managers at the gate; the manager brief; slippage reported at the week 5 readout by function, never by name |
| Finance basis agreed late | The rate is argued at closure; Finance validates half; the candidate feels cheated | §5: basis at week −4, class at Tollgate 2, evidence window at Tollgate 4 |
| Double counting across the portfolio | A Black Belt project and the Green Belt slice it grew from both report the same hours; the CI dashboard adds them | The register's origin-and-netting column; Tollgate 5's Finance review; the rule stated at the briefing |
| The client wants to review | The CI lead or the organization's own Master Black Belt asks to co-score, "to build internal capability" | Never, at any level. Offer the client's Black Belts the coaching bench and second-reader seats; offer the client's Master Black Belt the coaching-bench seat for their own candidates; the reviewer of record is the program's |
| Pre-reads not done (wave variant) | Wave 2 day 2 becomes a lecture on regression; the 60/40 standard fails; the own-data model is not run and Tollgate 3 slips | Pre-read deadlines enforced by the CI lead; the instructor's right to run the wave's own-data block as a clinic for those who read and a catch-up for those who did not |
| The Black Belt as project manager | After certification the organization uses its Black Belts to run rollouts and integration projects; the pipeline empties; recertification lapses at year 3 | The portfolio council's entry rule; the guild's story submission (which records "nothing to report" honestly); the year-3 conversation about what the function is for |
| The candidate promoted mid-project | Month 5 of closure: the candidate now runs one of the functions in scope and the sponsor is their peer | Decision classes, not names, on the charter; the steering group re-signs; the reviewer checks that the L1 evidence still shows the candidate leading people they did not manage *at the time* |
| The function built on headcount | "We need twelve Black Belts by year two" with a pipeline of three projects above the threshold | The program sizes seats by the portfolio and says so at the gate; nine chartered candidates for six seats, or fewer seats |

*HC example (illustrative).* A hospital's ED boarding project — hours from admission
decision to inpatient bed, per patient, calendar basis, median 5.8 hours — has a sponsor who
is the chief nursing officer and a steering group of the ED director, the bed-management
director and two inpatient unit directors. At Tollgate 3 the verified cause is a bed-cleaning
request queue that resets at shift change, an environmental-services process owned by a
facilities director who is not on the steering group and did not sign. Two rational managers
— the ED director who needs the beds and the facilities director whose staffing was set for a
different demand pattern — are now in the candidate's tollgate. The reviewer's record marks
★ A2 evidenced and D3 at risk; the sponsor's decision is a hold of ten business days; the
steering group adds the facilities director, who signs a time contract for two housekeepers
on the controlled pilot. The fix was a signature at week −6 that the selection filter's
second question should have found. The register carries the re-scope, and the next portfolio
runs every candidate's cause hypotheses against the org chart before the gate.
