# Certification Requirement 4 — Kaizen Facilitation (observed or video-reviewed · pass / redo)

You lead one rapid-improvement event — three to five days, a real process, a team you do not
manage — and an observer scores how you led it. Module 7 §7.8
([`../modules/m7-lean-systems.md`](../modules/m7-lean-systems.md)) taught the method and Lab 14
rehearsed the hard moments. The Kaizen Event Leader's Kit
([`../templates/kaizen-event-leaders-kit.md`](../templates/kaizen-event-leaders-kit.md)) holds
the charter table, the pre-work checklist, the roles, the day-by-day agenda and the 30-day
forms. **This document does not repeat the kit.** It says what makes an event eligible, how
you source one, how it is observed, what the observer scores, and what makes the requirement
complete rather than merely finished.

The gate is pass / redo. It is recorded on the credential as `kaizenFacilitation: completed`
with the event's month, the vertical and the observation mode (`observed` or `video-reviewed`)
— see [`../bok-crosswalk-and-credential.md`](../bok-crosswalk-and-credential.md) §2.4–2.5. The
event's 30-day follow-up is part of the evidence, not an afterword.

**Outcomes.** By the end of the requirement, the candidate can, observed:

1. Screen a candidate process against the five eligibility tests and say why a near-miss is
   not an event, before anyone blocks a calendar.
2. Negotiate decision rights, a signed numeric target and named backfill with a sponsor and
   three line managers, in writing, four weeks ahead.
3. Establish a charted baseline on an operational definition that the day-5 sample and the
   day-30 chart will still use.
4. Facilitate process rather than content across five days, with the design and the standard
   work produced by the people who do the work.
5. Hold the room safe — everyone speaks, no one's work is the villain, and no trial runs
   before its risk is checked.
6. Close the event on a comparison that states its own limits, hand the chart to a named
   owner, and return at day 30 with an honest reading.

## 1. What qualifies as an eligible event

### 1.1 The five tests

An event is eligible when all five are true. The practicum owner checks them on the
eligibility form (§1.3), and the observer checks them again at the pre-brief call (§3.2) —
because the cheapest place to stop an ineligible event is a week before it, not on day 5.

| Test | The standard | How it is checked | On the form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three to five days** | Three to five consecutive working days, the team on the event full-time. The three-day compression is the floor; it is used when the segment is small and the trials are physical | Charter dates; the daily attendance sheet with hours | Dates; hours per day per person |
| **A real process** | Work that runs at least weekly and preferably daily, in a segment you can walk in 20 minutes, with an output somebody downstream receives | Start step and stop step named; the measurement-system check done | Start step, stop step, frequency, metric with its operational definition |
| **A sponsor** | Someone with authority over the process who signs a target in numbers and attends the kick-off, a day-2 check-in and the report-out | Signed charter; the sponsor's three calendar entries | Name, role, signature date, the target as a number |
| **A team you do not manage** | Six to nine people, at least half of whom do the work; nobody on the team reports to you, directly or indirectly; the process owner is somebody else | Team list with reporting lines declared | The list, and the line "none of these people reports to me, directly or indirectly" |
| **Authority arranged in advance** | Decision rights written in two columns before day 1: what the team may change without asking, and what needs whom within what time | The charter's decision-rights rows | Both columns, each "needs whom" row carrying a named person and a turnaround in days |

The fourth test is the one candidates argue with, and it is the one the credential rests on.
Rubric item ★ L1 in [`../project/review-rubric.md`](../project/review-rubric.md) asks for
evidence that you can lead people you do not manage. An event you run with your own reports
tests something else — whether they do what you ask — and that is not what a Black Belt is
certified for.

### 1.2 What does not count

Each of these is a reasonable thing to do. None of them is this gate.

| Near-miss | Why it is not eligible | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **A training course with an improvement exercise** (TXN) | Nothing in the area is different on Friday; there is no baseline, no trial and no metric | Run the course if it is needed, then charter an event on one segment of the process the course used |
| **A three-day workshop that ends in a list of ideas** | Day 3 is try, not decide. A list is a plan for an event, not the event | Build the day-3 trial into the agenda and protect it from tidy-up pressure |
| **A project meeting series** — six Tuesdays, two hours each | The team is never released; the concentration is the mechanism | Convert the twelve hours into three full days |
| **Five half-days across a fortnight** | Same reason: people return to their work each afternoon and the room restarts every morning | Three consecutive days with named backfill |
| **A two-day blitz** | Below the minimum. Two days has no try-adjust day, so it verifies nothing | Use the kit's three-day version |
| **An event in your own area with your own reports** (MFG) | You are using authority, not facilitation | Lead one in another area; let a peer lead yours |
| **An event you co-facilitated** | The record cannot separate two leaders, and the rubric scores one | Co-lead one as practice; lead the gate event outright |
| **An event that installs a decision already made** (HC) | The team is being briefed, not designing. The design space is the point | If the decision is made, run it as an implementation. The gate needs a team that can still change the answer |
| **A classroom or simulated event** | Lab 14 is the rehearsal and it is already scored as a lab | Lab 14 prepares you for this; it does not replace it |
| **An event you attended at a supplier or partner as a guest** | You did not lead it | Ask to lead the next one, through the pool if necessary |
| **An event whose baseline is an estimate** | This is the theatre finding (§5.2), and it fails whatever else is true | Two weeks of charted data before the charter is signed |
| **Your project's pilot or designed experiment** | That is ★ I1, a different requirement with a different standard | The event may install part of the future state and evidence I3, I4 and C2 — but it must pass the five tests as an event |

### 1.3 The eligibility form

One page, filed by **week 12** and approved by the **practicum owner**. Not by your coach: the
coach/assessor separation in
[`../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md`](../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md)
§4.5 holds here as it does at every other gate. Your coach helps you write it and does not
approve it.

The form carries: the five tests with their evidence; the venue (employer or partner pool);
the proposed dates; the observation mode and the organization's written approval where
recording is proposed; the sponsor's name and role; the team list with reporting lines; the
baseline plan with its start date and chart type; the decision-rights columns in draft; and
any edge case from §1.4 you are declaring.

Three outcomes: **approved**; **approved with conditions** (the condition is written and
checked at the pre-brief); **not approved**, with the reason and the alternative named in the
same sentence. "Not approved" three weeks out is a service. "Not approved" on day 5 is a
failure of this form.

### 1.4 Rulings made in advance

These come up every cohort. They are decided here so that no candidate discovers a ruling
after the calendar is blocked.

| Case | Ruling | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| The event installs part of your own project's future state | Eligible and encouraged | The same event may evidence I3, I4, C2 and requirement 4. It is not automatically ★ I1: a piloted solution needs its own written prediction and comparison period |
| One team member is a dotted-line report of yours | Eligible if declared | They are not the process owner and not the only person on the team who does the work. The observer records whether the room defers to you |
| Your sponsor is your own manager | Eligible | Brief them at T−2 weeks like any other sponsor. The risk is a sponsor who solves from the doorway carrying extra weight; the mitigation is the same three appearances |
| A distributed or virtual event (TXN) | Eligible where the work itself is done on screen and the team can be in one virtual room full-time | Day 3 is still a real run of the changed process with the clock on it, not a walkthrough. Floor work does not qualify for a virtual event |
| Two shifts (MFG) | Eligible | The team spans both shifts, the day's close is repeated for the second shift, and the standard work is written by people from both |
| A three-day compression | Eligible | Only where the kit's rule holds: a small segment and physical trials. Not where IT or another department must act inside the week |
| An event in a regulated or clinical area (HC) | Eligible | Trials that touch a regulated record, a medication or a patient go through the organization's own approval route with its own controls, named on the form before the charter is signed |
| An event at a second organization while you work for a first | Eligible through the partner pool | Under the partner agreement, with your employer's awareness where your contract requires it |
| An event you facilitate as an external paid consultant | Eligible only with the client's written approval of the observation and the program's evidence terms | Otherwise not eligible: the observer cannot be in the room, and a gate cannot rest on a record the program may not keep |

## 2. Sourcing the event

### 2.1 At your employer

Most events come from here, and most of them are lost in the ask rather than in the room.

**Ask for the real thing.** "Three to five days, six to nine people, full-time, in weeks 13 to
20, on one segment of one process, with a target you sign." A sponsor who agrees to a vaguer
version has agreed to something else and will discover it on day 2.

**Price it out loud.** Eight people for five days is about 300 working hours, plus backfill
cover, plus your own six weeks around the event. Saying the number at the ask is what makes
the yes real. A sponsor who hears it and still says yes will also sign the backfill.

**Fix the calendar early.** The process owner blocks the days in writing by **week 10**; for
corporate cohorts the deployment playbook
([`../delivery/corporate-deployment-playbook.md`](../delivery/corporate-deployment-playbook.md))
makes the venue a pre-wave gate condition before week 1. A venue confirmed in week 13 is a
venue that will move.

**When release is the obstacle**, and it usually is: use the three-day version; take a segment
with fewer people in it; run across two shifts so no shift loses everyone; or move the event
to the quietest month in the demand profile you already built in Module 7. Shrinking the
segment is always better than shrinking the days.

### 2.2 Through the partner-project pool

Candidates with no employer venue use the program's partner-project pool. How the pool works —
sourcing, screening, matching, the partner agreement, data and safety, and what happens when a
partner project collapses — is written once, at Green Belt, in
[`../../green-belt/project/partner-pool-and-practicum-track.md`](../../green-belt/project/partner-pool-and-practicum-track.md).
Read §2 there. What follows is only what is different for a kaizen venue.

A kaizen venue is a harder ask than a Green Belt project, because it needs people released
full-time rather than an hour a week. The pool therefore screens it against six extra
criteria before it is listed:

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| **Release** | The sponsor confirms in writing, by name, that six to nine people are free of their normal work for the event days, with cover arranged |
| **Frequency** | The process runs daily, or near enough that two weeks of baseline gives at least 20 points and day 5 gives a sample worth reading |
| **A place to work** | A room in or next to the area, with wall space. A conference room across the site is a warning, not a detail |
| **Observation permitted** | Either an observer in the room for the blocks in §4.1, or a recording plan approved by the organization under §4.4 |
| **Change permitted inside the week** | Layout, work sequence, standard work and visual controls may be changed by the team within a stated spend limit |
| **Safety and privacy route named** | The safety induction, and — where relevant — the privacy approver and the works-council or employee-representative consultation, named before matching |

Matching runs at least **six weeks before the event window**, one candidate per event, with
conflicts of interest declared as at Green Belt; a candidate may decline one match with a
reason. Because these venues are scarcer, **declare your intent by week 8** so the coordinator
has five weeks to work with. The organization receives a bounded improvement, a running chart
with a named owner and the standard work its own people wrote; it does not receive a guarantee
of a result, your time after day 90, or a sales approach from the program.

### 2.3 The timeline

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Week 8 | Intent declared with a primary venue and a fallback | Candidate; partner-pool coordinator if no employer venue |
| Week 10 | Venue confirmed in writing; dates on the process owner's calendar; observation mode proposed | Candidate and sponsor |
| Week 11 | T−4 walk done with the process owner; scope drafted; measurement system checked; recording approvals started where video is proposed | Candidate |
| Week 12 | Eligibility form filed and approved; **baseline collection started** — at least two weeks before day 1 | Candidate; practicum owner |
| Weeks 13–20 | The event window | Candidate |
| T−1 week | Pre-brief call with the observer (§3.2) | Candidate and observer |
| Day 5 + 45 days | The day-30 package submitted (§6) | Candidate; process owner signs |

The window closes at week 20 so that the day-30 package lands well inside the project-closure
period. An extension to week 30 is granted by the practicum owner on a venue reason, in
writing. The gate must be complete before the project closes, whenever that is.

### 2.4 When the venue collapses

Written now so that nobody meets these rules for the first time in week 14.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| The sponsor leaves before the charter is signed | The fallback venue named at week 10 is activated. This is why there is a fallback at week 10 and not at week 13 |
| The days are cancelled inside two weeks of day 1 | Reschedule once inside the window. The baseline continues and is **not** restarted — unless the operational definition changed, in which case it starts again and the old data are kept and labeled |
| The team arrives at four people on day 1 | It does not run as a gate event. Say so in the room, run whatever is useful for the organization, and reschedule. Running it with four and calling it an event is the theatre finding wearing a different face |
| A site emergency stops the event on day 3 | Pause. Resume within 10 working days with the same team and the same baseline, or close it honestly as a shorter event and schedule a second. The observer records what happened and nothing is reconstructed afterwards |
| The organization withdraws recording permission after day 1 | Live virtual observation covers the remaining blocks, arranged the same day by the practicum owner. The candidate loses nothing (§4.4) |

## 3. The charter and pre-work you own

The kit is the instrument. This section says which parts of it are **yours** — the parts the
observer will attribute to you and score under Preparation, which is the first of the four
rubric areas and the one most events are lost in.

### 3.1 Yours, not the organization's

| Item | Why it is yours | What the observer sees |
|---|---|---|
| The scope decision | A segment you can walk in 20 minutes, with what is explicitly out | Start step, stop step, the out-of-scope line, on the charter |
| The metric and its operational definition | One metric, defined by reference so that two people counting get the same number | The definition, and the same words on the day-5 sheet and the day-30 page |
| The measurement check | A gauge or timestamp check, done, with n and result — before the baseline means anything | "40 invoices checked, 38 within ± 1 h, the two failures explained and a rule written" |
| The baseline chart | Started at least two weeks before day 1, on the chart the day-30 reading continues | The chart, printed large, on the wall on day 1 |
| The target | Negotiated with the sponsor and signed as a number | "6.4 → ≤ 3.2 working days at day 30", with a signature and a date |
| The decision-rights conversation | Two columns, each "needs whom" row with a named person and a turnaround | The columns on the charter and posted in the room |
| Team composition and backfill | Six to nine, half doing the work, cover agreed by each manager in writing | The team list; three managers' written agreements |
| The sponsor's brief | Three appearances; day 3 will look worse than day 1; the design is the team's | The sponsor behaving that way on days 1, 2 and 5 |
| The just-in-time training | Thirty minutes maximum, on the two or three tools day 2 needs | A timed block that ends on time |
| The observation plan | The mode, the approvals, the consent announcements | §4.4's approvals, dated before the charter |

The organization provides the people, the room, data access, the safety induction and the
privacy approval route. Everything in the table above is yours, and an observer who cannot
tell which is which has found the finding.

### 3.2 The pre-brief call (T−1 week, 30 minutes)

Five business days before day 1 you send the observer: the signed charter; a picture of the
baseline chart with its chart type, window, n and definition; the measurement-check result;
the team list with reporting lines; the backfill agreements; the decision-rights columns; the
observation plan with its approvals; the day-by-day agenda; and the one-page trial safety
check.

The observer confirms that eligibility still stands, or names the condition to fix. **A
pre-brief that finds no charted baseline stops the event as a gate event** — a week out, while
the organization can still use the days for something it chose, and before it spends 300
hours on a record that could not be scored. That is the entire reason the call sits a week
out and not on Sunday night.

### 3.3 The baseline standard

At least two weeks, at least 20 points where the process runs daily, on the chart the day-30
reading will continue — an I-MR of a daily median, an X̄-R, or a p chart, chosen for the data
as Module 6 taught. Read stability before the charter is signed: a special cause inside the
baseline window is either explained in writing or the window is extended. Control limits come
from the process; the target comes from the sponsor, and it is never drawn as a control limit.

Software parity, briefly: Minitab **Stat > Control Charts**; Excel with the stats add-in or a
plotted-limits template where the limits are computed in the sheet and not eyeballed; R
`qcc::qcc()`; Python with limits computed explicitly and plotted. Where the organization's own
system produces the chart, write down the formula it uses — several business-intelligence
tools draw ± 3 standard deviations of all the data, which is not a control limit.

**Data ethics, stated once and enforced at §5.2.** The operational definition is fixed before
day 1 and is the same on day 5 and at day 30. A point is never removed because it is
inconvenient. A baseline is never reconstructed from memory, from a second system, or from
"what we know it usually is". If the only data you have is an estimate, you do not have a
baseline yet — you have four more weeks of work before this event.

## 4. How the event is observed

Two modes, one standard. In person is the default and is better; video-review exists because
a candidate in Manitoba running an event in a plant two time zones from the nearest observer
should not be denied the gate for geography.
[`../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md`](../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md)
§4.5 sets who may observe: the instructor or a bench Master Black Belt in person, or **two**
observers for a video review. Your coach may attend the report-out as a guest and does not
score.

### 4.1 Observed in person by a Master Black Belt

The observer does not attend all five days. They attend the blocks where facilitation is
visible, and they debrief you at the end of each day they attend.

| Block | Coverage | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1, kick-off (30 min) | **Required** | The sponsor states the target and the decision rights; you set the rules of the room. Both are scored under Preparation and Facilitation |
| Day 1, the walk and current state | Recommended, ≥ 45 min | Whether the current state comes from observation or from the routing |
| Day 2, one analysis block (≥ 90 min) **including the sponsor check-in** | **Required** | The check-in is where a sponsor solves from the doorway and where a leader either protects the team or joins in |
| Day 3, the trial block (≥ 2 h) | **Required** | At least one full try-adjust cycle, with the clock on it |
| Day 4, standard work | Recommended, ≥ 45 min | Who holds the pen |
| Day 5, verification through report-out | **Required**, the whole morning and the readout | The comparison, the 30-day list handover and who presents |
| End of each attended day | **Required**, 15 min | The observer's debrief with you. It is feedback during, not a verdict after |

That is roughly eight observed hours out of forty. The observer also reads the wall — the
newspaper, the baseline chart, the yamazumi, the 30-day list — at the end of each attended
day, and photographs it with the organization's permission under §4.4.

**What the observer does not do.** Facilitate. Answer a team member's question. Suggest a
countermeasure. Rescue you when a block is going badly. Sit at the table. The observer sits
where they can see the room and takes notes; if a team member asks them something, the answer
is "ask your leader".

### 4.2 Video-reviewed

Permitted when live observation is not possible, with the practicum owner's approval on the
eligibility form and the organization's written approval under §4.4. Two observers score
independently and reconcile; both signatures go on the record, and a pass/redo disagreement is
discussed until it resolves.

The clips are **unedited and continuous**. The camera is on the room, not on you.

| Clip | Content | Length | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Day 1 kick-off, from the sponsor's first word | Whole block | Continuous; the sponsor and the room both audible |
| V2 | The wall at the end of day 1 | 3 min | A slow pan: baseline chart, newspaper, current state. Nobody narrates |
| V3 | Day 2 analysis, in a window the **observer names** the evening before | 45 min continuous | See the anti-selection rule below |
| V4 | The day-2 sponsor check-in | Whole block | Continuous |
| V5 | Day 3 trial, window named by the observer | 30 min continuous, including one full try-adjust cycle | A clock or timer visible, or the times called aloud |
| V6 | Day 4, standard work being written | 15 min | Whoever is holding the pen is in frame |
| V7 | Day 5 verification | Whole block | The sample being timed or read, and the arithmetic as it goes on the wall |
| V8 | Day 5 report-out | Whole block | Unedited, including the sponsor's questions |
| V9 | The 30-day list closed and accepted by the process owner | Whole block | The list legible, or photographed immediately after |
| Stills | Baseline chart on day 1 and day 5; the newspaper at each day's close; the standard work sheets; the visual control | — | Time-stamped by the device |

**The anti-selection rule.** You do not choose the day-2 and day-3 windows. The observer names
the clock times the evening before, after you send the next day's agenda. This is the single
device that separates a video review from a highlight reel, and it is why the video mode is
scored as equivalent to live observation rather than as a lesser version of it.

**Audio is the constraint, not video.** A room camera on a tripod at the back plus a second
recorder on the table will do. A lapel microphone on the leader alone will not: the criteria
under Safety of the room are about who speaks, and a clip in which only you can be heard fails
to evidence them. If the observers cannot hear the team, the clip does not count and the block
is unobserved.

Clips go to the program's assessment store within two business days, through the upload link
the practicum owner issues. Not to a personal cloud account, not by message, not on a phone
that leaves the site with the only copy.

### 4.3 What must be captured either way

Mode changes how these arrive, not whether they are required.

| Evidence | In person | Video-reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| The signed charter, decision rights, backfill agreements | Sent at T−1 week | Same |
| The baseline chart as it stood when the charter was signed | Photographed at kick-off | Still, day 1 |
| The kick-off, in full | Attended | V1 |
| One day-2 analysis block and the sponsor check-in | Attended | V3, V4 |
| One full try-adjust cycle on day 3 | Attended | V5 |
| Who wrote the standard work | Attended or photographed | V6 |
| The day-5 verification arithmetic | Attended | V7 |
| The report-out, and who presented | Attended | V8 |
| The 30-day list, accepted by the process owner | Attended | V9 |
| The attendance sheet, per day, with hours | Collected | Collected |
| The newspaper at each day's close | Photographed | Stills |
| The day-30 package (§6) | Submitted | Submitted |

### 4.4 Consent and privacy for recording in a workplace

A kaizen event happens in somebody's workplace, among people who did not enrol in anything.
The program's rule is stricter than the lowest legal bar anywhere it operates, and it is not
negotiable by a candidate who is short of time.

**1 · The organization decides, not you.** Recording is approved in writing by the sponsor
**and** by whoever the organization says also has to approve it: privacy or data-protection
officer, HR, legal, site security, and — where they exist — the works council, union or
employee representatives, consulted before the charter is signed rather than told afterwards.
The approval names what may be recorded, where, and what happens to it. It goes on the
eligibility form. No approval, no recording, and the mode changes to live observation.

**2 · Everyone in frame consents, in writing, whatever the local rule says.** Some
jurisdictions need every person's consent to record; some need only one party's. The program
requires every person's, everywhere. Consent is given on the attendance sheet, per day, with a
box to decline.

**3 · Declining costs nobody anything.** Anyone may decline without giving a reason, at any
point, including after they have already agreed. The block then runs with them seated out of
frame, or audio-only, or not recorded at all — and an unrecorded block is covered by a live
virtual observer or simply goes unobserved. Nobody is moved off the team, asked to justify
themselves, or asked again by you. A team member who declines is not your problem to solve;
they are the reason the alternative modes exist.

**4 · Announce it every time.** At the kick-off, out loud, with the recorder visible and a
sign on the door. Again at the start of every recorded block, including after each break. "We
are recording this block for my certification. Two assessors will watch it. Nobody outside
that sees it. Say the word and it stops."

**5 · Never recorded.** Patients, customers, clients, visitors or anyone who is not a
consenting participant. Any screen showing live personal data — a patient record, a customer
file, an employee file, a payroll or HR system. Any document, board or label carrying names or
identifiers. Badges, ID photos, vehicle plates. A person's own desk or screen while they work.
A safety incident involving a person. If one of these comes into frame, the recording stops
and that clip is deleted before anything is uploaded; the deletion is noted on the record and
does not count against you.

**6 · Healthcare (HC) has its own floor.** No patient-facing area, no clinical area during
care, and no protected health information on any wall, screen, whiteboard or paper in frame.
Whiteboards with patient names are photographed only after the names are gone. The go-see walk
in a clinical area is done **without a camera** and is evidenced by your written observation
sheets and time studies instead — a loss the rubric accounts for by scoring what you observed,
not what was filmed. The organization's privacy officer approves the plan before the charter
is signed, and their conditions override anything in this document that is looser.

**7 · Equipment and accounts are the program's.** The practicum owner issues the upload link.
You do not record on a personal phone into a personal account, and you do not keep a copy.

**8 · Retention.** Clips are held in the assessment store, accessible to the two scoring
observers and the assessment lead only. They are never shown to the cohort, never used for
teaching or marketing, and never attached to a credential — the observation record is an
assessment record, not an evidence item
([`../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md`](../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md)).
They are destroyed twelve months after the credential decision, or within 30 days of the event
if the organization's approval requires it, with a certificate of destruction on request.

**9 · When nothing may leave the site.** Some organizations will permit observation but not a
copy. Then the mode is **live virtual observation**: two observers join on the organization's
own platform for the blocks in §4.1, score in real time, and nothing is stored. This is a
normal mode, not a downgrade.

**10 · A refusal is never your failure.** If the organization will not permit recording and no
observer can travel, the practicum owner arranges live virtual observation. If none of the
three is possible inside the window, the event runs for the organization's benefit and your
gate moves to a second event or a partner-pool venue — with no score recorded and **no redo
recorded**, because nothing was assessed. An unobservable venue is a scheduling fact, not a
performance.

### 4.5 Observer conduct and the daily debrief

The observer's debrief happens at the end of each attended day, takes fifteen minutes, and
names what was seen rather than what it implies: "you answered four questions in that block
that a team member could have answered — here they are, with the times". In a video review the
debrief happens after V3 and V5 are uploaded, by call, inside 24 hours, for the same reason:
feedback on day 2 can change day 3, and feedback after day 5 cannot change anything.

The observer scores. The observer does not coach, does not facilitate, and does not tell you
what the team should do. If you ask them mid-block, the answer is the one they give the team:
"that is yours."

## 5. The facilitation rubric

Four areas, as published in the credential document: **preparation · facilitation · safety of
the room · closure**. Closure is read twice — at day 5 and again at day 30 — because an event
is not complete on Friday.

Every criterion is observable: the observer marks what they saw or read, not what they believe
you know. **Pass = every "must" criterion observed.** A "should" not observed is written on the
sheet as feedback and does not fail the event on its own; **three or more unobserved "should"
criteria trigger a redo of the facilitation** (§7.2).

### 5.1 Criteria

| Area | Must — observed as | Should — observed as |
|---|---|---|
| **Preparation** (read at the pre-brief and on day 1) | A charter signed at T−2 weeks or earlier with a numeric target and the sponsor's signature · a baseline of ≥ 2 weeks on the right chart, with window, n and chart type, on the operational definition that day 5 and day 30 will use · the measurement check done, with n and result · decision rights in two columns, each "needs whom" row naming a person and a turnaround · a team of 6–9 with ≥ half doing the work, none reporting to the candidate · backfill named per member and agreed by their manager in writing · the observation plan with the organization's approvals | Current-state observation: ≥ 10 timed cycles per step, WIP counted on two days, 12 weeks of demand · the just-in-time training prepared to ≤ 30 minutes on two or three tools · the sponsor briefed on the three appearances and told that day 3 will look worse than day 1 · the room in or beside the area, with the baseline chart printed large |
| **Facilitation** (days 1–4) | The team produces the future state and writes the standard work — the observer records who held the marker and the pen, and the answer is not the candidate · every asserted number is challenged for its basis at least once per observed block ("did you see that, or were you told it?") · anything outside the segment goes on the newspaper in the room, visibly, and the count is read at the report-out · the sponsor's check-in ends with a finding and a direction, and a countermeasure proposed from the doorway is routed to the team · at least one countermeasure that needs somebody else goes onto the 30-day list with an owner and a date **during** the event, not into "done" · at least half of day 3's working hours spent on try-adjust cycles, with two cycles timed | Timeboxes held, with the overruns named rather than absorbed · questions handed back to the room rather than answered · silence used after a question instead of filled · the day's close states what is known and what is not · the newspaper reviewed daily, not at the end · the just-in-time training ends on time |
| **Safety of the room** | Every person speaks in each observed block, or the candidate acts to make it possible (a round, pairs, written cards) — the observer counts by seat · when a delay or defect traces to somebody's step, it is restated as a design question, and the observer records the words used · the person who does a step has the last word on the standard work for that step · every trial is risk-checked on the one-page trial safety check before it runs, and the area's own safety rules hold throughout · trials touching a patient, a live customer interaction or a regulated record run only under the organization's written approval and its own controls · the recording conditions of §4.4 held: the announcement made, nobody recorded who declined | Seating and wall arranged so that everyone can see and reach · a dissenting voice is asked to say more, not managed · breaks taken on the hour they were promised · a team member whose idea is dropped is told why, in the room, on the day |
| **Closure — day 5** | Verification on the **same metric and the baseline's operational definition**, with sample size and window stated · the comparison sentence names its own limit ("2.1 days on a 40-invoice sample; the 30-day chart will confirm or not") · guardrails checked and reported · the 30-day list at ≤ 10 items, every item with a named owner and a date, accepted by the process owner in the room · the team presents the report-out and the candidate does not present the result · the chart handed over with a named plotter and the first weekly review already on a calendar | What did not work is on the report-out page · the off-shift or the people not in the room are trained, or their training is on the list with a date · the backfill release is on the list · the observer's debrief is taken without defending |
| **Closure — day 30** (§6) | The chart continued on the same definition, with the event marked as a stage, plotted by the named owner · the honest comparison written against both the baseline and the target, whichever way it went · every list item closed, or re-owned with a reason and a new date, with the escalation made to the person named in the decision-rights row · the process owner's own sentence, in their words and dated, about what they now own | Day 60 and day 90 readings scheduled · one standard-work audit of five observed cycles booked · guardrail metrics read again · what the candidate would do differently, written before reading the observer's sheet |

### 5.2 What fails regardless of the rest

- **Theatre.** No charted baseline of at least two weeks before day 1 on the day-5 definition,
  or a result announced from a sample whose size and window are not stated. The evaluation plan
  publishes the count of events stopped this way, because it is the cheapest honesty signal the
  level has ([`../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md`](../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md)).
- **A changed operational definition.** The definition differs between the baseline, the day-5
  sample and the day-30 chart; or the baseline was reconstructed from memory or pulled from a
  different system than the one that produces the day-30 points. This is the data-ethics
  standard from Module 7 and it ends the event as a gate event with a note to the assessment
  lead.
- **Points dropped for their value.** A slow changeover, a long discharge, a late invoice
  removed from the day-5 sample or the 30-day chart because it was "not typical".
- **An event that did not happen as recorded.** Dates, attendance or hours that do not match
  the sheet. This is an integrity referral under the terms of certification, handled by the
  assessment lead and not by the observer.
- **Recording without approval**, over a participant's refusal, or onto a personal device or
  account.
- **A hazard allowed to run.** A trial the candidate let start without the risk check, or
  continued after somebody raised a safety concern; or a trial on a patient, a live customer
  or a regulated record without the organization's approval.
- **Attendance made a condition.** A team member kept in the room after asking to leave, or
  told by the candidate that participation affects anything at all.

The first three are about the record. The last four are about people. Both kinds end the
event's score, and only the fourth carries an integrity process.

### 5.3 Observer questions (ask at least two; record which)

1. "Show me the baseline chart as it stood the day the charter was signed. What is the
   operational definition, and is it word for word the one on the day-5 sheet?"
2. "Which countermeasure did the team choose that you would not have chosen — and what did you
   do about that?"
3. "Who has not spoken since the break? What will you do about it in the next block?"
4. "Item 3 needs the change board. Who owns it, what is the date, and what happens on the day
   after that date?"
5. "Your day-5 number is a sample. What sample, over what window, and what would make it
   wrong?"
6. "What is on the newspaper that the sponsor has not seen yet, and when will they?"
7. "Name a number on that wall you have not checked yourself. Check it now."
8. "If the process owner moves to another role next month, what survives?"

### 5.4 The sheet, the debrief and the record

The observer completes the rubric sheet on the day and gives it to the candidate the same day,
as at the other practica. In a video review the sheet follows within five business days of the
last upload, and the two observers' sheets are reconciled into one signed record.

The record holds: the eligibility form; the pre-brief note; the rubric sheet with each must
criterion marked observed or not, and where; the observer questions asked and the answers; the
day-30 package and its acceptance; and both signatures. It goes to the candidate, to the
reviewer of record's file, and into the audit sample. It is not evidence on the credential and
it is never published — it contains other people's workplace.

## 6. The 30-day follow-up that makes it complete

The event ends on Friday. The requirement does not. An event scored on the report-out alone
would certify five days of energy, and five days of energy is the easiest thing in Lean to
produce and the least useful thing to certify. What the gate certifies is that you can hand a
changed process to somebody else and have it still be changed a month later — or find out
honestly that it is not.

### 6.1 The day-30 package

Submitted within **45 days of day 5**, on one page plus its attachments, signed by the process
owner. The kit's Part 5 has the forms; this is what the observer accepts against.

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| **The chart** | The baseline's chart continued, same definition, same subgroup rule, with the event marked as a stage boundary and limits recalculated from the new stage only when there are enough points to justify it. Plotted by the owner named in the charter, not by you |
| **The reading** | The day-30 value against both the baseline and the signed target, in the metric's units, with the window and n. Both numbers, whichever way it went |
| **The guardrails** | Each guardrail metric read again with its own basis. A primary metric that improved while a guardrail worsened is a finding, not a footnote |
| **The 30-day list** | Every item closed with its date, or re-owned with a reason, a new date and the escalation you made to the person named in the decision-rights row — with the date you made it |
| **The weekly reviews** | Four dated notes, ten lines each: who attended, what moved, what was escalated |
| **The process owner's sentence** | In their own words, dated and signed: what they now own, and what they will do when the chart signals. Not drafted by you |
| **Your honest paragraph** | What the event got, what it did not, and what you would do differently — written before you read the observer's sheet |

### 6.2 The honest-comparison rule

**An event whose metric did not move can pass this gate. An event whose gains were never
measured cannot.** These are not the same thing and the rubric is built so that they are never
confused.

*Worked example (HC), numbers illustrative and in the observed range.* A five-day event on an
outpatient pharmacy: segment from prescription received in the dispensary to bag ready on the
collection shelf. Baseline 41 minutes, daily median from dispensing-system timestamps, I-MR
over three weeks, 15 points, stable. Target signed by the pharmacy director: ≤ 25 minutes at
day 30. Day 5 gives 24 minutes on a timed sample of 30 prescriptions across one afternoon.

At day 30 the daily median is **33 minutes** over 21 working days on the continued I-MR chart,
with no rule violation after day 6. That is better than 41, worse than 24, and short of 25.
The day-30 page says exactly that, and then says why: the day-5 sample ran on an afternoon
with two pharmacists on the bench, and the standard work assumes two; on eleven of the
twenty-one days there was one. The countermeasure that would close the gap — a second bench
pharmacist between 10:00 and 14:00 — is a staffing decision that was outside the team's
decision rights, is item 2 on the 30-day list, is owned by the pharmacy director with a
day-45 date, and was escalated on day 18 when it slipped.

That package passes. Every must criterion under Closure is observable in it: the same chart,
the same definition, both numbers, the guardrail (dispensing errors, unchanged at zero on the
period), the list with owners and dates, the escalation, the owner's sentence. The candidate
did not extrapolate the 24-minute sample, did not quietly restate the target, and did not
present the afternoon sample as the result.

A package that reported "24 minutes, a 41% reduction" and stopped there would fail — not
because the number is wrong but because nothing verified it, which is the second required
failure mode in §8.

### 6.3 Day 60 and day 90

Not required for this gate; the gate closes at day 30. They are required when the event is
also carrying rubric items C1 or C2 on your capstone, where the control plan must be live for
at least 60 days at submission. Book them anyway: the kit's cadence has the chart read again
at day 60 and day 90 and one standard-work audit of five observed cycles, and the drift you
find at day 90 is the most useful thing the event will teach you.

## 7. Complete, redo, and what is neither

### 7.1 What complete means

All four of these, in order:

1. The eligibility form was approved **before** the event, and the event as run matched it.
2. Every **must** criterion in Preparation, Facilitation and Safety of the room was observed.
3. Every **must** criterion in Closure — day 5 was observed, and the day-30 package was
   accepted within 45 days of day 5.
4. The record is signed — by the observer and the practicum owner in person, or by both
   observers in a video review.

The gate is then recorded as `kaizenFacilitation: completed` with the month, the vertical and
the observation mode. Nothing about redos, scores or the organization goes on the credential.

### 7.2 The three kinds of redo

A redo is a second attempt, not a failure. In order of how often they happen:

| Redo of | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| **The day-30 package** | The event was facilitated to standard, but the follow-up evidence is incomplete: the chart stopped, list items have owners but no dates, the comparison is missing one of its two numbers, or the owner's sentence was written by the candidate | Return with a complete package at day 60 on the same event. No second event. If the chart stopped because the organization stopped plotting, you reconstruct nothing — you show what exists, what you asked for and when, and the package is scored on what was yours to control |
| **The facilitation** | A must criterion under Facilitation or Safety of the room was not observed — most often the candidate held the marker, or day 3 was not protected — while preparation and the event itself were sound. Also triggered by three or more unobserved "should" criteria | A second event within 120 days, with a different observer. It may be a three-day event on a smaller segment. It is not a re-scoring of the first event's video |
| **The event** | Theatre; a data-ethics stop; an ineligible event approved on an incomplete form; an event that did not happen as recorded | A new event at a new venue — the partner pool where the employer has none — with a different observer. Where the trigger was integrity, the assessment lead reviews the case under the terms of certification before anything is scheduled |

Two redos of the same kind is the limit before the assessment lead reviews the case, as at the
DOE practicum ([`doe-practicum.md`](doe-practicum.md) §5.4). Redo turnaround is tracked: the
median from decision to redo should be 21 days or fewer, and the practicum owner schedules it
before you leave the debrief.

### 7.3 What is not a redo

| Situation | Why it passes |
|---|---|
| **The metric did not move, or moved less than the target**, and the day-30 page says so with both numbers and a reason | Honest failure passes at every gate in this program. The rubric asks whether you led an event, not whether the process cooperated |
| **The organization did not close the 30-day list** | You show the four weekly reviews with dates, the escalation to the person named in the decision-rights row, and the state of each open item. The gate scores your facilitation and follow-through. An organization's execution is not misconduct and is explicitly not grounds for anything against the credential |
| **The sponsor cancelled day 4** for an operational reason, and the team ran a compressed close with the verification and the list done | Recorded as it happened. The observer scores what was observable in the days that ran |
| **A countermeasure was tried on day 3 and reversed on day 4**, and it is on the report-out page under "what did not work" | That is the try-adjust cycle working. An event with nothing under "what did not work" is the one that invites a question |
| **A team member was withdrawn on day 2** by their manager after backfill was agreed in writing, and you escalated the same day | Your escalation is the evidence. A team of seven finishing is an event; a team of four starting is not (§2.4) |
| **Recording was refused and no observer could travel** inside the window (TXN — a claims cohort whose sole venue was a client site under a no-recording clause) | Nothing was assessed, so nothing is scored. The gate moves to a second event or a pool venue, with **no redo recorded** (§4.4, rule 10) |

### 7.4 Records and what is never published

The observation record, the clips and the day-30 package are assessment records. They are
held, accessed and destroyed under §4.4 rule 8. The verification page shows the observation
mode and nothing else; it never shows whether a gate needed a redo, never names the
organization unless the holder chose that evidence option and the organization consented, and
never shows anything from the room.

## 8. Failure modes

Every one of these traces to a design decision that made sense to somebody at the time.

**1 · The event that was really a training session (TXN).** A shared-services team blocks
three days for a "kaizen". Day 1 is a lean overview. Day 2 is a value-stream mapping exercise
on a printed case. Day 3 the team maps its own onboarding process and produces twenty-two
improvement ideas on sticky notes, which are photographed and typed up. Nothing was tried.
Nothing in the area is different on Monday. No metric was measured before or after.

*Why it happens:* this is the easiest thing to get approved. Releasing eight people for
training is a line item every organization knows how to sign; releasing eight people to change
the process is a different conversation with a different person. The room feels productive,
the feedback forms are excellent, and a list of twenty-two ideas looks like output.

*How the observer sees it:* the charter has no signed numeric target; the baseline is an
estimate; the day-3 agenda says "prioritize" where it should say "try"; the day-5 page has no
sample.

*What you do instead:* ask the eligibility question at T−4 weeks, while the plan can still
change — **"what will be physically different in that area on Friday at 16:00, and which
number will say so?"** If the honest answer is "the team will understand the process better",
you are planning a good training session. Hold it, then charter an event on one segment of the
process it used. Both things are worth doing. Only one of them is requirement 4.

**2 · The event whose gains were never verified (MFG).** A five-day changeover event on a
packaging line. Baseline 47 minutes per changeover, from the changeover log, 22 changeovers
over four weeks. Day 5: the team runs one changeover in 22 minutes, with the three people who
designed the new sequence doing it and the event leader holding the stopwatch. The report-out
says "changeover time halved". Everyone applauds. Nobody plots a changeover again.

*Why it happens:* the event's energy ends on Friday afternoon and the chart is somebody's
Monday morning. The plotter was never named, the chart was the candidate's spreadsheet rather
than the log the area already keeps, and the one number was true — it just was not evidence of
anything repeatable.

*How it reads in the rubric:* the Closure — day 5 must criterion is met only when the sample
and window are stated, so "halved" from n = 1 fails it on day 5, before day 30 is even
reached. At day 30 there is nothing to read.

*What you do instead:* the plotter is named in the charter, not chosen on Friday. The chart is
the baseline's chart continued, which usually means the area's own log with limits added rather
than a new artifact nobody owns. The first weekly review is on a calendar before day 5 ends.
And the day-5 sentence states its limit out loud: "22 minutes on one changeover run by three
of the eight people who do it; the 30-day chart will confirm or not." A day-30 median of 31
minutes against a 47-minute baseline, on the same chart, is a better result than "halved" —
because it is a result.

**3 · The event in your own area (MFG or TXN).** The easiest venue to arrange is the one you
control, and it is the only one that cannot be used. Ineligible under §1.1, and the reason is
★ L1: the credential claims you can lead people you do not manage.

**4 · The sponsor who becomes the facilitator.** A sponsor who stays past the kick-off, or
arrives on day 3 with a design, is doing what has always worked for them. The countermeasure
is the T−2 week brief, in person, naming their three appearances and saying that day 3 will
look worse than day 1. When it happens anyway, route it: "that is exactly the decision the
team is on now — can they bring it to you at the check-in?"

**5 · Scope growth on day 2.** The segment is the segment. Everything else goes on the
newspaper, visibly, and the sponsor sees the newspaper at the report-out. A team that widens
the scope on Tuesday finishes nothing on Friday, and the observer will see it in the day-5
verification that never happened.

**6 · Consent arranged on Wednesday.** A candidate decides on day 2 to film the report-out,
asks the room, and gets nods. That is not approval: the organization has not approved,
individual consent was not recorded, and somebody nodded because their manager was watching.
Recording approval is a pre-work item with a named approver (§4.4), obtained before the
charter is signed. A Wednesday decision means there is no video, and the mode becomes live
virtual observation if anything can still be arranged.

**7 · The video that cannot be heard.** A room camera at the back with the leader on a lapel
microphone produces forty minutes of one voice. The Safety of the room criteria are about who
speaks; a clip that evidences only the candidate evidences the opposite of what it was meant
to. Two audio sources, tested on day 1 before the kick-off, on the same recording you will
upload.

**8 · The candidate who chooses the clips.** Left to choose, everybody sends the block that
went well. That is why the observer names the day-2 and day-3 windows the evening before
(§4.2). Candidates who know this in week 8 prepare differently, and that is the intended
effect.

**9 · The observer who facilitates.** A Master Black Belt watching a team go in circles for
twenty minutes will want to help, and helping ends the assessment. Observers are calibrated on
this specifically (§9). If the observer speaks to the team, the block is noted as compromised
and is not scored.

**10 · The 30-day list as a wish list.** Forty items, no owners, no dates, and a name at the
top that is yours. Ten at most, each owned by somebody in the organization, each with a date,
accepted out loud by the process owner in the room on day 5. The rest is a newspaper item or a
project, and saying so in the room is part of the job.

**11 · A team of watchers.** Six supervisors, two analysts and one clerk. The people who do
the work are the ones who know where the time goes, and a team that is mostly observers will
design the process it believes exists. At least half the team does the work, every time.

**12 · Backfill agreed verbally.** "We'll cover it" in a corridor becomes two people missing
on Tuesday afternoon. Each manager agrees in writing, naming who covers. This is also the
evidence that earns ★ L1 in the capstone: three managers' written agreements are exactly what
"led people they do not manage" looks like on paper.

## 9. Logistics

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Observer assignment** | Assigned by the practicum owner when the eligibility form is approved. The instructor or a bench Master Black Belt in person; two calibrated observers for a video review or live virtual observation. Never the candidate's coach, and never their capstone reviewer of record |
| **Calibration** | Observers score the kaizen anchor set — three recorded events, one pass, one facilitation redo, one theatre stop — before their first assessment and annually. Any disagreement on a must criterion is re-calibrated. Disagreement rates per cohort are published in the evaluation plan |
| **Travel and cost** | In-person observation inside the program's standard travel radius is included; beyond it the candidate chooses video review or live virtual observation at no cost, and this choice is never recorded against them |
| **Equipment** | Program-issued recorder or the organization's own, a tripod, two audio sources, and the upload link. Stills may be taken on the candidate's device and are uploaded and deleted the same day |
| **Scheduling load** | One observer covers at most two events in a week and never two in the same week as an immersion. Events cluster in weeks 13–20 and the practicum owner levels them across the bench, which is why intent is declared in week 8 |
| **Accessibility** | A candidate who cannot walk the floor for extended periods runs the walk with a team member paired to them and the observation sheets remain theirs; the standard is what they facilitated, not how far they walked. Standing-only facilitation is never a criterion. Room, wall height and seating are arranged so that every team member can see, reach and write — this is scored under Safety of the room, for the team as well as the candidate. Virtual and hybrid rooms carry captions and a written newspaper that everyone can edit |
| **Language** | An event may run in any language the candidate and team share; the observer is matched to it, or an interpreter agreed in advance sits with the observer and not with the team |
| **Records** | The rubric sheet to the candidate the same day (five business days for a video review); the signed record to the reviewer of record's file; clips and packages retained and destroyed under §4.4 rule 8; the file enters the annual audit sample |
| **Evaluation** | Completion and pass rates, the theatre check and redo reasons are tracked per cohort in [`../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md`](../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md); a completion rate below target is read as a venue problem first, by venue type, before anything is read as a candidate problem |

## Takeaways

- An event is three to five full days, on a real process, with a sponsor who signs a number
  and a team you do not manage. Everything else is a good meeting.
- The gate is decided in the six weeks around the five days: a charted baseline, written
  decision rights and named backfill before; a chart with a named plotter and a closed list
  after.
- Observed and video-reviewed are the same standard. The observer names the clip windows, so
  there is no highlight reel to assemble.
- Recording happens on the organization's approval and every participant's consent, or it does
  not happen — and a venue that will not be recorded costs the candidate nothing.
- A metric that did not move can pass. A gain that was never measured cannot.
