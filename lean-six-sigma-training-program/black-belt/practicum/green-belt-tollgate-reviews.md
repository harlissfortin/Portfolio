# Requirement 5 — Two Green Belt Tollgate Reviews (closure period · scored pass / redo)

The fifth certification gate ([`../README.md`](../README.md)) is the only one where you are
assessed on what you do to somebody else's project. You sit twice in the reviewer's chair at a
running Green Belt cohort's tollgates, under the supervision of that cohort's reviewer of
record, and you are scored on the record you write, the questions you ask, the silence you
keep and the feedback you produce.

Module 8 §8.6 ([`../modules/m8-leading-change-and-coaching.md`](../modules/m8-leading-change-and-coaching.md))
taught the chair and Lab 16 calibrated you in it. The standard you review *to* is the Green
Belt's own — [`../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md`](../../green-belt/project/tollgate-checklists.md)
for the gate and [`../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md`](../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md)
for the items. The standard you are *held to* is this document. The scheduling, conflict and
comparison rules come from the Black Belt tollgate standard's requirement 5 section
([`../project/tollgate-standard.md`](../project/tollgate-standard.md)); this file is where they
are worked out in full, with the rubric that scores you.

**The role has a name in the Green Belt system: second reader**
([`../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md`](../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md)
§1). A second reader scores the same one-pager independently, compares afterward, signs
nothing, and contacts the learner only in the room. Everything below follows from those four
words.

**Outcomes.** By the end of the requirement, the candidate can, observed:

1. Mark every Green Belt rubric item due at a tollgate *evidenced*, *at risk* or *not
   evidenced* against the published rubric, writing one sentence per mandatory (★) item that
   names the evidence or its absence.
2. Ask a question that serves the record and the sponsor's decision, and stop talking — without
   proposing a method, a chart, a countermeasure or a form of words.
3. Write the data-ethics line, the coaching flag and the Black Belt invitation in the reviewer's
   format, live, in the room.
4. Produce tollgate feedback in the Green Belt rubric's feedback format inside two business
   days: two strengths, each ★ item's status in a sentence, the single highest-leverage fix
   first.
5. Reconcile their marks against a calibrated reviewer's item by item, and name the sentence,
   exhibit or absence on the page that decided each mandatory item.
6. Record an item as not evidenced when the page does not carry it, on a project whose Green
   Belt has worked hard and whose sponsor wants to proceed.

---

## 1. What the requirement is, and what it is not

| | Requirement 5 — the reviewer's chair | L3 — coaching (rubric item) |
|---|---|---|
| What you do | Mark items against a rubric; record; ask | Ask questions; develop a person over time |
| What you produce | A record and feedback in the reviewer's format | A dated coaching log: who, which checkpoint, what they decided |
| Who reads it | Your supervising reviewer, and your own package appendix | Your reviewer of record, as L3 evidence |
| Who it is about | A Green Belt you have never coached and will not coach | A team member, a Yellow Belt or a Green Belt you coach |
| Where it is logged | The requirement 5 log (§11) | The coaching log, a different appendix heading |

The two are scored apart because they are opposite jobs, and the program separates them the way
it separates every coach from every assessor: **the coach says how to earn it; the reviewer says
whether it was earned.** You may not sit as reviewer for a Green Belt you coach or have coached;
a coaching log naming the Green Belt you are scheduled to review is a hold at your own Tollgate
5.

This is not a sixth Black Belt gate and it is not an immersion. It happens in the closure
period, on real gates, on real projects, with real sponsors in the room. Nothing about it is a
simulation, which is why it is the last thing the program lets you do and why there is a
readiness sign-off in front of it.

## 2. Eligibility — what you clear before you sit

Three things gate the chair, in this order.

**1 · The Immersion 2 readiness sign-off.** An uncleared redo from the change-leadership
practicum ([`change-leadership-practicum.md`](change-leadership-practicum.md) §8) postpones the
booking. Requirement 4 and requirement 5 are both performances in somebody else's workplace,
and the program does not send an unready candidate into one.

**2 · Lab 16 calibration.** In Lab 16 you score the case Tollgate 3 package alone, in writing,
against the Green Belt 100-point rubric, and the room compares your marks to the calibrated
reviewer's on the anchor set — a clean pass, a high-scoring fail on an unverified cause, an
honest-failure pass, and a data-ethics stop. You are calibrated when both of these hold:

- **exact agreement on every ★ item** — M3, A3, I3, C1 — earned or unearned, with no split
  difference; and
- **a total within 5 points** of the calibrated reviewer's across the 100 points.

Those are the Green Belt rubric's own calibration tolerances ("Calibration", in
[`../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md`](../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md)),
applied to you because you are scoring to that rubric and not to yours. Every ★ disagreement is
discussed until the room can say **which sentence on the page decided it**, and the resolution is
written down. A candidate who is outside either tolerance re-scores a second anchor package with
the instructor before the cohort closes; you do not sit live until you are inside both.

**3 · A schedule, written at Tollgate 5.** Your two reviews are named on your Tollgate 5 page
(week 16): which Green Belt cohort, which gates, which dates, which supervising reviewer. The
reviews then fall in the closure period, on that cohort's weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8.

Schedule them at Tollgate 5, not at month eight. The nine-month closure window does not move for
a Green Belt calendar that ran out of gates, and both reviews must be passed before you submit
your package for independent review. If the running cohort cannot offer two suitable gates
inside your window, the assessment lead places you on the next cohort — which is a decision
about the calendar, not about you, and it is made faster when you ask in week 16.

## 3. Assignment — which two gates, and who may not review whom

### 3.1 The placement

The assessment lead places you on a running Green Belt cohort and names your **supervising
reviewer**: the reviewer of record for those projects
([`../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md`](../delivery/instructor-reviewer-and-coaching-system.md)
§1). That pairing is deliberate. Because the supervising reviewer is the person who will score
the closed project, their record and yours are of the same page, read against the same rubric,
by someone whose later judgment on that file is already committed.

### 3.2 The two-gate rule

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Two different gates** — two different phases | Four gates ask four different questions; a reviewer who has only recorded a Define gate has not written the hard sentence |
| **At least one at Tollgate 2, 3 or 4**, where a ★ item is due | ★ M3 comes due at Tollgate 2, ★ A3 at Tollgate 3, ★ I3 and ★ C1 in progress at Tollgate 4. Tollgate 1 has no mandatory item, so two Define gates are not two reviews |
| **Two different projects**, where the cohort's calendar allows | Two gates on one project is one project's habits twice |
| Practicum-track projects count | The practicum instructor sits as sponsor ([`../../green-belt/practicum/practicum-track-project.md`](../../green-belt/practicum/practicum-track-project.md)); the gate, the rubric and the conflict rules are unchanged |

### 3.3 Conflict of interest

The Green Belt reviewer bench rules apply to you exactly as to a bench member, plus the two the
chair adds. You may not sit on a gate where any of the following is true.

| Exclusion | Source |
|---|---|
| You coach or have coached this Green Belt — your L3 evidence — or have instructed them at any level in the last 24 months | Green Belt blind-assignment rule; the coach/assessor separation |
| The project is from your own organization, or from a client of it, or you have been employed by or consulted to that organization in the last three years | Green Belt bench rule, applied to the candidate |
| You sit on the corporate account's engagement team, or the cohort is a corporate deployment at your own employer | Corporate cohorts take no reviewer from the client, ever |
| Your own sponsor sponsors this project | Requirement 5, tollgate standard |
| The project claims, or may claim, a benefit row your capstone also claims | You have an interest in their result; the no-double-counting check is not yours to sit over |
| You manage, or are managed by, anyone on the project team | Covered by the organization rule; stated because people forget matrix reporting lines |

Declare a possible conflict before the placement is made, not after. A conflict you are unsure
about is a five-minute message to the assessment lead; a conflict you sat through is a void
review.

**If a conflict surfaces later**, you stop, tell the assessment lead in writing, and a
replacement placement is made. A review you completed before the conflict arose stands and
counts, with the conflict and its date noted in the log; the review you had not yet sat is
re-placed.

**Show (TXN).** A candidate whose capstone runs in a payments processor's collections function
is placed on a Green Belt cohort at a business-process outsourcer, first review booked at
Tollgate 2. Between the placement and the gate, the candidate's employer signs that outsourcer
as a vendor for part of the process in scope. The candidate tells the assessment lead the day
they learn it. The Tollgate 2 review, already sat, stands with the date of the contract noted;
the second review moves to another cohort's Tollgate 4. Nobody is at fault and nothing is
hidden, which is the whole mechanism. Illustrative.

### 3.4 The Green Belt is told, and may decline

At scheduling, the assessment lead tells the Green Belt and their sponsor, by name: that a Black
Belt candidate will sit as second reader under supervision; that the candidate is being
assessed; that the record of file and the score of record remain the supervising reviewer's; and
that they may decline without giving a reason. A decline is not recorded against the Green Belt,
is not mentioned in their file, and moves you to another gate. Consent that cannot be refused is
not consent, and the Green Belt is the one person in the room with nothing to gain from your
certification.

### 3.5 One note on cohort variants

Some Green Belt cohorts run the gate with the reviewer in the room; some run it with the
reviewer reading the one-pager and the sponsor's recorded decision afterwards and returning a
gate note ([`../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md`](../../green-belt/delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md)
§3.3). Requirement 5 is a test of the chair, so the assessment lead places you on gates the
cohort seats a reviewer at. A package-only reading does not satisfy the requirement, however
good the record.

## 4. The reviewer standard you are held to

### 4.1 Their rubric, not yours

The Green Belt mandatory items are **M3, A3, I3 and C1**. Read them first at every gate. They
are different numbers from your own rubric's ★ items (A2, I1, C1, F1, L1), and one number
appears in both and does not mean the same thing.

| Green Belt ★ | Full marks look like | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **M3** Measurement system analysis attempted (6 pts) | Gage R&R or attribute agreement run on the primary metric, the result interpreted in a sentence, and — if the system failed — what was done before the baseline was collected. Or a written reason it does not apply that you, in the chair, accept: a system timestamp whose entry step has been checked counts | "We trust the system." A study run with no acceptance criterion stated beforehand and no action on what it found |
| **A3** Root cause verified with data (10 pts) | A cause tested with a method that fits the data type, assumptions checked and stated, the effect size in its units, and the sentence the Green Belt would tell their sponsor | A p-value alone. A stratum ("unit B is slower") mistaken for a cause. "The team agreed the cause is X" — that is A1 |
| **I3** Before/after evidence (8 pts) | Same metric, same operational definition, same collection method; a chart continued from the baseline or a paired comparison; the result set against the written prediction and read honestly. A documented "it did not work, here is what we learned and did next" can earn full marks | After-data collected a different way. A window that starts at the pilot |
| **C1** Control plan handed to a named owner (7 pts) | Metric, method, frequency, **owner**, response plan; the process owner has signed; **30 days** of live monitoring at submission | An owner without the authority the response plan needs. A frequency and a chart with no name |

Three traps the chair sets for a Black Belt:

- **C1 is 30 days here, not 60.** Yours asks 60 live days; theirs asks 30, with 60 for the
  `sustained` flag at day 90. Marking a Green Belt's C1 short because your own rubric is
  stricter is a rubric error, not rigor.
- **Finance is not mandatory at Green Belt.** C3 is worth 4 points and is not a ★ item. An
  unsigned calculation basis is a real finding and a coaching flag; it is not an unearned
  mandatory item, and you do not import F1.
- **Score the project that was chartered.** Not the one you would have run, and not the
  Black-Belt-sized version of it. Over-scope goes in the feedback; a completed, bounded slice is
  not penalized for being a slice.

### 4.2 The four rules of the chair

**Record; do not coach.** Your questions serve the record and the sponsor's decision. The moment
you name a method, a chart, a countermeasure or a form of words, you have become a second coach
and the gate has no assessor left. The coaching flag carries the topic — "effect size and the
sentence"; "acceptance criteria before a study" — and the Green Belt's own coach picks it up
that week. Lab 16's observer counted every time a reviewer coached from the chair; your
supervising reviewer keeps that count here, and the number that passes is zero.

**The hold is your most useful output.** A hold names one missing item and a date. Most projects
take one and it is not a failure; what the reviewer records on a hold is the item, not a
judgment. Candidates find this the hardest thing in the requirement, and it is the thing the
requirement exists to test: an item is not evidenced because the page does not carry it, not
because the Green Belt did not try.

**You do not decide.** The five decisions — proceed, proceed with conditions, hold, re-scope,
stop — belong to the sponsor. You record what the sponsor decided and every condition with its
date. If the sponsor turns to you and asks what they should do, you answer with what the record
shows and what the rubric asks for, and say that *how* is the coach's conversation.

**Data ethics are recorded, not adjudicated.** Write what you saw: points removed, an
operational definition changed between before and after, a baseline period chosen after the data
were seen, a baseline reconstructed from memory. At a tollgate this is a warning and a
conversation, not a stop; at final review it is a stop. You write the line; the supervising
reviewer's line is the one that goes to the assessment lead the same day.

### 4.3 Before the gate, and in it

**Two business days before.** You read the one-pager ★ items first, with the gate's checklist
page and the Green Belt rubric open. Mark the page in pencil and **date those pre-read marks
before the gate**: the comparison afterwards reads what changed between your pre-read and your
record, and a mark that moved in the room because you listened is evidence in your favor.

**Plan three questions.** Each tied to a rubric item due at this gate, each answerable from the
page or by the Green Belt, each ending in a question mark. A question that begins *have you
thought about* or *what you want to do is* is not a question.

**In the room.** The Green Belt gate is ten minutes of presentation, ten of questions, five for
the decision. Your questions come out of the ten; they are not added to them. You do not answer
a question the sponsor asks the Green Belt. You write your record live, and when you have asked
your three questions you stop — the silence belongs to the sponsor, who is the person the gate
exists for.

## 5. The record you write

The format is the Green Belt tollgate record, and it is the same format your own reviewer uses
on you.

1. **Every rubric item due at this gate**, marked *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced*.
2. **One sentence per ★ item** due or in progress, naming the evidence or its absence.
3. **The sponsor's decision** and every condition, with dates, and who said it.
4. **A data-ethics line** — *none observed*, or the specific concern. Every record has this
   line; an empty space is not a line.
5. **A coaching flag**: a topic, not advice.
6. **The Black Belt invitation** where the cause structure, the cross-functional reach or the
   control need exceeds the Green Belt scope.

**Show (MFG) — a record as written at a Green Belt Tollgate 2.** Cohort 26-GB-04, project
GB-114: label defects on a bearings plant's packaging line. Primary metric: proportion of
pallets released with a label defect, per shift, from the release scan.

| Item due | Mark | The sentence on the record |
|---|---|---|
| M1 Process map | evidenced | Current state observed 14 and 16 Feb with the two packers; lead time and %C&A on working time, basis stated |
| M2 Data collection plan | evidenced | Operational definition unchanged since Tollgate 1; a stranger could collect the same number |
| ★ M3 MSA | **at risk** | Attribute agreement run on the primary metric — 3 appraisers × 30 pallets × 2 repeats, κ = 0.61 against the standard — and interpreted; no acceptance criterion was stated before the study, and nothing was done about the two defect codes that carried the disagreement before the baseline was collected |
| M4 Stability before capability | **at risk** | p chart in time order with the subgrouping rationale; stability stated; the sigma level is quoted against the plant's internal 2% target, which is a target and not the customer's specification limit |
| D2 Target revisited | evidenced | Target revised against the measured baseline; sponsor's initials on the page |

*Decision:* hold, 10 business days, on ★ M3 — the acceptance criterion and the action on the two
codes (production manager, 8 March). Tollgate 3 stays on the calendar.
*Data ethics:* the baseline window begins 1 March; February is excluded for the film-supplier
change. The reason was given in the room and is not written on the page. Recorded: an exclusion
with a stated assignable cause is legitimate; an undocumented one is not auditable.
*Coaching flag:* acceptance criteria before a study; specification source versus internal target.
*Black Belt invitation:* none.

All figures illustrative. Note what is absent from that record: how to fix the kappa, which codes
to merge, what the acceptance criterion should have been. The candidate knew all three. Saying
any of them is a redo.

## 6. The feedback you produce

Within two business days of the gate you write the feedback block, in the Green Belt rubric's
published feedback format:

1. **Two strengths.** Specific, from the page — not "good effort".
2. **Every ★ item's status, one sentence each.** Including the ones not yet due, marked as such.
3. **The single highest-leverage fix, first.** One thing, stated as the next action, not the
   method. "State the acceptance criterion and re-run the two codes before the baseline is
   fixed" is a fix; "use a Kappa of 0.8 and merge codes 4 and 7" is coaching.
4. **The Black Belt invitation**, where the project reveals a Black-Belt-sized problem.

It is addressed as if to the Green Belt and it is delivered to your **supervising reviewer**, not
to the Green Belt. Theirs is the version that reaches the learner. Yours is an assessment
artifact: it goes into the comparison, into your log, and into your package appendix with the
project identified by cohort and project ID and every personal name removed.

Write it as though it were being sent. Feedback written knowing nobody will act on it reads that
way, and the rubric marks it.

## 7. Calibration against your supervising reviewer

### 7.1 The mechanics

The supervising reviewer sits beside you and writes their own record. Theirs is the record of
file and the one the Green Belt receives; yours goes to them.

**You file your record before either of you has seen the other's.** A comparison of two records
written together is a comparison of one record. Your supervising reviewer does not brief you on
what to mark before the gate and does not show you their marks until yours are filed.

Within **2 business days** you compare, item by item — the same two days in which the record of
file reaches the Green Belt, the sponsor and the coach.

### 7.2 The mandatory-item agreement rule

This is the rule the requirement turns on. Say it precisely.

**Every ★ item due or in progress at that gate must carry the same mark on both records —
*evidenced*, *at risk* or *not evidenced*.** The wording may differ; the mark may not. "Broadly
agreed", "nearly the same" and "the same concern in softer words" are disagreements.

- A ★ **disagreement** is discussed until you can both name the **sentence, exhibit or absence on
  the one-pager** that decides the mark, and the resolution is written into the comparison note.
  "I would have been harsher" is not a resolution; "page 1, the capability line names the
  internal target as the specification source" is.
- **The supervising reviewer's mark stands** as the record of file. If the discussion changes
  *their* mind, they amend their own record, dated, and the amendment goes to the Green Belt.
  Your record is annotated with the resolution and is never rewritten to match theirs — a record
  quietly edited into agreement teaches nothing and proves nothing.
- **One resolved ★ disagreement at your first review is expected**, not a redo. It is what the
  chair is for.
- **A redo is triggered** by: two ★ disagreements at a single gate; any ★ disagreement that
  cannot be resolved to a sentence on the page; or the same directional error at both reviews —
  always more generous than the calibrated reviewer, or always harsher. Direction is the signal.
  A candidate who is consistently generous cannot give a hold; a candidate who is consistently
  harsh is scoring the project they would have run.

**Non-★ items** due at the gate are compared too, with a working tolerance: at most one item may
differ by one step (*evidenced* ↔ *at risk*, or *at risk* ↔ *not evidenced*). A two-step
difference on any item, or two or more differing non-★ marks, is written up and is a redo
trigger only if it repeats at the second review.

**Also compared:** the data-ethics line (present, and the same fact recorded), the coaching flag
(a topic on both), the decision and conditions as recorded against what the sponsor actually
said, and the supervising reviewer's **coaching count**.

**Show (HC) — a ★ disagreement and its resolution.** Green Belt Tollgate 3, cohort 26-GB-07,
project GB-131: medication reconciliation completed within 24 hours of admission, three inpatient
units. The page shows a fishbone, a C&E matrix, box plots by unit, and a one-way ANOVA across the
three units: F(2, 87) = 8.4, p < 0.01, with unit B 6.4 hours slower on average, 95% CI 3.1 to
9.7 hours, residuals shown, and four rejected causes displayed. The candidate marks ★ A3
*evidenced* — the method fits, assumptions are checked, the effect size is in hours and there is a
plain-language sentence. The supervising reviewer marks ★ A3 *at risk*.

The comparison resolves in four minutes on one sentence on the page: *"Unit B is 6.4 hours
slower."* That names **where** the variation lives, not **why** — the unit is a stratum, which is
A2 evidence, and the cause the team intends to act on (pharmacist coverage hours on unit B) has
not itself been tested. A countermeasure aimed at it would be aimed at an unverified cause. Both
records are annotated; the supervising reviewer's *at risk* stands and reaches the Green Belt
with the coaching flag "cause versus stratum". The candidate's log records: *★ agreement reached
after one resolved disagreement on A3.* Illustrative.

That is the requirement working, not failing. The candidate read the statistics correctly and
the rubric slightly wrong, in the direction almost every Black Belt reads it wrong.

## 8. "To rubric standard" — the pass / redo rubric

**"To rubric standard" does not mean the Green Belt passed, the sponsor was satisfied, or the
gate felt good.** It means: you applied the Green Belt rubric as the calibrated reviewer applies
it, produced the record and the feedback in the published format, in the published time, and did
not coach. Scored by the supervising reviewer, per review.

**Pass = every "must" observed at both reviews, and the §7.2 agreement rule met at both.** A
"should" not observed is written on the sheet as feedback; three or more unobserved "shoulds"
across the two reviews trigger a redo.

### 8.1 Preparation

| Must — observed as | Should — observed as |
|---|---|
| The one-pager read two business days before, ★ items first; the pre-read marks dated before the gate | The previous gate's record and the checklist's "common holds" list read too |
| Three questions planned, each tied to a rubric item due at this gate and answerable from the page or by the Green Belt | The questions ordered so the ★ item comes first |
| The gate's ★ items named to the supervising reviewer before the room opens, with what full marks need for each | The 30-day C1 rule and the non-mandatory status of C3 said aloud, unprompted |

### 8.2 In the room

| Must — observed as | Should — observed as |
|---|---|
| Every question serves the record or the sponsor's decision; none proposes a method, a chart, a countermeasure or a form of words | The candidate asks one question and stops; the answer runs without interruption |
| The coaching count is **zero** | The candidate notices a near-miss afterwards and names it before the supervisor does |
| The candidate's questions come out of the gate's ten question-minutes; the gate ends inside its twenty-five | The questions take fewer minutes than they were allotted |
| The candidate does not answer a question the sponsor put to the Green Belt, and does not decide anything | Where the sponsor asks the chair what to do, the answer is the record and the rubric, and the word *how* is routed to the coach |
| Where an item due is absent from the page, it is marked *not evidenced* and said plainly, whatever the room's mood | The hold names one item and a date, and the candidate says nothing after it |

### 8.3 The record

| Must — observed as | Should — observed as |
|---|---|
| Written live, in the room, in the reviewer's format — not reconstructed afterwards | Legible enough to file without transcription |
| Every rubric item due at the gate marked *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced* | Items started but not due noted as such |
| One sentence per ★ item, naming the evidence or its absence, about the page and not about the person | The sentence quotes the page |
| The sponsor's decision and every condition with its date and its owner | Who said each condition, where more than one person spoke |
| A data-ethics line in every record, *none observed* or the specific concern, recorded and not adjudicated | The concern written as the fact observed, with no inference about intent |
| A coaching flag written as a topic | Two words, not two sentences |
| The Black Belt invitation recorded where the project exceeds the Green Belt scope | Its absence considered aloud at the comparison |

### 8.4 The feedback and the comparison

| Must — observed as | Should — observed as |
|---|---|
| Feedback filed within 2 business days in the rubric's feedback format: two strengths; each ★ item's status in one sentence; the single highest-leverage fix first | A stranger could act on the fix without asking a question |
| The Green Belt identified by cohort and project ID; no personal names, no patient, customer or employee data | The redaction done before filing, not asked for |
| Your record filed before you have seen the supervising reviewer's | Filed the same day |
| The comparison attended inside 2 business days; every ★ item resolved to a sentence, exhibit or absence on the page | The candidate names what changed between the pre-read mark and the record, and why |
| The candidate does not argue from the Green Belt's effort, the sponsor's mood, or what they would have done | Where the supervisor's mark stands, the candidate can state the supervisor's reason in their own words |

### 8.5 What fails regardless of the rest

- **Coaching from the chair.** One instance of naming a method, a chart, a countermeasure or a
  form of words. Redo of that review.
- **Contacting the Green Belt outside the room** — before, after, or "one more thought" by
  message. The second reader contacts the learner only in the room; questions go through the
  supervising reviewer.
- **Marking a ★ item evidenced on assertion.** "The team agreed the cause is X"; "we trust the
  system"; a p-value with no effect size and no sentence.
- **Softening a hold.** Recording an item as evidenced or at risk that the page shows as absent.
  The tell is the candidate's own sentence at the comparison: *I did not want to hold up the
  project.*
- **Carrying your own rubric into their gate.** Marking F1 unearned at a Green Belt gate;
  requiring 60 live days for C1; scoring the Black-Belt-sized project the candidate would have
  chartered.
- **A record written after the gate from memory.** It is the same fault the rubric stops a
  project for, committed by the person holding the rubric.
- **Signing anything**, or telling the Green Belt what your record says before the supervising
  reviewer's record is issued.
- **An unobservable review** — the gate was cancelled, the supervising reviewer could not attend,
  the Green Belt withdrew consent on the day. The review is **void, not failed**, and is
  re-placed at the program's cost and on the program's calendar.

### 8.6 Supervisor questions at the chair (ask one per review; record which)

1. "Which sentence on that page decided your ★ mark? Read it to me."
2. "You marked that at risk and I marked it not evidenced. What would have to appear on the page
   to move it one step?"
3. "What did you want to say in there and not say? Why was not saying it right?"
4. "The sponsor asked you what to do. What did you give them, and what did you route?"
5. "Your pre-read had that evidenced and your record has it at risk. What happened in the room?"
6. "What is the one thing on your feedback, and is it an action or a method?"
7. "Is there a Black Belt invitation on this project? Say why, or say why not."

## 9. Redo — a further gate, not a rewrite

**A redo is a second attempt, and it is a further gate.** You do not revise your record and
resubmit it. The review you missed happened; its record stays in your file with its reason code;
it does not count toward your two. You are placed on another Green Belt tollgate, under
supervision, and scored again from the beginning.

| Redo of | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| **One review** | One "must" not observed; a ★ disagreement unresolved or two at one gate; three or more unobserved "shoulds" | Another gate on the same or the next running cohort, at a different phase where the calendar allows, within 30 days of the comparison. A different supervising reviewer where the bench allows |
| **Both reviews** | The same directional error at both — consistently generous or consistently harsh — or coaching from the chair at both | Re-calibration on a fresh anchor package with the assessment lead first, then two new gates. Not booked until the re-calibration is inside both tolerances |
| **The calibration** | A ★ disagreement pattern that the comparison traces to reading the Green Belt rubric, not to the chair | A written re-score of two anchor packages against the calibrated reviewer before any further live gate |

Two redos of the same kind are the limit before the assessment lead reviews the case, as at both
immersions. Every redo carries a **reason code**, and the codes are counted per cohort:

*coached from the chair · softened a hold · marked a ★ item on assertion · imported a Black Belt
rubric item · no data-ethics line · record reconstructed after the gate · decided instead of the
sponsor · feedback late or out of format · unresolved ★ disagreement.*

A code above 40 percent of a cohort's redos is a Module 8 §8.6 section to rework and a Lab 16
planted case to check, not a candidate problem
([`../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md`](../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md)). Redos are expected: the
program's own target is that some candidates have one, and a first-attempt pass rate near 100 per
cent means the supervising reviewers are marking the intent and not the room.

**The calendar does not bend for a redo.** The nine-month closure window runs from your week 16
regardless, and both reviews must be passed before you submit. This is the whole reason the
schedule is written on your Tollgate 5 page.

## 10. The protection for the Green Belt being reviewed

Someone else's certification is happening in the room, and it is not theirs. Every rule below
exists so that the cost to them is zero.

**1 · Your score is never their score of record.** Your record carries no points and never
becomes the Green Belt's score — not at the gate, not at closure. Their project is scored once,
on the closed project, against the full 100-point rubric, by their own reviewer of record. You
are not that person and cannot become that person. Nothing you write is read during their final
review, and nothing you mark constrains what their reviewer marks.

**2 · One record reaches them: the supervising reviewer's.** It goes to the Green Belt, their
sponsor and their coach within 2 business days, in the format the Green Belt system publishes.
Yours goes to your supervising reviewer and your own package.

**3 · You sign nothing.** Not the decision line, not the record of file, not a condition.

**4 · You contact the learner only in the room.** No message before, none after, nothing routed
through a colleague, no connection request. A question you wish you had asked goes to the
supervising reviewer, who decides whether it matters.

**5 · The gate does not get longer or heavier.** Ten minutes, ten of questions, five for the
decision. Your questions come out of the ten. The comparison happens afterwards, without them.
The Green Belt spends exactly the time they would have spent.

**6 · They knew, and they could have said no.** §3.4. A decline costs them nothing and is not
recorded.

**7 · Their details do not travel.** In your package the project is a cohort and a project ID.
Personal names, patient, customer and employee data are removed before filing, per the redaction
standard in the credential evidence policy
([`../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md`](../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md)
§3) and the retention and privacy terms
([`../../program-operations/terms-of-certification.md`](../../program-operations/terms-of-certification.md)
§7). Nothing from their project appears in your public feedback or on your verification page.

**8 · Data-ethics findings route through the supervising reviewer.** You write the line as
practice. The line that goes to the assessment lead the same day is theirs. You do not tell the
Green Belt or the sponsor that a finding is a stop, because at a tollgate it is not one, and the
judgment about what it becomes is not yours.

**9 · Neither of you hears about the other's result.** The Green Belt is never told whether you
passed. You are not told their final score. A Green Belt who took a hold at your gate did not
cause your redo and is never given the chance to think they did.

**10 · The supervising reviewer can end your participation mid-gate.** If the chair goes wrong —
you coach, you argue a mark in front of the room, you put a question that the sponsor's decision
cannot survive — they take the chair back. The gate continues to its decision. Your review is
**void, not failed**, and it is re-placed. The Green Belt's gate is never the place where your
assessment is resolved.

The requirement 5 log is **one table in your package appendix, under its own heading**, separate
from the L3 coaching log. Coaching a Green Belt and reviewing one are different chairs and the

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Behind the table, for each review: your record, your feedback block, and the comparison note with
every ★ resolution and the sentence that decided it — all with the Green Belt's identifying
details removed. The supervising reviewer signs the table. The assessment lead holds the same log

**The Black Belt invitation is yours to route.** Where a Green Belt's project reveals a
cross-functional cause, an interaction that needs a designed experiment, or a control that needs
a system-level change, you record the invitation on your record and the assessment lead routes it
to the next cohort's portfolio. It is the one thing you produce in that chair that outlives the
gate.

## 12. Supervising-reviewer failure modes

Written for the bench, published to candidates so both sides know what the other is being held
to.

- **Briefing the candidate into agreement.** Telling them what you expect to mark before the
  gate. The comparison then measures compliance, and the requirement has measured nothing. Say
  the rubric; never say your marks.
- **Reading your record to them first.** Same fault, later in the sequence. Their record is filed
  before yours is opened, every time.
- **Rescuing the room.** Asking the candidate's question for them because the silence is
  uncomfortable. If the sponsor's decision genuinely needs a question the candidate did not ask,
  ask it at the end, as your own, and record that you did — the count is evidence about the
  candidate, and it is not a punishment.
- **Debating a mark in front of the Green Belt.** The mark of file is yours; the debate is
  afterwards. A learner who watches two reviewers disagree about their project in the room has
  paid for someone else's assessment.
- **Passing on effort.** The candidate prepared well, was pleasant, and softened a hold. Effort
  is not a criterion; the mark on the page is.
- **Letting the comparison slip.** Past two business days, the room is a memory and the resolution
  is a negotiation. The two-day clock is the same one the record of file runs on; keep both.
- **Coaching the candidate.** You are their supervisor for two gates, not their Master Black Belt
  coach and not their reviewer of record — and you may not become either. Feedback on the chair
  is the practicum rubric. Anything about their own project goes to nobody.

## Takeaways

- The chair is a different job from the coach's chair, and requirement 5 exists because most
  Black Belts have only ever sat in the other one.
- Their rubric, not yours: M3, A3, I3, C1, with C1 at 30 days and Finance not mandatory.
- The hold is the most useful thing you give, and the hardest; an item is not evidenced because
  the page does not carry it.
- Every ★ item must carry the same mark on both records, and every disagreement is resolved to a
  sentence on the page — not to a compromise.
- The Green Belt's project is scored once, by their reviewer, on the closed project. Your record
  carries no points and never reaches their file.
