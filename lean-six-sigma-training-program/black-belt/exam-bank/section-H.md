# Black Belt Exam Bank — Section H: Leading change and coaching

Part of the Black Belt certification item bank. The blueprint, the minimally competent
candidate statement, the form-assembly constraints and the pool rules are in
[`../exam-bank.md`](../exam-bank.md); item-writing policy is
[`../../assessment/standard-setting-and-item-policy.md`](../../assessment/standard-setting-and-item-policy.md).

**Tag format:** `[section · objective · Bloom · vertical · type · pool]` — **B8** lead change,
facilitate, coach Green Belts and review tollgates. Type: K = concept, S = scenario judgment,
X = exhibit interpretation. Correct option ✅; rationale after the dash.

**Conventions used in these items.** Resistance is diagnosed with the two models Module 8
teaches: ADKAR (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement — the first missing
element is where you work) and Kotter's stages (urgency; guiding coalition; vision; communicate;
remove barriers; short-term wins; consolidate; anchor). Tollgate-review exhibits are Green Belt
one-pagers scored against the Green Belt rubric
([`../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md`](../../green-belt/project/review-rubric.md)):
mandatory items ★ M3, A3, I3, C1; feedback format two strengths → each ★ item's status in one
sentence → the single highest-leverage fix; data ethics (removed points, a changed operational
definition, a remembered baseline) are a warning and a conversation at a tollgate and a stop at
final review. Black Belt leadership evidence is scored under L1–L3 of
[`../project/review-rubric.md`](../project/review-rubric.md). Nobody in these items is a villain;
every manager is responding to the system they inherited.

## Section H — Leading change: stakeholder strategy, resistance, influence, facilitation, executive communication, coaching and tollgate review (49 CERT)

**H1.** Exhibit — your stakeholder strategy canvas at week 2 of an OEE project across three plants:

| Stakeholder | Influence | Current stance | What you heard in the interview |
|---|---|---|---|
| COO (sponsor) | High | Supportive | "I want one OEE number I can trust" |
| VP Operations (the three plant managers report to him) | High | Opposed | "The plants report 78%; your observed 61% will make my people look like they have been lying for years" |
| Plant manager, plant 2 (pilot site) | Medium | Neutral | "Tell me what you need and when" |
| Finance business partner | Medium | Supportive | "Validate the availability loss in hours before anyone puts dollars on it" |

Your first move on this canvas:
A. Ask the COO to tell the VP that the observed figure is the official baseline from today · B. Move the VP to "informed only" and run the pilot through the plant 2 manager, who is willing · C. Meet the VP alone, take the definitions rather than the number — the plants exclude planned changeovers from available time and yours does not — and agree that the readout will present the gap as a definition every plant inherited, not a reporting failure ✅ · D. Put both numbers in front of the steering group and ask the group to decide which definition is official
`[H · B8 · Analyze · MFG · X · CERT]` — The opposition is about exposure, a rational response to a readout that would make his organization look dishonest, so the move removes the exposure while keeping the honest number; A spends the sponsor's authority on a problem that will recur at every plant, and D makes the exposure public before the VP has a way through it.

**H2.** Six weeks after a claims-intake team adopted the standard work you designed with them, a sample of 60 claims shows 21 followed it. Everyone attended both training sessions and can walk you through the steps. Three processors say the same thing in interviews: "When the queue backs up I go back to the old way, because my dashboard still counts claims touched per hour and the new way touches fewer." The team leader has communicated the reason for the change twice. The element to work on:
A. Awareness — a third communication that makes the customer impact concrete, delivered by the team leader rather than by you · B. Knowledge — a refresher session with a competency check, because 35% adoption after two sessions shows the training did not land · C. Desire — one-to-one conversations with the three processors about what the change would take for them to want it · D. Reinforcement — the productivity dashboard still pays for the old method, so change the measure with the team leader before touching training or communication ✅
`[H · B8 · Analyze · TXN · S · CERT]` — The processors can describe the steps and can name why they revert, so Knowledge and Ability are present and the gap is in what the system rewards; B is the reflex because training is the lever the Black Belt controls, and it addresses an element that is not missing.

**H3.** The Black Belt rubric scores the stakeholder strategy canvas at project start and at least one dated revision (L1). The revision counts as evidence because:
A. It shows you looked again once the project had met its stakeholders, and where a stance or an influence rating changed you had information the first canvas could not ✅ · B. It shows the first canvas was wrong, which is the honest admission the reviewer rewards over a canvas that was right from the start · C. It lets the reviewer check that every stakeholder moved toward "supportive" between the two dates · D. The rubric requires two dated documents for every leadership item so that neither can be produced after closure
`[H · B8 · Understand · NEU · K · CERT]` — The canvas is a working model the project corrects as it learns, so a revision is evidence of steering; C is wrong because stances need not all move, and a stakeholder honestly still "opposed" with a plan beside them is better evidence than a canvas where everyone is green.

```

    the source of truth"

```
Everything else on the page is complete. Your one sentence on ★ M3 for the reviewer's record:
A. "M3 evidenced — system timestamps are a reviewer-accepted reason, and the stable chart confirms the data are usable" · B. `[H · B8 · Evaluate · HC · X · CERT]` — The rubric accepts a system timestamp only with a written, reviewer-accepted reason, and "source of truth" is the "we trust the system" answer it names as insufficient, because provider-assignment time is entered by a person and sometimes later; C raises a real M4 question but scores the wrong item, and D demands a method that does not fit the measurement.

`[H · B8 · Apply · MFG · S · CERT]` — Influence without authority means finding what the other function needs and paying in it, and the manager is responding rationally to a queue your project would lengthen; A spends the sponsor's authority on the first obstacle and leaves less for the one that needs it, and C fires an escalation the path assumes you earned by trying the exchange first.

**H13.** A colleague plans to open a change with a full training program "so nobody can say they were not shown how." The reason the ADKAR sequence matters here:
A. Training belongs last in the sequence, after reinforcement mechanisms are in place to hold what was taught · B. The five elements are independent, so training can start anywhere provided all five are eventually covered · D.
