# Stakeholder Strategy Canvas — Executive Power and Interest, Current and Required Stance, Resistance Diagnosis, Planned Moves, Revision Log

*Black Belt toolkit · version 1 dated before the kickoff (Module 1, week 2), revised at least
once — usually before the pilot (week 8) and after the first resistance episode — and filed
with each dated version intact. Rubric ★ L1: "stakeholder strategy canvas at start and at
least one revision; a documented resistance episode with the diagnosis and what the
candidate did; team decisions recorded; a sponsor statement." Method:
[`../modules/m8-leading-change-and-coaching.md`](../modules/m8-leading-change-and-coaching.md)
(8.1–8.3); the executive rows come from the charter's cross-functional scope in
[`../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md`](../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md).*

## Purpose

A Black Belt project changes what three or four functions measure, spend and staff, and the
executives who own those numbers were not in the room when the charter was signed. Their
position is not what they say at the steering group; it is what they release — people, time,
a budget line, a scorecard adjustment. The canvas records, for every executive whose budget,
headcount or scorecard the change touches, what the change costs them in their own units,
where they stand on evidence and on what date, where they need to stand by when, what is
missing between the two, and the next move with its owner.

Three things distinguish this canvas from the Green Belt's map. The stance column carries
**evidence, dated**, not an adjective. The gap column carries a **diagnosis** — interest,
belief or capability — not a plan to persuade. And the move column names an **owner who is
usually not you**: most executive moves are sponsor-to-peer, and the evidence an executive
accepts is their own report re-cut, a walk, or a peer's result, never your slide.

The canvas is L1 evidence only because it is revised. A version 1 that survives to closure
unchanged says you did not look again.

## The canvas

### 1 · Header

| | |
|---|---|
| **Project / Black Belt / sponsor** | |
| **Version and date** (v1 before kickoff; each revision dated; earlier versions kept) | |
| **What the change touches** | budgets, headcount, scorecards, systems, physical space — by function |
| **Decision-rights table reference** (charter) | which decisions each executive on this canvas holds |

### 2 · Executive map — power and interest (one line each)

| Quadrant | Who is here (roles) | What the quadrant means for the plan |
|---|---|---|
| High power, high interest | | Steer with; the sponsor's peers; weekly evidence in their form |
| High power, low interest | | Keep satisfied: a one-line note when their number moves; a move only when the change touches their scorecard |
| Low power, high interest | | Keep close: the people who do the work and the managers who run it; the team roster |
| Low power, low interest | | Inform; watch for a move into another quadrant when the pilot starts |

Power is the authority to release or withhold what the project needs; interest is how much
their own number moves. Both change at the pilot, which is why version 2 is dated before it.

### 3 · The canvas rows

| Stakeholder (role) | Metric, budget or headcount they own; its units | Power / interest | Cost of the change to them (their units, over what period) | Current stance — evidence, date | Required stance — by date | Gap diagnosis: interest · belief · capability (Kotter condition / ADKAR element) | Evidence they trust | Planned move — what, owner, by when | Result (dated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | | |

**Stance, evidence, date.** Written as behavior: attended, released, asked, withheld,
adjusted, signed. "Supportive" is not evidence; "released the day-shift nurse on time three
weeks running" is.

**Cost of the change.** A number in the units they are judged on: percentage points of a
service level, FTE-weeks, dollars of a budget line, days of a turnaround metric. A row you
cannot fill is a conversation you have not had.

**Gap diagnosis.** Interest — they lose something real; the move is at the level that can
change the loss, usually the sponsor. Belief — they do not accept the evidence; the move is
evidence in the form they trust, or a trial they design. Capability — they cannot do the new
thing under real conditions; the move is design, not persuasion. Name the Kotter condition
the system is missing and the first missing ADKAR element (8.2). Persuasion is the right tool
only for belief, and only with evidence.

### 4 · Resistance episode record (one per episode; L1 needs at least one)

| Line | Entry |
|---|---|
| **1 · Observed (behavior, not attitude), date, by whom** | |
| **2 · Diagnosis and its evidence** (interest · belief · capability; confirmed by going to see) | |
| **3 · What you (or the move owner) did, date** | |
| **4 · What happened** — on the metric or the behavior, date | |
| **5 · What changed on the canvas** (row, version) | |

When the resistance is right, line 3 changes the design, not the person.

### 5 · Team decisions log

| Date | Decision | Options considered | Who decided (decision-rights row) | Who was consulted | Recorded where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

### 6 · Revision log

| Version | Date | What changed and why (rows added, stances moved, costs re-stated, diagnoses revised) | Trigger (kickoff · pilot design · episode · tollgate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | | | |
| v2 | | | |

### 7 · Sponsor statement (at closure, in the sponsor's words)

*How the candidate led a team they did not manage; one episode the sponsor saw; what the
candidate asked of the sponsor and when.* Signed and dated by the sponsor. This is the fourth
element of L1 and it is not written by the candidate.

## Filled example — HC: arrival to first antibiotic for sepsis, emergency department

The project from the charter's section 2.2. Version 3, revised after the episode below;
earlier versions on file. Figures are illustrative and typical of the process.

**What the change touches.** The countermeasure set from Analyze: a nurse-initiated sepsis
protocol with the physician review documented inside the protocol's time (ED nursing, ED
physicians); point-of-care lactate testing in the ED (laboratory: volume, analyzer quality
control, cartridge cost); a first-dose broad-spectrum antibiotic stocked in the ED automated
cabinet (pharmacy: inventory, expiry, controlled-storage capacity). Budgets touched: lab
consumables, pharmacy inventory carrying cost, ED nursing hours during the pilot. Scorecards
touched: lab turnaround-time attainment, pharmacy expired-dose waste, nursing hours per
patient visit.

**Executive map.** High power, high interest: chief medical officer (sponsor), ED medical
director, chief nursing officer. High power, low interest at v1: pharmacy director, laboratory
director, chief financial officer — all three moved to high interest at v2 once the
countermeasures touched their lines. Low power, high interest: ED nurse manager, ED pharmacist,
sepsis committee chair. Low power, low interest: inpatient unit managers (informed).

**Canvas rows (condensed to seven of the eleven).**

**Team decisions log (three of fourteen).** 12 Sep — protocol review window set at 30 min
(options 20/30/45): decided by the ED medical director per decision-rights row 2, ED
pharmacist and nurse manager consulted, minuted. 23 Sep — point-of-care QC at 03:00 assigned
to the night charge nurse with the lab supervisor on call (options: lab runner; charge nurse;
defer QC to 07:00 — rejected as a quality risk): decided by the lab director and nurse manager
jointly. 2 Oct — pilot on day shift only with night shift as comparison (options: both shifts;
day only): sponsor, on the Black Belt's recommendation, because night backfill was not in place
until 29 Sep.

**Revision log.** v1, 14 Aug (before kickoff): six rows, stances as adjectives, costs blank
for three executives. v2, 10 Sep (pilot design): lab director, CFO and sepsis chair added;
every cost re-stated in the owner's units; two "supportive" stances replaced with evidence
that said otherwise (the CNO's late night release; the pharmacy director's unactioned
request). v3, 7 Oct (episode): pharmacy row re-diagnosed; buyer row added; the CNO's
adjustment recorded as obtained.

**Sponsor statement (chief medical officer, at closure, excerpt).** *"She brought me the cost
of the change in my peers' numbers before she brought me the chart, and asked me to make two
conversations myself rather than trying to make them for me. When the pharmacy cabinet
stalled she came back with the reason, not a complaint — and the reason was partly ours."*

## Common mistakes

- **Adjectives in the stance column.** "Supportive", "neutral", "resistant" are not evidence.
  Attended, released, withheld, asked, signed — with a date.
- **Only the people in the room.** Every executive whose budget, headcount or scorecard the
  future state touches is a row, whether or not they have said anything. The rows that arrive
  at version 2 are the ones that stop pilots.
- **Cost column blank or in your units.** "Loses 90 lactates a month and gains a $15K line" is
  a cost the laboratory director recognizes; "some lab impact" is not.
- **Persuasion for an interest problem.** An executive whose scorecard worsens for twelve
  weeks is reading her scorecard. The move is the sponsor's, at the level that can change the
  scorecard, in writing.
- **Diagnosing from a distance.** Every diagnosis is confirmed by going to see — the buyer's
  screen, the cabinet, the productivity report.
- **The candidate as move owner on every row.** Most executive moves are sponsor-to-peer. A
  canvas where you make every move is a canvas the sponsor has not read.
- **Resistance treated as wrong by definition.** The pharmacy director was right about the
  refrigerated slot. When the resistance is right, line 3 changes the design.
- **Version 1 unchanged at closure.** L1 scores the revision. Date each version, keep the old
  ones, and say in the log what you learned.
- **The sponsor statement drafted by the candidate.** It is the sponsor's words, signed;
  offering a draft is offering the reviewer a reason to discount it.
