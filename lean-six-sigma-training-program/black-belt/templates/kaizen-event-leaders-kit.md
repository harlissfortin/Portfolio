# Kaizen Event Leader's Kit — Charter, Pre-work, Day-by-Day Agenda, Roles, 30-Day Follow-up

*Black Belt toolkit · used for certification requirement 4 (lead one rapid-improvement event,
observed or video-reviewed) and for the event that installs part of the project's future
state (Module 7, weeks 13–14). Rubric I4 (implementation management), I3 (flow and system
design), C2 (standard work written with the people who do it), L1 and L3. The observer's
facilitation rubric is published with the practicum; this kit is what the observer expects
to see in your hands. Method: [`../modules/m7-lean-systems.md`](../modules/m7-lean-systems.md) (7.8).*

## Purpose

A kaizen event is three to five days of concentrated change on one stream segment, done by
the people who work in it, with the authority to change it arranged in advance. It succeeds
or fails on what happens before and after: a charter the sponsor signed in numbers, a
baseline that was already on a chart, decision rights written down, backfill arranged, and a
30-day list that someone reviews weekly. The five days are the visible part. The leadership is
six weeks.

The kit has five parts in the order you use them: the charter (T−4 to T−2 weeks), the
pre-work checklist (T−2 weeks to T−1 day), the roles, the day-by-day agenda, and the 30-day
follow-up. The observer scores preparation, facilitation, safety of the room and closure; the
kit is arranged so that each of those has a page.

## Part 1 · Charter (signed T−2 weeks at the latest)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Event title, dates, location** | 3 or 5 days; the room is in the area, not a conference room across the site |
| **Stream segment in scope (start step, stop step)** | one segment you can walk in 20 minutes; what is explicitly out |
| **Link to the project's future state** (which of the eight design questions this event installs) | |
| **Primary metric, operational definition, measurement system** | one metric; the definition by reference; the gauge or timestamp check done |
| **Baseline** | value, chart type, window (≥ 2 weeks), n — already on a chart before the charter is signed |
| **Target, in numbers, signed by the sponsor** | "lead time 6.4 → ≤ 3.2 working days", not "improve" |
| **Guardrails** | quality, safety and the downstream metric that must not worsen |
| **Decision rights — may change without asking** | layout inside the area; work sequence; standard work; visual controls; roles inside the team; par levels within a stated range |
| **Decision rights — needs whom, within what time** | IT or ERP configuration (who, how many days); anything touching another department (whose manager); spend above $___ (sponsor); anything touching a customer or a regulated record |
| **Sponsor** | attends the kick-off, a day-2 check-in and the report-out; signs the target |
| **Process owner** | runs the area; owns the 30-day list after the event |
| **Team** (6–9) | ≥ half from the people who do the work; one upstream; one downstream; one fresh-eyes outsider; one from maintenance or IT if the change will need them |
| **Backfill, named per team member** | who covers, agreed by their manager in writing |
| **Observer** (requirement 4) | name; observed in person or the video plan |
| **Budget** | supplies, small spend, the sponsor's limit |
| **Signatures** | sponsor, process owner, each team member's manager (for the backfill), Black Belt |

## Part 2 · Pre-work checklist

| When | Item | Done |
|---|---|---|
| T−4 weeks | Segment walked with the process owner; scope drafted; the metric's operational definition and measurement check confirmed (or done) | |
| T−4 weeks | Baseline data collection started on the chart the event will continue | |
| T−3 weeks | Team named; backfill agreed with each manager; observer booked | |
| T−2 weeks | Charter signed; decision rights written; the IT or other-department contact briefed on what may come and their turnaround | |
| T−2 weeks | Sponsor briefed that day 3 will look worse than day 1 and that the report-out is the team's | |
| T−1 week | Current-state walk video; time observations (10 cycles per step, by an observer with a sheet); WIP counted at each queue on two days; demand for the last 12 weeks pulled | |
| T−1 week | Just-in-time training prepared: 30 minutes maximum, on the two or three tools the team will use on day 2 | |
| T−1 week | Room in the area booked; supplies (tape, cardboard, timers, flip charts, sticky notes, a printer); the baseline chart printed large | |
| T−1 day | Kick-off, day-2 check-in and report-out on the sponsor's calendar; attendance list; the "newspaper" (issue list) template on the wall, empty | |
| T−1 day | The team's normal work reassigned to the backfill from the morning of day 1 | |

## Part 3 · Roles

| Role | Who | What they do | What they do not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Event leader / facilitator** | the Black Belt | Runs the process: agenda, timeboxes, the rules of the room, the arithmetic check on every number, scope discipline, the 30-day list | Design the future state; present the result; decide content |
| **Sponsor** | the executive who signed the target | Kick-off (10 min); day-2 check-in (20 min); report-out; removes an obstacle the team names | Solve from the doorway; attend all day; change the target on day 3 |
| **Process owner** | the manager of the area | Full-time on the team; owns every change from day 4; owns the 30-day list | Veto the team's trial on day 3; delegate the follow-up |
| **Team members** | 6–9 as chartered | Observe, measure, design, try, write standard work, present | Return to their desks (backfill exists for this) |
| **Upstream / downstream member** | one each | Say what the change does to their step; test the hand-off | Defend their step's current state |
| **Fresh-eyes member** | from another area | Ask why; time things; hold the newspaper | Redesign their own area |
| **IT / maintenance member** | as needed | Tell the team on day 2 what can be done inside the week and what goes on the 30-day list, with a date | Take the change away to do later |
| **Scribe and timekeeper** | rotating, team members | Newspaper, decisions, timeboxes | — |
| **Observer** | as booked | Scores against the facilitation rubric; debriefs the leader at the end of each day | Facilitate |

## Part 4 · Day-by-day agenda (five days; the three-day compression below)

| Day | Block | Content | Leader's job | Sponsor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 08:00–08:30 | Kick-off: sponsor states the target and the decision rights; leader sets the rules of the room (every number has a basis; the people who do the work decide the work; the newspaper for anything out of scope) | Keep it to 30 min | Present |
| | 08:30–09:00 | Just-in-time training on the day-2 tools (30 min maximum) | Short | — |
| | 09:00–12:00 | Walk the segment as it runs; team members time and count; the current state drawn from observation, not the routing | Ask "did you see that or were you told it?" | — |
| | 13:00–16:00 | Baseline reviewed on its chart; demand and takt; WIP and Little's Law; the newspaper opened | Check the arithmetic on the wall | — |
| | 16:00–16:30 | Day close: what we know, what we do not; the observer debriefs the leader | | — |
| **2** | 08:00–11:30 | Analysis at event scale: where the time goes, where the queue is, the constraint, the changeover or setup content (Module 7, 7.2–7.7); the metric's driver stated in numbers | Ask for the basis of every number | — |
| | 11:30–12:00 | Sponsor check-in: the finding and the direction, not the design | Protect the team from a solution from the doorway | 20 min |
| | 13:00–16:00 | Future state for the segment; countermeasures ranked by effect on the metric and by decision right (can do now / needs whom, by when) | Stop scope growth: anything outside the segment goes on the newspaper | — |
| | 16:00–16:30 | Plan the day-3 trials: what, how long, how measured | | — |
| **3** | all day | Try: cardboard, tape, mock-ups, trial standard work, run for real for an hour, time it, adjust, run again — several cycles | Protect trial time from tidy-up pressure and from "we can't really do that" | — |
| | 16:00–16:30 | What the trials showed, in numbers; what needs another cycle on day 4; what goes to the 30-day list because it needs someone else | | — |
| **4** | 08:00–12:00 | Implement what worked; standard work written by the people who will do it, on the standard-work template, with times | Let them write it; check only that a stranger could follow it | — |
| | 13:00–16:00 | Visual controls in place; training of the shift not in the room; the metric measured on the new process | Stop scope growth again; start the 30-day list | — |
| **5** | 08:00–11:00 | Verify against the baseline on the same metric and definition — a timed sample, the day's chart point; the guardrails checked | The comparison is honest: sample size and window stated | — |
| | 11:00–12:00 | The 30-day list closed to ≤ 10 items, each with an owner and a date; the process owner accepts it | | — |
| | 13:00–14:00 | Report-out by team members: the segment before and after, the metric, the standard work, the 30-day list, what did not work | The team presents; you stand at the back | Present; asks one question of each presenter |
| | 14:00–15:00 | Team debrief; observer's debrief with the leader; the metric's chart handed to the process owner | | — |

**Three-day version.** Day 1 = kick-off, walk, current state, baseline and analysis (the
just-in-time training is done the week before). Day 2 = future state and trials. Day 3 =
implement, standard work, verify, 30-day list, report-out. Use it when the segment is small
and the trials are physical; do not use it when IT or another department must act inside the
event.

**Rules of the room (posted).** Every number has a basis. The people who do the work decide
the work. Try it before you debate it. Anything outside the segment goes on the newspaper.
The result is what the metric says, not what the room feels.

## Part 5 · The 30-day follow-up

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | |

### The follow-up cadence

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Day 5 | The metric continues on the same chart, same definition, plotted by the process owner's team | Process owner |
| Weekly to day 30 | 20-minute review of the list with the owner; items that slip are escalated the same week to the person named in the charter's decision-rights row | Process owner and Black Belt |
| Day 30 | The chart read against the baseline and the target; the list closed or the open items re-owned with reasons; the honest comparison written | Process owner, Black Belt, sponsor |
| Day 60 and day 90 | The chart read again; the standard work audited once (five cycles observed against the sheet); what drifted and why | Process owner; Black Belt reads |

## Filled example — TXN: accounts-payable matching cell, five-day event

The event from Module 7 (7.8), run as the installation of the intake-control part of the
project's future state. Numbers are illustrative and in the observed range for the process.

**Charter.** Segment: invoice first touch in the matching queue → approval released to
payments; out of scope: invoice receipt and scanning (upstream), payment runs (downstream),
the vendor portal project. Metric: working days from first touch to approval, daily median
of invoices approved that day, from ERP timestamps (timestamp check on 40 invoices, week 12:
38 within ± 1 h; the two failures were batch-entered approvals, rule written). Baseline: 6.4
working days, I-MR of the daily median over four weeks (20 points, stable). Target signed by
the finance director: ≤ 3.2 working days at day 30. Guardrails: duplicate-payment rate and
vendor-query volume not worse. Decision rights: the team may change desk layout, work
sequence, queue rules, standard work and the visual board without asking; ERP configuration
needs the IT change board (5 working days); anything touching vendor communications needs
the procurement manager; spend ≤ $2,000 the sponsor. Team of eight: three AP clerks (of nine
in the cell), the AP team leader (process owner), a buyer (upstream), a payments analyst
(downstream), an HR coordinator (fresh eyes), the ERP analyst. Backfill: two temporary clerks
from the shared-services pool, named; the buyer's and analyst's managers signed. Observer:
the cohort instructor, in person days 1, 3 and 5, video on days 2 and 4.

**Pre-work.** Baseline charted from T−4 weeks. Time observations: 10 cycles at each of the
four steps (match, exception research, approval, release). WIP counted on two days: 1,880 and
1,920 invoices. Demand: 410 invoices per day, 12-week average, one month-end spike at 590.
Just-in-time training: Little's Law and the standard-work sheet, 25 minutes.

**The five days.** Day 1: the walk shows the "exception research" desk holding 1,340 of the
1,900 invoices; Little's Law on the wall: 1,900 ÷ 410 = 4.6 days of queue against 0.4 days of
touch time. Day 2: exception research is the constraint; 61% of its invoices are waiting for
a PO number the buyer can supply in two minutes but is asked for by email in batches; the
future state co-locates one buyer session per morning in the cell and caps the matching
queue at two days' work (820 invoices) with intake to the cap. Sponsor check-in: the finding
and the cap, 20 minutes; she asks whether the cap will slow receipts (no — receipt is
upstream and unchanged). Day 3: cardboard desk layout with the buyer's session at 09:00;
trial standard work for exception research; two timed cycles of an hour each; the trial shows
the ERP screen has no hold-reason field, so exceptions cannot be sorted by what they wait for
— IT, 5 days: on the 30-day list with the ERP analyst as owner and a date, not into "done".
Day 4: layout moved, standard work written by the three clerks (four sheets), visual board
with the cap line, the six clerks not in the room trained by the three who were. Day 5: a
40-invoice timed sample gives 2.1 working days; the queue at 1,140 and falling. Report-out by
the clerks: *"2.1 days on a 40-invoice sample; the 30-day chart will confirm or not."* What
did not work: the first cap value (600) starved the approval step on day 4 afternoon; reset to
820 from the arithmetic.

**30-day list (six items).**

| # | Item | Why | Owner | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hold-reason field on the exception screen | ERP change board | ERP analyst | day 21 | Approved day 8; live day 19 |
| 2 | Vendor notice: PO number required on the invoice | Procurement manager's decision | Buyer | day 14 | Sent day 12 |
| 3 | Buyer's 09:00 session written into the buying team's standard work | Another department's standard | Procurement manager | day 10 | Done day 9 |
| 4 | Cap line and daily-median chart on the visual board, plotted by the shift lead | Ownership transfer | AP team leader | day 5 | Done day 5 |
| 5 | Temporary clerks' coverage ended; nine clerks on the new standard | Backfill release | AP team leader | day 7 | Done day 7 |
| 6 | Duplicate-payment guardrail added to the weekly review | Guardrail ownership | Payments analyst | day 14 | Done day 10 |

**Day 30.** Daily median 2.6 working days over 22 working days (I-MR continued from the
baseline, new stage from day 5; no rule violation after day 9). Above the 2.1-day sample,
below the 3.2 target; the honest line on the day-30 page says both. Guardrails: duplicate
payments 0 (baseline 1 per month); vendor queries down 18%. Day 90: 2.4 days; standard-work
audit five cycles, one deviation (the cap ignored on a month-end day, by the team leader's
decision — recorded, and the month-end rule added to the standard).

## Common mistakes

- **The event as theatre.** No baseline chart before the charter; a result announced from a
  two-hour sample; no 30-day chart. The observer scores preparation first.
- **Scope growing on day 2.** The segment is the segment. The newspaper is where the rest goes,
  and the sponsor sees it at the report-out.
- **The sponsor solving from the doorway.** Brief the sponsor at T−2 weeks: kick-off, a
  check-in on the finding, the report-out. The design is the team's.
- **Team members pulled back to their desks.** Backfill is named in the charter and signed by
  their manager. A team that loses two people on day 2 is a team of five.
- **Training that runs an hour.** Thirty minutes on the two tools the team will use. The
  learning is on the floor.
- **"Done" items that need someone else.** An ERP change is on the 30-day list with an owner
  and a date, however small it looks on day 3.
- **A 30-day list of forty items.** Ten at most; the rest are a project or a newspaper item.
  A list nobody closes is a list.
- **You presenting the result.** The team presents; you stand at the back. The observer scores
  this.
- **Facilitating content.** When you find yourself drawing the future state, stop and hand the
  marker to the person who does the work.
- **A result on a different definition.** The day-5 sample and the day-30 chart use the
  baseline's operational definition and chart. Anything else is a reconstructed baseline.
