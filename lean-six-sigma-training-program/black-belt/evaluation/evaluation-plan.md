# Black Belt — Evaluation Plan

What we measure at Black Belt, how, and what we do when the numbers are bad. Four levels
(Kirkpatrick framing), with the honest constraint stated up front: **Black Belt carries
Levels 1, 2 and 3 rigorously and Level 4 as fact per project — sponsor-verified,
Finance-validated or mission-metric-validated, measured over a stated window, and checked
for sustainment twice, at day 90 and at one year.** It can do that because the product is a
cross-functional project with five tollgates on a calendar, a Finance partner named at the
charter, a named Master Black Belt as reviewer of record and a control plan that has run for
at least 60 days before anyone scores it. What it still does not do is isolate the course's
contribution from the candidate's, the sponsor's and the organization's, or turn that into a
training ROI ratio. The program publishes what the projects produced, validated by people who
do not work for the program, and lets the buyer draw the ratio.

Three things are larger at this level and the plan is built around them. The money is larger
(a charter enters at a projected ≥ $75K annualized or the clinical or service equivalent), so
the temptation to report a projection as a result is larger. The project sits in a
**portfolio** with other projects on the same ledger lines, so double counting is the default
unless somebody stops it in writing. And a **named Master Black Belt signs every
certificate**, so a human reputation is attached to each credential and the audit of that
reviewer is part of the evaluation, not an afterthought.

Governs: the assessment lead (owns every figure here), the engagement lead (the cohort
report), reviewers of record (gate records, sustainment checks), supervising reviewers
(requirement 5 records), practicum observers (requirements 3 and 4) and Master Black Belt
coaches (session records). Agrees with the [level README](../README.md) (calendar, gates,
blueprint), the [review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md), the
[charter and strategic linkage document](../project/charter-and-strategic-linkage.md) (§3
impact threshold, §4 double counting), the practicum rubrics in [`practicum/`](../practicum/)
(DOE practicum, kaizen facilitation, Green Belt tollgate reviews), the instructor, reviewer
and coaching system in `delivery/`, the
[assessment policy](../../assessment/standard-setting-and-item-policy.md) and the
[outcomes page specification](../../program-operations/outcomes-page.md). The Green Belt
[evaluation plan](../../green-belt/evaluation/evaluation-plan.md) is the house pattern; the
[sustainment check](../../green-belt/project/sustainment-check.md), the
[sponsor verification and Finance validation form](../../green-belt/project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md)
and the attribution rule are inherited from it and tightened here, not replaced.

Three rules apply to every figure below:

1. **Per cohort, never per candidate to a manager.** A sponsor sees their own project's
   result; an employer sees aggregates. Item-level exam responses go to nobody outside the
   program (terms of certification §7).
2. **Definitions are fixed at enrollment.** Changing a definition mid-series is the same
   error the rubric fails a candidate for. A changed definition is a change-log entry, and
   the series restarts with the change dated.
3. **No figure is removed because it is inconvenient**, and the bad cohort is reported with
   the same prominence as the good one. The improvement board item that owns the bad number
   is linked from it.

## Level 1 — Reaction

**Instrument:** 12-item survey, five-point agreement scale plus two free-text items,
completed in the Lab 16 session (week 16), before the exam sitting (not emailed — email
surveys return around 15% and skew positive). The corporate wave variant runs it on the last
day of wave 4. A three-item **immersion pulse** follows each immersion, and a five-item
**closure pulse** follows when the project file is submitted, because most of what the client
controls happens in the nine months after week 16.

| # | Item | Diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I can run this module's methods on my own project data without help | The course |
| 2 | The labs, the immersions and the case datasets were relevant to my project and my vertical | The course |
| 3 | The course respected my time — the labs were application, not lecture | The course |
| 4 | I knew at every tollgate what the reviewer of record would need to see | The course |
| 5 | My Master Black Belt coach helped me earn the rubric without giving me my model, my design or my countermeasure | The coaching |
| 6 | The course was accessible to me (captions, keyboard, screen reader, materials in advance, language, the physical kit or simulator) | The course |
| 7 | I would recommend this course to a colleague who leads projects across functions | The course |
| 8 | My sponsor attended the five tollgates and made the decisions in the room | **The client** |
| 9 | The functional heads who signed my charter honored the decision-rights table and the time contracts | **The client** |
| 10 | My Finance partner stated the basis they would accept at Tollgate 2, not at closure | **The client** |
| 11 | I had the data access and the analyst or IT time the charter named, by the dates agreed | **The client** |
| 12 | My kaizen event had its backfill, its sponsor kick-off and its report-out as chartered | **The client** |
| F1 | What one thing would you change about the course? | |
| F2 | What stood between you and your project this fortnight that had nothing to do with the course? | |

**Immersion pulse (after each immersion):** X1 the apparatus, simulator or scenario cards
worked as briefed · X2 the observer's feedback named the specific sentence or move, not a
general impression · X3 I could do this on my own process next week.

**Closure pulse (at submission):** P1 my sponsor stayed the sponsor through closure · P2 the
team's time contracts held through implementation · P3 Finance or the mission-metric
executive validated on the basis agreed at Tollgate 2 · P4 the process owner ran the control
plan for the first 60 days without me · P5 the executive readout produced the decisions it
asked for.

**Items 8–12 and the closure pulse are the ones that matter, and they are not about the
course.** They measure whether the client did what the charter and the readiness gate said it
would. Because a Black Belt project crosses functions, they are reported to the executive
sponsor **by function** — the function that withheld the analyst, the function whose head
signed and then did not release the hours — verbatim, in the cohort report. A function
scoring 2.4 on item 9 has a decision-rights problem that the next cohort's charters will
inherit, and no lab redesign touches it.

**Thresholds:** items 1–4 and 7 ≥ 4.0 mean per cohort; items 2–4 ≥ 4.0 per instructor. Item
5 ≥ 4.0 per Master Black Belt coach; a coach below 3.5 gets an observed session, not a note in
a file. Item 6 ≥ 4.5, with any individual score ≤ 3 triggering an accessibility follow-up
within 2 business days (a kit that cannot be operated one-handed, a simulator with no keyboard
path, are item 6 findings and go to the practicum owner the same day). Items 8–12 have no
pass threshold; they are diagnostic, and a low score triggers a conversation with the
executive sponsor and the named functional head, not a content change. F2 free text is coded
to the coaching note's risk categories (sponsor · data · scope · time · Finance) plus the two
the Black Belt charter adds (decision rights · systems), and reported as counts.

## Level 2 — Learning

**Instruments:** the 12-item pre-assessment (PRE pool) at week −2; the calibration
assessment for externally certified Green Belts; the 150-item proctored open-notes exam; the
cumulative checks in the module files; the rubric-reviewed capstone; and the three observed
requirements (DOE practicum, kaizen facilitation, Green Belt tollgate reviews), whose pass
and redo rates say what the candidate can *do* on a Tuesday afternoon with an observer
present. (The calendar behaviors the project produces — tollgates held, closure, handover,
the 30-day list closed — are Level 3.)

### 2.1 Exam

| Metric | Target | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|
| First-attempt pass rate | 65–85% | **Above 95%: the exam is decorative.** Raise item difficulty, not the cut score, and re-run the Angoff. Below 55%: item and content review before the cut is touched; a cut set by panel is not lowered because a cohort struggled |
| Eventual pass rate (within the included retake) | ≥ 88% | A gap of more than 25 points between first-attempt and eventual means the retake carries the credential; look at the 14-day study period and the section profile first |
| Mean pre/post gain, matched PRE items | ≥ 20 percentage points | Check for ceiling first — every entrant is a certified Green Belt and arrives knowing sections A and B. A pre score above 60% on the PRE pool means the pool is too easy, not that the course taught little |
| External Green Belts against program Green Belts | First-attempt pass rates within 10 points, once each arm has 30 | A wider gap means the calibration assessment is not finding the gaps the bridging modules are meant to close; the assessment, not the bridging, is the first thing to read |
| Section-level mean, per blueprint section | No section below 65% on a form that passes overall | D (modeling, 24 items) and E2 (fractional designs, confounding, curvature) are the sections to watch. Below 65% on D across a cohort is a Module 4 lab question; on E2, a Module 5 sequencing question — not a candidate question |
| Exhibit (X) items against concept (K) items | X-item mean within 15 points of the K-item mean | At ≈ 45% X items, this gap is the exam's main signal. A wider gap means candidates can name a design and cannot read its effects table. Check exhibit rendering under proctoring before touching content |
| "When not to use it" items against method-application items, sections C–F | Within 10 points | Below that, the course is teaching methods and not restraint — the failure rubric item A3 exists to catch on the project. A cohort that models everything on the exam will model everything on the project |
| Cumulative-check accuracy (spaced retrieval) | ≥ 70% on re-tested items | Below that, the earlier module needs rework; this catches shallow learning the module's own lab misses |
| Internal consistency (KR-20) | ≥ 0.80 per form | Below 0.80, item analysis before anything else |

### 2.2 Item statistics

Per the assessment policy §4, after every 100 administrations of a form or quarterly,
whichever comes first:

| Statistic | Keep | Review | Retire or rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Difficulty (p-value) | 0.30–0.90 | 0.20–0.30 or 0.90–0.95 | < 0.20 or > 0.95 |
| Discrimination (point-biserial) | ≥ 0.20 | 0.10–0.20 | < 0.10 or negative |
| Distractors | Every distractor chosen by ≥ 3% | One dead distractor | Two or more dead |

Three Black Belt notes. First, an X item with p < 0.30 is checked for a rendering problem
before it is judged as an item — a DOE effects table or a regression output that wrapped
badly under the proctoring platform is the first suspect. Second, with cohorts of 8–16 and a
525-item bank, a form reaches 100 administrations after roughly seven to thirteen cohorts;
until then, flagged items are reviewed on judgment with the small n stated, and the outcomes
page says the statistics are forming. Black Belt will carry the *forming* label longer than
any other level, and the plan says so rather than pooling forms to hurry it. Third, an
open-notes exam changes what "too easy" looks like: an item with p > 0.95 is not necessarily
recall — it may be one whose answer can be looked up in the candidate's notes without
judgment, and the rewrite makes the exhibit carry the decision. Point-biserial and KR-20 are
computed in Excel (CORREL of the item score against the total minus that item; KR-20 from the
item p-values and total variance), in R or Python (the `psych` or `ltm` packages;
`pingouin` in Python) or in Minitab (Item Analysis, which reports Cronbach's α and
item–total correlations) — the numbers agree.

### 2.3 Capstone review

| Metric | Target | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|
| First-submission pass rate (120 points and every ★ item) | 50–80% | Above 90% on ≥ 30 reviews: reviewer drift toward leniency — calibration before content. Below 45%: read the ★ failure profile before anything else |
| Eventual pass rate (after the one revise-and-resubmit cycle) | ≥ 85% | Below that, the resubmission cycle is not doing its job; check whether the first review's "highest-leverage fix first" was written as the rubric requires |
| ★ item failure profile | No single ★ item accounts for more than 30% of first-submission failures across ≥ 30 reviews | ★ A2 → Module 4 and the residual-diagnostics lab; ★ I1 → Module 5 and the confirmation-run discipline from the practicum; ★ C1 → Module 6 and the Tollgate 5 record of days live; ★ F1 → Module 8 §8.7 and whether the Finance basis was actually written at Tollgate 2; ★ L1 → Immersion 2 and whether the canvas was dated at Tollgate 1. The module goes on the improvement board as a PDCA item with the profile as its measure |
| A3 restraint earned | ≥ 80% of submissions | A cohort that cannot show one place it chose not to model is a Module 4 "when not to model" question and mirrors the exam metric above |
| Data-ethics stops | Tracked, by type (removed points · changed definition · reconstructed baseline · experiment re-run until it agreed) | Any rise is a Module 3 and Tollgate 2 question about how baselines are being fixed, and a Module 5 question about the experiment logbook; the individual case follows the terms of certification §4 |
| Resubmission rate by reviewer and by Master Black Belt coach | Read against caseload and vertical before anything else | A reviewer whose resubmission rate sits 20 points from the bench median re-calibrates on the anchor set |

The fourth data-ethics type is new at this level and named on purpose: an experiment whose
"bad runs" were dropped or repeated until the effects came out as hoped is the DOE form of
removing inconvenient points. The experiment logbook, with every run in the order it was run,
is what the reviewer reads against the analysis.

### 2.4 The three observed requirements

Requirements 3, 4 and 5 are scored pass/redo by an observer or a supervising reviewer, and a
redo is a second attempt, not a failure. The rates are Level 2 because they measure what the
candidate can do under observation; the calendar and follow-through behaviors they produce
are in Level 3.

| Requirement | Metric | Target | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DOE practicum** (Immersion 1, week 10) | First-attempt pass ÷ candidates presenting; redo pass ÷ redos | 70–90% first attempt; ≥ 95% on redo | Above 95% first attempt on ≥ 30 candidates: the practicum rubric is not discriminating — planted difficulty (a confounded alias, a curvature the center points should catch) is raised, not the pass line. Below 60%: the run-of-show is not leaving time for the confirmation run |
| DOE practicum, by platform | Physical apparatus (catapult or helicopter kit) against the process simulator (transactional cohorts) | Within 15 points once each has 30 | A wider gap is a platform question — kit reliability, simulator noise level — never a cohort question. The simulator's noise is set so its signal-to-noise matches the kits'; if the gap persists, that setting is the first thing checked |
| DOE practicum, redo reasons | Coded: prediction written after the result · re-runs of a "bad shot" · alias pattern misread · operating window stated outside the design region · effects reported without the residual plot | Tracked | A reason above 40% of redos across ≥ 30 candidates names the Module 5 section to rework; the logbook discipline (prediction *before* the run) is the one that matters most, because it is the data-ethics rule in miniature |
| **Change-leadership practicum** (Immersion 2, week 15) | First-attempt pass ÷ candidates; redo pass ÷ redos; scored on the role-plays, the facilitated 20 minutes and the timed readout | 70–90% first attempt; ≥ 95% on redo | Redo reasons coded per Module 8: responded before diagnosing · persuaded an interest problem · facilitated content · readout opened with the fishbone · decision not asked for. A reason above 40% is a Module 8 section, and the scenario cards are checked for a planted type that is too easy to name |
| **Kaizen facilitation** (requirement 4) | Events held, observed or video-reviewed and scored ÷ candidates, by closure; pass on the facilitation rubric ÷ events scored | Completion ≥ 90%; pass ≥ 85% | Completion below 90% is almost always a venue problem (employer would not release a team; partner-pool event not matched by week 13) and is reported by venue — employer against partner pool — before anything else. A pass rate below 85% is read against the four rubric areas (preparation · facilitation · safety of the room · closure) |
| Kaizen facilitation, the theatre check | Share of scored events with ≥ 2 weeks of charted baseline before day 1 and a day-5 verification on the same metric | 100% | An event without a charted baseline is scored as theatre per Module 7 §7.8 and is redone; the count of such events is published because it is the cheapest honesty signal the level has |
| Redo turnaround | Days from a redo decision to the redo | Median ≤ 21 days | A redo that waits months is a candidate who has moved on; the practicum owner schedules the redo before the candidate leaves the room |

*TXN example (illustrative).* A shared-services cohort of 12 runs Immersion 1 on the process
simulator. Nine pass first attempt; three redo, and all three wrote the prediction after the
confirmation run came in. The redo pass is 3 of 3 the following week. At n = 3 that reason is
one lab's debrief; at n = 30 with the same reason it is Module 5 §5.11 and the logbook
template, and it goes on the board. Nobody in the three was careless: the run-of-show had put
the prediction step after the run, and the fix is in the run-of-show.

## Level 3 — Behavior

What a Black Belt is supposed to *do* is lead a cross-functional project to closure on a
calendar with a team they do not manage, leave a control plan running for at least two
months before anyone scores it, close out a kaizen event rather than announce one, and sit in
a reviewer's chair without coaching from it. Because the calendar is fixed (tollgates in
weeks 2, 5, 7, 12 and 16; closure within nine months of week 16), slippage is measurable, and
its causes — sponsor time, decision rights, data access, IT change windows, Finance basis —
are designable. None of them is the candidate's character, and the cohort report never reads
that way.

| Metric | Definition | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tollgates held on calendar** | A gate held in its calendar week (or the wave variant's stated week) with the sponsor or named deputy in the room; a hold cleared within 10 business days still counts as on calendar | Reviewer gate record | ≥ 85% of gates; reported per gate. Tollgate 2 (an MSA plan for every metric and a baseline with the right distribution by week 5) and Tollgate 4 (an experiment run and analyzed by week 12) are where it breaks — the first is a data-access failure from the charter, the second is usually a change window the charter never named |
| Sponsor decisions by gate | Proceed · proceed with conditions · hold · re-scope · stop, per gate | Gate record | Tracked. A hold rate above 40% at any gate on ≥ 30 projects is a content or readiness question for that phase, named on the board |
| Escalations used | Escalation-path entries invoked per project, by function escalated to | Decision log (rubric ★ L1) | Tracked, not targeted. Zero escalations across a cohort is not good news; it usually means the decision-rights table was never tested |
| Master Black Belt sessions held | Each of the six monthly sessions held within its calendar window, week 1 to closure | Coaching record | ≥ 90% |
| **Project closure within 9 months** | Project submitted for independent review within nine months of week 16 ÷ charters signed at Tollgate 1; and passed (eventually) ÷ charters at Tollgate 1 | Assessment record | Submitted ≥ 80%; passed ≥ 70%. Re-scopes count in the denominator; honorable stops are shown as their own count with the reason (problem gone · priority changed · cause outside authority · sponsor left · process retired by a capital project) |
| Median days from Tollgate 5 to submission | Calendar days | Assessment record | Tracked. A median above 120 days, or two consecutive months with no movement on more than a quarter of open files, is the "project that never ends" and triggers the three-way scope conversation the coaching system specifies |
| **Control plan live ≥ 60 days at submission** | At submission, the monitoring record covers days 0–60 after the handover date in the sponsor attestation, the named owner (not the Black Belt) kept the chart, the response plan is written and the owner can explain the chart alone | Project file, Part A3; rubric ★ C1 and C2 | ≥ 90% of submissions. This is ★ C1's threshold, so a passing project always has it; the figure is over *all* submissions to see how many arrive short and go back |
| Median days from Tollgate 4 to handover | Calendar days from the sponsor's implementation approval to the process owner's signature | Part A3 | Tracked. Handover slipping past 90 days after Tollgate 4 eats the 60-day monitoring window and is where closure is lost |
| **Kaizen event follow-up completed at 30 days** | 30-day list items closed by day 30 ÷ items listed at the day-5 report-out; and the 30-day metric check recorded on the same chart as the baseline | Event report, kaizen leader's kit; observer's follow-up note | Items closed ≥ 80% per event; metric check recorded for ≥ 90% of events. A list longer than 10 items is a closure failure on day 5, not a follow-up failure on day 30, and is coded that way |
| Kaizen event result held at 60 and 90 days | The event's metric at the post-event level at the 60- and 90-day checks | Event chart | Tracked; the reason an event's gain is lost (backfill withdrawn · IT item never closed · standard work not owned) is coded and read against Module 7 §7.8 |
| **Green Belt tollgate review agreement** (requirement 5) | For each of the two supervised reviews: the candidate's per-item status (*evidenced · at risk · not evidenced*) against the supervising Master Black Belt's on the same gate, exact agreement on every ★ item; the count of times the candidate coached from the chair, as the supervising reviewer recorded it; the data-ethics line and the sponsor's decision recorded | Supervising reviewer's record | ★ agreement ≥ 90% across the pair; zero coaching-from-the-chair incidents on the second review; both records complete. Agreement rising from the first review to the second is the learning signal; a first-review ★ agreement below 75% across ≥ 30 candidates is a Lab 16 anchor-calibration question |
| Coaching hours logged | Dated coaching log hours with at least one team member or Green Belt (rubric L3), carried to the badge | Coaching log | Tracked; the badge shows the hours, the plan does not target them |
| **Second project or coaching within 24 months** | Certified Black Belts who submit a documented new project on the A3, are named coach on a certified Green Belt charter, sit on a reviewer bench, or log ≥ 40 coaching hours within 24 months of award — the recertification evidence, arriving early | Renewal record; guild record | ≥ 40% — the single best behavior-change indicator this level has, because it is unprompted and the credential does not need it for another year |
| Master Black Belt progression | Black Belts admitted to the Master Black Belt fellowship within 36 months | Enrollment data | Tracked, not targeted — a commercial metric, not a learning one |

Three things Level 3 does not count. A project that closed as documented learning without the
credential is not a failure of the candidate and is reported as a stop with its reason. The
Green Belt whose gate a candidate sat is unaffected by the candidate's record — the
supervising reviewer's record is the record of record; the candidate's is training. And a
kaizen event at a partner-pool organization is measured on the same rows as one at the
candidate's employer, with the venue shown, because a host that would not close its own
30-day list is a screening finding for the pool, not a mark against the candidate.

*HC example (illustrative).* A hospital-system cohort of 10 charters across three sites.
Gates on calendar: Tollgate 1 10 of 10; Tollgate 2 7 of 10 — the three holds were all the
same hold, a medication-record timestamp whose provenance walk needed the vendor's field
definitions, sized at three weeks; Tollgate 3 9 of 9 (one honorable stop when a reorganization
moved the sponsor out of the service line — reason *sponsor left*); Tollgate 4 7 of 9, the two
misses on projects whose pharmacy configuration change fell in the IT change window of week
14, not week 12; Tollgate 5 9 of 9. At the nine-month deadline: 8 submitted, 7 passed, 1 in
its resubmission cycle. The cohort report puts two sentences in front of the executive
sponsor, by function — one for the medication-record team, one for IT change management — and
the next cohort's charters name the change window at the charter. Nobody on the cohort was
slow; two functions were never asked for a date.

## Level 4 — Results (stated as fact, labeled as such)

Black Belt produces validated results and the program says exactly which kind each one is.
The candidate's own number never appears on any program surface; Finance's figure or the
mission-metric executive's does. The charter's projection — the figure that met the ≥ $75K
threshold — is a projection, labeled at the charter, and is **never reported as a result**.
What is claimed, and how it is labeled:

| Claimed outcome | Evidence basis | How it is labeled on every surface |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor-verified projects | Part A signed; the reviewer's ★ I1 and ★ C1 read against it | Fact — count and share of closed projects |
| Financial impact by class (hard · soft · cost avoidance · revenue) | Part B, Finance partner's signature, measured over the evidence window; project costs netted (rubric F2) | **"Finance-validated, measured over N weeks, net of project cost; not annualized."** Classes are never added into one headline; soft benefit is stated in hours or units first and in money only at Finance's rate, labeled soft; revenue only when booked in the window |
| Clinical or service impact | Part C, mission-metric executive's signature, guardrail stated | **"Sponsor-verified, mission metric; no money claimed."** Reported in the metric's own unit — minutes to first antibiotic, disputes per 1,000 invoices, % on time — with the same standing as a financial row, never converted to money on a program surface |
| Sponsor-verified, no benefit claimed | Part A only; an honest failure (a confirmation run that did not hold the predicted window, read honestly under ★ I1) or a project without a benefit class | Fact — count. A passed project whose experiment did not deliver the operating window is in this row, in the open |
| Kaizen event results | The event report's day-5 verification and 30-day check, in the metric's unit | **"Event result, 30-day check, in the metric's unit; no money claimed."** An event inside the project's scope contributes to the project's Part B or Part C rows and is not counted again; an event outside it, or at a partner-pool host, is a count with its metric result and nothing more |
| **Day-90 sustainment** | The reviewer of record's `sustained` flag, set from the monitoring record per the sustainment check | **Rate = `true` ÷ projects checked**, with `unverified`, `not-applicable` and `pending` shown as their own counts, never folded silently into the denominator |
| **One-year sustainment** | The reviewer of record's second check at day 365 from handover, same five questions plus one (has the owner changed, and did the chart continue through the change), same flag values | Rate = `true` ÷ projects checked, shown beside the day-90 rate with its own n — the two are never averaged |
| Charter projection against Finance's run rate | The charter's projected annualized figure against Finance's run-rate annualization at closure, per project | **Cohort report only**, both columns labeled *projected*; a distribution (how many closed below half the charter figure, how many above it), never a mean. Never on the outcomes page, which carries no annualized figure of any kind |

**The `sustained` flag** is set only by the reviewer of record, only from the monitoring
record, and never changes the credential — the Green Belt sustainment check's procedure,
timings and flag rules apply unchanged, with two Black Belt notes. First, because ★ C1 needs
60 days live at submission and the review takes up to 20 business days, day 90 often falls
before or at the award; a badge issued before the check resolves reads `pending` and updates.
Second, the one-year check is sent by the same reviewer of record; if they have left the
bench, the assessment lead assigns another bench Master Black Belt, and the record says so.
The reason categories where a flag is `false` are the Green Belt's — owner left without
replacement · monitoring stopped · countermeasure removed · metric drifted with no response ·
process changed — plus one the one-year check adds: *standard work not owned after the owner
changed*. A rising share of any one reason is a Module 6 or Module 8 curriculum question, not
a candidate question. A false flag at n = 1 tells you about one handover; a reason category at
n = 30 tells you about the course.

### Attribution discipline

The rule inherited from White Belt and Green Belt, now with a portfolio attached:

1. **Counted once, at the level that closed it, with the origin noted.** A Black Belt project
   that grew from a Green Belt project credits the Green Belt for its own measured window
   before the Black Belt charter date and nothing after; the Black Belt's rows start at the
   charter date and the cohort report shows "grew from project [ref]". A White Belt
   observation or a Yellow Belt mini-project in the lineage is shown as origin and carries
   no amount.
2. **Once across the portfolio.** Every ledger line a Black Belt project claims is listed in
   the cohort report with the other projects — Green Belt, Black Belt, consultant-led or
   system implementation — that touch it in the same period, and the split Finance initialed
   (charter document §4; form B4). Where two projects claim the same line and no split exists,
   Finance signs one and the other's ★ F1 is unearned; the cohort report says which.
3. **Finance's figure, never the candidate's.** Where Finance reduces or declines a claim, the
   reduced figure is the only one that exists on any program surface. Where the sponsor's
   portfolio dashboard already double-counts, the cohort report is the place that does not,
   and the report says so.
4. **Classes are never added.** Hard, soft, cost avoidance and revenue are reported in separate
   rows with separate n; "total impact" and "portfolio value" do not appear. Mission-metric
   results sit beside them in their own units.
5. **Nothing is annualized or extrapolated on a program surface.** Measured window, dated,
   with n, net of project cost. Finance may annualize by run rate for the client's own
   dashboard, capped at twelve months from implementation, no roll-out assumed; the cohort
   report shows both, labeled; the outcomes page carries the measured figure only. A result
   "when applied to all four plants" is scope the charter did not have and is not a result.
6. **Mission-metric equivalents are equivalents at the charter, not conversions at closure.**
   The clinical or service equivalence test (charter document §3.2) decides whether a project
   is Black-Belt-scale; it does not turn minutes into money. A conversion Finance chooses to
   make is a Part B row with Finance's basis, labeled, and the mission metric keeps its own
   row in its own unit regardless.
7. **Adjacent slices are split in writing.** Two candidates on adjacent segments of one value
   stream with a shared sponsor each carry their own metric and their own Part B; an overlap
   is split on the form with Finance's initials.
8. **Nothing from a Green Belt the candidate coached or reviewed credits the candidate.** The
   Green Belt project's result is the Green Belt's, on the Green Belt cohort's report; the
   Black Belt's L3 evidence is the coaching log, not the coached project's number.
9. **Estimates are labeled estimates.** At Black Belt two exist: the charter projection
   ("projected at charter, run rate, assumes current volume") and Finance's run-rate at closure
   ("projected, assumes current volume"). A candidate-reported figure is not an estimate; it
   is not published.

*MFG example (illustrative).* A plant cohort of 8 charters inside a site portfolio of 14
improvement projects (the other six run by Green Belts and one by an outside consultant).
Seven submitted within nine months; six passed, one of them after resubmission; one stopped
honorably at Tollgate 3 when a capital project scheduled the line for replacement (reason
*process retired by a capital project*). Of the six: four Finance-validated hard rows — scrap
at the molding cell, overtime on the finishing line, expedited freight, warranty returns —
measured over windows of 8 to 16 weeks, net of trial materials and gauge calibration, reported
as four rows with their windows and never summed; two soft rows in hours; one cost-avoidance
row for a temporary hire budgeted and not made, dated before the project; and one project whose
confirmation run did not hold the predicted operating window, read honestly under ★ I1 and
certified with a zero-benefit memo Finance signed. The molding-cell scrap project and a Green
Belt changeover project both touched press time: Finance signed press-hours to the changeover
project and scrap to the Black Belt project, and both leads and the cost accountant initialed
the split. One project grew from a Green Belt project certified fourteen months earlier; the
Green Belt's ten-week window stays on the Green Belt cohort's report, and the Black Belt rows
begin at the Black Belt charter date. At the charter the eight projects had projected $86K to
$210K annualized; at closure Finance's run rate on the four hard-row projects ran from 45% to
130% of the charter projection, and the cohort report shows both columns labeled *projected*.
Day 90: six checks sent; five `true`, one `unverified` — monitoring stopped for a line move and
restarted with no record of the gap (*reported held, no record*). One year: `pending` for all
six. The cohort report sentence: *"Eight chartered, six certified, five of six sustained at
day 90, four with hard benefit Finance signed over their measured windows, net of project
cost; one-year checks due next quarter."* The outcomes page shows the same facts as counts and
rates with n = 6, pooled with other cohorts, and no dollar total.

### What Level 4 refuses

- No training ROI ratio, at any n. The program cannot isolate its own effect from the
  sponsor's, the team's and the candidate's, and any figure that pretended to would be an
  estimate presented as fact.
- No "average savings per Black Belt" headline, which is the exact number the market asks
  for at this level. Medians with interquartile range, per class, once n allows — the
  benchmark library's rule.
- No portfolio total, no annualized figure and no charter projection on the outcomes page.
- No testimonial in place of a number, and no number without its class, window, n and the
  words *net of project cost*.

## Coaching quality and the reviewer-of-record audit

The instructor, reviewer and coaching system names what it measures about itself; this
section sets the thresholds and adds the audits that protect the credential's independence.
The reviewer of record's name is on the certificate and the verification page, which is why
the audit of that reviewer is heavier here than at Green Belt: a signature the program cannot
show was independent is a signature the market should not trust.

| Measure | Definition | Target | If missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reviewer-of-record audit** | Quarterly, the assessment lead samples 20% of live assignments (minimum three) and checks: the conflicts were checked and logged at assignment in week 1; the reviewer did not coach this candidate, this cohort or this candidate at any level in the last 24 months, and has no employment or consulting tie to the candidate's organization in the last three years; the name that will sign the certificate matches the assignment log and the scoring record; the project file carries a cohort ID and no instructor or coach name; no coaching note or session record sits in the project file; the reviewer attended no coaching session, immersion, live lab or tollgate meeting; every gate note is ≤ 5 sentences and states status without advice (any "try", "consider", "you should" is a finding); the final feedback names the Master Black Belt fork or the specialist fork where the rubric says to | Zero breaches | A breach re-assigns the file to a fresh reviewer who re-scores blind; the original score is voided; the breach goes on the improvement board with its cause. Two breaches by one reviewer in a year end their bench place for the year; their name stays on certificates already issued, with the period of service noted |
| Reviewer calibration agreement | Every active reviewer scores the four-file anchor set (clean pass; high-score fail on an undiagnosed model; honest-failure pass; Finance-unsigned fail) each quarter they review: within 5 points of the anchor total on all four, exact agreement on every ★ item | 100% of active reviewers calibrated in the quarter; drift events tracked by reviewer | A drift event stops that reviewer's live reviews until re-calibrated; a second in twelve months adds second-reading on their next three reviews |
| Coach–reviewer ★ agreement | The Master Black Belt coach's last-session status per ★ item (*model diagnosed · experiment or pilot read against prediction · monitoring live 60 days · Finance basis written · canvas revised and episode documented*) against the reviewer's decision | ≥ 85% | Calibration session for the coach on the anchor set, then an observed session; never a ranking |
| Supervising-reviewer agreement (requirement 5) | The candidate's gate record against the supervising Master Black Belt's, as defined in Level 3 | Tracked as the candidate's measure; the supervising reviewer's own calibration is the quarterly one above | — |
| Practicum observer agreement | Two observers score the same DOE presentation or role-play on a 10% sample of each immersion | Pass/redo agreement ≥ 90% | Below that, the practicum rubric's language is not decisive and is rewritten before the next immersion; the anchor recordings are re-cut |
| Reviewer turnaround | Gate notes within 5 business days; final reviews within 20 | Each ≥ 95% | A reviewer above the live-file cap the coaching system sets is the usual cause; the assignment log shows it |
| Appeal re-score difference | Stage-1 blind re-scores against the original | Within 5 points, ★ items agree, on ≥ 90% of appeals | Below that, the anchor set is not covering the live cases; add an anchor file from the appeals |
| Coaching-session record completeness | All fields on a 20% sample, including "what the candidate decided" | ≥ 95% | The decision field missing is the one that matters — the coach is deciding, and at this level the decision is which model to fit or which design to run |

None of these ranks instructors, coaches or reviewers publicly. The outcomes page carries the
program-wide figures; each reviewer and coach sees their own, and a reviewer's audit findings
are never published against their name.

## The annual 10% re-review

Per the credential specification the Black Belt crosswalk and credential document carries
(the procedure is the Green Belt's, in its
[crosswalk and credential document](../../green-belt/bok-crosswalk-and-credential.md) §2.6,
with the additions below): each year the assessment lead draws a random 10% of projects
certified in the previous twelve months (at least five, or all when fewer), stratified by
vertical and by impact class (financial · mission metric · both · none claimed) where numbers
allow; a calibrated Master Black Belt who was not the original reviewer, instructor or coach
re-scores blind; the sponsor is re-contacted to confirm the attestation and the day-90 answer;
and — the Black Belt addition — **the Finance partner or mission-metric executive is
re-contacted** to confirm that the Part B or Part C figure stands on the basis they signed
and that no later portfolio reconciliation reassigned the ledger line. At Black Belt volumes
(cohorts capped at 16, most companies sending two to eight seats a year) the sample will be
"all of them" for the first years, and the page says so.

| Published each year, with n and period | Target |
|---|---|
| Sample size and the population it was drawn from | 10%, minimum five |
| ★ item agreement rate between original and re-score | ≥ 95% |
| Mean absolute score difference | ≤ 5 points on 150 |
| Share of pairs more than 7 points apart | ≤ 10% |
| Sponsor re-contact confirmation rate | ≥ 90%; a sponsor who cannot be reached is a count, not a `no` |
| Finance or validator re-contact confirmation rate | ≥ 90%; a figure Finance later reduced is a count with the reason category (portfolio reassignment · basis revised · window restated), and the reduced figure replaces the original on every program surface |
| Integrity referrals | Count, with no detail |

A ★ disagreement goes to a third calibrated Master Black Belt, two of three decide, and the
holder's credential is unchanged whatever the outcome — the error is the program's, and the
holder is told so in writing with the no-fee coached resubmission offer. The reviewer of
record's name stays on the certificate. The re-review's job is to audit reviewers, and its
figures are published so that an un-audited badge is never read as a weaker one and a named
reviewer is never read as a rubber stamp.

## Program-level aggregation — the published figures

These are the Black Belt slots on the published outcomes page. The page's rules apply: the
definition is one click from the figure; below minimum n the slot shows *"n = X, not yet
reportable"* and is never hidden; figures are program-wide by level and by vertical where n
allows; no cohort, client or reviewer is identifiable; nothing is annualized; the bad quarter
is published with a link to its board item.

| Figure | Definition | Minimum n to publish |
|---|---|---|
| Exam first-attempt pass rate | Passed on attempt 1 ÷ all first attempts | 50 attempts |
| Eventual pass rate | Passed within the included retake ÷ all who attempted | 50 |
| Pre/post gain | Mean post − mean pre on matched PRE items, with the pre score shown | 50 matched pairs |
| Capstone first-submission pass rate | Passed at first review ÷ first reviews | 30 reviews |
| Capstone eventual pass rate | Passed after the included resubmission ÷ projects reviewed | 30 |
| **Sampling rate** | Projects independently reviewed by a named Master Black Belt ÷ projects submitted — always 100% at this level, and published so the reader can see it | always |
| ★ item failure share | First-submission failures by ★ item | 30 first submissions |
| DOE practicum pass rate | First-attempt pass ÷ candidates presenting, with the redo pass rate beside it, by platform where n allows | 30 candidates |
| Change-leadership practicum pass rate | First-attempt pass ÷ candidates, with the redo pass rate | 30 candidates |
| Kaizen facilitation completion | Events held, observed and scored ÷ candidates certified or stopped; the theatre-check count always | 30 candidates |
| Kaizen 30-day follow-up | Events with the 30-day list ≥ 80% closed and the metric check recorded ÷ events scored | 30 events |
| Green Belt tollgate review ★ agreement | As defined in Level 3, first and second review shown separately | 30 candidates (60 reviews) |
| Tollgates held on calendar | As defined in Level 3, per gate | 30 projects (150 gates) |
| Closure within 9 months | Submitted, and passed, ÷ charters at Tollgate 1, from cohorts whose nine-month window has closed | 30 charters |
| Control plan live ≥ 60 days at submission | As defined in Level 3 | 30 submissions |
| **Day-90 sustainment rate** | `true` ÷ projects checked, with `unverified`, `not-applicable` and `pending` as counts | 30 resolved checks |
| **One-year sustainment rate** | The day-365 check, same rule, shown beside the day-90 rate with its own n | 30 resolved checks |
| Sponsor-verified share | Part A signed ÷ projects certified, and the verified-impact distribution (financial · mission metric · both · none claimed) | 30 certified |
| Finance-validated impact, by class | Per class: number of projects, median measured benefit net of project cost with interquartile range, range of evidence windows in weeks | 10 validated projects per class; below that, the count only |
| Mission-metric results | Number of projects; per vertical, the median relative change in the primary metric with its range and the guardrail's direction | 10 projects per vertical for the median; the count always |
| Honest-failure share | Projects certified with no benefit claimed ÷ projects certified | 30 certified — published because a program with a zero here is not reading its confirmation runs honestly |
| Master Black Belt sessions held | Sessions held in window ÷ sessions due | always |
| Coach–reviewer ★ agreement | As defined above | 30 projects |
| Reviewer-of-record audit breaches | Breaches found ÷ files sampled, per quarter | always, from the first quarter |
| Annual re-review | Sample n, ★ agreement, mean absolute difference, sponsor and Finance confirmation rates, integrity referrals | the year's sample (≥ 5) |
| Reviewer turnaround | Share of gate notes and reviews inside the published turnaround | always |
| Second project or coaching within 24 months | As defined in Level 3 | 50 certified holders |
| L1 items 1–5 and 7 | Means; items 8–12 shown as a range, because they diagnose client organizations, not the course | 50 responses |

**The honest n = 0 state.** At launch every slot above is on the page with `n = 0` and the
outcomes page's sentence: *"This program has not yet run a live cohort. These are the numbers
we will publish, with their definitions. The first update follows the first cohort's close."*
The Black Belt slots carry a second sentence, because their clocks are the longest in the
program: *"Closure figures lag a cohort's start by up to thirteen months, day-90 sustainment
by up to sixteen, and one-year sustainment by about two years; at this level's cohort sizes
the minimum n for most project figures takes several cohorts to reach. Those slots stay at
'not yet reportable' until the windows close and the n is met, and a figure that arrives
sooner was not measured the way its definition says."* A Black Belt page that shows every
project slot empty for two years after launch, with the exam, practicum and audit slots
filling around them, is what makes the same page believable when the sustainment slots fill.
