# Green Belt Tollgate Checklists — Tollgates 1 to 4

*Four gates on the cohort calendar (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8), each ten minutes and one page, each with
the sponsor in the room. Every checklist item is keyed to the rubric item it evidences —
[`review-rubric.md`](review-rubric.md). Mandatory rubric items are marked ★. The charter these
gates refer to is [`charter-template.md`](charter-template.md).*

---

## The standard: ten minutes, one page, sponsor in the room

**One page.** You bring the tollgate one-pager for that phase — the A3 or storyboard page,
exhibits over prose. If it needs a second page, cut until it does not. The page is the record;
a deck is not.

**Ten minutes.** You present for ten minutes, timed. Then ten minutes of questions, then five
minutes in which the sponsor decides. Twenty-five minutes per project; the calendar holds
regardless of how the project feels.

**Who is in the room and what each does**

| Role | At the gate |
|---|---|
| **Green Belt** | Presents the page; answers questions; writes the decision on the page before leaving |
| **Sponsor** | Asks; decides; signs the decision line. The gate exists for this person |
| **Process owner** | From Tollgate 2 on: confirms the map and the baseline are the process as worked |
| **Tollgate reviewer** | A certified Black Belt or Master Black Belt who is not your instructor or coach — usually the reviewer who scores the closed project. Asks questions; records; does not coach |
| **Instructor / coach** | Present; silent during the decision; coaches afterwards, in a separate conversation |
| **Black Belt candidate** *(where assigned)* | May sit as tollgate reviewer under the calibrated reviewer's supervision, as part of their Green Belt tollgate-review requirement |

The tollgate reviewer's record is formative. It tells you which rubric items are evidenced so
far and which are at risk. It is not the score; the score is given once, on the closed
project, against the full rubric.

**The five decisions a sponsor can make**

| Decision | Meaning | What happens to the calendar |
|---|---|---|
| **Proceed** | The phase is done to the standard | Next phase starts this week |
| **Proceed with conditions** | Done, with named items due by a named date | Next phase starts; conditions are checked at the next gate |
| **Hold** | One specific item is missing; return within 10 business days with that item | The next gate stays on the calendar; the hold is cleared in a 10-minute follow-up |
| **Re-scope** | The problem or scope changes; the charter is re-signed | Return to the affected gate; the 6-month closure window still applies |
| **Stop** | The sponsor closes the project honorably (problem gone, priority changed, cause outside their authority) | You are matched to a new project through the partner pool or move to the Practicum track — see [`partner-pool-and-practicum-track.md`](partner-pool-and-practicum-track.md) |

A hold is not a failure. Most projects take one. What the reviewer records on a hold is the
item, not a judgment.

**What the reviewer records, at every gate**

- Rubric items due at this gate, each marked *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced*, with
  one sentence per ★ item.
- The sponsor's decision and any conditions, with dates.
- A data-ethics line: *none observed* or the specific concern. Evidence of removed data
  points, a changed operational definition, or a baseline reconstructed from memory is
  recorded and sent to the assessment lead the same day. At a tollgate this is a warning and
  a conversation; at final review it is a stop.
- A one-line coaching flag for the coach — a topic, not advice (the reviewer does not coach).
- Where the project reveals a Black-Belt-sized problem: the note that the Black Belt
  invitation applies.

The record goes to you, your sponsor and your coach within 2 business days, and to the final
reviewer's file.

## Tollgate 1 — Define (week 2)

**Rubric items due:** D1, D2, D3, D4. **Started, not due:** M1 (the current-state map is
this week's project work; the reviewer notes whether it has begun as observed, not as
documented).

### Before the gate — what you bring on the one page

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Problem statement: what, where, since when, how big, why it matters; no cause, blame or solution; the team has signed it | D1 |
| ☐ | Charter, sponsor-signed after the week 1 rewrite: start and stop points; in and out of scope; team and roles; timeline against the cohort calendar | D2 |
| ☐ | Primary metric named with its operational definition, basis and data type; guardrail metric named | D2, M2 |
| ☐ | Baseline stated as known or as "collected weeks 3–4"; target labeled provisional or confirmed | D2 |
| ☐ | At least one customer requirement translated to a measurable CTQ with target and specification source; the customer named (internal counts) | D3 |
| ☐ | SIPOC at 5–8 steps, built with someone who works the process daily | D4 |
| ☐ | Stakeholder map with at least one named resistance risk and the planned response | D4 |
| ☐ | Data access confirmed: who gives you the extract or the log, by what date | M2 |
| ☐ | The first go-see done: date, what you watched, one thing the documented process did not show | M1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Is this the problem you want solved, at this scope, with these start and stop points —
   and do you have authority over everything between them?
2. Is the primary metric the number you would want to see move, defined the way it is
   written? (If the sponsor wants a different number, that is a re-scope now, not at
   Tollgate 4.)
3. Are the named people released for the stated hours, and is the data access real?
4. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

### The reviewer records

D1–D4 status; whether the charter carries the sponsor's post-rewrite signature (D2 is not
evidenced without it); whether the operational definition would let a stranger collect the
same number; whether any charter smell from the template's checklist survived the clinic;
decision and conditions; data-ethics line (at this gate, usually about how the baseline will
be collected, not how it was); coaching flag.

### Common holds at Tollgate 1

- Problem statement with a smuggled cause or a "lack of".
- Metric named but not operationally defined; or defined so that before and after could be
  collected differently.
- No data access date. "IT will send it" without a name and a date is a hold.
- Stop point outside the sponsor's authority.
- Stakeholder map with no resistance risk named. Every project has one; a map without one has
  not been thought about.

## Tollgate 2 — Measure (week 4)

**Rubric items due:** M1, M2, ★ M3, M4. **Revisited:** D2 (the target is confirmed or revised
against the measured baseline).

### Before the gate — what you bring on the one page

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Current-state map or value stream map as observed (dated go-see; the people who work it helped); lead time, %C&A and process cycle efficiency with basis stated (calendar vs working time) | M1 |
| ☐ | Data collection plan: who, what, when, how, how many; sampling stated; the operational definition unchanged since Tollgate 1 or changed with the change dated and both versions shown | M2 |
| ☐ | ★ MSA run on the primary metric: Gage R&R (variable) or attribute agreement (pass/fail, codes) — result interpreted in a sentence; if the system failed, what you did before collecting the baseline. Or the written reason MSA is not applicable, for the reviewer to accept: a system timestamp with the entry step checked counts; "we trust the system" does not | ★ M3 |
| ☐ | Baseline control chart on the right chart for the data type (I-MR, X̄-R, p, np, c, u); stability stated in a sentence; special causes investigated and explained, never deleted | M4 |
| ☐ | Capability only after stability: Cp/Cpk or DPMO/sigma level with the specification source named (the customer's limit, not the control limit) | M4 |
| ☐ | Guardrail metric baseline | D2 |
| ☐ | Target confirmed or revised now that the baseline is measured, with the sponsor's initials | D2 |
| ☐ | Finance partner (or mission-metric validator) has seen the metric and stated, in a line, the calculation basis they will accept at closure | C3 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you accept this as *the* baseline — collected this way, over this period, with these
   points in it? (After this gate the baseline is fixed. No point is removed later; no
   definition changes between before and after.)
2. Do you accept the measurement system result and what was done about it? If the MSA
   failed and the team fixed the gauge or the coding before measuring, that is the project
   working as designed; the sponsor hears it here.
3. Given the measured baseline, is the target still the target? Confirm or revise it now.
4. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

### The reviewer records

M1–M4 status with a sentence on ★ M3 (attempted and interpreted; not applicable for an
accepted reason; or not evidenced); whether stability was stated before capability was
claimed; which chart was used and whether it matches the data type; whether the
specification source is the customer's and not the control limits; the target decision and
the sponsor's initials; data-ethics line (any points removed? any definition drift since
Tollgate 1? any baseline period chosen after seeing the data?); coaching flag.

### Common holds at Tollgate 2

- MSA not attempted because "the data comes from the system". The reviewer asks who enters
  the timestamp and whether that entry was checked.
- Capability stated on an unstable process. The hold is: state stability first, investigate
  the special causes, then decide whether capability means anything yet.
- p chart on continuous data, or I-MR on daily proportions with wildly varying denominators.
- A baseline period that starts conveniently after a bad month. The reviewer asks why that
  start date.
- No Finance partner line. The calculation basis argued at closure is a closure delay.

## Tollgate 3 — Analyze (week 6)

**Rubric items due:** A1, A2, ★ A3, A4.

### Before the gate — what you bring on the one page

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Fishbone (or equivalent) → C&E matrix or working-level FMEA → the short list of checkable causes; the narrowing logic visible on the page, not in your head | A1 |
| ☐ | Stratified graphical analysis that shows where the variation lives: box plots by shift, day, branch, product; multi-vari; scatter where a continuous X exists; every chart labeled with metric, units, period and n | A2 |
| ☐ | ★ At least one cause tested with an appropriate method — two-sample t, one-way ANOVA, chi-square, correlation or simple regression, or a designed comparison; assumptions checked and stated; effect size with its units; the sentence you would tell your sponsor, in plain language. A p-value alone does not verify anything | ★ A3 |
| ☐ | Which test and why, from the hypothesis-test selector; what would have changed the choice (data type, number of groups, paired or not) | A3 |
| ☐ | Causes tested and rejected, shown with the same care as the ones that survived; sample-size limits acknowledged ("with n = 18 per group, we could only have detected a difference of about X") | A4 |
| ☐ | Causes outside scope or authority named, with a proposed home (parking lot, escalation, Black Belt project) | A4, D2 |
| ☐ | A prediction: if the verified cause is removed, the primary metric moves from [baseline] to about [value] — written before Improve starts | I2 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you accept the verified cause or causes as the target for countermeasures? The
   sponsor is not asked to agree with a p-value; the sponsor is asked whether the sentence
   in plain language, with its effect size, is something they would act on.
2. For causes that did not verify — including any the sponsor believed in — do you accept
   that they are off the list, on this evidence, for now?
3. For causes outside scope: escalate, park, or re-scope?
4. Decision: proceed / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

### The reviewer records

A1–A4 status with a sentence on ★ A3: the method, whether it fits the data, whether
assumptions were checked, whether the effect size and plain-language conclusion are present
— and, explicitly, whether the countermeasure direction the team is heading in targets the
verified cause and not an asserted one; whether rejected causes are shown; the prediction;
data-ethics line (subgroups chosen after the test? points removed to make a test pass? a
definition changed to sharpen a difference?); coaching flag; Black Belt invitation where the
cause structure is bigger than the scope.

### Common holds at Tollgate 3

- "The team agreed the cause is X." That is A1 evidence. A3 needs a test.
- A p-value with no effect size and no sentence. The hold is one sentence long.
- The wrong test for the data type (a t-test on pass/fail counts; a chi-square on times).
- Only the surviving cause shown. Where did the other four go?
- A cause verified, and a countermeasure already chosen for a different one.

## Tollgate 4 — Improve/Control (week 8)

**Rubric items due:** I1, I2, I4; C1 drafted, C2 drafted, C3 basis agreed; S1 rehearsed.
**In progress:** ★ I3 (the pilot is running or scheduled; partial evidence is expected, not
full evidence); ★ C1 (the control plan exists and has a named owner; handover is scheduled;
30 days of live monitoring come after this gate, before closure).

This gate is the tollgate rehearsal on the calendar: the same ten minutes and one page you
will use at closure, with the sponsor in the room. It is the last gate on the cohort
calendar and not the last conversation — coaching checkpoints run monthly until closure.

### Before the gate — what you bring on the one page

| ✓ | Item | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | More than one countermeasure generated; selection against stated criteria (impact, effort, risk); the chosen one aimed at the verified cause from Tollgate 3 | I1 |
| ☐ | Pilot design: bounded (where, when, how long); the written prediction; success measure identical to the baseline metric and operational definition; a stop rule (what result, or what harm, stops the pilot) | I2 |
| ☐ | ★ Before/after evidence to date: same metric, same definition, same collection method; control chart continued from the baseline, or a paired comparison; result against the prediction, honestly read — partial, unmet, or not yet run, stated as such | ★ I3 |
| ☐ | Mistake-proofing considered (poka-yoke, or FMEA-based risk reduction); residual risks named | I4 |
| ☐ | ★ Control plan drafted: metric, method, frequency, owner, response plan (what reading triggers what action by whom); the process owner named and the handover date set; the chart they will keep | ★ C1 |
| ☐ | New standard work drafted in the vertical's format, written with the people who do the work; training plan with names and dates | C2 |
| ☐ | Benefit class stated (hard, soft, cost avoidance, clinical/service); the Finance partner's or mission-metric validator's calculation basis agreed; the no-double-counting question asked (was any part of this claimed by another project or a Yellow Belt mini-project?) | C3 |
| ☐ | The one-page summary a stranger can follow in under ten minutes: problem → cause → change → result | S1 |
| ☐ | Closure date proposed, within 6 months of week 8, with ≥ 30 days of live monitoring before submission | C1 |

### The sponsor is asked to decide

1. Do you approve the countermeasure for implementation beyond the pilot — or, if the pilot
   has not run, do you approve the pilot as designed, with its stop rule?
2. Who owns the control plan? The sponsor names the process owner and confirms their
   authority to run the response plan. Without a name here, C1 cannot be earned.
3. Do you commit the Finance partner (or mission-metric validator) to validate at closure, on
   the basis stated?
4. Closure date agreed? Sustainment check date noted (90 days from handover)?
5. Decision: proceed to closure / proceed with conditions / hold / re-scope / stop.

### The reviewer records

I1, I2, I4 status; ★ I3 evidence to date and whether the design would produce comparable
before/after evidence if it continues as written; ★ C1 draft status — metric, method,
frequency, owner, response plan, handover date, and whether the named owner was in the room;
C2 and C3 readiness; S1 in one sentence (could a stranger follow it?); decision and closure
date; data-ethics line (was the pilot window chosen after seeing early results? is the after
definition the before definition?); coaching flag for the monthly checkpoints.

### Common holds at Tollgate 4

- A countermeasure aimed at a cause that did not verify, chosen because it was already
  budgeted. The sponsor hears this from the reviewer's question, not from the coach.
- Pilot with no stop rule or no prediction. "We will see how it goes" is unfalsifiable.
- A control plan with a frequency and a chart but no name, or a name without authority.
- After-data collected by a different method (a new system, a different clerk, a shorter
  definition) than the baseline.
- A benefit stated in money with no Finance partner in the conversation.

## After Tollgate 4 — the closure submission check

Not a fifth gate. A one-page check you run with your coach before you submit for independent
review, so the review is about the project and not about missing pages.

| ✓ | Item |
|---|---|
| ☐ | Every ★ item present: M3 attempted or accepted as not applicable; A3 with method, effect size and sentence; I3 same metric, same definition, same method, chart or paired comparison; C1 signed by the named owner with ≥ 30 days of live monitoring shown |
| ☐ | The control chart runs continuously from baseline through pilot to monitoring, one operational definition throughout, or the change is dated and explained |
| ☐ | Sponsor attestation signed; Finance validation memo or mission-metric validation attached — [`sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md`](sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md) |
| ☐ | The one-page summary (S1) is on top; the exhibits are behind it; nothing is a deck |
| ☐ | No personal data, patient data or customer data anywhere in the package; processes described, not individuals |
| ☐ | Rejected causes and unmet predictions are in the package, not in a drawer |
| ☐ | The handover date is stated: it is day 0 for the sustainment check — [`sustainment-check.md`](sustainment-check.md) |

Reviewer feedback returns within the rubric's published turnaround; one revise-and-resubmit
cycle is included. The reviewer reads the ★ items first.
