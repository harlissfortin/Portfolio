# The Partner-Project Pool and the Practicum Track

*Green Belt certification requires a real, sponsored DMAIC project. Most learners bring one
from their employer. Those who cannot have two routes: a **partner-pool project** — a real
problem at a nonprofit, small or mid-sized business or public body that the program sources
and matches — or the **Practicum track**, a simulated project with the instructor acting as
sponsor, which leads to the distinctly labeled credential "Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt
(Practicum)". This page is how both work, what a partner sponsor commits to, and exactly what
a Practicum credential can and cannot claim.*

Level context: [`../README.md`](../README.md). Rubric: [`review-rubric.md`](review-rubric.md).

---

## 1. Why there are two routes and not one

There is no path to a Green Belt without human review of applied work, and no path to the
unlabeled credential without a real process. A simulated project can show that you can run
every tool correctly on realistic data; it cannot show that you led a team, held a sponsor,
and left a control plan running. The program does not pretend otherwise, so it labels the
difference on the credential rather than in a footnote.

The partner pool exists so that as few learners as possible need the label. Its purpose is to
get real projects to individual enrollees — people between roles, in roles without process
authority, or at employers who will pay tuition but not sponsor a project.

## 2. The partner-project pool

### 2.1 What a partner project is

A bounded process problem at a partner organization, owned by a named sponsor with authority
over the process, worked by the learner as a Green Belt under the same charter, tollgates,
rubric, sponsor verification and sustainment check as any employer project. The organization
pays nothing. The learner is not an employee, a contractor or a consultant; they are a Green
Belt candidate leading a project the organization wants done, under a written partner
agreement.

Partner organizations: nonprofits, businesses of roughly 250 staff or fewer, public bodies
(a clinic, a library system, a municipal permits office), and the community partners or
suppliers of corporate clients who offer them.

### 2.2 Sourcing

| Source | How |
|---|---|
| **Alumni organizations** | Certified Green and Black Belts nominate a problem at their own or a partner organization; the nominator may not be the sponsor of a project they also coach |
| **Corporate clients** | The rollout agreement offers clients the option of listing supplier or community-partner problems; the client is not the sponsor and sees no learner data |
| **Nonprofit capacity networks and SMB associations** | The program presents the offer once a year to each network: a real improvement, no fee, ten hours of your time across four months |
| **Yellow Belt escalations** | A mini-project reviewer's note that a problem is Green-Belt-sized, passed on by the organization itself, never from the storyboard without the organization's consent |
| **Direct intake** | A public intake form on the program site |

Sourcing is a program task with a named owner (the partner-pool coordinator) and a target:
the pool holds at least one screened project per two individual enrollees six weeks before
each cohort starts. The first ten sponsored projects are an open item before commercial
launch, and the outcomes page reports pool depth.

### 2.3 Screening — what gets into the pool

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| **Bounded** | Start and stop points inside one sponsor's authority; one site; a process, not a system implementation or a reorganization |
| **Measurable** | A primary metric can be operationally defined, and at least 20 data points can be collected in four weeks — the process runs at least weekly |
| **Data reachable** | An extract, log or observation plan can exist within 10 business days of matching |
| **Observable** | The learner can go see the process: on site at least twice, or by screen-sharing and shadowing for a transactional process that is worked on screen |
| **Sponsored** | A named sponsor who will sign the charter and attend four tollgates, and a named process owner who is not the sponsor |
| **Safe and lawful** | No patient, client or employee data leaves the organization; on-site work has a safety induction; the problem is not a headcount reduction or a disciplinary matter in disguise |
| **Honest** | The sponsor accepts that the result may be "did not move the metric, here is what we learned" and signs for that too |

The coordinator screens with the intake call, using the charter smells checklist in
[`charter-template.md`](charter-template.md) as the script. A problem that fails one
criterion is coached toward one that passes ("not the whole intake process; the 40% of
applications that come back — start there"), not rejected outright.

### 2.4 Sponsor obligations

The sponsor signs these before the project is listed. Hours are the real ask; the program
states them so nobody discovers them in week 3.

| Obligation | When | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sign the charter agreement; rewrite and re-sign after the week 1 clinic | Before day 1; week 1 | 1 h |
| Attend Tollgates 1–4 (ten minutes, one page, twenty-five minutes in the room) | Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8 | 2 h |
| Release the process owner and one or two people who work the process | Weeks 1–8 and through closure | 1–2 h each per week |
| Provide data access — an extract, a log, or permission to observe and record | Within 10 business days of matching | Varies; a named person owns it |
| Name a Finance partner or mission-metric validator and let them state a calculation basis | Tollgate 2 | 1 h |
| Approve or decline the countermeasure and name the control plan owner | Tollgate 4 | In the gate |
| Sign the sponsor attestation; the validator signs Part B or C | Closure | 1 h |
| Answer the 90-day sustainment check | Day 90 from handover | 10 min |
| Name an alternate who can act if the sponsor is unavailable | At listing | — |
| Not ask the learner for work outside the charter | Throughout | — |

What the organization receives: a bounded improvement with a running control plan, a one-page
record it owns, and the standard work its own people wrote. What it does not receive: a
guarantee of a result, the learner's continued time after closure, an endorsement, or a sales
approach from the program.

### 2.5 Matching

1. The pool is published to individual enrollees without an employer project **six weeks
   before day 1**: problem statement as screened, vertical, location and mode (on site /
   remote-observable), process frequency, data type.
2. The learner ranks up to three projects and declares any conflict of interest: a current or
   former employer, a customer or competitor of their employer, a family business, a
   personal relationship with the sponsor. A declared conflict is a reason to match elsewhere,
   not a mark against anyone.
3. The coordinator matches on, in order: vertical fit with the case the learner will work in
   the live labs; ability to go see (time zone, distance, mode); the learner's background
   against the data type (a first hypothesis test on a p chart of returns is a kinder start
   than on destructive measurements); pool balance (a sponsor's second project waits until
   every sponsor has one).
4. **One learner per project, one project per learner.** Two learners never share a project;
   the rubric scores one leader.
5. Matched **at least three weeks before day 1**, so the charter agreement carries a real
   signature before the course starts, as the prerequisite requires. The learner has an
   introductory call with the sponsor and process owner in that window and may decline once,
   with a reason, for a re-match.
6. The program, the organization and the learner sign the partner agreement: data and
   confidentiality; no employment relationship; safety on site; the organization's right to
   stop the project honorably; the learner's right to describe the process (never individuals
   or identifying figures) in the project package; consent to be named on the credential,
   which defaults to no.

### 2.6 Data, confidentiality and safety

The learner works with the organization's data under the organization's rules — on its
systems where it has them, on the learner's own with the organization's written permission
where it does not, and never with patient, client or employee identifiers in either case. The
project package describes the process; the organization marks any figure it considers
sensitive, and the program's redaction pass removes it before any evidence is attached to a
credential. On-site work starts with the organization's safety induction, and a learner who
sees a hazard reports it to the sponsor the same day, whatever the charter says.

### 2.7 When a partner project collapses

Sponsors leave, data does not arrive, priorities change. The rules are written at matching so
that no learner discovers them in week 5.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Before Tollgate 2** | Re-matched from the pool within 10 business days. The learner stays with the cohort for the learning; Tollgates 1 and 2 for the new project are held in the monthly coaching checkpoints with the reviewer present. The 6-month closure window runs from the cohort's week 8, unchanged |
| **After Tollgate 2, before Tollgate 4** | The learner chooses: continue with the surviving slice of the chartered project (the rubric scores the project that was chartered and a completed bounded slice can pass), or move to the Practicum track with the labeled credential. The program puts the choice and its consequence in writing within 5 business days |
| **After Tollgate 4** | The project is closed on the evidence it has. A pilot that ran and a control plan that was handed over are scored as they stand; if handover never happened because the organization withdrew, ★ C1 cannot be earned and the learner may complete C1 on a later project within 12 months at no further review fee, or take the Practicum credential now |
| **Sponsor stops the project honorably** (problem gone, priority changed) | Treated by timing as above; the sponsor's reason is recorded and never held against the organization for future listings |

A collapse caused by the learner — not showing up, not collecting data, working outside the
charter — is a coaching matter first and, if it recurs, the end of the learner's pool access
for that cohort.

### 2.8 Pool health — what is measured and published

Projects screened, listed, matched and closed per cohort; median days from matching to data
access; sponsor attendance at tollgates; day-90 response rate for pool sponsors against
employer sponsors; the share of pool projects that pass review, alongside the employer share.
Reported in the evaluation plan and on the outcomes page with n. A pool whose projects pass
at a materially different rate from employer projects is a screening problem and goes on the
improvement board.

## 3. The Practicum track

### 3.1 Who it is for

- An individual enrollee with no employer project for whom no pool match can be made three
  weeks before day 1.
- A learner whose partner or employer project collapsed after Tollgate 2 and who chooses the
  track (§2.7).
- A learner who is between roles or in a role with no process to touch at all.

It is not for corporate cohorts. A corporate learner without a project is a sponsor-readiness
question for the account, handled by the cohort playbook, and the program does not offer the
Practicum as a way around it. It is not offered as a faster or cheaper route, and it is not
sold separately.

### 3.2 What the simulated project is

The Practicum project is the vertical case from the live labs, extended into a full project
with a dataset built to behave like a real process. The learner picks the vertical. The case
is specified in the practicum folder's Practicum-track project file, and it carries, by
design:

- a measurement system with a real problem in it, which the Gage R&R or attribute agreement
  study finds if it is run properly (★ M3);
- a baseline with at least one special cause that has a discoverable explanation in the
  case's change log (M4);
- a cause structure in which at least one candidate cause verifies with the right test and at
  least one plausible, widely believed cause does not (★ A3, A4);
- a **response pack for every countermeasure a learner could reasonably choose**: pilot data
  released after the learner submits the pilot design and written prediction, generated from
  the case's planted structure — a countermeasure aimed at the verified cause moves the
  metric about as far as the effect size predicts; one aimed at the unverified cause does not,
  and the learner's honest reading of that earns ★ I3 as it would on a real project;
- a simulated 30-day monitoring pack released after the control plan is accepted, with one
  planted drift that exercises the response plan (★ C1);
- a simulated cost table and scorecard for C3, every figure labeled *simulated*.

The learner does not see the planted structure. Data packs are released on request, dated,
the way an organization releases an extract; asking for the right data at the right time is
part of what is scored.

### 3.3 The instructor acting as sponsor

A practicum instructor — a certified program instructor who is not the learner's coach for
the project — plays the sponsor and, where needed, the process owner and the Finance
partner. They sign the charter *as instructor acting as sponsor*, sit the four tollgates in
the sponsor's chair and make the sponsor's decisions, answer sponsor questions in character
from the case brief, release data packs, and sign Part A of the sponsor attestation with the
words *instructor acting as sponsor (Practicum)*. Parts B and C are completed against the
simulated tables and labeled.

Everything else is unchanged: the exam is the same proctored form; the rubric is the same
100 points with the same four ★ items; the reviewer is an independent Black Belt or Master
Black Belt who is neither the practicum instructor nor the coach; the one-page, ten-minute
tollgate standard applies; the data-ethics stop applies exactly as it would to a real
dataset. Sponsor items — the D2 signature, the Tollgate 2 baseline acceptance, the C1 owner —
are scored from the instructor's decisions in the sponsor role, as the rubric states.

### 3.4 The credential — what it can and cannot claim

The credential is **"Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (Practicum)"**. The parenthetical is
part of the name on the badge, the PDF, the verification page and the LMS record. The
verification page carries the exit-competence statement of the level with one added
sentence: *"This holder's project was completed on a simulated dataset with the instructor
acting as sponsor; no real-process impact, sponsor verification or sustainment is claimed."*

| It can claim | It cannot claim |
|---|---|
| The same proctored exam, at the same cut score, as the unlabeled credential | A verified improvement on a real process |
| A full DMAIC cycle completed against the same rubric, with every ★ item earned, scored by an independent reviewer | Sponsor verification by anyone other than the instructor in role |
| Correct use and plain-language interpretation of the Green Belt toolkit on realistic, messy data: MSA, stability and capability, graphical analysis, basic hypothesis tests, pilot design, control plans | Finance-validated or mission-metric-validated impact; badge verified-impact field reads *not applicable (Practicum)* |
| Tollgates presented to a sponsor role to the ten-minute, one-page standard | A `sustained` flag; it reads `not-applicable` |
| Eligibility to lead a real project and to submit it later for the unlabeled credential (§3.5) | Satisfying the Black Belt prerequisite of one completed real DMAIC project — the Practicum meets the Green Belt certification prerequisite only; a real completed project is still required for admission |
| Recertification on the same three-year cycle as the level | Inclusion in the program's published impact figures; Practicum completions are published as their own count |

A holder describing the credential on a résumé writes the name as issued. The program's
verification page is what an employer sees, and it says the same thing.

### 3.5 Upgrading to the unlabeled credential

A Practicum holder who later leads a real, sponsored DMAIC project may submit it for
independent review against the rubric with the full sponsor verification, at the program's
published project-review fee and without retaking the exam within the current recertification
cycle. On a pass, the credential is reissued as "Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt" with
the original issue date retained and the upgrade date shown; the Practicum badge is
superseded, and its verification URL resolves to the upgraded record. The sustainment check
runs on the real project as normal. The partner pool is open to Practicum holders for this
purpose.

### 3.6 What the program refuses here

Consistent with the published refusals
([what we refuse](../../program-operations/what-we-refuse.md)):

- no Practicum credential without the parenthetical, anywhere, ever;
- no Practicum route for a corporate cohort in place of sponsor readiness;
- no "Practicum Plus" or any tier between the two credentials;
- no impact figure that includes a simulated result.
