# Green Belt A3 — The One-Page Project Record

*Green Belt toolkit · started in week 1, grown at every tollgate, finished at closure. Rubric
★ S1 is scored from this page; every other rubric item is evidenced somewhere on it, and the
item numbers sit in the margin so you and the reviewer look at the same block for the same
reason. The gate-by-gate slice of this page, with its decision strip, is
[`tollgate-one-pager.md`](tollgate-one-pager.md); the checklist of what each gate needs is
[`../project/tollgate-checklists.md`](../project/tollgate-checklists.md); the rubric is
[`../project/review-rubric.md`](../project/review-rubric.md).*

## Purpose

The A3 is the project. Not the deck, not the workbook, not the folder of charts — the one
page a stranger reads in ten minutes and retells as *problem → cause → change → result*. It
is named for the paper size (297 × 420 mm; 11 × 17 in is the same idea) and it earns the name
by fitting on it. The size is a discipline, not a format: if the story needs a second page,
the story is not yet clear, and the reviewer will score the confusion, not the effort.

The page has a left side and a right side. The left is **what was true** — the problem, the
process as observed, the baseline, the cause you verified and the ones you rejected. The
right is **what you did** — the countermeasure, the pilot and its evidence, the control plan
and the benefit, and what is left over. Read left to right, top to bottom, the layout is the
argument: you cannot write block 5 until block 4 has a verified cause, and the reviewer can
see at a glance whether you tried.

Three rules for everything on it:

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Exhibits over prose** | Three charts and four sentences carry most projects. Every chart carries its metric, units, period and n in the title or the caption. A paragraph that describes a chart is replaced by the chart |
| **Same font for the unmet result** | The prediction you missed, the cause that did not verify, the guardrail that moved — written in the same size and place as the wins. The rubric passes honest failure; it stops on a hidden point |
| **The page grows; it is never rewritten** | Blocks fill in at the gate where they are due and are dated. A changed operational definition, a re-scored baseline or a re-scope shows on the page with its date, not in a fresh page that hides the history |

### Filling the blocks

**Block 1.** The problem statement is the charter's, unchanged unless a re-scope changed it, in
which case both versions are on the page with dates. The number in it has a basis: "6.8% of
sampled brackets (10 per shift, 2 Mar–8 May)", not "about 7%".

**Block 3.** The exhibit is the chart, not a table of the chart's numbers. The stability
statement is one of the three sentences in
[`capability-worksheet.md`](capability-worksheet.md) Part A, verbatim. If the MSA failed and
was fixed, both studies' headline numbers are here — a failed and fixed measurement system is
stronger M3 evidence than a clean one with no story.

**Block 4.** The sentence for the verified cause follows the test card in
[`hypothesis-test-selector.md`](hypothesis-test-selector.md): effect, interval, comparison to
the tolerance or target, practical meaning, next step. A p-value appears after the interval,
never instead of it. The rejected causes are one line each and they are on the page at
Tollgate 3; the reviewer's first question is "where did the other four go?"

**Block 6.** The chart is the same chart as block 3, continued — same metric, same definition,
same collection method. The prediction is quoted from the pilot plan with the date it was
posted. "Not yet run" is a legitimate entry at Tollgate 4; "we will see how it goes" is not.

**Block 7.** The owner is a name and a title. Days of live monitoring is a number. The benefit
is a class before it is a figure; a figure in money has a Finance partner's name beside it or
it is not on the page.

**Block 8.** The decision log is the audit trail the reviewer and the sponsor attestation read
against. Conditions carry a due date and a status at the next version.

### How the page grows across the gates

| Version | Blocks filled | Exhibits on the page | Rubric items due |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tollgate 1 (week 2) | 1, 2; block 3's collection plan and data-access line; block 8's log | SIPOC row; the map if the go-see is done | D1–D4 |
| Tollgate 2 (week 4) | 3 complete; block 1's target confirmed or revised with the sponsor's initials | Baseline chart with stability statement; capability or the withheld line; MSA headline | M1, M2, ★ M3, M4 |
| Tollgate 3 (week 6) | 4 complete; the prediction drafted into block 6 | Stratified chart; the test result with its interval | A1, A2, ★ A3, A4 |
| Tollgate 4 (week 8) | 5; 6 to date; 7 drafted with the owner named and day 0 set; 8 with closure date | Staged chart to date; selection matrix summary; control plan rows | I1, I2, I4; ★ I3 and ★ C1 in progress; C2, C3 basis |
| Closure (≤ 6 months after week 8) | All; ≥ 30 days of live monitoring in block 7; the owner's signature date | Staged chart through monitoring; after-capability on the new stable stage | Everything, scored once, against the full rubric |

Print at A3 or 11 × 17 in, landscape; on a screen, one landscape page. Font floor 9 pt. Two
Letter or A4 landscape sheets side by side are acceptable when the large size is not
available — the left and right halves, never a page one and a page two.

**Accessibility.** Real headings for the eight blocks (the fillable version uses them); every
chart has alt text that says what the chart shows, not what it is ("daily median stable at
141 after day 0; baseline 190" rather than "I-MR chart"); stages and groups are labeled in
text, not distinguished by color alone; the reading order is left column top to bottom, then
right column.

## Common mistakes

- **A deck with the page attached.** The page is the record. Slides are for the alumni case
  clinic, later, if at all.
- **A second page "for methods."** Exhibits sit behind the page in the submission pack; the
  page carries their headline and their alt text.
- **Prose that describes a chart.** Replace the paragraph with the chart, titled with metric,
  units, period and n.
- **The verified cause with no rejected ones beside it.** Block 4 has the survivors and the
  rejected causes in the same font; A4 is scored from the same block as A3.
- **A p-value where the sentence should be.** The test card's sentence — effect, interval,
  practical meaning — is what goes on the page. p follows it.
- **"Improved 31%" from two weeks.** Block 6 states the result against the prediction with
  its interval; the honest reading in the example ("not yet shown on 300 parts") is the one
  that earned ★ I3.
- **Rewriting the page for each gate.** The page grows and is dated. A definition that changed
  in week 3 shows on the page with both figures; a baseline re-scored shows both numbers.
  Rewriting it clean is the data-ethics conversation at the gate.
- **A control plan block with a chart and no name.** "Owner: line lead, line B, signed 6 July"
  is the C1 evidence; "production will monitor" is not.
- **Money with no Finance partner.** The benefit row carries a class and a validator by role
  before it carries a figure.
- **Color as the only cue.** Stages, groups and signals are labeled in text; a reviewer
  reading a grayscale print or a screen reader hears the same story.
