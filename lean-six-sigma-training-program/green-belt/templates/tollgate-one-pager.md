# Tollgate One-Pager — Ten Minutes, One Page, Sponsor in the Room

*Green Belt toolkit · used at Tollgates 1–4 (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8) and rehearsed in the week 8
lab. The one-pager is your [A3](a3.md) as it stands at that gate, with a **gate strip** across
the top that says what is due, what you are asking the sponsor to decide, and where the
decision is written. What must be on the page at each gate is the checklist in
[`../project/tollgate-checklists.md`](../project/tollgate-checklists.md); this template says
where it goes and how the ten minutes are spent. Rubric S1 is rehearsed here and scored at
closure — [`../project/review-rubric.md`](../project/review-rubric.md).*

## Purpose

A tollgate is a decision, not a presentation. The sponsor decides whether the phase is done
to the standard and whether the project goes on; you give them what they need to decide in
ten minutes and nothing else. The page limit is what makes the ten minutes possible: a page
can be read in the room, held next to the last one, and filed. A deck cannot.

The standard, stated once and never varied:

| | The standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **One page** | The A3 at this gate — A3 or 11 × 17 in landscape, or two Letter/A4 landscape sheets side by side. Exhibits behind it in the pack, never on a page two | The page is the record the reviewer of record keeps; the sponsor reads the same page at every gate and sees it grow |
| **Ten minutes** | Timed. Stopped at 10:00 by the reviewer, mid-sentence if necessary. Then ten minutes of questions, then five in which the sponsor decides | The calendar holds regardless of how the project feels; every project in the cohort gets the same twenty-five minutes |
| **Sponsor in the room** | The sponsor asks, decides and signs the decision line. The process owner from Tollgate 2. The reviewer records. The coach is present and silent | The gate exists for the sponsor's decision; a gate without the sponsor is a rehearsal |
| **The decision is written before you leave** | One of five: proceed · proceed with conditions · hold · re-scope · stop, with conditions and dates, on the page | A decision that lives in someone's memory is not a decision |

## The template

### The gate strip (top of the page, four lines)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Gate · date · version** | ☐ Tollgate 1 Define ☐ Tollgate 2 Measure ☐ Tollgate 3 Analyze ☐ Tollgate 4 Improve/Control · date · A3 version |
| **Rubric items due, with your own status** | each item: *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced* — e.g., "★ M3 evidenced (Gage R&R failed, fixed, re-run) · M4 at risk (capability withheld — unstable, investigating the April subgroups)". Your honest status, before the reviewer's |
| **Conditions from the last gate** | each with its due date and status: *closed* (date) / *open* / *overdue* |
| **What you are asking the sponsor to decide** | the numbered questions for this gate from the checklist, in your own words, with your recommendation beside each |

### The page body

The A3 blocks due at this gate, filled; later blocks left visibly empty with the gate they are
due at. Three exhibits at most, each titled with metric, units, period and n; four sentences
that carry the story. The block layout and the margin rubric numbers are in
[`a3.md`](a3.md).

| Gate | Blocks on the page | The three exhibits | The four sentences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Define** | 1, 2; block 3's collection plan and data-access line | The SIPOC row; the stakeholder map; the first go-see note (date, what you watched, the one thing the documented process did not show) | The problem statement · the primary metric's operational definition, read aloud · the customer and CTQ with its source · the resistance risk and the response |
| **2 — Measure** | 3 complete; block 1's target confirmed or revised | The baseline control chart with the rule set named and every signal annotated; the MSA result (both studies if it failed and was fixed); the capability summary with the specification source, or the "withheld" line | The stability statement, verbatim · the MSA sentence · the capability or DPMO sentence · the target decision you are asking for |
| **3 — Analyze** | 4 complete; the prediction drafted into block 6 | The stratified chart that shows where the variation lives; the test result with its interval; the rejected causes in a table | The narrowing in one line · the verified cause's sentence — effect, interval, practical meaning · the rejected causes and the sample-size sentence · the prediction |
| **4 — Improve/Control** | 5; 6 to date; 7 drafted; 8 with the closure date | The staged chart baseline → pilot to date; the selection matrix summary; the control plan rows with the owner's name and day 0 | The countermeasure and the cause it removes · the result against the prediction, honestly read · who owns the control plan and from when · the benefit's class and validator |

### The decision line (bottom of the page)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sponsor's decision** | ☐ proceed ☐ proceed with conditions ☐ hold ☐ re-scope ☐ stop |
| **Conditions / the one missing item / the re-scope** | each with a due date and an owner |
| **Sponsor signature · date** | |
| **Process owner (from Tollgate 2)** — *the map and the baseline are the process as worked* | |
| **Reviewer's record attached** | ☐ sent to Green Belt, sponsor and coach within 2 business days ☐ copied to the final reviewer's file |

### The reviewer's record (one form, every gate)

| Line | Content |
|---|---|
| Rubric items due | each marked *evidenced* / *at risk* / *not evidenced*; one sentence for every ★ item |
| Decision and conditions | as written on the page, with dates |
| Data-ethics line | *none observed*, or the specific concern — points removed, a definition changed between before and after, a baseline reconstructed from memory or chosen after seeing the data. At a gate: a warning and a conversation, sent to the assessment lead the same day. At final review: a stop |
| Coaching flag | one topic for the coach — not advice; the reviewer does not coach |
| Black Belt invitation | ☐ not applicable ☐ applies — the problem the project revealed |

## The ten minutes — run of show

Rehearse to this clock; the reviewer runs it in the room.

| Clock | You say | You point at |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:00 | The problem statement and the primary metric with its basis. One breath | Block 1 |
| 1:00–3:00 | What was true when you started, or since the last gate: the baseline, the status of the last gate's conditions | Block 3 (Tollgates 2–4); the gate strip's conditions line |
| 3:00–6:00 | What this phase found. The exhibit, read aloud in the order a stranger's eye takes: title, axes, the point that matters. Then the second exhibit | This gate's exhibits |
| 6:00–8:00 | What it means: the sentence you would tell your sponsor — effect, interval, practical meaning — and the honest reading (partial, unmet, not yet run, withheld) | The sentence on the page |
| 8:00–9:00 | What you need decided, numbered, with your recommendation | The gate strip's decision line |
| 9:00–10:00 | What is next and what is at risk: the next phase's first action; the resistance risk's status; the item most likely to hold you | Block 8 |
| 10:00 | Stop. The reviewer calls time; you stop mid-sentence and sit down | — |
| 10:00–20:00 | Questions. The sponsor first, then the process owner, then the reviewer. Answer from the page; if the answer is in the pack, say which exhibit and stop | The pack |
| 20:00–25:00 | The sponsor decides; you write it on the page; the sponsor signs | The decision line |

**Rehearsal protocol** (week 8 lab, and on your own before every gate): time yourself twice.
Then hand the page to someone outside the project — a cohort partner from another vertical
is ideal — for ten minutes, and ask for four sentences back: problem, cause (or "not yet"),
change (or "not yet"), result (or "not yet"). Every stumble in their sentences is an edit on
your page, not a note for your talk.

**Building the page.**

- Font floor 9 pt; a chart that needs more than a third of the page is a chart with too
  much on it.
- Every exhibit: metric, units, period, n in the title; the rule set on a control chart; the
  specification source on a capability summary; the tool and the method choice in the
  caption ("Minitab 22, 2-sample t, unpooled" — [`software-parity.md`](software-parity.md)).
- Numbers carry a basis: per shift or per day, calendar or working time, per unit or per
  opportunity.
- The unmet prediction, the withheld capability, the failed MSA and the rejected causes are
  on the page in the same font as the successes.
- No methods appendix. The pack behind the page holds the exhibits at full size, the
  worksheets and the raw output; the page carries their headlines.
- Accessibility: real headings for the blocks; alt text on every exhibit saying what it
  shows; stages and groups labeled in text, never by color alone; the page reads left
  column top to bottom, then right column, on a screen reader as on paper.

## Common mistakes

- **A deck.** The reviewer records "not the standard" and the sponsor decides on what they
  could read in the time. Bring the page.
- **A page two.** Cut until it fits; the pack is where the full exhibits live.
- **Talking to the clock instead of the page.** The ten minutes are spent pointing at
  exhibits and reading sentences; a narrated history of the project runs out of time before
  the decision line.
- **Asking the sponsor to agree with a p-value.** The sponsor is asked whether the sentence,
  with its effect size, is something they would act on. Write the sentence.
- **Self-status of "evidenced" on every line.** The reviewer's record will disagree with
  some of them; a strip that says "M4 at risk — capability withheld, here is why" is a
  stronger page than one that claims everything.
- **Conditions from the last gate not on this page.** An open condition the sponsor has to
  ask about is a hold waiting to happen.
- **The rejected causes left in the pack.** They are on the page at Tollgate 3, in a table,
  in the same font.
- **A control-plan row with no name at Tollgate 4.** "Who owns the control plan?" is a
  question the sponsor answers in the room; the page carries the answer before they leave.
- **Rehearsing alone.** Time yourself, then give the page to a stranger and collect four
  sentences. The stranger's sentences are the test the reviewer applies at closure.
