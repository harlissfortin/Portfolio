# Green Belt — Facilitator and Coaching System

Who may teach a Green Belt cohort, who coaches each project, who certifies it, and how the
program keeps those last two apart. The product at this level is a verified real project, and
a verified project needs two different people: one who helps the learner earn the rubric and
one who decides, blind to the first, whether they earned it. Everything in this document
serves that separation or the quality of the coaching on either side of it.

Governs: cohort instructors, coaches, producers, reviewers of record, second readers, the
assessment lead and the engagement lead. Agrees with the
[review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md) (which controls where they differ), the
[terms of certification](../../program-operations/terms-of-certification.md) §3.4 and the
[credential evidence policy](../../program-operations/credential-evidence-policy.md) §5.

---

## 1. Roles and the one rule

| Role | Who | Does | Does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instructor** | Certified Green Belt facilitator (§2): a certified Black Belt or Master Black Belt | Runs the eight live labs, office hours and the tollgate rehearsal; coaches the cohort in the room | Score any project from a cohort they taught; know who will review their learners |
| **Coach** | The instructor for up to 12 project files; a second certified coach from the coaching bench above 12 | The three 1:1 checkpoints, monthly coaching to closure, the coaching notes | Score, sign, or advise the reviewer; attend the reviewer's reading of the file |
| **Producer** | Program staff or a certified facilitator in training | Breakouts, dataset distribution, chat triage, timing, accessibility support, in every live lab above 12 learners | Teach content |
| **Reviewer of record** | Certified Black Belt or Master Black Belt on the reviewer bench, calibrated on the anchor set within the last quarter, not this cohort's instructor or coach | Tollgate gate notes; the 100-point rubric review; sponsor verification check; the day-90 sustainment check; signs the credential evidence | Coach, suggest countermeasures, or read the coaching notes |
| **Second reader** | Black Belt candidate meeting their two-tollgate-review requirement | Scores the same tollgate one-pager independently; compares against the reviewer of record afterward | Sign anything; contact the learner |
| **Assessment lead** | Program | Assigns reviewers blind (§3.2); runs calibration; owns appeals, integrity cases and data-ethics stops; signs the outcomes page | Coach or review live projects at Green Belt |
| **Engagement lead** | Program (corporate cohorts) | Readiness gate, sponsor briefings, cohort report, per the [corporate cohort playbook](corporate-cohort-playbook.md) | Any assessment role |

The one rule, stated so that every role can repeat it: **the coach says how to earn it; the
reviewer says whether it was earned.** A coach who starts scoring is grading their own
homework. A reviewer who starts coaching has become the second coach and the program has no
assessor left. When either of you feels the pull to cross — and you will, because the
learner asks — say the rule out loud and route the question to the other role.

## 2. Facilitator certification pathway

### 2.1 Who may teach

Green Belt labs are taught by a **certified Black Belt or Master Black Belt** who has led at
least three DMAIC projects to Finance-validated or mission-metric-validated closure, at least
one in the cohort's vertical, and who holds the program's facilitator certification below.
A Green Belt does not teach Green Belt, whatever their experience; Yellow Belt workshops
(facilitator: GB+) are where a strong Green Belt starts teaching. External Black Belts join
the pathway after the Black Belt calibration assessment; gaps are bridged with the relevant
Green Belt week, not waived.

Licensed internal (client) facilitators may deliver White and Yellow Belt under audit. They
do not deliver Green Belt. Certification authority at this level stays in-house at every
scale, per the program blueprint.

### 2.2 The pathway

Four stages, in order, with the evidence each produces. Nobody skips a stage on seniority;
a Master Black Belt with twenty years of projects still shadows one cohort, because what is
being certified is delivery of *this* course, not expertise in the subject.

| Stage | What you do | Evidence to advance | Typical elapsed time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Calibrate** | Score the reviewer anchor set (rubric, "Calibration"), even though you will not review — you must coach toward the same standard the reviewer scores | Within 5 points of the anchor total on all four; exact agreement on every ★ item | 1 day |
| **1 · Shadow** | Sit in every live lab of one full cohort as producer; read the week files and facilitator notes before each lab; attend three 1:1 checkpoints as an observer with the learner's consent | Producer duties performed; written debrief of each lab (what the run-of-show said, what happened, what you would change) | 8 weeks |
| **2 · Co-teach** | Lead at least three of the eight labs, including week 3 (MSA) or week 6 (stats) — the two labs where facilitation quality most affects the project — with the certified instructor present; carry four 1:1 checkpoint files as coach under supervision | Observation record on each lab you lead (§2.3); four coaching notes reviewed by the instructor | 8 weeks |
| **3 · Observed solo** | Teach a full cohort as instructor of record; the assessment lead or a Master Black Belt observes two labs live (one of them week 6) and reads a sample of six coaching notes | Both observations meet the standard; cohort L1 items 2–4 ≥ 4.0; no data-ethics stop traced to coaching | 8 weeks |
| **Certified** | Instructor of record without observation; eligible for the coaching bench | Recorded on your own verification page as facilitator status, with the date | — |

Fastest path is three consecutive cohorts, roughly seven months. The pathway is paid at the
producer rate at stage 1 and the co-facilitator rate at stage 2.

### 2.3 Observation

What an observer records at stages 2 and 3, and twice a year after certification (one
observation each half-year, one of which the instructor does not choose):

- **Talk ratio.** Minutes the instructor talks versus minutes learners work on data or their
  own project. The standard (§4.4) is that learners work for at least 60% of the lab.
- **Fidelity to the run-of-show**, and whether departures were for the learners in the room
  or for the instructor's preferences. Departures are fine when named.
- **The failure moment.** Every lab has a planted failure (the Gage R&R that fails in week 3,
  the test whose assumptions do not hold in week 6). Did the instructor let the learners
  hit it, or rescue them early?
- **The debrief was protected.** The last 20 minutes happened, at length, with learners
  talking.
- **Coaching language.** "What would the reviewer need to see?" rather than "here's what I'd
  do." Any instance of the instructor telling a learner what their root cause is, or which
  countermeasure to choose, is recorded verbatim.
- **Accessibility.** Captions on, materials distributed in advance, no instruction that
  assumed a mouse, breakout roles assigned so that nobody was silent for a whole lab.

An observation that misses the standard leads to a second observation within one cohort. Two
consecutive misses return the facilitator to stage 2 for one cohort. This is a design
response, not a disciplinary one: the most common miss is a strong practitioner lecturing
because lecturing feels like value, and it is corrected by co-teaching next to someone who
does not.

### 2.4 Keeping the status

Facilitator status stays live while all of the following hold: at least one cohort taught in
any 18 months; calibration on the anchor set each quarter a cohort is taught; both annual
observations met; cohort L1 items 2–4 at or above 4.0 across the year's cohorts (a single
cohort below is a conversation, not a lapse). A lapsed status is regained through stage 3
only. Facilitators are named on their own verification page and in every cohort's course
record; the program does not run anonymous faculty.

## 3. Coach and assessor: the separation, mechanically

### 3.1 What each side sees

| | Coach | Reviewer of record |
|---|---|---|
| The learner's name and project | Yes | Yes (needed for the credential evidence record) |
| The sponsor's name | Yes | Yes (needed for sponsor verification and the day-90 check) |
| The instructor's and coach's names | — | **No.** The project file the reviewer receives carries a cohort ID, not a cohort roster or instructor name |
| Coaching notes | Writes them | **Never.** Coaching notes are stored outside the project file and are not discoverable by the reviewer |
| Tollgate one-pagers and the sponsor's gate decisions | Yes | Yes |
| The reviewer's gate notes | Yes, after the learner receives them | Writes them |
| The final rubric score and feedback | Yes, after the learner receives it | Writes it |
| The learner's exam result | No | No |

The reviewer sees the instructor's identity only after the final review is returned, when the
assessment lead publishes the cohort's coach–reviewer ★ agreement (§6). Instructors learn who
reviewed their cohort's projects at the same moment. Neither can request or decline a
specific pairing.

### 3.2 Blind assignment

The assessment lead assigns the reviewer of record for every project in the cohort's week 1,
before the first tollgate, so that the same reviewer reads all four tollgate one-pagers and
the final submission. The procedure:

1. Start from the reviewer bench roster (minimum four certified Black Belt or Master Black
   Belt reviewers who are not instructing, per the level README's open items).
2. Remove any reviewer who: instructs or coaches this cohort; has instructed or coached this
   learner at any level in the last 24 months; works for, consults to, or has in the last
   three years been employed by the learner's organization; sits on the same corporate
   account's engagement team; or is the learner's Black Belt sponsor or colleague. Corporate
   cohorts add: no reviewer from the client organization, ever — the White Belt practice of
   client CI leads reviewing field exercises does not extend to Green Belt, because the
   credential's independence is the product the client is paying for.
3. Assign round-robin from the remaining bench, capped at 12 live project files per reviewer
   at any time.
4. Log the assignment (reviewer ID, project ID, conflicts checked, date) in the assessment
   record. The log is what the annual audit re-review reads first.
5. Tell the learner and the sponsor the reviewer's name at Tollgate 1. Tell nobody else.

If a conflict surfaces later — the reviewer recognizes the process from a past engagement,
the learner changes employer — the reviewer stops, tells the assessment lead, and a
replacement is assigned with the same procedure. The replacement re-reads all prior gate
notes; the prior reviewer's notes stand.

*TXN example (illustrative).* A cohort of 14 at an insurance claims operation, taught by an
instructor who consulted to that insurer four years ago. The instructor is eligible to teach
(the three-year employment rule is for reviewers). Of the bench of six, one reviewer coached
two of these learners at Yellow Belt, one is on the account's engagement team; four remain,
and 14 files distribute four–four–three–three.

### 3.3 Gate notes are assessment, not coaching

The blueprint puts a certified reviewer at each tollgate. To keep the reviewer out of the
coach's chair, the reviewer does not attend the tollgate meeting. Within five business days
of each calendar tollgate (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8) the reviewer reads the one-page tollgate
summary and the sponsor's recorded decision from the
[tollgate checklists](../project/tollgate-checklists.md) and returns a **gate note** of at
most five sentences: the status of each mandatory item that has come due (*M3 evidence
present / not yet present / not applicable, reason accepted*), and any data-ethics concern.
It says what is and is not there. It does not say how to fix it — that is the coach's next
conversation, and the coach reads the gate note with the learner at the checkpoint.

A learner who receives "★ M3 not yet present" at Tollgate 2 knows exactly what the final
review will look for, months in advance. That transparency is the point; the rubric is
published in week 1 for the same reason.

### 3.4 The final review, resubmission and appeal

The reviewer scores the full rubric within 15 business days of submission (the rubric's
published turnaround for this level), mandatory items first, in the rubric's feedback format.
One revise-and-resubmit cycle is included; the resubmission goes to the same reviewer, who
re-scores the whole rubric and does not carry over unearned items on trust. A data-ethics
stop goes to the assessment lead the same day and is put to the learner in writing per the
terms of certification §4.

Appeals follow the terms of certification §5: a second calibrated reviewer re-scores blind
to the first score; the coach is not consulted at either stage. The coach may write a
statement of fact for the file (what was submitted when, what the sponsor decided at each
gate); it carries no opinion on the score.

### 3.5 Practicum track

Practicum-track projects have no employer sponsor; the practicum instructor acts as sponsor
and signs the sponsor items. That instructor is therefore the learner's coach *and* sponsor
and is excluded from reviewing with the same rule as any other coach. The credential says
"(Practicum)", and the reviewer scores against the simulated dataset's answer key without
seeing which cohort produced the submission.

## 4. Coaching

### 4.1 The three checkpoints

Every learner in every format — live-virtual cohort, corporate on-site, self-paced with live
tollgates — gets three 45-minute 1:1 checkpoints with their named coach. In the self-paced
option they are the only scheduled human contact besides the tollgates, which is why they are
mandatory; there is no zero-human-contact path to this credential.

| Checkpoint | When | Purpose | The coach leaves with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Chartered and measurable** | Week 2–3, after the Tollgate 1 gate note | Is the scope one process the learner can touch; is the primary metric operationally defined; is the MSA planned for a system the learner can actually study; is there a data-access risk that will surface in week 4 | A written data-collection plan the learner will run this week, and the ★ M3 plan |
| **2 · Verified or not yet** | Week 5–6, before Tollgate 3 | Which of the short-listed causes have been tested; whether the test matched the data type; whether the effect size and the plain-language sentence exist; what did *not* verify and whether it is written down (rubric A4) | The one test to run or re-run before Tollgate 3, and the sentence the learner will tell the sponsor |
| **3 · Pilot and handover** | Week 8, around Tollgate 4 | Pilot prediction written before the pilot; same metric, same definition, same method for before and after; who owns the control plan and has that person been asked; is Finance engaged on the benefit class | The pilot plan or its result, the control-plan owner's name, and the closure calendar |

Checkpoint timing is on the cohort calendar, not "when the learner is ready." A learner who
arrives at checkpoint 2 with no test run still has the checkpoint; the conversation is about
why, and the coaching note records the reason as a risk (§4.3), because tollgate slippage is
a Level-3 measure and the causes are almost always sponsor time, data access or scope — all
designable, none the learner's character.

### 4.2 Monthly coaching to closure

Project completion may run up to six months after week 8. From month 3, the coach holds a
30-minute checkpoint each month until the project file is submitted, using the same note
format. Two consecutive months with no movement on the closure calendar trigger a
three-way conversation — learner, sponsor, coach — about scope: finish a bounded slice with
honest evidence, or close the project as documented learning without the credential this
cycle. The rubric passes honest failure; it does not pass a project that never ends.

Coaching load: no coach carries more than 12 open project files. At 13–20 learners a second
certified coach joins the cohort from the coaching bench in week 1, meets the cohort in the
week 1 lab, and takes the files by round-robin, not by the instructor's choice.

### 4.3 The coaching note

One note per checkpoint, written during or immediately after it, shared with the learner (who
can add a line), stored in the coaching record, retained three years, never placed in the
project file, never scored. Fields, in order:

```
COACHING NOTE
Learner · Project ID · Cohort ID · Vertical · Checkpoint (1 / 2 / 3 / monthly-N) · Date · Coach

1. Where the project is vs. the calendar
   Phase reached; the tollgate this maps to; days ahead/behind the cohort calendar.

2. Mandatory items as the coach sees them today (not a score; the reviewer decides)
   ★ M3  planned / running / result interpreted / N/A reason drafted
   ★ A3  causes short-listed / test chosen / test run / sentence written
   ★ I3  prediction written / pilot running / before-after on same definition
   ★ C1  owner named / owner asked / plan drafted / monitoring live since <date>

3. The one thing before the next checkpoint
   A single action, with the tool or template, and the date.

4. Risks (sponsor · data · scope · time · Finance), each with the mitigation agreed

5. What the learner decided
   Their decision, in their words. The coach records; the coach does not decide.

6. Flags (tick any; each opens a same-day route)
   [ ] data-ethics concern (removed points, changed definition, reconstructed baseline)
   [ ] safety / regulatory / patient- or customer-harm risk surfaced
   [ ] sponsor withdrawn or changed
   [ ] scope exceeds Green Belt (cross-functional, no single process owner) → Black Belt conversation
   [ ] accessibility or accommodation need
```

Field 2 is the most useful line in the program and the most dangerous. It exists so that the
learner and coach are both looking at the same four items the reviewer will read first. It is
phrased as *status*, never as *earned*, and the coach never predicts the score: "I think A3
will pass" teaches the learner to argue with the reviewer instead of building the evidence.

*HC example (illustrative).* Checkpoint 2, a project on discharge-order-to-departure time on
a medical ward, calendar basis, primary metric the median minutes per patient per day. Field
2: ★ M3 — attribute agreement run on the "order time" stamp between two ward clerks and the
system, 89% agreement, accepted with the disagreements traced to a paper-order path; ★ A3 —
box plots by discharge pharmacy batch show the variation; the two-sample comparison is chosen
but not yet run because the pharmacy batch log arrived Friday. Field 3: run the comparison on
the batch log this week using the hypothesis-test selector, write the sentence, check the
sample sizes in each batch group before reading the p-value. Field 4: scope — the learner's
sponsor is the ward manager; the pharmacy batch process is owned by pharmacy, so any
countermeasure there needs a second sponsor's agreement, raised this week rather than at
Tollgate 4. Field 6: nothing ticked.

### 4.4 Live-lab standards

The weekly 2.5-hour lab is an **application lab, not a lecture.** The self-paced three hours
carry the teaching; the lab is where learners run the tool on the vertical case dataset and
then on their own project data, in that order, with the instructor floating. Standards every
lab meets, and that the observation record (§2.3) checks:

- **60/40.** At least 60% of lab minutes are learners working, in breakouts or alone on data,
  with the instructor and producer moving between rooms. A lab that runs a 40-minute
  slide deck has failed the standard whatever the L1 says.
- **Case first, then own project.** Every tool is run on the week's vertical case (where the
  answer key exists and the planted failure is safe to hit) before it is run on the learner's
  own data (where the failure is real). The week files' run-of-show sets the timings.
- **Protect the debrief.** The final 20 minutes are the debrief and are never traded for
  finishing the exercise. Learners state what the data told them and the sentence they would
  tell their sponsor; the instructor's job in the debrief is to ask "what would the reviewer
  need to see?" until the room can answer it.
- **Cohort cap 20, producer above 12.** Above 12 learners the lab has a producer running
  breakouts, distributing datasets, triaging chat and watching the clock so that the
  instructor never stops coaching to fix a room assignment. Above 20 the cohort splits; the
  program does not run a 24-seat Green Belt lab because the corporate buyer asked.
- **Software parity in the room.** Every analysis is shown in Minitab, Excel (or the stats
  add-in) and R or Python per the [software parity guide](../templates/software-parity.md);
  a learner is never told to switch tools to follow the lab.
- **Accessible by default.** Materials and datasets 48 hours ahead; live captions; breakout
  roles (recorder, analyst, presenter) rotate so that participation does not depend on who
  speaks fastest; no instruction assumes a mouse.
- **The tollgate rehearsal (week 8)** is run to the tollgate standard: ten minutes, one page,
  a sponsor or a stand-in sponsor in the room, timed, with the debrief on what the sponsor
  could and could not follow.

*MFG example (illustrative).* Week 4 capability lab on a machining case: the dataset is
stable on the I-MR chart until subgroup 31, where a tool change is planted. Learners who
compute Cpk on the whole run get a capable-looking process; the debrief asks who checked
stability first, and the instructor lets three teams report a Cpk of 1.4 before the fourth
team says the chart is not stable after 31 and the number means nothing yet. That sequence is
the lab. An instructor who announces the tool change at minute five has removed it.

## 5. Learner support and reinforcement

### 5.1 Office hours

One 60-minute open office hour per cohort per week, in the days between the self-paced
content and the lab, run by the instructor (or the second coach, alternating). Drop-in, no
agenda, screen-sharing the learner's own data is the expected mode. Office hours are
coaching; the reviewer never attends. Recorded only with everyone's consent and never
distributed outside the cohort, because learners share their real process data there.

Individual self-paced learners join the monthly public office hour instead, staffed by a
certified facilitator, in addition to their three checkpoints.

### 5.2 Case clinics

A monthly 60-minute clinic, open to the whole Green Belt community (current cohorts and
alumni), rotating through the three verticals. One alumnus presents a real project — one page,
ten minutes, in the tollgate format — and the room works the same question the reviewer would:
what verified the cause, what the before/after evidence shows, what the control plan owner
actually does on a Tuesday. A certified facilitator chairs. Clinics are how alumni keep the
rubric standard in their hands, and they are the main free route to renewal CEUs (§5.4).
A quarterly **tool AMA** (one tool, one facilitator, questions only) sits alongside.

Clinic presenters are recruited from the showcase nominees (corporate playbook §9) and from
projects the reviewers flagged as teaching examples, with the learner's consent and after the
credential evidence policy's redaction pass. No employer is named unless the rollout
agreement allows it.

### 5.3 Alumni access

Every certified Green Belt, including Practicum, holds free access to: the community forum;
the versioned Green Belt template library, with update notices; the monthly case clinic and
quarterly tool AMA, live and recorded; the job board; and the vertical case datasets for
practice. Alumni may bring a new project to office hours during any cohort with capacity,
and may request a coach for a documented renewal project (§5.4) at the coaching-bench rate.
The paid community tier (masterclasses, consulting circles, priority coaching) exists at
Black Belt level and above; nothing a Green Belt needs to renew sits behind it.

Forum norm, as at every level: describe the process, never the person or the employer.

### 5.4 Three-year renewal, earnable free

Green Belt certification renews every three years on **12 renewal CEUs**, where the program
counts one renewal CEU per contact hour of qualifying activity or its documented
equivalent. (This is the program's renewal unit, stated on the badge; it is not the IACET
CEU, which is ten contact hours and applies to the course itself.) Every route below is free
through the community; the only charge is the modest administrative fee for the evidence
review stated in the terms of certification §9. Certification is never contingent on buying
anything, including renewal-eligible content.

| Activity | Renewal CEUs | Evidence | Free? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case clinic attended, live or recorded with the reflection form | 1 each | Attendance record or reflection form | Yes |
| Case clinic presented | 3 | The one-pager, chaired | Yes |
| Tool AMA attended | 1 each | Attendance record | Yes |
| A documented new project, any size, on the A3 template with before/after evidence | 6 | The A3; sampled for review at 10% | Yes |
| Serving as a team member on a certified project (named on its charter) | 3 | Charter and closure record | Yes |
| Serving as a Yellow Belt workshop producer or reviewer (calibrated) | 2 per cohort | Program record | Yes (paid) |
| Advanced workshops, masterclasses, external conferences | 1 per hour, up to 6 | Certificate of attendance | No — and never required |

Thirty-six clinics over three years, or one documented project plus six clinics, renews the
credential without spending anything. Lapse moves the credential to `inactive` on the
verification page — never deleted, reinstated on completing the current cycle's requirements
— and the renewal reminder sequence begins at month 30 with a statement of the CEUs already
logged, so that nobody is surprised.

## 6. What this system measures about itself

Feeds the [evaluation plan](../evaluation/evaluation-plan.md) coaching-quality section, and
is reported per cohort to the assessment lead:

- Checkpoint on-time rate: checkpoints held within the calendar window (target ≥ 90%).
- Coaching-note completeness: all six fields present on a 20% sample (target ≥ 95%).
- **Coach–reviewer ★ agreement:** for each mandatory item, the coach's last-checkpoint status
  ("result interpreted", "sentence written", "before-after on same definition", "monitoring
  live") set against the reviewer's decision. Target ≥ 85% agreement. A coach whose learners
  are told "on track" and then fail a ★ item is not a bad coach; they are an uncalibrated one,
  and the fix is a calibration session, then observation.
- Reviewer turnaround: gate notes within 5 business days and final reviews within 15, each
  ≥ 95%.
- Calibration drift events per quarter, by reviewer and by facilitator, and the number of
  re-calibrations run.
- Cohort L1 items on lab quality and coaching (evaluation plan L1, items 2–4), per instructor,
  per cohort.
- Resubmission rate by coach, read against caseload and vertical before anything else.

None of these is used to rank facilitators publicly. The outcomes page carries program-wide
figures; the facilitator sees their own.
