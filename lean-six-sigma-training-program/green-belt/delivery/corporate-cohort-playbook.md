# Green Belt — Corporate Cohort Playbook

For a sponsored cohort of 8–20 learners inside one organization. What is sold is not sixteen
seats on an eight-week course; it is **twelve to sixteen chartered projects, each with a
sponsor who will sit through four ten-minute tollgates, closed to Finance-validated or
mission-metric-validated results within six months, led by people who can then do it again
without us.** The course is how that happens. This playbook is how the program makes sure
the projects exist before the course starts, because a Green Belt cohort with no projects on
week 1 is an expensive statistics class.

Companion documents: the [facilitator and coaching system](facilitator-and-coaching-system.md)
(who coaches, who reviews, why the client never reviews), the
[review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md), the
[charter template](../project/charter-template.md), the
[sponsor verification and Finance validation form](../project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md),
and the White Belt [corporate rollout playbook](../../white-belt/delivery/corporate-rollout-playbook.md),
whose report is where this cohort's projects come from.

## 1. Where the projects come from

The White Belt rollout report and the Yellow Belt quarterly report already contain the
pipeline. The White Belt report clusters observations into themes with a triage status; the
Yellow Belt report aggregates mini-projects and carries every instructor's note that a
storyboard revealed "a problem bigger than a Just-Do-It." The conversation at the end of
those reports is, in the program's own words, *"30 cluster into four cross-departmental
problems with unknown causes and real money attached — that's a Green Belt cohort."* This
section turns that sentence into charters.

### 1.1 The selection filter

Run every theme cluster and every flagged Yellow Belt storyboard through six questions with
the client CI lead. A candidate that fails one is not a Green Belt project — it may be a
Just-Do-It, a Yellow Belt PDCA, or a Black Belt project, and saying which is a service.

| Question | Green Belt project if… | Otherwise it is… |
|---|---|---|
| Is the cause unknown? | Nobody can say with evidence why the problem happens; opinions differ | A Just-Do-It or a Yellow Belt PDCA if the cause is known and the fix is local |
| Is it one process with one owner? | The problem sits inside a process a single manager can change | A Black Belt project if it crosses functions with no single owner |
| Is there a measurable primary metric? | A quantity can be operationally defined and collected within four weeks, with a baseline of at least 20–30 observations obtainable | Not yet chartered; a data-collection Just-Do-It first |
| Is there money or a mission metric attached? | A defensible benefit class (hard, soft, cost avoidance, clinical or service impact) that Finance or the clinical lead recognizes | A worthwhile local fix, not a certification project |
| Is it bounded? | Closable within six months by one Green Belt with 5–8 hours a week and a team of two to four | Split it; charter one slice |
| Is it not already solved? | No countermeasure has been chosen; the sponsor is willing to be wrong about the cause | A rollout of a decided change; the program declines it as a project because the rubric cannot pass an unverified cause |

The sixth question loses the most candidates and is the one to hold firm on. A sponsor who
already knows the answer wants a project manager, and the rubric's ★ A3 will fail the
learner for the sponsor's certainty.

### 1.2 Pipeline sizing

Charter **1.5 projects per seat** into the pipeline at the readiness gate. Roughly a third of
candidates fail the filter on closer look, lose their sponsor to a reorganization, or turn out
to have no baseline data; a cohort of 14 with 14 charters on week −6 has 9–10 on week 1 and
a scramble. With 21 in the pipeline the cohort starts full and the surplus is next cohort's
pipeline, already filtered.

*MFG example (illustrative).* A plant's White Belt rollout produced 340 observations; the
theme report clustered 41 of them into six themes. Two were local 5S and labeling fixes
(Yellow Belt PDCA). One — inbound material shortages across three suppliers and two
planning teams — crossed functions with no single owner and was held for a Black Belt
candidate. Three passed the filter: changeover time on the two blow-molding lines (owner:
line manager; metric: minutes per changeover, per shift; hard benefit in capacity), rework
on the finishing cell (owner: cell leader; metric: rework units per 1,000 produced; hard
benefit in scrap and labor), and late shipment of make-to-order items (owner: shipping
supervisor; metric: % of orders shipped on the promised date, calendar basis; soft benefit
plus customer credits). Each was then split into two to three bounded slices with their own
primary metric (changeover on line 1 only; rework on the top-two Pareto defect codes), which
gave nine charters from three themes. Add four flagged Yellow Belt storyboards and the plant
has thirteen candidates for an eight-seat cohort.

### 1.3 One learner, one project

Each learner leads one chartered project with their own primary metric, because the
credential attests to work that person led. Two learners may work adjacent slices of the same
theme with a shared sponsor; they may not share one charter. Team members on each project
are the Yellow Belts and frontline staff who touch the process; the project is where the
Yellow Belt cohort goes to work.

## 2. Readiness gate (before any seat is provisioned)

Four conditions, all in writing, signed by the executive sponsor. A cohort that launches
without them produces exam passes and a stack of open projects, and it teaches the
organization that Green Belt means "trained," which is the belt inflation the program exists
to refuse.

| Condition | Evidence | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| **A named project sponsor for every seat** — the manager who owns the process and will make the four tollgate decisions | Name, role, and the process they own on each charter; each sponsor briefed (§5) | Do not launch that seat. Fill the seat from the surplus pipeline or run a smaller cohort |
| **Every charter signed before week 1** — problem statement, primary metric with an operational definition, scope, team, sponsor's signature, on the program's [charter template](../project/charter-template.md) | Signed charters on file at week −1; the charter clinic in week 1 rewrites them, it does not create them | Delay the start, not the sale. A cohort that starts with unsigned charters spends weeks 1–3 negotiating scope instead of measuring |
| **A named Finance partner** — one person from the client's Finance function who will agree the benefit classification at Tollgate 1 and validate money claims at closure, on the program's form | Named, briefed in week −2, has read the [validation form](../project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md) and the benefit classes | Do not launch. Without Finance named up front, every validated figure at closure becomes an argument, and the outcomes page cannot carry the cohort |
| **Learner time agreed** — 5.5 hours a week of structured time (3 h self-paced + 2.5 h lab) plus 5–8 hours of project work, during working hours, for eight weeks, then 2–4 hours a week to closure | Written and in the launch comms; each learner's own manager has confirmed it where the manager is not the sponsor | Resolve first. This is the top predictor of tollgate slippage, and slippage is published |

Plus the prerequisites the program checks, not the client: every learner holds a Yellow Belt
or passes the bridge assessment by week −2; data access for each primary metric is confirmed
by IT or the process owner by week −1 (the most common week-3 failure is a dataset that
needs a request ticket nobody raised).

Stated plainly to the buyer: the program will not seat a learner without a sponsor, a signed
charter, and a Finance partner. That refusal is the difference between a certification and a
certificate, and it is why the program can publish its closure rate.

## 3. Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Client senior leader | Signs the readiness gate; opens week 1; attends the showcase; unblocks sponsors |
| Project sponsor (one per seat) | Client process owner | Signs the charter; makes the four tollgate decisions in ten minutes each; signs the control plan handover; signs sponsor verification; answers the day-90 sustainment check |
| Finance partner | Client Finance | Agrees benefit class per project at Tollgate 1; validates money claims at closure; may decline a claim — their figure is the one published |
| CI lead / deployment lead | Client | Runs the pipeline (§1); coordinates sponsors and data access; receives the cohort report; never reviews projects |
| Learner | Client | Leads one chartered project; attends labs, checkpoints and tollgates |
| Team members | Client Yellow Belts and frontline staff | Map, collect data, test countermeasures with the learner |
| Instructor and coach(es) | Program, certified per the facilitator system | Labs, office hours, three checkpoints, monthly coaching to closure |
| Reviewer of record | Program reviewer bench; **never from the client** | Gate notes, rubric review, sustainment check |
| Engagement lead | Program | Readiness gate, briefings, cohort dashboard, cohort report and QBR, showcase |

Managers who are neither sponsor nor team member get the one-page manager brief: protect
the hours, ask "what did the data say?" in one team meeting a month, and do not ask the
learner to "just fix it" before the cause is verified.

## 4. Timeline — live-virtual cohort (default)

| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| −8 | Pipeline built from the rollout and Yellow Belt reports (§1); selection filter run with the CI lead |
| −6 | Readiness gate signed. Sponsors named per seat. Charters drafted with sponsors. Reviewer bench capacity confirmed |
| −4 | Sponsor briefing (§5). Finance partner briefed. Yellow Belt prerequisite verified; bridge assessments scheduled |
| −2 | Charters signed. Data access confirmed per project. Reviewers assigned blind by the assessment lead. Pre-assessment opens |
| −1 | Launch comms from the executive sponsor. Learner time confirmation from each manager on file |
| 1–8 | The cohort calendar as published in the [level README](../README.md): labs, office hours, checkpoints in weeks 2–3, 5–6 and 8; tollgates on the calendar in weeks 2, 4, 6, 8 with the sponsor in the room and the reviewer's gate note within five business days |
| 4 | **First sponsor readout to the executive sponsor** — every project's baseline chart and MSA result on one page each. The equivalent of the White Belt week-3 theme report: the moment the organization sees its own processes measured |
| 9 | Exam (proctored on site). Cohort L1 survey |
| 9–26 | Monthly coaching to closure; projects submitted for review as each closes; Finance validation at each closure |
| 26 | Closure deadline (six months post week 8). **Showcase** (§9). Cohort report and QBR (§10). Black Belt nominations |
| 26 + 90 days | Day-90 sustainment checks complete; sustainment rate reported to the sponsor and the outcomes page |

## 5. Sponsor briefing (90 minutes, week −4, all project sponsors together)

What sponsors are told, in substance:

> **What you are sponsoring.** One person, one process you own, one metric, six months. Your
> learner will find out why the problem happens before proposing anything. That is slower
> than deciding, and it is the point.
>
> **What you do.** Four times, ten minutes each, on the cohort's calendar, you hear one page
> and decide: proceed, proceed with a condition, or hold. Between tollgates you protect the
> hours, you open doors to data, and when the learner tells you a cause you believed did not
> verify, you say "good — what did?" At closure you sign that the project happened, the
> results are real, and the control plan is owned. At day 90 you answer one question about
> whether it held.
>
> **What you must not do.** Do not name the countermeasure before Tollgate 3. Do not move
> the goalposts: the metric, its operational definition and its collection method are fixed
> at Tollgate 2, and a result measured against a different definition will fail the review
> whatever it shows. Do not ask for a number the data does not support; Finance validates
> what is claimed and the program publishes only what Finance validates.
>
> **What you get.** A verified cause for a problem you have been managing around, a
> countermeasure with before/after evidence, a control plan with an owner, and a person who
> can do it again.

The tollgate discipline is the sponsor's contribution and the program's leverage. A
tollgate that runs 45 minutes because the sponsor wants a deck teaches the learner to build
decks. The [tollgate checklists](../project/tollgate-checklists.md) are given to sponsors at
the briefing, and the week 8 lab rehearses the ten-minute format with sponsors invited.

## 6. Timeline — the 2 × 3-day on-site variant with virtual tollgates

For organizations that cannot release learners weekly or that want the cohort in one room.
The content hours are the same; the phase lock holds; the tollgates stay on a calendar. What
changes is that self-paced content is done before each intensive and the labs are stacked.

| When | What | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks −8 to −1 | Pipeline, readiness gate, briefings, charters — identical to §4 | — |
| Weeks −2 to 0 | Weeks 1–4 self-paced content (12 h) released with a deadline; office hour weekly online | Virtual |
| **Intensive 1 (3 days, on site)** | Day 1: charter clinic, VSM lab on the vertical case then own process. Day 2: MSA lab (the Gage R&R that fails), data-collection plan clinic. Day 3: capability lab, baseline plan; checkpoint 1 held 1:1 during breakouts. Ends with each learner's data-collection plan and MSA plan signed off by their coach | On site; producer present; two coaches for 13–20 |
| Week +2 | **Tollgate 1 (Define)** — sponsor and learner, ten minutes, virtual; gate note within five days | Virtual |
| Weeks +1 to +4 | Learners collect baseline and run the MSA on their own process; weekly virtual office hour | Virtual |
| Week +4 | **Tollgate 2 (Measure)** — virtual, on the calendar. A project without a baseline by now goes into Intensive 2 anyway and the gap is on the coaching note | Virtual |
| Weeks +3 to +4 | Weeks 5–8 self-paced content (12 h) released | Virtual |
| **Intensive 2 (3 days, on site, one month after Intensive 1)** | Day 1: cause-narrowing lab, then own project. Day 2: stats lab — every test on the case data, then the learner's own; checkpoint 2 during breakouts. Day 3: pilot design and mistake-proofing clinic, control-plan drafting, tollgate rehearsal with sponsors invited for the last 90 minutes | On site |
| Week +7 | **Tollgate 3 (Analyze)** — virtual; the test run on the learner's own data after Intensive 2 | Virtual |
| Week +8 to +10 | Pilot runs; checkpoint 3 virtual in week +9 | Virtual |
| Week +11 | **Tollgate 4 (Improve/Control)** — virtual; control plan drafted; handover scheduled | Virtual |
| Week +12 | Exam, on site or proctored online per the client's choice; L1 survey | Either |
| To week +34 | Monthly coaching to closure; showcase at closure deadline; sustainment checks | Virtual, showcase on site |

Three things the on-site variant needs that the weekly cohort does not. First, the self-paced
content deadline is enforced by the CI lead, because an intensive where half the room has not
watched the MSA module becomes the lecture the lab standard forbids. Second, data access for
every primary metric is confirmed *before* Intensive 1, because there are only four weeks
between the intensives to collect a baseline. Third, sponsors attend the last afternoon of
Intensive 2; a sponsor who has heard the ten-minute format once runs Tollgates 3 and 4 the
way the program needs.

## 7. Cohort composition

- **8 to 20 learners; 12 to 16 is the package.** Below 8 the labs lose the cross-project
  learning; above 20 the cohort splits, whatever the buyer asks.
- **At least three departments or value streams**, so that the cohort's projects are not all
  the same problem with the same sponsor, and so that no sponsor carries more than three
  projects — sponsor attention is the scarce resource, not learner time.
- **Vertical.** One vertical per cohort so that the exam form draws NEU plus that vertical's
  items and the case datasets match the room. A mixed organization (a hospital's supply chain
  team in a healthcare cohort) uses the healthcare case with the learner's own process
  supplying the manufacturing content.
- **Software.** One primary tool per cohort agreed at the gate (Minitab or Excel with the
  stats add-in); the R/Python notebooks are available to any learner who wants them.
- **Prior program learners.** Prefer the Yellow Belts whose storyboards were flagged and the
  White Belts whose observations became the themes — they have already shown the behavior,
  and the certificate packet cites their own earlier work.
- **No learner is placed on a project whose sponsor is their direct manager's manager's
  problem to solve.** The sponsor owns the process; the learner touches it; if those do not
  line up the charter is wrong, not the person.

## 8. Finance partnership

The Finance partner is named at the gate and does four things, on the calendar:

| When | Finance does |
|---|---|
| Week −2 | Reads the benefit classes (hard, soft, cost avoidance, clinical or service impact) and the validation form; agrees which internal rates and cost bases will be used |
| Tollgate 1 | Agrees each project's benefit class and the basis on which any money will be counted, written on the charter |
| Closure | Validates the money claimed, on the form, using the before/after evidence from the project file; may reduce or decline a claim. The program publishes Finance's figure, never the learner's |
| Cohort report | Signs the cohort's validated total, by benefit class |

Two rules stated at the briefing. A benefit is counted in the period it was measured and is
not annualized on any program surface (Finance may annualize for the client's own purposes;
the cohort report shows both, labeled). And a benefit is counted once: a project that grew
from a White Belt observation or a Yellow Belt Just-Do-It credits the project, notes the
origin, and never lets the same money appear in two reports. The client's own CI dashboard
usually already double-counts; the program's cohort report is the place that does not, and
saying so to the CFO is the most credible sentence in the readout.

*TXN example (illustrative).* A shared-services cohort closes a project that cut invoice
rework from 6.1% to 2.4% of invoices over a twelve-week measurement window. The learner's
file claims 1,640 avoided rework hours a year. Finance validates 380 hours in the twelve
weeks measured at the loaded clerical rate, classifies it as soft benefit (capacity, not
headcount), and annualizes it in the client's own dashboard. The program's cohort report
shows 380 hours, soft, twelve weeks; the outcomes page aggregates that figure and no other.

## 9. Showcase and Black Belt nomination

At the closure deadline the cohort presents to the executive sponsor, the project sponsors,
Finance and any leaders the sponsor invites. Every closed project, ten minutes, one page, in
the tollgate format — the same page the reviewer scored. Open projects present their status
honestly; the showcase is not curated for success, because a showcase that only shows wins
teaches the organization to hide the losses.

The instructor nominates the top projects for the program's annual showcase, where nominees
receive the Black Belt scholarship. Nomination criteria, published to learners in week 1:
every ★ item earned on first submission; a verified cause that was not the sponsor's opening
theory; before/after evidence with an honest reading of any shortfall; a control plan the
reviewer's day-90 check found live. The instructor nominates; the reviewer of record's
feedback is the evidence; neither knows the other's view until the cohort report.

Separately, the reviewer flags any project that revealed a Black-Belt-sized problem — a
cross-functional cause outside the sponsor's process, an interaction that needs a designed
experiment, a control plan that needs a system-level change — with the rubric's Black Belt
invitation. The engagement lead brings those flags, and the pipeline's held candidates (§1.1,
"no single owner"), into the QBR as next year's Black Belt seats and their projects.

## 10. Cohort report and the next-cohort conversation

The cohort report, delivered as a 45-minute readout to the executive sponsor with Finance
in the room: exam first-attempt and eventual pass rates for the cohort; tollgates held on
calendar; projects submitted, passed, resubmitted; closure rate at the six-month deadline;
sponsor-verified projects; Finance-validated benefit by class, in the measured periods, with
the attribution note; day-90 sustainment rate when available; L1 items on sponsor engagement
and learner time, by department, verbatim, because a department scoring low on "my sponsor
made the tollgate decisions" has a sponsor problem the next cohort will inherit.

The next conversation writes itself: *"Twelve projects, ten closed, nine sustained at day
90, four with hard benefit Finance signed. Three learners found problems that need a Black
Belt. The pipeline has seven filtered candidates left and your Yellow Belt cohort just
flagged five more."* The engagement lead names the surplus charters and their sponsors, and
the sponsors who ran clean tollgates — they are the ones to give two projects next time.

## 11. Common cohort failure modes

| Failure | Signature | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| The solution wearing a charter | The sponsor names the countermeasure at the briefing; the learner's fishbone has one bone | Selection filter question 6; the sponsor briefing's "do not name the countermeasure before Tollgate 3"; ★ A3 explained to the sponsor as the reason |
| Sponsor drift | Tollgates rescheduled twice; the sponsor sends a delegate; the reorganization moves the process | Sponsor named per seat at the gate; no more than three projects per sponsor; sponsor change is a coaching-note flag and a same-week re-charter or re-scope |
| No baseline by Tollgate 2 | Data lives in a system nobody has export rights to; the request ticket was raised in week 3 | Data access confirmed per project at week −1, by name; on-site variant: before Intensive 1 |
| "We trust the system" | MSA skipped on a system timestamp that turns out to be entered by hand at shift end | Week 3 lab's failing Gage R&R; ★ M3's rule that "we trust the system" is not a reason; checkpoint 1 asks who enters the stamp |
| Learner time eroded | Checkpoint on-time rate falls after week 4; the learner's manager (not the sponsor) has reassigned the hours | Gate condition 4 with the manager's own confirmation; the manager brief; slippage reported to the executive sponsor in the week 4 readout, by department, never by name |
| Finance at the end | Finance first hears of the cohort at closure and rejects half the claims | Finance partner named at the gate; benefit class agreed at Tollgate 1 on the charter; Finance's figure is the published one, and everyone knows it in week −2 |
| Client wants to review | The CI lead, a client Black Belt, asks to score or co-score the projects "to build internal capability" | Never at Green Belt. Offer the client Black Belt a second-reader seat on tollgates, unsigned, as their own Black Belt coaching requirement — capability built without the credential's independence spent |
| One-department cohort | Fourteen learners, one sponsor, one process; the labs have no cross-project learning and the sponsor cannot attend fourteen tollgates | Composition rule: three departments, three projects per sponsor at most |
| Over-claiming | The learner's slide says "60% improvement" from a two-day window; the sponsor repeats it upward | The rubric's ★ I3 standard; Finance validation; the cohort report's measured-period rule; the same standard in the showcase |
| The project that never ends | Month 5, still piloting, scope has grown to the department | Monthly coaching to closure; the two-months-no-movement three-way conversation; the rubric passes a bounded slice honestly finished |
| The learner leaves | Learner changes employer at month 4 with the project open | The charter's team and sponsor keep the process; the credential follows the learner only if they can close and the sponsor still verifies; otherwise the file closes as documented learning and a new learner may re-charter the slice next cohort |

*HC example (illustrative).* A hospital cohort's project on medication-reconciliation
completeness at admission has a sponsor who is the pharmacy director, while the admitting
nurses who complete the reconciliation report to the nursing director. By Tollgate 2 the
baseline is collected but nursing has not agreed to a pilot, and the learner is caught between
two rational managers each protecting their staff's time. The coaching note flags "scope
exceeds a single process owner"; the fix is a re-charter to the pharmacy-owned half of the
process (the pharmacist verification step, with its own metric) and a handover of the nursing
half to the pipeline as a Black Belt candidate with both directors named. The learner
finishes a bounded slice honestly; the organization gets its cross-functional problem
named and owned.
