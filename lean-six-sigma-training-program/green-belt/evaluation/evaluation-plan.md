# Green Belt — Evaluation Plan

What we measure at Green Belt, how, and what we do when the numbers are bad. Four levels
(Kirkpatrick framing), with the honest constraint stated up front: **Green Belt carries
Levels 1, 2 and 3 rigorously and Level 4 as fact per project — sponsor-verified,
Finance-validated or mission-metric-validated, measured over a stated window.** It can do
that because the product at this level is a real project with a calendar, a reviewer and a
sponsor who signs. What it still does not do is isolate the course's contribution from the
learner's, the sponsor's and the organization's, or turn that into a training ROI ratio. The
program publishes what the projects produced, validated by people who do not work for the
program, and lets the buyer draw the ratio.

Governs: the assessment lead (owns every figure here), the engagement lead (the cohort
report), reviewers of record (gate records, sustainment checks) and coaches (checkpoint
records). Agrees with the [level README](../README.md) (calendar, gates, blueprint), the
[review rubric](../project/review-rubric.md), the
[facilitator and coaching system](../delivery/facilitator-and-coaching-system.md) §6, the
[sustainment check](../project/sustainment-check.md), the
[sponsor verification and Finance validation form](../project/sponsor-verification-and-finance-validation.md),
the [assessment policy](../../assessment/standard-setting-and-item-policy.md) and the
[outcomes page specification](../../program-operations/outcomes-page.md). The White Belt
[evaluation plan](../../white-belt/evaluation/evaluation-plan.md) is the house pattern and
the source of the attribution rule this plan inherits.

Three rules apply to every figure below:

1. **Per cohort, never per learner to a manager.** A sponsor sees their own project's
   result; an employer sees aggregates. Item-level exam responses go to nobody outside the
   program (terms of certification §7).
2. **Definitions are fixed at enrollment.** Changing a definition mid-series is the same
   error the rubric fails a learner for. A changed definition is a change-log entry, and
   the series restarts with the change dated.
3. **No figure is removed because it is inconvenient**, and the bad cohort is reported with
   the same prominence as the good one. The improvement board item that owns the bad number
   is linked from it.

## Level 1 — Reaction

**Instrument:** 12-item survey, five-point agreement scale plus two free-text items,
completed in the week 9 session after the exam (not emailed — email surveys return around
15% and skew positive). Individual self-paced learners complete it at their exam sitting.
A four-item **closure pulse** follows when the project file is submitted, because most of
what the client controls happens after week 8.

| # | Item | Diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I can run this week's tools on my own project data without help | The course |
| 2 | The labs and the case datasets were relevant to my project and my vertical | The course |
| 3 | The course respected my time — the labs were application, not lecture | The course |
| 4 | I knew at every tollgate what the reviewer would need to see | The course |
| 5 | My coach helped me earn the rubric without telling me my root cause or my countermeasure | The coaching |
| 6 | The course was accessible to me (captions, keyboard, screen reader, materials in advance, language) | The course |
| 7 | I would recommend this course to a colleague who has a project | The course |
| 8 | My sponsor attended the four tollgates and made the decisions in the room | **The client** |
| 9 | I had the agreed project hours, during working hours | **The client** |
| 10 | I received the data I needed by the date that was agreed | **The client** |
| 11 | The Finance partner or mission-metric validator was engaged before Tollgate 2, not at closure | **The client** |
| 12 | I believe the control plan will be run after I hand it over | **The client** |
| F1 | What one thing would you change about the course? | |
| F2 | What stood between you and your project this week that had nothing to do with the course? | |

**Closure pulse (at submission):** P1 my sponsor stayed the sponsor through closure · P2 I
kept the project hours through closure · P3 Finance or the validator validated on the basis
agreed at Tollgate 2 · P4 the process owner ran the control plan for the first 30 days
without me.

**Items 8–12 and the pulse are the ones that matter, and they are not about the course.**
They measure whether the client did what the readiness gate said it would. They are reported
to the executive sponsor by department, verbatim, in the cohort report, because a department
scoring 2.4 on item 8 has a sponsor problem the next cohort will inherit, and no lab redesign
touches it. Practicum-track learners answer items 8–11 about the instructor acting as
sponsor; those answers stay inside the program.

**Thresholds:** items 1–4 and 7 ≥ 4.0 mean per cohort; items 2–4 ≥ 4.0 per instructor
(the facilitator system §2.4 uses them to keep facilitator status live). Item 5 ≥ 4.0 per
coach; a coach below 3.5 gets an observation, not a note in a file. Item 6 ≥ 4.5, with any
individual score ≤ 3 triggering an accessibility follow-up within 2 business days. Items
8–12 have no pass threshold; they are diagnostic, and a low score triggers a conversation
with the executive sponsor, not a content change. F2 free text is coded to the coaching
note's five risk categories (sponsor · data · scope · time · Finance) and reported as counts.

## Level 2 — Learning

**Instruments:** the 12-item pre-assessment (PRE pool) at week −2; the 100-item proctored
exam; the cumulative checks in the week files; and the rubric-reviewed project, whose pass
rates say what the learner can *do* with the tools. (The calendar behaviors the project
produces — tollgates held, closure, handover — are Level 3, below.)

### 2.1 Exam

| Metric | Target | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|
| First-attempt pass rate | 70–88% | **Above 95%: the exam is decorative.** Raise item difficulty, not the cut score, and re-run the Angoff. Below 60%: item and content review before the cut is touched; a cut set by panel is not lowered because a cohort struggled |
| Eventual pass rate (within the included retake) | ≥ 90% | A gap of more than 25 points between first-attempt and eventual means the retake carries the credential; look at the 14-day study period and the section profile first |
| Mean pre/post gain, matched PRE items | ≥ 25 percentage points | Check for ceiling first — Yellow Belt holders arrive knowing Define. A pre score above 60% on the PRE pool means the pool is too easy, not that the course taught little |
| Section-level mean, per blueprint section | No section below 65% on a form that passes overall | C2 (t-tests through simple regression) is the section to watch. Below 65% on C2 across a cohort is a week 6 lab question, not a learner question |
| Exhibit (X) items against concept (K) items | X-item mean within 15 points of the K-item mean | A wider gap means learners can recite a test and cannot read its output — the exact failure the interpretation-first design exists to prevent. Check exhibit rendering on the proctoring platform before touching content |
| Cumulative-check accuracy (spaced retrieval) | ≥ 70% on re-tested items | Below that, the earlier week needs rework; this catches shallow learning the week's own lab misses |
| Internal consistency (KR-20) | ≥ 0.80 per form | Below 0.80, item analysis before anything else |

### 2.2 Item statistics

Per the assessment policy §4, after every 100 administrations of a form or quarterly,
whichever comes first:

| Statistic | Keep | Review | Retire or rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Difficulty (p-value) | 0.30–0.90 | 0.20–0.30 or 0.90–0.95 | < 0.20 or > 0.95 |
| Discrimination (point-biserial) | ≥ 0.20 | 0.10–0.20 | < 0.10 or negative |
| Distractors | Every distractor chosen by ≥ 3% | One dead distractor | Two or more dead |

Two Green Belt notes. First, an X item with p < 0.30 is checked for a rendering problem
(the exhibit table or chart description did not display as authored) before it is judged as
an item. Second, with cohorts of 8–20 and a 350-item bank, a form reaches 100 administrations
after roughly six to twelve cohorts; until then, flagged items are reviewed on judgment with
the small n stated, and the outcomes page says the statistics are forming. Point-biserial and
KR-20 are computed in Excel (CORREL of the item score against the total minus that item;
KR-20 from the item p-values and total variance), in R or Python (the `psych` or `ltm`
packages; `pingouin` in Python) or in Minitab (Item Analysis, which reports Cronbach's α and
item–total correlations) — the numbers agree.

### 2.3 Project review

| Metric | Target | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|
| First-submission pass rate (75 points and every ★ item) | 55–80% | Above 90% on ≥ 30 reviews: reviewer drift toward leniency — calibration before content. Below 50%: read the ★ failure profile before anything else |
| Eventual pass rate (after the one revise-and-resubmit cycle) | ≥ 85% | Below that, the resubmission cycle is not doing its job; check whether the first review's "single highest-leverage fix" was written as the rubric requires |
| ★ item failure profile | No single ★ item accounts for more than 30% of first-submission failures across ≥ 30 reviews | ★ M3 → week 3 lab; ★ A3 → week 6; ★ I3 → week 7; ★ C1 → week 8 and the Tollgate 4 owner question. The week goes on the improvement board as a PDCA item with the profile as its measure |
| Data-ethics stops | Tracked, by type (removed points · changed definition · reconstructed baseline) | Any rise is a week 4 and Tollgate 2 question about how baselines are being fixed; the individual case follows the terms of certification §4 |
| Resubmission rate by reviewer and by coach | Read against caseload and vertical before anything else | A reviewer whose resubmission rate sits 20 points from the bench median re-calibrates on the anchor set |

### 2.4 Practicum track against real projects

The Practicum credential is labeled precisely because the tracks differ in what they can
claim; the exam and the rubric are the same, so the *learning* should not differ. Reported
per period once each arm has 30:

| Compared | Standard track (employer and partner pool) | Practicum | If they differ by more than 10 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exam first-attempt pass rate | | | The exam is identical, so a gap is who enrolled, not what was taught. Say so; do not adjust either |
| Rubric first-submission pass rate | | | Practicum higher: the simulated dataset is too clean or the response pack too generous — the practicum file gets a harder planted structure. Practicum lower: data-pack release timing or the instructor's sponsor-role decisions |
| ★ item failure profile | | | A ★ that fails only on one track names the design flaw. ★ M3 failing on Practicum means the planted measurement problem is too easy to miss; ★ C1 failing on standard means real handovers are slipping (Level 3) |
| Mean section scores (Define … Storytelling) | | | Define and Control are where a simulation is weakest; a Practicum Define score above the standard track's is a sign the case brief answers questions a real sponsor would not |

Partner-pool projects are compared with employer projects on the same lines, per the
partner-pool document §2.8: a pool that passes at a materially different rate from employer
projects is a screening problem, and it goes on the improvement board, not on the learner.

## Level 3 — Behavior

What a Green Belt is supposed to *do* is lead a bounded project to closure on a calendar and
leave a control plan running. Because the calendar is fixed (tollgates in weeks 2, 4, 6 and
8; closure within six months of week 8), slippage is measurable, and its causes — sponsor
time, data access, learner hours, scope — are designable. None of them is the learner's
character, and the cohort report never reads that way.

| Metric | Definition | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tollgates held on calendar** | A gate held in its calendar week (or the on-site variant's stated week) with the sponsor or named deputy in the room; a hold cleared within 10 business days still counts as on calendar | Reviewer gate record | ≥ 85% of gates; reported per gate. Tollgate 2 is where it breaks — a baseline that does not exist by week 4 is a data-access failure from week −1 |
| Sponsor decisions by gate | Proceed · proceed with conditions · hold · re-scope · stop, per gate | Gate record | Tracked. A hold rate above 40% at any gate on ≥ 30 projects is a content or readiness question for that phase, named on the board |
| Checkpoint on-time rate | Checkpoints 1–3 and monthly checkpoints held within their calendar window | Coaching record | ≥ 90% (facilitator system §6) |
| **Project closure within 6 months** | Project submitted for independent review within six months of week 8 ÷ charters signed at week 1; and passed (eventually) ÷ charters at week 1 | Assessment record | Submitted ≥ 85%; passed ≥ 75%. Re-scopes count in the denominator; honorable stops are shown as their own count with the reason (problem gone · priority changed · cause outside authority · sponsor left) |
| Median days from Tollgate 4 to submission | Calendar days | Assessment record | Tracked. A median above 90 days, or two consecutive months with no movement on more than a quarter of open files, is the "project that never ends" and triggers the three-way scope conversation the coaching system specifies |
| **Control plan live at day 30** | At submission, the monitoring record covers days 0–30 after the handover date in the sponsor attestation, the named owner (not the Green Belt) kept the chart, and the response plan is written | Project file, Part A3 | ≥ 90% of submissions. This is ★ C1's threshold, so a passing project always has it; the figure is over *all* submissions to see how many arrive without a live plan and go back |
| Median days from Tollgate 4 to handover | Calendar days | Part A3 | Tracked. Handover slipping past 60 days after Tollgate 4 is where sustainment is lost before it is measured |
| **Second project or coaching within 12 months** | Certified Green Belts who submit a documented new project on the A3, are named on another certified project's charter, or coach a Yellow Belt mini-project within 12 months of award | Renewal record | ≥ 25% — the single best behavior-change indicator this level has, because it is unprompted and uncredentialed |
| Black Belt progression | Green Belts admitted to Black Belt within 24 months | Enrollment data | Tracked, not targeted — a commercial metric, not a learning one |

Two things Level 3 does not count. A project that closed as documented learning without the
credential is not a failure of the learner and is reported as a stop with its reason. And
the Practicum track has no tollgate slippage worth publishing — its sponsor is an instructor
with a calendar — so Practicum projects are excluded from every row above except checkpoint
on-time rate.

*TXN example (illustrative).* A shared-services cohort of 14. Gates on calendar: Tollgate 1
14 of 14, Tollgate 2 9 of 14, Tollgate 3 12 of 13 (one honorable stop), Tollgate 4 12 of 13.
The Tollgate 2 figure is 64% and the gate record shows the reason in every case: the invoice
extract needed a request ticket that IT sized at three weeks. The cohort report puts that
sentence in front of the executive sponsor, by department, and the next cohort's readiness
gate confirms data access by name at week −1 — which the playbook already says, and which
this figure shows was not done. Nobody on the cohort was slow.

## Level 4 — Results (stated as fact, labeled as such)

Green Belt produces validated results and the program says exactly which kind each one is.
The learner's own number never appears on any program surface; Finance's figure or the
validator's does. What is claimed, and how it is labeled:

| Claimed outcome | Evidence basis | How it is labeled on every surface |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor-verified projects | Part A signed; the reviewer's C3 and I3 read against it | Fact — count and share of closed projects |
| Financial impact by class (hard · soft · cost avoidance) | Part B, Finance partner's signature, measured over the evidence window | **"Finance-validated, measured over N weeks; not annualized."** Classes are never added into one headline; soft benefit is stated in hours or units first and in money only at Finance's rate, labeled soft |
| Clinical or service impact | Part C, mission-metric executive's signature, guardrail stated | **"Sponsor-verified, mission metric; no money claimed."** Reported in the metric's own unit — minutes, readmissions per 100, % on time — with the same standing as a financial row, never converted to money on a program surface |
| Sponsor-verified, no benefit claimed | Part A only; an honest failure or a project without a benefit class | Fact — count. A passed project whose pilot did not move the metric is in this row, in the open |
| **Day-90 sustainment** | The reviewer's `sustained` flag from the sustainment check, set from the monitoring record | **Rate = `true` ÷ projects checked**, with `unverified`, `not-applicable` and `pending` shown as their own counts, never folded silently into the denominator |
| Practicum completions | The rubric and the labeled credential | Count only. Nothing from a Practicum project enters any impact figure |

**The `sustained` flag** is set only by the reviewer of record, only from the monitoring
record, and never changes the credential. The evaluation plan uses it twice: the sustainment
rate per cohort and per period, and the reason category where the flag is `false` — owner
left without replacement · monitoring stopped · countermeasure removed · metric drifted with
no response · process changed. A rising share of any one reason is a week 8 curriculum
question, not a learner question. A false flag at n = 1 tells you about one handover; a
reason category at n = 30 tells you about the course.

### Attribution discipline

The rule inherited from White Belt, now with money attached:

1. **Counted once, at the level that closed it, with the origin noted.** A Green Belt project
   that grew from a Yellow Belt mini-project or a White Belt observation credits the Green
   Belt project; the earlier level's report shows "became project [ref]" and no amount. A
   Black Belt project that grows out of a Green Belt project credits the Green Belt for its
   own measured window before the Black Belt charter date and nothing after.
2. **Finance's figure, never the learner's.** Where Finance reduces or declines a claim, the
   reduced figure is the only one that exists on any program surface. Where the sponsor's
   dashboard already double-counts, the cohort report is the place that does not, and the
   report says so.
3. **Classes are never added.** Hard, soft and cost avoidance are reported in separate rows
   with separate n; "total impact" does not appear. Mission-metric results sit beside them in
   their own units.
4. **Nothing is annualized or extrapolated.** Measured window, dated, with n. Finance may
   annualize for the client's own dashboard; the cohort report shows both, labeled; the
   outcomes page carries the measured figure only.
5. **Adjacent slices are split in writing.** Two learners on adjacent slices of one theme
   with a shared sponsor each carry their own metric and their own Part B; an overlap is
   split on the form with Finance's initials, per the form's B4.
6. **Estimates are labeled estimates.** At Green Belt the only estimate that exists is
   Finance's own run-rate annualization, and it is labeled "projected, assumes current
   volume" wherever it appears. A learner-reported figure is not an estimate; it is not
   published.

*HC example (illustrative).* A hospital cohort of 12 charters. Eleven submitted within six
months; ten passed, two of them after resubmission; one stopped honorably at Tollgate 3
when the verified cause sat in pharmacy, outside the ward sponsor's authority, and went to
the Black Belt pipeline. Of the ten: four Finance-validated hard rows (overtime, agency
hours, consumables — measured over windows of six to twelve weeks, reported as four rows
with their windows, never summed), two soft rows in nursing hours, four mission-metric
results (discharge order-to-departure median, medication-reconciliation completeness, two
others), each with its guardrail. Day 90: ten checks sent; seven `true`, one `false` (the
owner left and the replacement was never named — reason category *owner left without
replacement*), one `unverified` (reported held, no record), one `pending` (late handover).
The cohort report sentence: *"Twelve chartered, ten certified, seven of nine resolved checks
sustained at day 90, four with hard benefit Finance signed over their measured windows."*
The outcomes page shows the same facts as counts and rates with n = 10, pooled with other
cohorts, and no dollar total.

### What Level 4 refuses

- No training ROI ratio, at any n. The program cannot isolate its own effect from the
  sponsor's and the learner's, and any figure that pretended to would be an estimate
  presented as fact.
- No "average savings per Green Belt" headline. Medians with interquartile range, per class,
  once n allows — the benchmark library's rule.
- No testimonial in place of a number, and no number without its class, window and n.

## Coaching quality

The facilitator system §6 names what it measures about itself; this section sets the
thresholds and adds the two audits that protect the credential's independence.

| Measure | Definition | Target | If missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coach/assessor separation audit** | Quarterly, the assessment lead samples 20% of live assignments and checks: the conflicts in §3.2 were checked and logged; the reviewer's file carries a cohort ID and no instructor or coach name; no coaching note sits in the project file; the reviewer attended no checkpoint, office hour or tollgate meeting; every gate note is ≤ 5 sentences and states status without advice (any "try", "consider", "you should" is a finding) | Zero breaches | A breach re-assigns the file to a fresh reviewer who re-scores blind; the original score is voided; the breach goes on the improvement board with its cause. Two breaches by one reviewer in a year end their bench place for the year |
| Reviewer calibration agreement | Every active reviewer scores the four-file anchor set each quarter they review: within 5 points of the anchor total on all four, exact agreement on every ★ item | 100% of active reviewers calibrated in the quarter; drift events tracked by reviewer | A drift event stops that reviewer's live reviews until re-calibrated; a second in twelve months adds second-reading on their next three reviews |
| Coach–reviewer ★ agreement | The coach's last-checkpoint status per ★ item against the reviewer's decision | ≥ 85% | Calibration session for the coach on the anchor set, then an observation; never a ranking |
| Second-reader agreement | A Black Belt candidate's independent gate score against the reviewer of record's | Tracked as the candidate's training measure, not as a Green Belt quality figure | — |
| Reviewer turnaround | Gate notes within 5 business days; final reviews within 15 | Each ≥ 95% | A reviewer above the 12-file cap is the usual cause; the assignment log shows it |
| Appeal re-score difference | Stage-1 blind re-scores against the original | Within 5 points, ★ items agree, on ≥ 90% of appeals | Below that, the anchor set is not covering the live cases; add an anchor file from the appeals |
| Coaching-note completeness | All six fields on a 20% sample | ≥ 95% | Field 5 ("what the learner decided") missing is the one that matters — the coach is deciding |

None of these ranks facilitators or reviewers publicly. The outcomes page carries the
program-wide figures; each facilitator and reviewer sees their own.

## The annual 10% re-review

Per the credential specification §2.6 in the
[crosswalk and credential document](../bok-crosswalk-and-credential.md): each year the
assessment lead draws a random 10% of projects certified in the previous twelve months (at
least five, or all when fewer), stratified by track and vertical where numbers allow; a
calibrated reviewer who was not the original reviewer, instructor or coach re-scores blind;
sponsors of sampled standard-track projects are re-contacted to confirm the attestation and
the day-90 answer.

| Published each year, with n and period | Target |
|---|---|
| Sample size and the population it was drawn from | 10%, minimum five |
| ★ item agreement rate between original and re-score | ≥ 95% |
| Mean absolute score difference | ≤ 4 points |
| Share of pairs more than 5 points apart | ≤ 10% |
| Sponsor re-contact confirmation rate | ≥ 90%; a sponsor who cannot be reached is a count, not a `no` |
| Integrity referrals | Count, with no detail |

A ★ disagreement goes to a third calibrated reviewer, two of three decide, and the holder's
credential is unchanged whatever the outcome — the error is the program's, and the holder is
told so in writing with the no-fee coached resubmission offer. The re-review's job is to
audit reviewers, and its figures are published so that an un-audited badge is never read as
a weaker one.

## Program-level aggregation — the published figures

These are the Green Belt slots on the published outcomes page. The page's rules apply: the
definition is one click from the figure; below minimum n the slot shows *"n = X, not yet
reportable"* and is never hidden; figures are program-wide by level and by vertical where n
allows; no cohort or client is identifiable; nothing is annualized; the bad quarter is
published with a link to its board item.

| Figure | Definition | Minimum n to publish |
|---|---|---|
| Exam first-attempt pass rate | Passed on attempt 1 ÷ all first attempts | 50 attempts |
| Eventual pass rate | Passed within the included retake ÷ all who attempted | 50 |
| Pre/post gain | Mean post − mean pre on matched PRE items, with the pre score shown | 50 matched pairs |
| Project first-submission pass rate | Passed at first review ÷ first reviews | 30 reviews |
| Project eventual pass rate | Passed after the included resubmission ÷ projects reviewed | 30 |
| **Sampling rate** | Projects independently reviewed ÷ projects submitted — always 100% at this level, and published so the reader can see it | always |
| ★ item failure share | First-submission failures by ★ item | 30 first submissions |
| Tollgates held on calendar | As defined in Level 3, per gate | 30 projects (120 gates) |
| Closure within 6 months | Submitted, and passed, ÷ charters at week 1, from cohorts whose six-month window has closed | 30 charters |
| Control plan live at day 30 | As defined in Level 3 | 30 submissions |
| **Day-90 sustainment rate** | `true` ÷ projects checked, with `unverified`, `not-applicable` and `pending` as counts | 30 resolved checks |
| Sponsor-verified share | Part A signed ÷ projects certified, and the `verifiedImpact` distribution | 30 certified |
| Finance-validated impact, by class | Per class: number of projects, median measured benefit with interquartile range, range of evidence windows in weeks | 10 validated projects per class; below that, the count only |
| Mission-metric results | Number of projects; per vertical, the median relative change in the primary metric with its range | 10 projects per vertical for the median; the count always |
| Practicum completions | Count of Practicum credentials issued | always |
| Partner-pool health | Projects screened, listed, matched, closed; pool pass rate beside employer pass rate | 30 per arm for the pass rates; depth always |
| Coach–reviewer ★ agreement | As defined above | 30 projects |
| Separation-audit breaches | Breaches found ÷ files sampled, per quarter | always, from the first quarter |
| Annual re-review | Sample n, ★ agreement, mean absolute difference, integrity referrals | the year's sample (≥ 5) |
| Reviewer turnaround | Share of gate notes and reviews inside the published turnaround | always |
| Second project within 12 months | As defined in Level 3 | 100 certified holders |
| L1 items 1–5 and 7 | Means; items 8–12 shown as a range, because they diagnose client culture, not the course | 50 responses |

**The honest n = 0 state.** At launch every slot above is on the page with `n = 0` and the
outcomes page's sentence: *"This program has not yet run a live cohort. These are the numbers
we will publish, with their definitions. The first update follows the first cohort's close."*
The Green Belt slots have a second sentence, because their clocks are long: *"Closure and
sustainment figures lag a cohort's start by up to nine and twelve months; those slots stay at
'not yet reportable' until the first cohort's windows close, and a figure that arrives sooner
was not measured the way its definition says."* A page that goes live before the first sale
with every slot empty is what makes the same page believable when the slots fill.

## Review cadence

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Per cohort (week 9) | L1 and exam results to the assessment lead; corporate: items 8–12 by department to the executive sponsor; accessibility follow-ups closed |
| Per cohort (closure deadline, month 6) | Cohort report and quarterly business review per the corporate playbook §10: closure rate, sponsor-verified projects, Finance-validated benefit by class over measured windows, L3 gate figures, ★ failure profile; sustainment rate added at closure + 90 days |
| Monthly | Item statistics where a form has reached 100 administrations; flagged items to the assessment lead; open-file movement and the "no movement" list to coaches; separation-audit sample drawn |
| Quarterly | Reviewer calibration on the anchor set; coach–reviewer ★ agreement published to the cohort's instructor and reviewers; separation-audit findings closed; F1 free-text themes to content decisions; outcomes page updated |
| Annually | 10% re-review and its published figures; full content review against the BoK crosswalk; Angoff re-run if more than 30% of the bank has turned over; sustainment reason categories read against week 8; Practicum-track comparison read against the practicum file |
