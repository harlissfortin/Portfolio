# White Belt — Corporate Rollout Playbook

For an all-employee or all-department White Belt deployment. The product sold is not 400
seats of e-learning; it is **a functioning problem-surfacing capability plus its first
inventory of problems.** This playbook is how that gets delivered — and it is also the
mechanism that generates the Yellow and Green Belt pipeline.

---

## 1. Readiness gate (before any seat is provisioned)

Four conditions. A rollout that launches without them produces completion statistics and no
capability, and it burns the client's appetite for the next level.

| Condition | Evidence | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| **A named response owner** — who reads observations and acts | Named individual with time allocated (rule of thumb: 2 hrs per 100 submissions) | Do not launch. Offer a phased rollout by department instead |
| **A stated response cadence** — how themes get reported back | Cadence in writing, e.g. monthly themes to all staff | Do not launch |
| **A visible executive sponsor** willing to make the commitment in the kickoff | Confirmed for the kickoff, briefed | Delay the kickoff, not the sale |
| **A decision on learner time** — work hours or own time | Written answer, communicated in the launch comms | Resolve first; it is the top completion-rate factor |

Stated plainly to the buyer: this program will not run a rollout that has no answer for
"what happens to what people report." That refusal is a differentiator, not a lost sale — it
is what prevents the failure the client would otherwise blame on the training.

## 2. Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Client senior leader | The kickoff commitment; receives theme reports; unblocks |
| Response owner / CI lead | Client | Reads submissions, routes and triages, reports themes back |
| Manager (each) | Client line managers | Enables time, receives the manager brief (§5), does not review submissions |
| Program engagement lead | Program | Rollout plan, kickoff, dashboards, QBR, reviewer calibration |
| Reviewer(s) | Program and/or client CI lead | Field-exercise rubric review (calibrated) |

## 3. Timeline (typical 6-week rollout, 200–1,000 learners)

| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| −3 | Readiness gate signed. Vertical variant chosen. LMS integration and SSO tested. Cohort list loaded |
| −2 | Sponsor and CI lead briefed. Manager brief distributed. Reviewer calibration run |
| −1 | Launch comms from the sponsor (not from L&D, and not from the vendor). Pre-assessment opens |
| 1 | **Kickoff webinar.** Modules open. Help channel staffed |
| 2–4 | Completion nudges at day 7 / 14 / 21 (§4), each carrying one go-see prompt ("this week: entry 5 — find one thing waiting"). Submissions begin arriving; CI lead triages weekly |
| 3 | **First theme report back to all staff** — the single highest-value action in the rollout. Built from the submissions, which are the one-per-learner public face of the [process dossiers](../templates/process-dossier.md) every learner has been keeping since Module 1; a forty-person cohort is a forty-item problem inventory by this week |
| 5 | Deadline week. Manager-level completion visibility (completion only, never content) |
| 6 | Badges issued. Rollout report and QBR. Yellow Belt nomination conversation |

## 4. Completion mechanics

Benchmarks for a rollout that has passed the readiness gate: **≥ 85% completion** by the
deadline, **≥ 90% of completers submitting a usable observation** (rubric ≥ 5/8 on sampled
review). Below 70% completion, the cause is nearly always one of: no answer on learner time,
no manager enablement, or no visible response to early submissions — diagnose in that order.

Nudge sequence (from the CI lead's name, not the platform's), each ending with the current
go-see task — the dossier is where transfer happens, and a learner who is three modules in but
has done no go-see is a learner who will submit a generic observation: day 7 encouragement
with the completion rate so far; day 14 a peer observation of the week ("here's one your colleague
submitted, and here's what happened with it"); day 21 deadline and a direct offer of help.
**Never** a nudge that shames or copies a manager on an individual's status.

## 5. Manager brief (one page, distributed at week −2)

What line managers are told, verbatim in substance:

> **What your team is doing.** A 3–4 hour awareness course plus one written observation of a
> process problem they see in their own work.
>
> **What you need to do.** Three things: protect the time (say out loud that it counts as
> work); ask about it in your team meeting once ("what did you notice?"); and respond to what
> you hear with curiosity, not defensiveness.
>
> **What you must not do.** Do not review, edit, approve, or pre-screen your team's
> observations. Do not use them in performance conversations. Do not treat an observation
> about a process you own as a complaint about you — the course is explicit that most
> problems are process-designed, and that includes processes you inherited.
>
> **What you get.** A list of the specific, real friction in your area, written by the people
> who hit it daily — most managers report this is the most useful thing they receive all year.
>
> **The one sentence that makes this work.** When someone on your team surfaces something,
> the first words out of your mouth are *"thank you — tell me more."*

## 6. Observation gallery & rollout dashboard

The client-facing asset that converts an awareness rollout into a CI program.

**Gallery** (client-hosted or program-hosted): submissions displayed with the learner's name
(their choice at submission — attributed or anonymous), tagged by waste category, department,
and process. Filterable. Every entry carries a status: `new` → `read` → `triaged`
(`just-do-it` / `local PDCA` / `escalated to project` / `noted, not now` / `noted — not a
problem, here's why`) with a one-line reason. The last status exists because an observation
that turns out to be a misunderstanding still deserves an answer, not silence — it is the
same set the program uses on its own public improvement board. **A gallery without statuses is a suggestion box, and suggestion boxes teach people
to stop suggesting.**

**Dashboard metrics:**

| Metric | Why the buyer cares |
|---|---|
| Completion and badge rate, by department | Program delivery |
| Pre/post learning gain | Level-2 evidence |
| Observations submitted; % usable on rubric | Quality of the capability built |
| Waste-category distribution | Where the organization's waste concentrates |
| Department heat map | Where to aim the first Yellow/Green Belt work |
| **Triage status split, incl. % still `new` after 30 days** | The honest health metric — a rising `new` count predicts rollout death |
| Themes: clustered problem statements | The Yellow/Green Belt project pipeline |
| Estimated impact of `just-do-it` items closed | Early ROI, conservatively stated |

Impact figures are labeled **estimates from learner-reported frequency and time**, never
presented as validated savings. Validated financial impact starts at Green Belt with Finance
sign-off; overclaiming here is exactly how a program loses credibility with a CFO.

## 7. Rollout report & the next-level conversation (week 6)

The report: completion, learning gain, observation volume and quality, theme clusters, triage
status, actions taken with named examples, and three recommended next steps. Delivered as a
30-minute readout to the sponsor, not a PDF attachment.

The progression conversation writes itself from the data, and should be put in exactly these
terms: *"Your people found 412 problems. 180 are local fixes their supervisors can own —
that's Yellow Belt. 30 cluster into four cross-departmental problems with unknown causes and
real money attached — that's a Green Belt cohort, and here are the four candidate projects
with the sponsors they'd need."* Name the specific people whose observations and feedback
flagged them as Yellow/Green candidates (per the field-exercise rubric's invitation line).

## 8. Common rollout failure modes

| Failure | Signature | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Suggestion-box death | Submissions arrive, nothing visibly happens, second-wave volume collapses | Readiness gate + week-3 theme report + public triage statuses |
| Manager interception | Observations get "polished" or filtered before submission; volume suspiciously low in one department | Manager brief's explicit prohibition; submissions route to the CI lead, never through the line |
| Compliance theater | High completion, generic observations | Rubric sampling; the kickoff's "no way to fail by being honest" framing; nudges that share real examples |
| Weaponization | An observation cited in a performance review | Contracted prohibition; aggregated manager reporting; immediate escalation to the sponsor if it occurs — this one ends the program's credibility overnight if tolerated |
| Time ambiguity | Completion stalls at 50–60% | Readiness gate condition 4 |
