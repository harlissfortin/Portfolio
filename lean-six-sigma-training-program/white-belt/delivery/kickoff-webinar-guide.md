# White Belt Kickoff Webinar — Facilitator Guide

**Duration:** 60 minutes, live virtual · **Audience:** a corporate cohort starting White Belt
(works from 20 to 500+ attendees) · **Facilitator:** program instructor (GB+), with the
client's CI lead and one visible senior leader.

Optional for individual enrollment; **strongly recommended** for corporate rollouts, because
the thing that makes an all-employee awareness rollout succeed or fail is not the content —
it is whether employees believe that surfacing problems is safe and wanted.

**Outcomes.** Attendees leave (1) knowing why their employer is doing this and what happens
to what they report, (2) having seen a leader commit to responding, (3) having already
practiced one waste observation in the chat, (4) knowing their deadline and where to get help.

## Preparation checklist (T-minus)

- [ ] **2 weeks:** confirm the senior leader (see §"The leader's 5 minutes"). Brief them
      personally; do not send a slide and hope.
- [ ] **2 weeks:** agree with the CI lead what happens to submissions — who reads them, how
      often, and how feedback gets back. **Do not run this webinar without that answer.**
- [ ] **1 week:** LMS enrollment live; test the links; confirm the vertical variant.
- [ ] **1 week:** collect three real examples of process problems already known in the
      organization, to use in §3 (with permission and no blame attached).
- [ ] **Day of:** poll and chat tested; captions enabled; recording consent stated.

## Run of show

### 0:00–0:05 — Why you are here (CI lead)
Not a course overview. One honest sentence about the business situation and one about what
this is not: *"This isn't a cost-cutting exercise and it isn't about working faster. We are
asking 4,000 people to tell us where the work is badly designed."*

### 0:05–0:10 — The leader's 5 minutes (senior leader)
The most load-bearing five minutes of the rollout. The leader must say, in their own words,
three things:
1. **They expect problems to be surfaced** — and they are asking for it explicitly.
2. **A specific commitment on response** — who reads the observations, how often, and that
   themes will be reported back to the organization on a named cadence.
3. **One personal example** of a process that has frustrated them, ideally one they own.

Facilitator's job: brief them that a 5-minute "this is a strategic priority" speech with no
commitment and no example is worse than not appearing at all, because employees calibrate
safety from exactly this moment. If the leader will not make the response commitment, escalate
before the session — that is a rollout readiness problem, not a webinar problem.

### 0:10–0:20 — What the course is, and what it asks of you (facilitator)
- The 12-module map, the 3–4 hours, the deadline, the badge.
- The two gates: knowledge check (unlimited retakes, no time limit, not a trap) and the
  **See a Waste** exercise.
- Say plainly: *"There is no way to fail this by being honest. There is one way to waste it:
  filling in the observation with something generic to get the badge."*
- Where help lives: help channel, office hours, accessibility contact.

### 0:20–0:35 — Live practice: one waste, together (facilitator)
1. Teach the 8 wastes in 4 minutes — names and the two discriminators only (transportation
   moves the work / motion moves the worker; overproduction breeds the rest).
2. Show one of the three real organizational examples collected in prep. Ask the chat to name
   the wastes. Affirm the range of answers; show that several are defensible.
3. **Chat-storm:** "One waste you have seen this week — facts only, no names." Expect a flood.
4. Pick 3–4 from the chat and coach them live into good observations — *this is the teaching
   moment.* Model the rewrite: strip the blame, add the affected party, name the waste.
   Thank each contributor by name.

### 0:35–0:45 — What happens to what you report (CI lead)
The section most kickoffs skip and the reason most rollouts die. Concretely:
- Who reads submissions, and when.
- How themes are grouped and reported back, on what cadence, to whom.
- What kinds of things will get acted on quickly (local, cheap, obvious) versus escalated to
  a Yellow/Green Belt project — and that "escalated" will be visible, not a black hole.
- What will **not** happen: no individual attribution to a manager, no performance use, no
  one penalized for what they report. Say the safety-hazard route too.

### 0:45–0:55 — Q&A
Pre-seed three questions, because the first real one never comes unprompted:
- *"What if the waste I see is my own manager's decision?"* — Answer honestly: describe the
  process, not the person; that is exactly why the form forbids naming people.
- *"What if nothing happens with mine?"* — The response commitment, restated, plus the
  cadence.
- *"Do I have to do this on my own time?"* — Client-specific; **get this answer before the
  session.** An unresolved answer here quietly sinks completion rates.

### 0:55–1:00 — Close (facilitator)
Deadline, first step ("module 1 takes 15 minutes — do it today"), and a named human to ask
for help. End with the leader's commitment repeated once, by the facilitator, so it is the
last thing heard.

## Facilitator notes
- **Attendance is not the metric; the chat-storm is.** A silent chat in §3 means safety has
  not been established — slow down, put your own bad example up, and let the CI lead respond
  to one live. Completion rates track this moment more closely than they track deadlines.
- **Do not let this become a tools lecture.** You are teaching one skill (observe without
  blaming) and selling one belief (it is safe and it matters). Everything else is in the course.
- **Record it** and make the recording the standard onboarding asset for new hires and for
  anyone who missed it; the leader's five minutes is the part that matters in the recording.
- **Large audiences (200+):** keep everything the same; use chat only (no breakouts) and add
  a second facilitator purely to triage chat so no contribution goes unacknowledged.
