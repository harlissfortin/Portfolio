# Your Process Dossier

*One page that grows with you through the course. Every module ends with a five-minute
"go see" task in your own workplace — Lean calls this **gemba**, the real place where the
work happens, and *genchi genbutsu*, "go and see for yourself" — and each one adds a line here. By Module 12 you will have
found your first three improvement opportunities — before you have submitted anything.*

**This is yours.** It is saved on your device (or in your workbook, if you print it) and is
never transmitted by the course. Only the entry you choose to submit in Module 12 leaves this
page, and only when you submit it. In a corporate rollout your organization's CI lead sees
submissions, never dossiers.

## The twelve entries

Each task takes about five minutes and is done *in the workplace*, not at the screen.
Write in facts. If you would not say it with the person standing next to you, rewrite it.

| # | After module | Go see (five minutes) | Your entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why Processes Fail People | Take the process version from your Module 1 reflection to the place where it happens. Stand there for five minutes and write down one thing you can see that makes the problem likely. | |
| 2 | What Is a Process? | For the process you touch most: name its customer (the next person who receives your output) and one thing they need from it to be "good". | |
| 3 | TPS, Deming, and Variation | Find one thing in your work that varies from day to day — arrival time, order size, how long a step takes. Write down the range you have seen. | |
| 4 | Value-Added vs. NVA | Watch one work item for five minutes. Estimate how much of that time it was being *changed in a way the customer would pay for* — not inspected, moved, or waited on. | |
| 5 | The 8 Wastes: D-O-W-N | Find one example of **Waiting** you can see from where you stand. Who or what waits, and for what? | |
| 6 | The 8 Wastes: T-I-M-E | Find one example of **Motion** (a person moving) and one of **Transportation** (a work item moving). | |
| 7 | Waste in Your World | Take the anchor story's elapsed-vs-value question to one of your own items: how long does it take start to finish, and how much of that is work? | |

## Where this page goes next

- **Your submission (Module 12).** One entry, in the four-part form, goes through the
  program's completeness gate and rubric review. That is the White Belt applied artifact.
- **Your first three opportunities.** Entries 5, 6 and 8 are usually three distinct,
  small, fixable things. You found them. Most people finish an awareness course with none.
- **Yellow Belt.** The ★ item is the seed of your Yellow Belt mini-project. The storyboard
  template asks for it by name ("From your White Belt dossier: ___"). Bring the whole page.
- **Corporate rollouts.** The CI lead sees only what you submit. But a cohort of forty
  dossiers, each with one submitted entry, is a forty-item problem inventory on day one of
  the rollout — the playbook's week-3 theme report is built from it.

## A filled entry, so you can see the size of it

> **Entry 5 (Waiting), office version:** "Signed contracts wait in the shared inbox for
> the one person with the ERP customer-setup permission. Tuesday's batch: four contracts,
> first one opened after 3 hours 10 minutes. Sales is the customer of this step."
>
> Three lines. A place, a count, a customer. That is a complete entry.

## Rules the course keeps

1. The dossier is never scored. The one submitted entry is.
2. No entry names a person as a cause. The fairness self-check applies to every line.
3. **Go see safely and lawfully.** Follow your site's rules for being on the floor, in a
   ward, or in a restricted area — PPE, permissions, escort. If you cannot stand there
   safely, observe from where you are allowed to be. Never record a patient, customer, or
   employee identifier; describe the step, not the person.
4. If an entry reveals a safety hazard, do not wait for Module 12: use your organization's
   reporting channel today. The course will thank you for it. The on-screen form recognizes
   hazard language and tells you the same thing.
