# Waste Walk Checklist

*A guided 30-minute observation of one process area. Used in corporate White Belt rollouts
(often as a team activity after Module 7) and by anyone who wants a structured way to look.*

## Before you walk (5 min)
- [ ] Pick **one** process area, physical or digital (a cell, a unit, a queue, a workflow).
- [ ] Tell the people working there what you're doing and why: *"I'm learning to see process
      waste — I'm observing the process, not evaluating people."* Ask permission where
      appropriate.
- [ ] Bring: this checklist, the 8-wastes pocket card, something to write with. **Not** a
      clipboard-and-frown demeanor.

## Ground rules
1. **Observe the work, not the workers.** You are watching how the process treats people.
2. **Ask, don't tell.** The people in the process are the experts; your job is curiosity.
   Best question: *"What gets in your way the most?"*
3. **No fixing today.** Ideas go in the notes column, not into the room.
4. **Facts only.** Write what a camera would record.

## During the walk (20 min)

Follow one work item (an order, a patient, a ticket, a part) or watch one station. For each
observation, note the time, what you saw, and the waste category.

Prompts while watching:
- [ ] Where did the work item **wait**, and roughly how long?
- [ ] Did anyone **search** for tools, information, or people?
- [ ] Was anything **corrected, redone, or clarified**?
- [ ] How many **handoffs** (people or systems) did you count?
- [ ] What was **piling up**, and where?
- [ ] What did a worker do that the customer of the step **wouldn't pay for**?
- [ ] What did someone tell you they've **been saying for years**? ← write that one down verbatim

## After the walk (5 min)
- [ ] Circle your **top one or two** observations — highest frequency or biggest impact.
- [ ] Convert your best one into a **"See a Waste" submission** (see `see-a-waste.md`).
- [ ] Thank the team, and tell them the one thing you learned from them. Close the loop later
      on anything that gets acted on — nothing kills future candor like observations that
      vanish into a void.
