# White Belt — Vertical Variant Scripts (Modules 5–7)

Modules 5, 6 and 7 ship in three flavors. The teaching skeleton in
[`modules.md`](modules.md) is identical across all three; **only the walkthrough footage,
the click-targets, and the anchor story change.** This document is the production script for
each variant: what is filmed, what the learner clicks, and what the reveal says.

Corporate deployments are provisioned with one default flavor; individual learners choose at
enrollment and may switch (progress carries — the underlying module IDs are shared).

**Production note.** Each variant needs one continuous 3-minute walkthrough, filmed once and
cut into two segments: Segment A for Module 5 (D-O-W-N) and Segment B for Module 6
(T-I-M-E). Module 7 re-uses the same location for the anchor story, told over stills and a
running clock graphic rather than new footage. One shoot day per vertical.

**Consent and safety.** Everyone on camera signs a release; no patient, customer, or client
data is visible (healthcare footage uses a simulation suite or a closed clinic with actors);
nothing filmed identifies a real defect attributable to a named person. Any real employer
appearing on camera approves the final cut.

## Variant MFG — Manufacturing & Supply Chain

**Location:** a small machining and assembly cell making a metal bracket, plus its
adjacent staging area and shipping bench.

### Segment A — Module 5 click-targets (D-O-W-N)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 0:12 | Operator sets a finished bracket in a "REWORK" tote already holding a dozen | **Defects** | "That tote is a defect record. Twelve brackets means twelve times the process produced something unusable — plus the time to fix each one." |
| A2 | 0:31 | Machine runs a 200-piece batch; the whiteboard order quantity reads 120 | **Overproduction** | "Eighty extra brackets nobody ordered — made because the machine was already set up. They now need storing, moving, and possibly reworking." |
| A3 | 0:58 | Operator stands at an idle machine, looking toward the staging area | **Waiting** | "The operator is waiting on material. Idle time here isn't laziness — it is a scheduling and supply design outcome." |
| A4 | 1:24 | Operator adjusts a fixture with a shim, unprompted, then continues | **Non-utilized talent** | "She solved a fixture problem with a shim she cut herself — and nobody has ever asked her about it. That fix has lived in her toolbox for two years." |

### Segment B — Module 6 click-targets (T-I-M-E)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 1:48 | Pallet of brackets loaded on a cart and wheeled out the bay door toward another building | **Transportation** | "The work item is moving between buildings. Nothing about the bracket improved on that trip." |
| B2 | 2:05 | Aisle stacked with cartons, min/max lines nowhere visible | **Inventory** | "A month of cartons 'just in case.' It ties up cash and space — and if one carton is defective, nobody will find out for weeks." |
| B3 | 2:26 | Operator crosses the cell to a shared tool board and back, twice | **Motion** | "The worker moves, the work doesn't. Forty trips a shift is a designed-in walk." |
| B4 | 2:44 | Operator deburrs an internal surface to a mirror finish; drawing shows no finish spec | **Extra-processing** | "Beautiful work the customer never sees and never asked for." |

### Module 7 anchor story — "One bracket, eleven days"

Running clock over stills. Order received Monday 09:00 → **11 days elapsed**, **47 minutes
value-added**.

| Stage | Elapsed | What happened | Wastes accumulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order entry → release | 2 days | Sat in the planner's queue awaiting a weekly release run | Waiting |
| Material staging | 1.5 days | Pulled to staging, then moved twice as the schedule changed | Waiting, Transportation, Inventory |
| Machining | 22 min VA | Run in a batch of 200 against an order of 120 | Overproduction |
| First inspection | 1 day | Waited for the inspector's morning round | Waiting |
| Rework | 3 hrs | Two of the batch out of position; fixture clamp had slipped | Defects, Extra-processing |
| Deburr & finish | 18 min VA | Includes the unspecified mirror finish | Extra-processing |
| Transfer to assembly building | 0.5 day | Cart, bay door, other building | Transportation |
| Assembly & pack | 7 min VA | — | — |
| Awaiting carrier | 4 days | Shipped on the twice-weekly consolidation | Waiting, Inventory |

**Closing beat:** "Eleven days. Forty-seven minutes. Nobody in this story did a bad job. The
gap between those two numbers is not a people problem — and it is the reason this course
exists."

## Variant HC — Healthcare

**Location:** an outpatient clinic — reception, waiting area, vitals alcove, exam rooms,
supply room, and the specimen hand-off point. Filmed in a simulation suite or closed clinic
with actors.

### Segment A — Module 5 click-targets (D-O-W-N)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 0:15 | MA re-labels a specimen tube after checking the requisition twice | **Defects** | "The first label was wrong. Every mislabeled tube is either a re-draw for the patient or a result attached to the wrong chart." |
| A2 | 0:34 | Printer produces a 6-page packet; MA hands the patient one page and recycles the rest | **Overproduction** | "Six pages printed, one used — every visit, every patient." |
| A3 | 0:52 | Waiting room; wall clock shows 10:20 against a 09:45 appointment card in the patient's hand | **Waiting** | "Thirty-five minutes so far. The patient experiences this as the visit." |
| A4 | 1:20 | Bilingual front-desk clerk interprets briefly, then returns to typing demographics | **Non-utilized talent** | "She interprets fluently and spends her day on data entry — while the clinic pays for a phone interpreter line." |

### Segment B — Module 6 click-targets (T-I-M-E)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 1:45 | Specimen rack carried down a corridor to a courier pickup point | **Transportation** | "The specimen is the work item, and it is traveling. Three trips a day, each with a chance of delay or loss." |
| B2 | 2:02 | Supply room shelf overflowing with one item; two other bins empty | **Inventory** | "Over-stocked here, stocked out there. The pile isn't abundance — it's a signal the replenishment method is guessing." |
| B3 | 2:22 | Nurse leaves the exam room twice for gloves and a thermometer probe cover | **Motion** | "Two trips out of the room, per patient. Over a shift that's miles walked and minutes taken from care." |
| B4 | 2:41 | Clinician re-enters the same vitals into a second screen "for the report" | **Extra-processing** | "The data already exists. Typing it again adds nothing to the patient's care." |

### Module 7 anchor story — "One referral, thirty-one days"

Referral received → patient told their result. **31 days elapsed**, **34 minutes of clinical
value time.**

| Stage | Elapsed | What happened | Wastes accumulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral received → triaged | 4 days | Faxed referral sat in a shared inbox | Waiting, Extra-processing |
| Insurance verification | 3 days | Two callbacks; one missing field on the referral | Waiting, Defects |
| Scheduling | 5 days | Phone tag with the patient; no online option | Waiting, Motion |
| Waiting for appointment date | 9 days | Next available slot | Waiting |
| Check-in and vitals | 6 min VA | Patient also waited 35 min in the lobby | Waiting |
| Clinician encounter | 18 min VA | — | — |
| Specimen to lab | 1 day | Courier run, then a re-label | Transportation, Defects |
| Result available → reviewed | 6 days | Sat in the clinician's queue between clinic days | Waiting, Inventory |
| Result communicated | 10 min VA | Phone call, third attempt | Waiting |

**Closing beat:** "Thirty-one days of the patient's life. Thirty-four minutes of care. Every
person in this clinic worked hard today."

## Variant TXN — Transactional / Services

**Location:** a back-office floor — shared mailbox intake, an accounts-payable bench, an
approver's desk, and a dispute-resolution pod.

### Segment A — Module 5 click-targets (D-O-W-N)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 0:14 | Clerk opens a returned invoice flagged "WRONG PO" and retypes a field | **Defects** | "One mistyped field. It travels to the approver, comes back, and costs a day and a half of payment time." |
| A2 | 0:33 | Scheduled report generates and lands in a folder; analyst closes it unread | **Overproduction** | "Produced weekly for a distribution list. Nobody has opened it in a year." |
| A3 | 0:55 | Queue dashboard: 47 items in "Awaiting approval," oldest 4 days | **Waiting** | "Forty-seven work items sitting still. This queue, not anyone's typing speed, is where the lead time lives." |
| A4 | 1:22 | Clerk mentions to a colleague that the intake form "could just pull the PO automatically" | **Non-utilized talent** | "She has described the fix. She has described it for a year. No forum exists for her to be heard." |

### Segment B — Module 6 click-targets (T-I-M-E)

| # | Timecode | What the camera shows | Waste | Reveal text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 1:47 | Emailed invoice printed, carried to a second desk, keyed into the ERP | **Transportation** | "The work item moved from a system, to paper, to a desk, to another system. Every hop is a chance to drop it." |
| B2 | 2:04 | Inbox showing 600 unread; ticket board with an aging column past 3 days | **Inventory** | "Backlogs are inventory. The pile hides which single item is actually on fire." |
| B3 | 2:23 | Rep tabs through five systems to answer one routine call | **Motion** | "Nobody walked anywhere. Twenty-two clicks is still the worker moving instead of the work advancing." |
| B4 | 2:42 | Three signatures collected on a $40 order; each approver signs without reading | **Extra-processing** | "Three signatures, zero scrutiny. A control that controls nothing still costs a day." |

### Module 7 anchor story — "One dispute, nineteen days"

Customer emails a disputed invoice → dispute resolved. **19 days elapsed**, **22 minutes of
actual investigation.**

| Stage | Elapsed | What happened | Wastes accumulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email → logged as a case | 2 days | Landed in a shared mailbox checked twice daily | Waiting, Inventory |
| Assignment | 1 day | Round-robin at the next stand-up | Waiting |
| First investigation | 14 min VA | Analyst pulls the order and the invoice | — |
| Awaiting internal answer | 6 days | Emailed the warehouse; no owner, no SLA, one chase | Waiting, Non-utilized talent |
| Re-investigation | 8 min VA | Analyst re-reads the case from scratch, having lost context | Extra-processing |
| Credit note prepared | 1 day | Keyed into two systems | Transportation, Extra-processing |
| Approval queue | 4 days | Three signatures, under threshold | Waiting, Extra-processing |
| Rejected on a wrong field | 2 days | Corrected and resubmitted | Defects |
| Customer notified | 3 days | Batch communication run | Waiting |

**Closing beat:** "Nineteen days for the customer. Twenty-two minutes of thinking. The
customer is not angry about the twenty-two minutes."

## Cross-variant consistency rules

1. **Same wastes, same order, same count.** Every variant shows all eight wastes, four per
   segment, in DOWNTIME order. A learner who switches verticals loses nothing.
2. **One "invisible" and one "obvious" instance each.** A2, A4, B2, B4 are the subtle ones;
   the module 7 anchor story is where the subtle ones pay off.
3. **The talent waste is always a real idea from a real frontline worker**, never a
   caricature. It is the emotional pivot of Module 5 and the setup for Module 11.
4. **No one on camera is shown doing a bad job.** Every waste is traceable to a design
   decision — layout, schedule, form, system, or policy. If a click-target reads as "that
   person was careless," the shot is re-cut. This rule is not cosmetic: Module 1's entire
   claim collapses if the footage contradicts it.
5. **Elapsed-to-value ratio stays under 1%** in every anchor story, because that is the
   honest finding — and it is checked against a real observed process before the shoot, not
   invented to be dramatic.
