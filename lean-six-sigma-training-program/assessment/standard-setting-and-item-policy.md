# Assessment Standards — Standard Setting, Item Writing & Bank Management

Program-wide policy governing every certification exam and knowledge check (White Belt
through Black Belt). These are the mechanics that make the credential defensible: a pass
mark set by a documented method, item banks large enough to make retakes meaningful, and
items that are tagged, reviewed, and statistically monitored.

---

## 1. Standard setting — how cut scores are established

Cut scores are **never** set by convention ("70% sounds right"). Every certification exam's
pass mark is set by a **modified Angoff** procedure, documented, and re-run when the bank
changes materially.

### 1.1 Panel
- 6–8 judges: at least two Master Black Belts, two Black Belts, two Green Belts working as
  practitioners (not trainers), and one learning-and-development professional from a
  client organization. Mix of the three verticals.
- Judges must not have authored the items they rate.
- Panel chair (program assessment lead) facilitates and does not rate.

### 1.2 Procedure
1. **Define the minimally competent candidate (MCC)** for the level, in writing, before any
   item is seen. Example (Yellow Belt): *"Can serve reliably on a DMAIC team — map a real
   process, collect and chart data honestly, contribute checkable causes — but would not be
   expected to lead a project or select a statistical test."* The MCC statement is
   published with the exam blueprint.
2. **Round 1 — independent ratings.** For each item, each judge estimates the probability
   (0.05–0.95, in 0.05 steps) that an MCC answers it correctly.
3. **Discussion.** Items with a rating spread > 0.30 across judges are discussed; the
   chair shares empirical item difficulty (p-value) from pilot data where available.
4. **Round 2 — revised independent ratings.** Judges may change any rating.
5. **Cut score** = mean across judges of the sum of round-2 item probabilities, expressed
   as a percentage of the form. Report the standard error of the cut (SD of judges' sums /
   √judges); if the SE exceeds 2 items, add judges or re-run.
6. **Policy adjustment.** The program may shift the cut by at most ±1 standard error of
   measurement of the exam, only with a written rationale (e.g., consequences of
   false-pass at Black Belt).
7. **Documentation:** MCC statement, panel roster, both rounds' ratings, final cut,
   rationale, and date — retained for audit and summarized on the public methodology page.

### 1.3 Re-running
- Whenever > 30% of a bank's active items are replaced.
- At least every three years per level.
- Whenever the blueprint (section weights) changes.

### 1.4 Reporting the cut
Cut scores are published as "set by modified Angoff, panel of N, [month year]" rather
than as bare percentages. Current provisional cuts (pending first panel):
White 70% · Yellow 75% · Green 80% · Black 80%. **These are placeholders and must be
replaced by panel-derived values before public certification at each level.**

## 2. Item writing standards

Every item is written to the following rules and reviewed by a second SME before entering
the active bank.

### 2.1 Structure
- One best answer; four options; no "all/none of the above"; no negative stems
  ("Which is NOT…") except where the discrimination genuinely requires it (limit 5% of bank).
- Stem carries the full problem; options are parallel in length and grammar; the correct
  option is not systematically the longest.
- Scenario items describe a concrete, plausible workplace situation with enough detail to
  decide, and ask for a judgment or application — never a definition in disguise.

### 2.2 Unambiguity rule (the waste-overlap problem)
Lean waste categories overlap in reality (a backlog is inventory *and* each item waits).
Items that depend on such overlaps are contestable and are **not permitted** unless the
stem contains an explicit discriminator ("*for the claim itself*," "*as a category, the
pile is*," "*the effort invested, not the copies*"). Reviewers reject any item where a
competent practitioner could defend a second option.

### 2.3 Numerical items
Every quantity states its units and basis (calendar vs. working hours, per day vs. per
shift). The distractor set must not contain the answer a reasonable alternative basis would
produce — if it does, the stem must close off that basis.

### 2.4 Required tags
Each item carries: **section** (blueprint area), **objective** (course objective code),
**Bloom level** (Remember / Understand / Apply / Analyze), **vertical** (MFG / HC / TXN /
NEU = neutral), **type** (K = knowledge, S = scenario), and **pool** (CERT / PRE / PRAC —
see §3.4). Tags drive form assembly; untagged items cannot be drawn.

### 2.5 Vertical rule
A certification form for a vertical-specific cohort draws only NEU items plus items in
that cohort's vertical. Open-enrollment forms draw NEU plus a balanced mix. Every section
must therefore carry enough NEU items to fill its quota alone.

## 3. Bank sizing, forms & exposure

### 3.1 Minimum bank size
Active CERT-pool bank ≥ **3.5 × form length**, so that two randomly assembled forms overlap
by ≤ 30% in expectation and a deliberate retake form can be built with ≤ 25% overlap.

| Level | Form length | Minimum CERT bank | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | 20 | 70 | 80 |
| Yellow | 40 | 140 | 160 |
| Green | 100 | 350 | 400 |
| Black | 150 | 525 | 600 |

Until a bank reaches its minimum, the level's retake policy is limited to **one** retake
built from unused items only, and this limitation is disclosed in the exam guide.

### 3.2 Form assembly
Forms are assembled to the blueprint (section quotas), with constraints: ≥ 50% scenario
(S) items at Yellow and above (≥ 40% at White); ≥ 50% of items at Apply or Analyze at
Yellow and above; vertical rule (§2.5); no two items sharing a scenario stem; correct-
answer position balanced (no letter > 35%).

### 3.3 Retakes
A retake form shares ≤ 25% of items with the candidate's prior attempt(s), never the items
the candidate answered incorrectly. Two included retakes at Yellow and above; further
attempts require a 30-day wait and the level's retake fee.

### 3.4 Pools
- **CERT** — certification-eligible items.
- **PRE** — pre-assessment items (10–12 per level): used before the course to enable
  test-out and to measure pre/post learning gain. Never appear on certification forms.
- **PRAC** — practice / cumulative-check items embedded in modules; may be retired CERT
  items. Never appear on certification forms.

## 4. Item analysis & maintenance

After every 100 administrations of a form (or quarterly, whichever first), compute per item:

| Statistic | Keep | Review | Retire / rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Difficulty (p-value) | 0.30–0.90 | 0.20–0.30 or 0.90–0.95 | < 0.20 or > 0.95 |
| Discrimination (point-biserial) | ≥ 0.20 | 0.10–0.20 | < 0.10, or negative |
| Distractor analysis | Every distractor chosen by ≥ 3% | One dead distractor | Two+ dead distractors |

Items flagged for review go to the assessment lead and one SME; decisions are logged.
Appeals: a candidate may challenge an item within 14 days; a sustained challenge credits
all candidates who saw the item and retires it.

---

## 5. Reliability & security targets
- Internal consistency (KR-20/Cronbach's α) ≥ 0.80 for Yellow and above; ≥ 0.70 for White.
- Proctoring: online proctoring at Green and above; at Yellow, identity verification plus
  exposure control; White is unproctored (awareness credential — stated on the badge).
- Item exposure: no CERT item appears on more than 40% of forms assembled in a quarter.

---

## 6. Learning-measurement policy (what the exams are for)
- **Pre/post gain** (PRE pool before, CERT form after) is reported per corporate cohort as
  the Level-2 learning evidence in the quarterly business review.
- **Cumulative checks** (PRAC pool) are embedded so that each module re-tests at least two
  earlier modules; the spacing is the learning design, not an add-on.
- Application evidence (field exercise, mini-project, projects) is the Level-3 evidence and
  is never substituted by exam performance.
